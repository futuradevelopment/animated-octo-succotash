# P2210: NOx heater-sense connection is shorted to ground

P2210 is the low-circuit fault for the heater-sense path of the engine-out NOx sensor. The reviewed GM row identifies the connection as HTemp and describes a short to ground. It is distinct from the heater-control ground-short code, even though both concern the same sensor assembly.

## Sense and control require different evidence

The HTemp path supports heater-resistance measurement. P2210 does not directly test NOx concentration or prove that the heater-control output is grounded. A generic heater-current measurement therefore cannot independently settle the specific sense-circuit diagnosis.

For Continental generation 3.5 sensors, the source notes an internal 1.5-second electrical-fault debounce used to identify the affected wire condition. The ECM then uses its own counter of 80 failures within 160 samples at a 25-millisecond task rate. These are distinct stages; the hardware-specific debounce must not be assumed for all NOx sensors.

## Conditions and confirmation

Dewpoint must be reached, the NOx bus relay commanded on, relay voltage above 11 volts and sensor supply above 9.90 volts. Lost communication and invalid CAN-data faults are excluded. Reporting is Type B over two trips.

Verification should allow valid sensor reporting and controller qualification under those conditions, then confirm the HTemp ground-short status is absent. A no-result observation during communication loss or early warm-up is inconclusive. The page supplies no physical pin number or component-replacement instruction. Exact sensor generation, vehicle applicability and safe electrical access require the appropriate service information.
