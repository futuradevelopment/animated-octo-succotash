# P200A: key-off swirl-flap learning and response checks

P200A is an intake-manifold runner performance code, but the reviewed GM strategy performs its checks at key-off. It evaluates learned end positions and whether the swirl valve moves too slowly. A normal-looking running position does not necessarily exercise either branch.

## Endpoint offset and slow movement are separate

The endpoint branch compares the SENT position representation with calibrated fully closed and wide-open windows. The published closed window is 11.70–17.60% of the 5-volt scale; the open window is 84.25–91.33%. A learned endpoint outside its window supports an offset judgment rather than an ordinary open/short diagnosis.

The continuation adds slow-response checks: opening or closing response beyond 0.50 second, and a separate overall test-phase time limit of 10 seconds. The latter row has inconsistent comparison wording, so the precise boundary should be taken from applicable service data rather than inferred here.

## Completing the appropriate test

The key-off learning or slow-response procedure must complete successfully. Listed prerequisites include coolant temperature from 30°C to 150°C, outside-air temperature at least −40°C, relay supply at least 9.5 volts and no relevant sensor, actuator or position-deviation faults. The end-of-trip event must have elapsed.

No additional debounce is listed once the qualifying error is present; reporting is Type B over two trips. Verification must distinguish endpoint learning from movement speed and allow the relevant key-off procedure to run. This table does not establish a failed part or universal model coverage, and its inconsistent overall-time comparator remains a source limitation.
