# P2206: NOx heater-control short to ground after dewpoint

P2206 is the low-circuit diagnosis for the engine-out NOx sensor's heater-control connection. In the reviewed GM table, its specific fault mechanism is a short to ground on the heater-control pin. It does not mean that the NOx concentration itself is low.

## Dewpoint is an enable condition

The source requires the sensor's dewpoint condition to be reached before this short-to-ground monitor is eligible. That differs from the adjacent heater-control open-circuit row, which does not list the same gate. A quick key-on check may therefore not exercise P2206 even when communication and supply appear normal.

Other prerequisites are powertrain-relay voltage above 11 volts, sensor supply above 9.90 volts and a commanded-on NOx bus relay. Lost communication and invalid CAN-frame faults must be absent.

## Qualification and interpretation

The counter needs 80 failures within 160 samples at 25 milliseconds per task, with Type B reporting over two trips. These sample counts describe qualification of the electrical status; they do not identify where the ground path lies or prove a failed sensor assembly.

Verification should confirm that the dewpoint and electrical prerequisites permit the heater-control check, then examine its result. A normal heater-temperature reading on another diagnostic channel cannot independently prove this control connection is healthy. The source supplies no pin-voltage limit or circuit isolation procedure. Apply the monitor facts only after confirming the installed sensor system and vehicle-specific service information; the generic code alone does not establish model coverage.
