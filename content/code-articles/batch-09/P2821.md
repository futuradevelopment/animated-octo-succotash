# P2821: how a park-lock valve is diagnosed hydraulically stuck on

P2821 has a generic solenoid-J stuck-on label, but the cited 25OBDG07A Part 2 table describes the park-lock engagement valve. Its diagnostic uses commanded hydraulic states and park-lock position, rather than assuming that an electrical circuit code proves a stuck valve.

## A controlled release sequence

During the documented disengagement routine, the controller first opens the parking lock using both hydraulic valves. It then energizes the hold solenoid so the lock remains mechanically held away from engagement if piston pressure is removed. The engagement valve is commanded hydraulic-off while the latching valve remains hydraulic-on.

The expected position changes from open to hold. If the sensor continues reporting open after the engagement-valve off test, the controller diagnoses a hydraulic stuck-on response. The listed confirmation timer exceeds 200 milliseconds. Conditions include vehicle speed no greater than 3 km/h, oil temperature no higher than 120°C and battery voltage at least 10 volts. Relevant park-system electrical faults must be absent.

## Read position and command together

An “open” park-lock position is normally desirable when driving, but becomes the failure observation during this specific pressure-draining step. Looking at position without the active routine can therefore reverse the interpretation. The hold solenoid also explains why an off command does not require the parking lock to become fully locked in this test.

The source describes additional engagement strategies on following pages; their criteria should not be merged into this release sequence. Professional diagnosis should reproduce the correct routine safely and confirm the expected position transition before declaring the hydraulic behavior restored.
