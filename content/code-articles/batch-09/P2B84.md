# P2B84: understanding the pump underspeed monitor without misusing its threshold

P2B84 is the charge-air cooler coolant pump underspeed diagnosis in the cited table. The strategy says it detects an out-of-range low actual pump speed. Its printed threshold is negative, below −115 rpm, rather than a conventional positive minimum running speed.

## Why the printed number needs care

A negative value in a speed-reporting system can require knowledge of signal encoding or diagnostic conventions. The supplied table does not explain that representation. It would therefore be unsafe to turn this entry into a universal claim that the pump must physically rotate backward, or that a pump turning slowly at a positive speed necessarily sets this particular code.

The documented decision requires eight failing samples out of ten at one-second intervals. The pump must be enabled, its hardware configured as present, the engine out of cranking and the diagnostic system active. Pump communication-loss and response-fault conditions must not be active.

## Useful evidence despite the limitation

Preserve the raw reported speed, pump command and communication status. Determine how the applicable controller encodes stopped, invalid and faulted speed before interpreting the number as mechanical motion. This is especially useful when a scan tool normalizes a raw value into a simple zero-rpm display.

Verification should follow the exact calibration's speed-reporting definition and show a completed underspeed diagnostic with valid feedback. The published table supports a range-monitor explanation but does not establish a universal pump output test or replacement decision. The unresolved negative threshold is explicitly retained as a source-review limitation.
