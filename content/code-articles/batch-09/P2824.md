# P2824: T93 default-disable valve open-circuit detection

In the cited GM table, P2824 is expressly limited to the T93 controller and the ten-speed default-disable control circuit. The generic pressure-control-solenoid-J label conceals that useful restriction. This monitor evaluates electrical voltage behavior associated with an open circuit; it does not directly test whether the valve moves hydraulically.

## How the decision is qualified

The controller's open-circuit classification corresponds to very high circuit impedance, listed above 200 kilohms. That is a controller fault criterion, not permission to apply a resistance test to a connected powered module. The table accumulates more than 0.30 seconds of failure during a sample longer than 0.50 seconds, with a 6.25-millisecond update interval.

The assigned high-side driver must be on. Battery voltage must exceed 9 volts and remain below 32 volts, and the calibration must enable the monitor. Thus, an ignition-off continuity observation and the actual powered diagnostic are different pieces of evidence.

## Useful next decisions

First establish that the vehicle actually has the documented T93 arrangement. Next determine whether the circuit fails when the relevant driver is enabled; an inactive supply can make an otherwise intact circuit appear electrically absent if the measurement is interpreted without its operating state. Use controller-specific service information to separate a disconnected load, interrupted wiring and internal driver behavior.

A successful verification should include a completed open-circuit monitor with normal driver operation. Merely clearing the code or observing normal gear engagement does not demonstrate that this particular electrical path now satisfies its diagnostic.
