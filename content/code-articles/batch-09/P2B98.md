# P2B98: low supply to the exhaust pressure valve motor

The cited P2B98 monitor asks whether the exhaust throttle valve DC motor is correctly supplied. Its failure criterion is driver/system supply below 6 volts, reported by the hardware input/output layer. Separately, the powertrain relay supply must be above 11 volts for the diagnostic to operate.

## The two supplies are different evidence

The comparison of local motor supply with the qualified relay supply is more informative than the phrase “low voltage” alone. A healthy upstream voltage does not prove that the motor driver receives adequate power. Conversely, a reading taken during cranking is outside this monitor's listed operating phase.

Confirmation requires 240 failed counts out of 300 samples at a 12.5-millisecond task interval. The hardware error status must be determinate, and no diagnostic reset or end-of-trip inhibition may prevent evaluation.

## Use supply evidence before judging the valve

A motor unable to obtain adequate supply may not respond correctly to a position request. Establish the supply behavior before treating a stationary exhaust valve as proof of a mechanical jam. The table does not isolate the voltage loss to a particular connector, wiring segment or internal electronic component.

Verification should demonstrate adequate motor supply during commanded operation with a valid hardware status and a completed monitor. A battery voltage reading taken elsewhere or with no motor demand does not answer the local supply question. Any access around the exhaust actuator must follow the relevant hot-component and moving-actuator service precautions.
