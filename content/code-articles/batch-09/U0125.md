# U0125: missing inertial data rather than a generic transmission timeout

The U0125 row is headed lost communication with the inertial sensor. Its monitored signals include initialization, voltage, calibration and temperature status, plus primary/secondary lateral acceleration, longitudinal acceleration and yaw information. The listed timeout for those signals exceeds 0.050 seconds.

## Follow the actual signal list

One strategy sentence mistakenly refers to a transmission, but the component heading and HPID signal family identify inertial sensing. This article follows those specific data fields rather than expanding the apparent copy/paste sentence into a transmission diagnosis.

The monitor requires supply between 9 and 16 volts, an active participating partial network and enabled calibration. Transport mode, the first five seconds after reset or voltage recovery, and an active bus-off condition are exceptions. The source states that fault detection disables adaptive cruise control.

## Availability is separate from sensor accuracy

Missing acceleration or yaw messages is different from receiving a biased acceleration or yaw value. Inspect whether the protected inertial stream arrives on time before evaluating its physical plausibility. A cluster of primary and secondary signal timeouts can reflect loss of their common message path rather than simultaneous failure of every sensing axis.

Verification should demonstrate timely delivery of the required inertial/status signals after startup qualification. A static zero reading on a scan tool does not establish that fresh messages are arriving. Professional service information remains necessary for network, calibration and safety-function checks; this code does not determine vehicle stability-system performance by itself.
