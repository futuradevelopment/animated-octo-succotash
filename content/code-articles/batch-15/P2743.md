# P2743: A high cooler-temperature circuit does not prove hot fluid

The P2743 monitor checks the cooler temperature input for an electrically high value. GM’s narrative and units describe voltage, with a threshold above 4,745 mV and 5 mV hysteresis. The criterion’s label says resistance, creating a source inconsistency that should not be silently converted into an ohmmeter specification.

## Persistence and supply context

The input must meet a 500 ms confirmation interval. Monitoring is enabled with battery voltage above 8.75 V and below 18 V, outside cranking and stop-key states. P0882 or P0883 disables the check. These conditions distinguish an eligible sensor-input result from an observation during a broader controller supply problem.

“Circuit high” is not evidence that the transmission oil is excessively hot. The source is rejecting an electrical input, not reporting a validated fluid-temperature measurement.

## What a verification record needs

Keep the raw signal and supply voltage together with whether the monitor actually completed. A plausible temperature value seen later does not exclude an earlier high electrical interval. Resolve the ambiguous column label through applicable service information before testing hardware. The DTC alone does not identify the cooler sensor, wiring or controller as the proven failed part.
