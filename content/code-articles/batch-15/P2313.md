# P2313: Interpreting an ignition output that stays high when commanded on

The important observation for P2313 is a high voltage during an **on** command to the ignition low-side driver. In the cited GM strategy, that combination is classified as a short-to-power condition. Merely finding voltage on a coil connector with the engine stopped does not reproduce the monitored event.

## What makes an observation comparable

GM lists ignition synchronization, engine speed above 1,400 rpm and sufficient ignition-counter progress to ensure firing events occurred. The fault interval is 0.4 seconds and reporting is two-trip. Verification therefore needs the command state and a valid operating window, not a disconnected measurement interpreted in isolation.

The distinction from P2312 is the state being assessed: P2313 concerns an output that remains high when driven on, whereas the paired low fault is evaluated with the driver off.

## Channel identification remains open

The dictionary names coil E, but the primary table calls this coil B. This article does not resolve that inconsistency by guessing a cylinder. Confirm the module’s exact circuit assignment in the vehicle’s wiring information before narrowing the investigation. The monitor supports an electrical fault classification, not automatic replacement of a coil or controller.
