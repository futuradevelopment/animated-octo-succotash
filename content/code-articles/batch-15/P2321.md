# P2321: A low ignition-control signal with an unresolved coil label

P2321 appears in the generic catalog as an ignition coil H primary-control low fault. The GM row used here instead names coil F. That unresolved mapping is important: the article can explain the monitored electrical behavior without safely assigning it to a physical cylinder.

## The supported electrical finding

The row describes voltage remaining low during the driver’s off state and treats it as a short-to-ground condition. It requires synchronized ignition, speed above 1,400 rpm and enough ignition-counter advancement for firing events. A 0.4-second confirmation interval and two-trip reporting are listed.

A low signal while the driver is on is not the same observation. Retaining command timing alongside the feedback avoids confusing normal switching with the off-state failure being monitored.

## How to use the limitation

Resolve the exact controller and circuit designation in the vehicle’s service information before identifying a coil location. Do not silently translate H to F, or assume either letter directly gives a cylinder number. Once the channel is established, the relevant diagnostic question is why the circuit cannot achieve its expected off-state behavior. The source gives no component-isolation procedure that would justify replacing a coil, harness or module solely from this DTC.
