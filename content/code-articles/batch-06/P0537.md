# P0537: evaporator temperature sensor voltage persistently low

P0537 is an evaporator air-temperature sensor low-voltage diagnosis in the cited body control module table. It identifies a debounced raw sensor voltage below 0.17 V, described as a continuous short-to-ground condition.

## Timing separates a fault from wake-up behavior

The controller must have been awake for more than 25 seconds and the diagnostic must be enabled. The low-voltage result must occupy eight seconds within a ten-second window. A brief reading immediately after wake-up therefore is not equivalent to the complete documented fault condition.

The threshold concerns sensor voltage. It does not mean the evaporator's actual air temperature is below a stated temperature limit, and it does not establish refrigerant quantity or compressor performance.

## What to retain and verify

Record the raw voltage and elapsed wake time rather than relying only on a converted temperature display. A persistent low voltage after the wake-up gate supplies different evidence from an erratic signal that repeatedly returns to normal.

The source lists Type B reporting over two trips. Verification should establish a normal sensor-circuit reading across an eligible observation window using the correct electrical procedure. The table does not isolate a wiring short from a connected-component problem or prescribe opening the refrigerant circuit. A temperature-sensor electrical code alone is insufficient reason to recharge the air-conditioning system or replace the compressor.

## Source and applicability

2025 GM 25OBDG04B diagnostic parameter tables; the cited monitor only. Vehicle, engine and calibration applicability must be confirmed separately. These tables describe detection logic, not a complete repair procedure or a component replacement verdict.
