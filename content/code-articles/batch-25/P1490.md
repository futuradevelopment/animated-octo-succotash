# P1490: Altima vacuum-cut bypass has an electrical control fault

P1490 in the 2001 Nissan Altima manual identifies improper voltage through the vacuum-cut valve bypass solenoid. The monitor's immediate evidence is electrical, so a stored code does not by itself establish a blocked vapor hose or a leaking fuel cap.

## Understand when the bypass should open

The bypass valve normally remains closed and opens for onboard diagnosis. The ECM supplies the operating ground command when that diagnostic opening is needed. Its key-on reference state is OFF, making a closed inactive valve an expected condition rather than proof of failure.

This purpose distinguishes the bypass from the purge valve that meters vapor to the engine. Selecting a replacement from a generic description of “an EVAP valve” risks identifying the wrong component.

## Separate supply, control path and valve

Nissan's diagnostic sequence checks the solenoid power supply, then the ECM-side circuit for continuity and shorts, before inspecting the component. The possible causes are harness/connectors and the bypass valve itself. The result of those branches determines whether an electrical repair or valve replacement is supported.

The related P1491 procedure evaluates operation of the bypass arrangement and its pressure path. It should not be substituted for P1490's circuit investigation simply because both mention the same assembly.

Document the supply and control-circuit findings, the operating state used for the test and the post-repair confirmation result. Work near fuel-vapor components should follow the service manual; directly grounding an unidentified wire can bypass the driver protections rather than diagnose them.
