# P0447: Titan vent-valve electrical fault versus a valve left open

P0447 concerns the EVAP canister vent-control valve circuit in the 2004 Nissan Titan manual. Nissan detects an improper voltage through the valve and identifies the valve circuit and the solenoid as the relevant possibilities.

## Normally open is a legitimate state

The vent valve is normally open and is energized to seal the canister during EVAP diagnosis. This means that finding an open vent when the system is not requesting a seal does not demonstrate P0447. The command and electrical response must be interpreted together.

The reference scan value is OFF with the ignition on. That expected command is different from the code's electrical meaning. A technician should establish whether the circuit can deliver the requested state rather than infer failure from the word “open” in both descriptions.

## Why the fault affects other EVAP checks

A sealed vent lets the system develop the vacuum used by other evaporative-emission monitors. If electrical trouble prevents that seal, a later pressure or leakage observation may be secondary to the vent problem. Restoring the vent circuit's operation is therefore relevant to interpreting subsequent EVAP test results.

The diagnostic path checks the supply, the ECM-side connection and the valve according to the wiring diagram. It is not a fuel-cap replacement instruction. After a demonstrated defect is repaired, use the document's confirmation procedure and then assess whether other EVAP monitors complete. Keep any remaining leak code separate in the record, because successful electrical operation does not establish that all vapor plumbing is leak-free.
