# P0041: rear oxygen-sensor bank assignment needs verification

P0041 concerns rear oxygen-sensor signals assigned to the wrong banks. The cited 2010 GM table compares bank 1 sensor 2 with bank 2 sensor 2 and explicitly limits this particular row to the GMX521 Camaro application within its LLT section.

## Two signals form the diagnosis

The monitor evaluates opposing voltage states across the two rear sensors. It is not a standalone high- or low-voltage test on one wire. That makes confirmation of bank identification and signal routing a more relevant starting point than selecting a replacement sensor from whichever trace looks unusual.

This is an interpretation of the monitor's design, not a complete GM connector-inspection procedure. The summary does not supply the vehicle wiring layout or prove that every P0041 was caused by physical connector interchange.

## The comparison needs controlled conditions

The table requires front oxygen-sensor closed-loop operation and a qualified airflow range. It also uses modeled exhaust temperature and a timing condition affected by catalyst aging. A cold idle snapshot therefore does not reproduce the documented comparison.

Several existing sensor, heater, fuel-system and EVAP faults inhibit the evaluation. Their presence can explain why the monitor has not completed after a repair.

Preserve the two bank traces, operating conditions and companion faults together. The evidence supports checking whether the controller is receiving the correctly identified signals; it does not establish a catalyst or sensor replacement without the applicable service diagnosis.

## Application limits

2010 GM 10OBDG02 engine diagnostic table, LLT unique section. The P0041 row explicitly says GMX521 Camaro only; exact vehicle build and service procedure still require confirmation.
