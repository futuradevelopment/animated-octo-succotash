# P0450: Maxima EVAP pressure sensing includes its hose connection

P0450 in the 2001 Nissan Maxima manual means the EVAP pressure sensor has supplied an improper voltage signal. This sensor monitors pressure in the purge line for onboard diagnosis; Nissan states that it is not used to control normal engine operation.

## A pressure sensor needs a valid pressure connection

The diagnostic sequence begins by examining the sensor's rubber tube for blockage, kinking, disconnection or incorrect routing. That is significant: a good electrical sensor connected to the wrong pressure path can still produce misleading diagnostic information.

The procedure next addresses engine grounds and water in the connector before separating supply, ground and signal-circuit checks. Replacing the sensor without those inspections can leave a hose or moisture fault in place.

## Interpret the voltage in its operating context

Nissan describes rising voltage with rising pressure and supplies a key-on reference value. That reference belongs to the documented system condition. It should not be treated as the correct value during every purge or sealing event.

The confirmation routine also includes ambient, fuel-temperature and battery-voltage prerequisites. A cold or electrically unstable test setup may not be equivalent to the specified check. Record the conditions alongside the actual pressure response.

Other EVAP components, including the vent and purge valves, appear among the possible causes because they influence the pressure presented to the sensor. The code does not isolate the sensor by name alone. A useful diagnosis distinguishes plumbing, electrical integrity and system pressure behavior before selecting a repair.
