# P0445: why Titan purge-solenoid high feedback needs circuit diagnosis

P0445 is the short-circuit diagnosis for the 2004 Nissan Titan EVAP purge-volume solenoid. Nissan assigns it to excessively high voltage reported through the valve circuit. That is an electrical observation, not evidence that the fuel tank has a small vapor leak.

## A high signal does not identify the damaged part

The documented alternatives are a shorted harness or connector circuit and the solenoid valve itself. The factory procedure checks electrical supply and the control-side connection before deciding whether the component is at fault. A technician needs those results to distinguish an external wiring problem from a valve problem.

The solenoid normally meters vapor using a duty-controlled opening. Longer energized pulses produce more flow. A scan tool showing a purge percentage therefore describes a command, while the P0445 monitor describes abnormal electrical feedback. Neither display alone proves the valve's mechanical position.

## Preserve the paired-code distinction

The neighboring P0444 branch uses excessively low feedback. Recording the exact branch prevents a low-signal investigation from being applied to this high-signal result. Also preserve any other EVAP findings: an electrical repair and a separate leak repair can be different tasks.

For confirmation, the manual specifies sufficient battery voltage and an idle interval before checking first-trip results. A cleared memory without that confirmation does not demonstrate that the short-circuit condition is gone. Electrical testing around a fuel-vapor component should follow the vehicle wiring diagram and factory precautions; this code is not an instruction to jumper the valve or bypass its driver.
