# P1491: Altima bypass performance depends on valves and vapor plumbing

P1491 in the 2001 Nissan Altima manual means the vacuum-cut bypass arrangement is not operating as expected. The vacuum-cut valve normally prevents intake vacuum from reaching the fuel tank; a parallel solenoid bypass opens during diagnosis to permit the required connection.

## This is broader than the bypass coil

Nissan lists the bypass valve, the vacuum-cut valve, clogged bypass or tank/canister hoses, the pressure sensor, vent valve and canister. These are different ways the expected pressure communication can fail. The code does not prove that the solenoid's electrical winding is defective.

The component procedure compares flow behavior in different directions and with the bypass inactive versus commanded open. That checks the combined function of the two valves, rather than relying only on an audible click.

## Follow the result into the correct branch

If the valve assembly behaves correctly, the factory sequence checks the surrounding purge line and tank port for restriction or disconnection. It then considers the pressure sensor and other EVAP components. A mechanically functional bypass cannot compensate for a blocked path elsewhere.

The work-support routine has specific warm-engine, load and movement conditions, and must actually complete before its result is interpreted. A stationary component check alone is not equivalent to that monitored result.

Record directional-flow findings, hose condition, pressure-signal evidence and the completed confirmation. The electrical-only P1490 diagnosis is a separate concern; preserve it if present rather than merging all bypass-related information into one assumed valve replacement.
