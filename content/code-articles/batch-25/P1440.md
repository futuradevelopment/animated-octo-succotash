# P1440: Maxima small-leak diagnosis uses positive tank pressure

P1440 in the 2001 Nissan Maxima manual is the positive-pressure EVAP small-leak diagnosis. It uses vapor pressure in the fuel tank, distinguishing it from the related negative-pressure P0440 test that uses intake vacuum.

## A sealed path must exist before pressure is meaningful

The vent valve closes and the vacuum-cut bypass opens the relevant route between the tank and purge plumbing. The pressure sensor can then observe the tank-side pressure behavior. If those components fail to establish the intended boundary, the result may resemble a leak without identifying its location.

Nissan explicitly prioritizes a simultaneous P1448 vent-valve result. That dependency is more useful than assuming the cap is responsible for every small-leak code.

## Look beyond the cap while retaining its role

The factory possibilities include an incorrectly fitted or unsuitable cap, vapor-line leaks, a damaged vent-valve seal, obstructed sensor tubing and other EVAP components. Fuel temperature, fuel level and pressure sensing also appear in the documented investigation because they influence interpretation of the test.

The P1440 section directs the reader to the P0440 confirmation and diagnostic procedures. That shared workflow does not erase the different pressure mechanism used to recognize this code.

A useful record should identify the vent fault status, test conditions, actual sealing or leakage evidence and the completed confirmation result. Do not infer a particular leak diameter or replacement part from the phrase “small leak” when this cited section does not establish it for the individual vehicle.
