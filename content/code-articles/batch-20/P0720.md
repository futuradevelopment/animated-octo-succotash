# P0720: An output-speed circuit fault can be detected while the vehicle is stationary

P0720 describes an output-shaft speed circuit problem in the cited Ford monitor, with P0720_14 covering an open circuit or short toward ground. The important distinction is between the integrity of the circuit and whether the shaft is expected to be turning.

## Electrical evidence and movement evidence are separate

Ford states that the described eight- and ten-speed sensors support electrical fault detection without the vehicle being driven, with faults maturing in about 1.5 seconds. A stationary zero-speed value is therefore neither proof of a fault nor proof that the circuit is healthy.

The same section has a different no-signal test: vehicle movement is inferred under positive driving torque while output speed remains very low. It also describes noise, dropout and commanded-gear plausibility checks. Their movement thresholds and event counts are not the electrical criterion for every P0720 presentation.

## Why the suffix changes the investigation

The architecture tables distinguish open-or-ground, short-to-ground and short-to-power classifications, and show separate failure types for plausibility or missing information. Save the complete scanner display and module identity before choosing a diagnostic branch.

A useful record places the circuit status alongside output, turbine and wheel-speed information. Agreement among speeds after the event cannot identify the origin of a stored electrical fault. The monitor description does not specify a connector pin, sensor replacement or internal transmission repair. Those decisions require the applicable circuit procedure and evidence that isolates the fault path.
