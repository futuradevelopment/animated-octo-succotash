# P2804: Confirm Ford’s general B-circuit classification before assuming intermittency

P2804 is called an intermittent range-sensor B circuit in the generic dictionary, but Ford's cited summary labels P2804/P2800_01 as a general electrical B fault. That difference must remain visible because the table does not establish a specific intermittent-event threshold for every P2804 report.

## Preserve the actual reporting format

Ford separately assigns B frequency, low and high categories to P2801, P2802 and P2803. It also lists A/B correlation as P2805. A broad B-circuit result should not be automatically interpreted as one of those narrower categories without the full controller definition.

The range-sensor section describes digital, analog and PWM designs with different checks. A frequency criterion from a PWM implementation cannot diagnose every general electrical result, and an averaged decoded selector position may conceal an underlying signal problem.

## Verify the representation before choosing a test

Record the reporting module, full suffix, range-sensor architecture and available B signal information. If intermittency is confirmed by the vehicle-specific definition, preserve the event timing. If the controller reports a more general circuit fault, use its applicable diagnostic branch rather than inventing a dropout count.

The source provides no universal B terminal, physical sensor location or replacement rule. The unresolved generic-versus-document label is retained here while the useful distinction between frequency, level, correlation and general circuit results remains supported.
