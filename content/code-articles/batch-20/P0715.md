# P0715: Why turbine-speed circuit evidence comes before a road-speed comparison

P0715 is the turbine or transmission-input speed circuit classification in Ford's cited conventional transmission tables. The P0715_14 representation groups an open circuit and a short toward ground. That grouping matters: the code does not independently identify which electrical defect exists.

## A circuit check need not wait for movement

The summary says the described eight- and ten-speed sensor systems can detect electrical opens and shorts without driving the vehicle. Its typical circuit maturation is approximately 1.5 seconds. A zero speed reading on a stationary vehicle therefore cannot by itself establish that the electrical monitor has passed or failed.

The same section separates plausibility, absent-signal and erratic-signal conditions. Those comparisons use operating information from other speed sensors and driving conditions. Their logic should not be substituted for the P0715 circuit classification merely because all involve turbine speed.

## Keep the full scanner result

Retain the reporting module and suffix where available. Ford's tables place short-to-power, intermittent and plausibility results under different displayed codes or failure-type suffixes. Losing that detail can turn an electrical investigation into an unnecessary mechanical ratio investigation.

A useful verification compares circuit-fault status with the sensor's current reporting state under the applicable procedure. Normal rotation data after an intermittent connection returns does not reconstruct the earlier circuit event. Neither the table nor a successful speed comparison identifies a connector, internal harness or sensor for replacement; those require transmission-specific evidence.
