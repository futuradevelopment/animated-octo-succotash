# P1226: Jaguar mechanical-guard sensor range and mechanical causes

P1226 identifies a mechanical-guard position-sensor range or performance fault in Jaguar's 1997 AJ26 summary. The possible causes include both the sensing circuit and the mechanism it observes.

## Electrical plausibility is not the only issue

Jaguar lists open, shorted or resistive sensor wiring, supply or reference-ground trouble and sensor failure. It also includes a seized mechanical-guard actuator or broken spring. A plausible supply voltage therefore does not settle whether the mechanism can move correctly, while a movement problem does not prove the sensor electronics are defective.

That dual scope distinguishes P1226 from the neighboring low-voltage and high-voltage entries. Those circuit-direction codes provide different evidence and should not be merged into a generic throttle fault.

## What the controller does

The monitor condition is ignition on for more than five seconds. Jaguar describes cruise-control inhibition when the first-trip amber warning is activated, with a two-trip check-engine entry. Loss of cruise operation may therefore be a response to this fault rather than a separate cruise-switch failure.

Professional evaluation should establish whether the sensor signal and mechanical position agree using the appropriate AJ26 procedure. The summary provides no actuator travel limit or bypass method. Do not defeat the guard mechanism to test whether normal throttle behavior returns; that would ignore the function this fault is monitoring.
