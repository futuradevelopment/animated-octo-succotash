# P1188: Jaguar upstream oxygen-heater high resistance

P1188 identifies excessive resistance in the upstream oxygen-sensor heater system in Jaguar's 1995 AJ16 diagnostic summary. The row covers both upstream heaters rather than assigning the problem to one exhaust bank.

## Follow the whole heater path

Jaguar lists an open or intermittent-open ECM-to-heater connection, a wiring short to ground, and excessive resistance or an open circuit in the heater elements. Both-heater failure is also included. These possibilities mean the fault classification alone cannot separate harness damage from defective sensor heaters.

The mention of an intermittent open is particularly useful. A circuit that tests normally while the vehicle is stationary may still differ from its condition when the event was recorded. Preserve the observed fault status and evidence of intermittency instead of treating one normal static reading as a complete diagnosis.

## A different window from the long-idle faults

The listed monitoring condition is engine idle exceeding 25 seconds, with two consecutive qualifying trips for the warning. That is a shorter requirement than the three-minute-and-twenty-second condition attached to P1187. The two codes should not be merged merely because both involve heaters.

Use the exact AJ16 wiring and test specifications to determine which portion of the path is abnormal. The summary does not state a numerical resistance threshold, so a guessed universal ohm value would add unsupported precision.
