# P1611: Jaguar AJ26 throttle-angle fault and protective response

P1611 identifies a throttle-angle malfunction in Jaguar's 1997 AJ26 summary. The listed causes involve throttle-position feedback, pedal-position information, the throttle assembly and the ECM.

## Requested and reported position can become unreliable

A throttle-angle complaint does not isolate the motor. Jaguar explicitly includes both throttle-sensor and pedal-sensor circuit faults. This means the position problem can involve feedback or the information used to determine demand, as well as the assembly itself.

The useful diagnostic distinction is which input or actuator evidence is abnormal before the controller enters its protective mode. The source does not supply a numerical target-angle tolerance or justify replacing an ECM merely because it appears in the possible-cause list.

## The first response precedes the two-trip engine warning

Monitoring is listed after ignition on for more than five seconds. With the first-trip red warning, the ECM defaults to mechanical guard mode and inhibits idle control, cruise, traction/stability control and power limiting. The check-engine entry is two trips.

Those inhibited functions may be consequences of P1611, rather than independent failures of every system named. Unexpected throttle behavior requires professional assessment using the exact AJ26 procedure. Do not bypass the guard or force the throttle to test whether the protective response can be overcome.
