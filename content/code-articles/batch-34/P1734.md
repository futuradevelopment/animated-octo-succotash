# P1734: Jaguar fifth-gear control failure and fourth-gear fallback

P1734 identifies a fifth-gear control malfunction in Jaguar's 1997 5 HP 24 summary. The transmission responds by adopting fourth-gear mechanical limp-home operation, which is distinct from the gear that triggered this entry.

## The retry history is useful evidence

Jaguar notes that P1731 is logged before P1734. The P1731 row describes an inconsistent ratio, a change out of the problem gear and a renewed shift attempt. Persistent trouble then leads to P1730 or P1734.

The cause reference for P1734 points to P1730: low transmission oil, output-speed sensor problems, input-speed sensor problems and mechanical failure. This shared list means a gear-ratio result still needs verification against the quality of the speed inputs.

## Do not diagnose the fallback as a second failure

Fourth-gear operation and engine-power limitation are documented responses to detection. They do not independently prove that fourth gear is defective. The check-engine entry is two trips, while the amber gearbox-fault indication occurs when the fault is detected.

The service monitor involves repeated shifts through the gears. Professional diagnosis should decide whether dynamic testing is appropriate and preserve the earlier ratio-fault record. The summary provides no clutch replacement instruction or fluid specification. It establishes the fifth-gear scope, the retry relationship and the protective response for this particular transmission.
