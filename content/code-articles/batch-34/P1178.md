# P1178: Jaguar AJ16 lean air-flow adaptation and idle evidence

P1178 is the lean air-flow adaptation fault in Jaguar's 1995 AJ16 engine-management summary. The source treats it separately from the fuel-flow adaptation identified by P1176.

## Idle is part of the distinction

Jaguar describes a warm-engine sequence containing more than three minutes at idle, more than three minutes steadily above 20 mph, and another idle period exceeding three minutes. This is materially different from checking only a brief highway interval. The table lists two consecutive qualifying trips for the check-engine warning.

The listed possibilities are an intake leak, low fuel pressure at idle, a blocked injector and an airflow signal biased low. The explicit reference to idle fuel pressure is useful: a measurement taken under a different operating condition may not answer the question raised by this row.

## What the code can establish

The adaptation tells the technician which calculation has reached an abnormal lean condition. It does not decide whether air entered unexpectedly, fuel delivery fell short or the airflow input became inaccurate. Evidence gathered during both the idle and driving portions helps distinguish those explanations.

Use the exact AJ16 service information before performing leak or fuel-pressure tests. The published sequence describes the monitor; it is not an instruction to undertake a road test when vehicle behavior makes driving unsuitable. A cleared warning without a completed monitor is not equivalent to a confirmed repair.
