# P1176: Jaguar AJ16 lean adaptive fuel-flow correction

P1176 concerns an excessively lean adaptive fuel-metering correction in the fuel-flow calculation on the 1995 Jaguar AJ16 system. Jaguar distinguishes this from its air-flow adaptation fault, P1178.

## What can drive this correction

The diagnostic summary includes restricted fuel delivery, leakage, low regulator pressure, inadequate pump output and obstructed injectors. It also includes injector supply wiring shorted to ground and an airflow signal biased low. On a supercharged engine, an incorrectly specified airflow sensor is another documented possibility.

This mix of causes explains why “fuel trim” is not a diagnosis of the oxygen sensor. The controller may be responding to genuinely insufficient delivered fuel or to inaccurate information used to calculate it. Comparing the fuel-delivery evidence with the airflow input helps distinguish those paths; the fault code cannot do that comparison on its own.

## Use the right operating window

The row describes a normally warm engine operated steadily above 20 mph for more than 25 seconds. Two consecutive qualifying trips are listed for the check-engine warning. That differs from the extended idle-and-drive sequence documented for P1178.

Preserve the distinction between fuel-flow and air-flow adaptation when reading a scanner's abbreviated label. Any pressure or injector-circuit testing should follow the exact AJ16 workshop procedure, with fuel-system work handled professionally.
