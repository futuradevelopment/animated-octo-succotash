# P1193: Jaguar downstream heater fault with inferred operating causes

P1193 is the downstream oxygen-heater open-circuit classification in Jaguar's 1995 AJ16 diagnostic summary. Its referenced cause list includes operating effects as well as electrical defects.

## Why an intact connector is not the full answer

The row points to P1187, which includes excessive heater resistance, resistive or open heater wiring, an airflow-sensor signal fault, and ignition retard that produces high exhaust temperature. These alternatives explain why the open-circuit wording should not be read as proof of one severed wire.

For P1193, the affected group is the pair of downstream sensors. The cross-reference to the upstream row supplies fault mechanisms without changing that location. Keep the distinction when correlating heater codes with sensor data.

## Separate direct evidence from inference

A confirmed open in the heater wiring is direct electrical evidence. An airflow or ignition fault is a different line of investigation because it may alter the operating conditions the monitor evaluates. The code alone does not choose between those explanations.

The stated monitoring condition is idle below 1,000 rpm for more than three minutes and twenty seconds, followed by the two-consecutive-trip warning logic. Use the applicable workshop information for confirmation. This summary does not specify a universal heater resistance or establish that replacing a downstream oxygen sensor will correct the underlying condition.
