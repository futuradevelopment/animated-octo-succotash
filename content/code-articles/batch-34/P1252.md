# P1252: Jaguar AJ26 mechanical guard during cruise operation

P1252 identifies a mechanical-guard position malfunction during cruise operation in Jaguar's 1997 AJ26 summary. A catalog may abbreviate this as a vacuum switching valve fault, but the manufacturer row describes the broader position-monitoring result.

## Vacuum and feedback both matter

The listed possibilities include vacuum or electrical faults involving the three vacuum switching valves, an open, shorted or resistive guard-sensor circuit, sensor failure, a seized actuator or broken spring, a throttle vacuum actuator fault and ignition-module failure. The code therefore does not isolate one vacuum valve.

The distinction is between a mechanism that did not reach the expected position and feedback that did not represent its position correctly. Evidence from only one side cannot establish the other. That is a diagnostic interpretation of Jaguar's cause list, not a substitute for its workshop tests.

## Why cruise operation is relevant

The listed condition includes driving with cruise engaged for more than five seconds. Jaguar inhibits cruise when the fault is detected and associates an amber warning/message, while listing no check-engine activation for this row.

Consequently, cruise inhibition can be the response to the fault. Professional diagnosis should preserve the mechanical guard and distinguish vacuum control from sensing trouble. The summary gives no justification for bypassing the guard or replacing a valve solely from a shortened code title.
