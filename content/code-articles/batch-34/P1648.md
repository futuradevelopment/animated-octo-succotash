# P1648: Jaguar AJ27 knock-control self-test failure

P1648 identifies failure of the ECM's knock-sensing self-test in Jaguar's 1999 AJ27 summary. The source lists ECM failure, rather than directly identifying an external knock sensor as the failed part.

## Do not confuse a self-test with detected engine knock

This is an integrity check of the control function. It is not evidence that combustion knock occurred or that replacing a knock sensor will correct the fault. The summary also does not specify a particular internal chip repair despite catalog wording that may mention an input chip.

The monitor condition is starting the engine and running it for more than five seconds. When the fault is first logged, Jaguar describes setting ignition retard to its maximum and limiting engine speed to 3,000 rpm. The check-engine column lists two trips, while the amber warning/message response appears on the first trip.

## Interpret performance after the fallback

Reduced engine-speed capability and changed ignition behavior can therefore reflect the documented protective response. They should not be treated as independent proof of fuel starvation or mechanical knock.

Professional assessment should establish the exact AJ27 controller and perform the applicable internal-fault tests before a repair decision. The row does not supply reprogramming instructions, coding requirements or a guaranteed ECM replacement remedy. It identifies the failed self-test and its consequences within this specific system.
