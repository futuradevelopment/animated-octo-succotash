# P0448: Volkswagen EVAP vent circuit short classification

P0448 identifies a shorted evaporative-emissions vent-control circuit in Volkswagen's 2014 CBTA/CBUA chart. The monitor is electrical, rather than a direct measurement of a fuel-vapor leak.

## Two electrical observations are listed

Volkswagen gives a high-current condition with a threshold range of 2.2–4 amperes, or a low-voltage condition with a threshold range of 2.74–3.26 volts. The ranges should remain ranges: the quick-reference table does not identify which exact calibration uses each endpoint.

The alternative conditions explain why a single voltage check may not capture every way this row can classify a fault. They are malfunction criteria, not normal commanded values or a specification for energizing the valve externally.

## Different from a leak test

The same page lists leak codes using pressure-drop timing. P0448 instead concerns the vent's control circuit. A loose cap or leak result should not be substituted for electrical evidence merely because all the codes belong to EVAP.

Use the fitted engine's wiring and complete test procedure to determine the cause. The table does not locate the short or identify a failed valve. Fuel-vapor system work should remain within appropriate professional procedures; this row supplies no pressure-test setup, vent bypass or parts replacement instruction.
