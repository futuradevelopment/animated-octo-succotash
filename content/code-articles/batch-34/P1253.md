# P1253: Jaguar AJ26 mechanical-guard check after shutdown

P1253 is the engine-off mechanical-guard position fault in Jaguar's 1997 AJ26 diagnostic summary. Its manufacturer description is broader than catalog wording that names a vacuum switching valve alone.

## The shutdown check has prerequisites

Jaguar describes a warm engine, a preceding driving/acceleration event, deceleration to a stop, more than five seconds with ignition off and the accelerator released, and then ignition on. These are service-monitor conditions, not a recommendation to perform aggressive driving on public roads. A simple key-on check may not reproduce the relevant sequence.

The possible causes include vacuum or electrical problems involving the switching valves, inaccurate guard-position feedback, mechanical seizure or a broken spring, throttle vacuum-actuator trouble and ignition-module failure. The source does not identify a particular valve as the failed component.

## Interpret the protective response

The table associates an amber warning/message and cruise inhibition with the fault, without check-engine activation. Loss of cruise operation can therefore reflect this guard fault even when the engine warning lamp is absent.

The useful diagnostic separation is between actual guard movement and the signal reporting that movement. Obtain the exact AJ26 workshop procedure to evaluate those paths. Do not bypass the guard or infer a repair from the code's shortened dictionary wording.
