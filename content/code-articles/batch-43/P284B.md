# P284B: Volkswagen’s fifth/seventh actuator fails to finish disengaging in time

Volkswagen identifies shift fork C as the 5–7 actuator in the DQ381 transmission table. For P284B, the stuck description corresponds to a timeout exceeding two seconds in the actuator’s disengage state. The intended state is disengage, which distinguishes this event from a failed request to engage a gear.

## The code reports a failed timed response

The table supplies a functional check, not a direct observation of a damaged gear or bent fork. A professional diagnosis needs to establish what the controller requested, what position information it received and whether the physical response agrees with that information.

The separate monitoring-time entry is two seconds, the frequency is continuous, and MIL operation is listed after two driving cycles. These fields should be preserved without inventing a different total timeout or assuming the fault is confirmed on every momentary delay.

## Use other C-channel faults in their proper roles

P283B identifies missing position feedback; P2847 evaluates departure from neutral when neutral is nominal. P284B instead addresses completion of disengagement. These distinctions matter because an apparently stuck actuator can require further signal and control evaluation before mechanical repair is justified.

The brief source provides no forced-movement procedure, adaptation-reset remedy or component-replacement decision. Verification should follow the correct 0GC service checks and demonstrate that the fifth/seventh actuator completes the relevant transition. Successful selection of another gear pair cannot prove that the original C-channel disengagement condition has been resolved.
