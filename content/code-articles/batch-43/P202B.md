# P202B: Audi assesses reductant-heater conductance at heating start

Audi’s CNRB diesel table associates P202B with the reductant tank-heater control circuit and conductance below 0.30 inverse ohms at the start of heating. Both the electrical quantity and the timing qualify the criterion. The source does not describe a low reductant temperature as the fault itself.

## Conductance is not resistance

The row expresses conductance in 1/ohm. Rewriting 0.30 as an ohmic resistance limit would reverse the electrical meaning of the quantity. The table also does not say that the same figure can be applied to a disconnected heater with a handheld meter; its criterion belongs to the controller’s monitored heating event.

The neighboring P202C entry uses conductance above 1.50 inverse ohms. Those opposite boundaries show why the exact low/high DTC should be retained instead of collapsing both records into a general heater failure. They do not, on their own, identify a particular wiring defect or justify a heater replacement.

## Preserve the start-of-heating context

A later reading after the system has been operating is not automatically equivalent to the value evaluated at heating start. The reference supplies no detailed enable-temperature window, sampling method or required duration. The matching service procedure is needed before a measured result can be treated as confirmation.

Verification should establish that the tank-heater control circuit satisfies the applicable monitor under its prescribed conditions. The table does not support bypassing controls, energizing a heater directly, or changing reductant components solely because the code is stored. The useful diagnostic starting point is the documented low-conductance event, with its units and timing intact.
