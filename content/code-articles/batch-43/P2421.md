# P2421: Toyota detects an EVAP vent valve that fails to close during key-off testing

Toyota maps P044672 to generic P2421 in the covered RAV4 HV procedure. The fault concerns the vent valve built into the canister pump module: the key-off monitor detects a failure of its commanded closed state. The code is distinct from a loose-cap leak description and from the neighboring leak-detection-pump on/off faults.

## The monitor works after the vehicle is switched off

The manufacturer’s procedure describes a pump-generated vacuum and canister-pressure measurements used to establish reference behavior. Key-off monitoring has supply-voltage, atmospheric-pressure and temperature prerequisites, and can begin after specified multi-hour shutdown intervals. The fault uses two-trip logic.

This explains why a brief engine-running check cannot establish that the vent-valve monitor completed. The source groups several EVAP faults in the same document; its shared reference-pressure criteria should not all be presented as a unique numerical trigger for P2421 alone.

## Use the controlled EVAP check for verification

Toyota directs diagnosis into the EVAP system procedure rather than immediately replacing the canister module. The listed diagnostic areas include the module, its wiring, relevant hoses and the ECM. A stuck-open classification does not independently identify which of those areas caused the observed pressure behavior.

The automatic EVAP check has important conditions: the engine remains stopped, excessive tank fill can invalidate the leak check, and hot fuel can make results inaccurate. A qualified technician should follow the complete procedure and then review All Readiness for P044672. Disconnecting the auxiliary battery or simply clearing codes can disrupt diagnostic history; a completed normal judgment is the meaningful verification result.
