# P21C9: Audi checks reductant-relay voltage before activation

P21C9 is the reductant control-module power-relay circuit-high entry in Audi’s CPNA diesel chapter. The listed event is supply voltage already above 7 V before relay activation. The sequence is central: voltage present before the activation command is a different observation from a supply that fails to appear afterward.

## Interpret the voltage together with relay state

Reading only the seven-volt number would discard the useful part of the criterion. A supply voltage measured while the relay is intentionally active cannot be evaluated as though it were a pre-activation reading. The service procedure must establish the relevant controller state and the circuit being monitored.

The adjacent P21CA entry watches for supply below 1 V with the main relay closed, lasting at least 300 milliseconds. That contrast helps separate an unexpected early supply from a missing commanded supply. It does not establish that both codes share the same failed part.

## The summary does not isolate the fault source

Audi’s quick-reference row does not specify a connector pin, a relay resistance test or a replacement instruction. The presence of voltage at an unexpected stage needs to be traced with the exact wiring and diagnostic information. It should not be converted directly into a verdict that the reductant pump or tank heater is defective.

Verification should reproduce the applicable relay-command sequence and demonstrate the expected circuit response. A later normal voltage with the system active, or a cleared warning, cannot alone explain the pre-activation event. Keep this CPNA interpretation separate from different engine-table descriptions rather than combining their thresholds into one universal test.
