# P2849: Volkswagen’s fork-A stuck code is a disengagement timeout

Volkswagen’s DQ381 table maps shift fork A to the 1–3 actuator. P2849 is labeled shift fork A stuck, but the malfunction description is more specific: a timeout in the actuator’s disengage state, with a threshold greater than two seconds. The nominal state is also disengage.

## A timeout does not identify the physical obstruction

The code records failure to complete the required state transition in time. It does not independently show that the fork is bent, a gear is damaged or a particular hydraulic component has failed. Those conclusions require the actual service procedure and supporting evidence.

The table separately lists two seconds in the monitoring-time column, continuous checking and two driving cycles for MIL operation. The timeout threshold and the monitoring-time entry are distinct fields, even where the numbers are similar.

## Keep the first/third channel and state clear

P2845 concerns the same actuator leaving neutral unexpectedly, while P2831 describes missing position feedback. Neither should be substituted for P2849’s disengagement event. Reliable position information and the requested transition are both relevant when explaining why completion was delayed.

A professional investigation should use the matching 0GC diagnostic process to relate actuator command, position feedback and movement. The summary does not authorize a forced manual shift or an adaptation reset as a repair. Verification must demonstrate successful first/third disengagement under the prescribed conditions, rather than rely solely on clearing the DTC or confirming that a different gear pair operates.
