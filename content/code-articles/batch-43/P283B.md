# P283B: Volkswagen loses fifth/seventh shift-fork position feedback

Volkswagen’s DQ381-7A/F 0GC table identifies P283B as shift fork C position circuit, associated with the 5–7 gear-shift module. Its criterion is no signal for more than one second. This narrows the general circuit title to a specific missing-feedback event.

## A missing signal differs from an implausible waveform

The companion P283C range/performance entry evaluates the position signal’s PWM duty cycle and period. P283B does not publish either of those as its malfunction boundary; it records that the position signal is absent. Combining the two descriptions would obscure the controller’s actual classification.

The manufacturer lists two seconds of monitoring time, continuous checks and MIL operation after two driving cycles. The table does not explain the relationship between the one-second no-signal boundary and the separate monitoring-time field, so they should not be collapsed into a single invented persistence requirement.

## Keep feedback and movement diagnosis separate

The same gear pair has distinct functional checks for neutral-position disagreement and disengagement timeout. Those use position or state evidence and do not automatically prove the cause of P283B. The correct 0GC wiring and component procedure is needed to identify the C-channel path.

Verification should establish that fifth/seventh position feedback is present and credible under the applicable checks. A cleared fault or normal operation of the first/third module does not resolve this separate channel. No adjustment value or replacement instruction can be inferred from the missing-signal row alone.
