# P0D30: Audi’s inverter voltage-sensor monitor has a 440 V boundary

Audi’s CHJA hybrid reference associates P0D30 with a high drive-motor A inverter voltage-sensor circuit. Its listed criterion is DC-link voltage above 440 V. This is the source’s diagnostic boundary, not a statement that any voltage below it is safe to touch or suitable for every inverter.

## Similar descriptions have different thresholds

On the same page, Audi gives P0C79 a separate inverter-voltage-too-high entry with a boundary above 420 V. The 20 V difference is significant when interpreting a stored code: these are not interchangeable labels for one universal test. The chart does not explain the complete relationship between the two monitors, so it would be wrong to invent a staged protection sequence from the numbers alone.

P0D30 also differs from P0D2E, which compares DC-link readings between controllers. A sensor-circuit high record does not automatically establish a disagreement between those reporting paths. The actual recorded DTC and associated data should lead the choice of diagnostic procedure.

## Verify the reading and its application professionally

The reference does not identify an external test point, sampling duration or replacement decision. Establishing whether the voltage information reflects the physical system requires the matching high-voltage service documentation and approved equipment. The summary cannot determine whether a sensor, connection, controller or operating condition caused the event.

Useful verification addresses the original high-input condition and the applicable controller evidence. A subsequent lower reading or an erased code is insufficient by itself to explain why the monitor crossed its boundary. No opening or live probing of the traction system is implied by this explanation.
