# P22B3: Toyota checks the downstream sensor’s negative-control voltage for a low limit

Generic P22B3 maps to Toyota P22B211 in this RAV4 procedure. The affected component is air-fuel ratio sensor 2, despite the O2 terminology in the code title. The circuit is the negative-current-control signal, identified as A1B-.

## Negative identifies the channel, not a negative voltage requirement

Toyota’s fault boundary is A1B- at 1.07 V or less for five seconds or longer, using two-trip detection logic. A “negative” signal name does not mean the voltage must be below zero. The source categorizes this event as a short to ground.

The monitor’s enabling conditions include battery voltage of at least 10.5 V and at least five seconds after ignition-on. It also excludes related open-circuit, correlation and positive-control range faults. Those restrictions matter when a post-repair monitor has not yet completed.

## Compare the correct signal with its test condition

Toyota’s initial electrical test disconnects sensor 2 and expects a different harness-side bias on A1B- than on A1B+. It also checks the voltage difference between the terminals. The live monitor threshold and the disconnected-connector specification serve different purposes and should not be interchanged.

Abnormal electrical results lead to harness continuity and isolation checks before the appropriate component decision. The code alone does not prove that the sensor element has failed or that the exhaust is lean. Verification requires the confirmation sequence, pending-code review and an All Readiness judgment for P22B211. INCOMPLETE means the judgment remains unfinished, not that the circuit has passed.
