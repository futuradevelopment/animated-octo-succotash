# P202C: Audi’s reductant-heater monitor detects high conductance

P202C is the high tank-heater control-circuit entry in Audi’s CNRB TDI table. Its criterion is conductance above 1.50 inverse ohms at the start of heating. This should not be described as a heater resistance above 1.50 ohms or a reductant temperature above a particular limit.

## The unit changes the diagnostic meaning

Conductance and resistance are different quantities. The source deliberately expresses its boundary in inverse ohms, so a resistance measurement cannot simply be compared with the printed number as though the units matched. The summary does not explain the controller’s calculation or provide a standalone component resistance test.

Timing is another necessary condition. The check is described at heating start, not at an unspecified point during warm operation. A test performed under different conditions may be useful within a complete procedure, but this row does not establish that it reproduces the monitor.

## Keep the high and low branches distinct

Audi lists P202B separately for conductance below 0.30 inverse ohms. A record of the high-side event therefore carries information that would be lost by reporting only a generic heater code. Neither row identifies the fault location or tells the technician to replace a complete reductant tank.

The next diagnostic decisions require the correct CNRB circuit and repair information, including its measurement method and prerequisites. Verification should account for the original high-conductance event under the specified operating state. A cleared fault or satisfactory operation on a later occasion alone does not establish why the controller observed the out-of-range value.
