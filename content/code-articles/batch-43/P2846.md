# P2846: Volkswagen’s second/reverse actuator disagrees with nominal neutral

For P2846, Volkswagen’s DQ381 0GC table identifies shift fork B as the 2–R actuator. The functional check detects a departure from neutral greater than 3 mm while neutral remains the nominal actuator state. Both position and state are necessary to understand the fault.

## A neutral-position fault is not a reverse-gear diagnosis

The 2–R mapping associates the actuator with second and reverse, but the monitored condition is neutral. It does not by itself show that reverse gear has mechanically failed or that the vehicle was trying to reverse when the fault was recorded.

The table gives two seconds of monitoring, continuous checking and two driving cycles for MIL operation. The three-millimeter criterion is not an installation tolerance or a requested adjustment; it is the controller’s functional boundary for leaving the expected neutral position.

## Establish whether the position evidence is credible

The same module has a missing-signal code, P2836, and a disengagement-timeout code, P284A. Those distinct categories should remain visible in the diagnostic record. A neutral disagreement cannot be interpreted confidently without reliable B-channel feedback and the correct actuator-state context.

The source provides no connector test or direct part-replacement branch. The appropriate 0GC procedure is needed to separate signal, control and mechanical findings. Verification should demonstrate correct second/reverse actuator behavior in nominal neutral and completion of the relevant checks. Selecting another gear successfully, or clearing the warning before that condition occurs again, does not establish that P2846 has been resolved.
