# P284A: Volkswagen times the second/reverse fork’s disengagement

In Volkswagen’s DQ381 0GC table, shift fork B is the 2–R actuator. P284A’s stuck classification is tied to a timeout in the disengage state exceeding two seconds. This is more precise than a general statement that second or reverse gear will not work.

## Identify which transition failed

The monitor’s nominal state is disengage. It does not describe an engagement timeout, an incorrect neutral indication or loss of the position signal. Those are separate categories elsewhere in the table. The stored state and requested transition should therefore remain part of the diagnostic evidence.

Volkswagen lists continuous checks, a two-second monitoring-time entry and two driving cycles for MIL illumination. The document does not translate that timing into a specific repair or say which physical component must be binding.

## Separate signal evidence from mechanism conclusions

The B-channel missing-feedback code is P2836, and its incorrect-neutral entry is P2846. Related faults can help establish whether the controller had credible position information when the disengagement timed out. A timeout alone is insufficient to select a fork, sensor or mechatronic assembly for replacement.

The appropriate professional 0GC procedure is needed to evaluate control and actual movement without forcing the mechanism. Verification should show that the second/reverse actuator completes the prescribed disengagement and that the relevant checks finish successfully. A normal shift involving another actuator, or a temporary absence of warnings after clearing memory, does not explain the original B-channel timeout.
