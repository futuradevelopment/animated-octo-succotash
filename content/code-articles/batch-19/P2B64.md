# P2B64: Pump controller temperature is different from coolant temperature

P2B64 identifies a pump-control overtemperature classification in Ford’s auxiliary pump B section. The detailed representation is P261A_98. This concerns the pump control system’s reported thermal condition; it is not automatically proof that engine coolant temperature is excessive.

## Keep the thermal quantity separate from entry conditions

The shared pump monitor is continuous, lists five seconds and requires at least 11 V battery voltage. The battery value is an eligibility condition, not a temperature limit. The summary does not publish a numerical thermal threshold for P2B64.

A normal coolant reading therefore cannot by itself dismiss a control-system overtemperature report, and a hot coolant reading does not establish that this particular internal classification occurred. The relevant measured or reported thermal quantity must be identified from the vehicle-specific information.

## A printed A/B mismatch limits component assignment

The source’s heading and P261A code family identify pump B, while the P2B64 description prints pump A. This article preserves that unresolved mapping rather than selecting a physical pump by assumption.

Retain the complete identifier, reported temperature status, commanded operation and related pump faults. A later cooled condition does not recreate a thermal event. The source does not identify the heat source, prescribe a cooling-system repair or justify external heating to reproduce the fault. Verification must follow the applicable pump-control procedure and its actual thermal criteria.
