# P2602: Low pump-control evidence must be separated from low supply voltage

P2602 is the low control-circuit classification for Ford’s auxiliary coolant pump A. The documented detailed code is P2600_11, and the listed mechanism is an output-circuit fault reported by a smart driver. This is a control-path assessment, rather than a measurement of coolant temperature or flow.

## Low circuit and low supply are different questions

The same pump family includes a separate supply-voltage-out-of-range entry, P2B9D/P2600_1C. It is therefore incorrect to assume that every low-control fault simply means the vehicle battery is low.

The circuit monitor requires at least 11 V battery voltage, executes continuously and lists five seconds of monitoring. Those conditions describe eligibility and timing; the summary does not give a connector voltage that can be used as a universal low-circuit cutoff.

## Use the pump’s stated role

In this document, pump A is the high-temperature auxiliary cabin-heating pump. Confirm that role against the vehicle before selecting a diagram, especially where several coolant pumps are fitted.

A useful verification record distinguishes the smart-driver report from battery voltage, requested pump operation and any separate supply or performance code. The source does not localize the fault to a wire, motor or module. A suitable circuit procedure must establish which path produced the low classification, rather than treating the code as proof that the coolant system needs a mechanical pump replacement.
