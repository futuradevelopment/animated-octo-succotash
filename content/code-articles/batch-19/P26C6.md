# P26C6: A low exhaust-valve circuit report must retain the bank1 identity

P26C6 is identified in Ford’s bank 1 exhaust flow-control table as valve A control circuit low, represented by P26C5_11. The classification concerns the electrical output path. It should not be interpreted as a direct measurement that exhaust flow is too low.

## Distinguish output faults from position rationality

The electrical monitoring group is continuous and lists five seconds. The page separately describes valve-performance reporting and a comparison between measured and commanded position. That rationality monitor has an ambient-dependent duration, so its criteria should not be substituted for the low-circuit check.

The source attributes circuit failures to smart-driver output-fault reporting. It does not provide a numerical low-voltage cutoff, connector pin or universal resistance limit that could be used without the applicable vehicle procedure.

## Preserve the published code discrepancy

The main DTC list names P26C6, but the threshold paragraph prints P25C6 alongside the same detailed P26C5_11 representation. This article retains the explicitly listed P26C6 meaning and does not silently claim the conflicting bare-code text is an exact mapping.

Record the full scanner identifier, bank and driver status, and resolve the applicable mapping against vehicle-specific information. A low output report does not itself identify whether wiring, the connected actuator or control electronics caused it. Nor does it establish a stuck valve. Those conclusions require evidence beyond the summary’s electrical classification.
