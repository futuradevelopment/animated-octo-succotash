# P04C1: a Ford fuel-door fault can leave the door latched or unlatched

The Ford hybrid example of P04C1 shows why a circuit-low label does not predict one visible symptom. The fuel-fill-door unlock circuit can be shorted to ground or open, and those two conditions have different effects on the latch in the documented system.

## The door's position does not identify the electrical cause

Ford describes a ground short as leaving the fuel door unlatched. An open circuit can leave it latched, preventing normal access for refueling. A technician therefore needs the electrical fault classification and the latch state; either observation on its own can be misleading.

The monitor operates with the engine off after a refueling request and lists a five-second monitoring duration. It belongs to the unlock-output stage. A missing request from the cabin switch is assessed separately, so the driver's report that the door will not open does not automatically identify P04C1's circuit as the original problem.

## The request also manages tank pressure

In Ford's sealed hybrid fuel arrangement, requesting refueling serves a purpose beyond releasing a door. The refueling valve provides the pressure-management path. Ford specifically notes the possibility of fuel spitback if a permanently unlatched door encourages refueling without the normal cabin request.

That warning makes a latch fault relevant even when the door appears convenient to open. The appropriate diagnostic question is whether the request, depressurization and unlock sequence are functioning together for the particular vehicle.

The source mentions an override for a latched door but does not provide the vehicle-specific override procedure here. It should not be improvised from the fault code. Verification should establish correct commanded latch behavior and a completed approved refueling sequence, rather than relying solely on a cleared warning.
