# P2806: Ford's range-alignment title and parking-pawl criterion need confirmation

P2806 is titled transmission range-sensor alignment in Ford's hybrid monitor list. A later section in the same summary associates it with a mechanical parking-pawl condition: motor rotation exceeding nine radians when the pawl is expected to be engaged. Those descriptions are not silently merged into one universal alignment test.

## Range recognition and physical holding are different observations

Ford's architecture explains that the park pawl locks the final drive when the transaxle is in Park. It also describes a separate factory-calibrated range-sensor assembly used for range selection, torque calculations and reverse-lamp operation. Correctly recognizing a requested range does not, by itself, establish that the mechanical parking function has engaged.

The later rotation criterion therefore addresses a different observation from simply reading the lever-position signal. The exact vehicle's diagnostic procedure must confirm how its P2806 implementation combines or distinguishes those functions. This article retains a review flag for that unresolved source mapping.

## Do not turn the criterion into an improvised movement test

The printed motor-rotation value is a description of a controller observation, not an instruction to attempt to move a vehicle against its parking mechanism. The code alone supplies neither a safe vehicle-securing procedure nor proof that the parking function will hold.

Useful records preserve the reporting module, requested and recognized range, and any parking-control or sensor findings. A repair should follow the confirmed vehicle-specific procedure and required parking-system verification. Clearing P2806, observing one successful range selection or assuming that sensor adjustment is always the remedy does not resolve the distinction between signal alignment and the physical holding behavior described by the source.
