# P0C00: Ford's motor-current-low check runs during a power-up phase test

Ford's P0C00 example concerns low drive-motor current during a defined phase-circuit check. It is not a general conclusion that the vehicle is producing insufficient propulsion. The monitor relies on a fault declaration from the motor/generator control unit during the power-up test.

## The measured current belongs to a commanded check

The source describes current below 5 amps for the duration of a 100 millisecond test at power-up. That is not equivalent to observing low current when the motor is idle and no torque is requested. The operating context is part of why the reading is considered abnormal.

Entry conditions include elapsed time after power-up, a valid 6 to 19 volt low-voltage supply and passed internal-reference checks. Ford also excludes the terminated operating mode for this current-out-of-range branch. Those conditions should remain separate from the code's abbreviated description.

## Low-current and overcurrent reports answer different questions

The same section describes much larger overcurrent criteria with different timing. Copying those limits into P0C00 would confuse two different monitor branches. The source's low-current explanation instead focuses on the phase circuit's response during its startup assessment.

The summary does not identify a single open winding, wire or inverter component from P0C00 alone. A useful diagnostic record includes the test stage, control-unit declaration and accompanying reference or circuit faults. The correct vehicle procedure is needed to establish a cause safely. Verification should demonstrate that the permitted phase-circuit check completes normally and that related high-voltage checks pass. Clearing the code outside a power-up test does not reproduce the condition it originally reported.
