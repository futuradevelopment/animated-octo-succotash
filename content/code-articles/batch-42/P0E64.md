# P0E64: Ford's charger is ready while the battery controller is not

Ford's P0E64 example concerns coordination between the charger and the battery energy controller. The charger has reached its ready state, a charging wake-up request is active, but the battery controller continues reporting that it is not ready. This is a state-comparison fault, not proof of an open enable wire by itself.

## Readiness has more than one owner

The summary specifies the charger-ready state and a high charging-wake-up input as the monitor's operating conditions. If the battery controller reports not-ready for 30 seconds, the listed malfunction condition is met. A charger-ready indication therefore does not mean every participant has authorized high-voltage charging.

Ford's architecture shows why that distinction matters. The battery controller supplies charging requests and controls the battery-side sequence, while the charger reports its internal output information over the hybrid network. A diagnosis should preserve the status of both sides rather than treating the word ready as one system-wide flag.

## Follow the reason for the disagreement

P0E64 identifies the sustained mismatch but the summary does not assign a single failed component or give a universal enable-circuit voltage specification. Associated battery, charger and communication findings are needed to establish why the states did not converge. Guessing that the charger must be replaced would go beyond the supplied evidence.

Verification should show the intended progression from wake-up through compatible ready states under the exact vehicle's approved charging checks. Deleting the fault while no charging request is present does not exercise that progression. Nor does a later ready indication prove that the original 30 second mismatch has been explained. High-voltage charging diagnosis requires the applicable professional service information rather than improvised enable-signal bypasses.
