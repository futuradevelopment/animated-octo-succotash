# P0E59: Ford's converter sensor C monitor combines electrical and thermal checks

Ford's P0E59 example concerns the variable-voltage converter's inductor-temperature information. The code's range/performance title covers more than one kind of evidence: electrical signal plausibility, excessive reported temperature and disagreement after a long soak. Those branches should remain distinguishable.

## Three observations can lead to the same report

The source lists inductor-temperature sensor signal below 0.067 volt or above 4.86 volts. It also gives an inductor overtemperature condition above 165°C for one second, and a difference from engine coolant temperature above 30°C after 180 minutes of engine-off time.

The one-second duration belongs to the overtemperature branch. The summary does not assign it to every electrical-signal or soak-comparison condition, so it should not be generalized across the entire code. Likewise, a signal-voltage limit is not a physical temperature limit.

## Determine the branch before deciding what overheated

An electrical-range fault can make the temperature information unreliable. A soak comparison asks whether two measurements are plausible together. Neither, by itself, proves that the inductor genuinely exceeded the thermal limit. Associated reference and timing checks are therefore useful context.

Ford's section identifies the inductor for sensor C, while the adjacent A/B entries use a shared power-electronics temperature comparison. That distinction should be retained rather than copying one sensor family's conditions into another.

The exact vehicle's approved high-voltage procedure is needed to establish the cause and verify correction. The summary does not authorize opening the converter or deliberately heating it. Clearing P0E59 or observing a lower temperature after a rest is insufficient to show which branch failed or that the underlying measurement or thermal problem has been resolved.
