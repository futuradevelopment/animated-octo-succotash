# P0C79: Ford's motor-inverter DC bus exceeds its monitored limit

P0C79 is the drive-motor inverter high-voltage category in Ford's hybrid summary. The documented monitor evaluates the inverter's DC-bus voltage, rather than treating the code as a general battery state-of-charge warning. Its confirmation interval is short enough that later ordinary readings may not reproduce the recorded event.

## The voltage and timing belong together

Ford lists DC-bus voltage above 516 volts for 10 milliseconds as the malfunction condition. Its entry conditions include more than 500 milliseconds after power-up and low-voltage supply between 6 and 19 volts. The source also names battery-controller communication and inverter-voltage-sensor checks among the supporting conditions.

Those details matter because the monitor depends on credible voltage information within a valid control-system state. The 516 volt figure is a historical calibration value, not a universal inverter rating or an acceptable target for a service test.

## Separate a high value from disagreement between measurements

Ford uses P0DA8 for a comparison between high-voltage battery and inverter measurements. A correlation fault and an absolute high-bus-voltage result describe different observations. Their relationship can help organize an event, but neither code alone proves which physical component caused it.

Useful evidence includes the operating stage, reported inverter voltage and associated sensing or communication faults. A normal pack-voltage display later does not establish that the inverter bus never crossed its timed threshold. Verification requires the correct high-voltage diagnostic procedure and relevant checks; the summary is not an instruction to probe the bus or reproduce an overvoltage. A cleared fault memory by itself leaves the original 10 millisecond event unexplained.
