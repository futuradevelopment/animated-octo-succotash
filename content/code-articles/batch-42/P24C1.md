# P24C1: Ford's changeover valve does not support the EVAP vacuum pull

P24C1 is Ford's performance or stuck-off result for the EVAP leak-detection changeover valve. In the documented hybrid sequence, it is evaluated during Phase 2, when the pump and valve should be configured to evacuate the system being checked. That is a different operating state from the initial reference measurement.

## The phase explains the pressure observation

Ford compares the leak-check pressure sensor's rate of change with a calibrated limit. A slope that is too high in this phase suggests that the changeover valve has remained in its deenergized position. The monitor aborts with P24C1 before continuing to interpretations that depend on the intended flow path.

The functional-check table lists a two-second Phase 2 observation period. It also requires the leak-monitor entry conditions and valid supporting hardware. The five-second electrical-driver check described elsewhere is not this pressure-response test and should not be substituted for its timing.

## Do not diagnose a tank valve from the wrong stage

After a successful changeover-valve check, Ford proceeds to assess the tank-pressure control and refueling valves. An early P24C1 abort consequently limits what the sequence can establish about those downstream components. A leak or tank-valve assumption based only on an incomplete cycle can skip the actual failed prerequisite.

The useful diagnostic record identifies the failed phase, commanded valve state, pressure behavior and any circuit or pump faults. The summary supplies a functional model rather than the vehicle-specific electrical procedure. Confirmation after repair should show that the changeover test completes and permits the later phases to execute. Merely seeing the pump run, or deleting the code before another qualifying after-run cycle, does not demonstrate correct switching.
