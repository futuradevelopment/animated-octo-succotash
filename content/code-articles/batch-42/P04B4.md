# P04B4: why Ford watches pressure during a refueling request

P04B4 describes a refueling vapor valve that appears to remain closed. Ford's hybrid-system example evaluates the pressure response after an explicit request to refuel, rather than inferring valve movement from an electrical continuity test alone. The engine is off during this check.

## A valid request must produce a measurable change

The monitor runs once per refueling event. Its entry table includes a filtered tank-pressure condition above 10 inches of water, a separate minimum pressure above 5 inches of water, and battery voltage between 11 and 18 volts. These are documented calibration conditions, not instructions to pressurize a fuel tank for testing.

Ford compares the starting pressure with a later reading. A decrease below 3.0 inches of water, approximately 747 Pa, is the stated failure criterion; the operation table lists a duration under 15 seconds. This makes the pressure change important, not simply whether a displayed pressure value looks high.

## Interpret a failed response in its system context

The same summary distinguishes the refueling valve from the parallel tank-pressure control valve. It also identifies P04B0 as the refueling valve's electrical-circuit check. Circuit faults and pressure-sensor faults therefore deserve attention before interpreting a failed response as proof of a mechanically seized valve.

Ford exposes related valve results through Mode 06. Its warning about default zero values matters: a zero result can mean the monitor never completed, rather than that the valve passed. A useful follow-up confirms that the applicable refueling monitor actually ran under valid conditions. The stored code does not establish that tank pressure has been safely released or that opening fuel-system components is appropriate.
