# P0C30: Ford's high state-of-charge monitor depends on charging-contact state

P0C30 is Ford's high-state-of-charge report for the documented hybrid battery system. The detailed monitor considers both estimated state of charge and individual cell voltage. It also distinguishes the charging contactors from the main vehicle contactors, which is important when interpreting a stored event.

## An estimate and a cell measurement can trigger different branches

With the charge contactors open, Ford gives a state-of-charge condition at or above 100 percent with different PHEV and FHEV durations. It also lists separate high-cell-voltage conditions, again with different limits and timing for those configurations. The main contactors may be open or closed in that part of the check.

With the charge contactors closed, the summary instead lists state of charge at or above 101 percent for 15 seconds, or cell voltage above 4.25 volts for three seconds. These are historical calibration-specific criteria, not charging targets or safe operating limits to reproduce deliberately.

## Do not collapse the monitor into one dashboard percentage

A displayed overall state-of-charge estimate cannot reveal every individual-cell condition or identify which branch failed. Likewise, assuming that main-contactor status controls all the timing would substitute the wrong switching element for the charge-contactors named by the source.

Useful diagnostic records preserve the battery configuration, charge-contactor state and whether the observation involved estimated charge or measured cell voltage. Related voltage-sense and correlation faults also matter to the reliability of that evidence. The summary does not identify a mandatory component replacement from P0C30 alone. Professional verification follows the applicable high-voltage battery procedure and confirms valid behavior through the relevant sequence, rather than simply observing a lower percentage after the event or clearing the code.
