# P24C0: Ford's EVAP valve appears energized during the reference phase

Ford's P24C0 example concerns a leak-detection changeover valve that appears to remain energized when the monitor expects its unpowered state. That distinction matters because the first phase is intended to establish a trustworthy reference, before the system's sealing performance is judged.

## A suspect reference stops the larger test

At the start of the after-run sequence, Ford vents trapped vacuum and obtains a barometric reading. The pump then moves air through the reference-orifice path with the changeover valve unenergized. An unexpectedly low pressure slope suggests that the valve has instead remained energized and flow is taking the wrong path.

The initial P24C0 conclusion is conditional: Ford says a later pump functional failure can prevent that valve inference from standing. Weak pressure response must therefore be read alongside pump operation. The monitor's purpose is not merely to record that pressure failed to reach a particular final value.

## The printed threshold needs clarification

The source gives incompatible inch-of-water and pascal values for this check and describes a rate without stating the associated time unit. Those numbers are not reproduced here as a usable calibration limit. The reliable observations are the reference-phase location, the expected deenergized valve state and the pressure-response comparison.

Ford separately uses P24C1 to examine the opposite switching problem during Phase 2. Electrical faults also have their own codes. A technician should establish which stage failed, whether the pump passed, and whether the applicable vehicle's service information resolves the threshold discrepancy. That evidence is stronger than assuming P24C0 alone identifies a seized valve or a physical system leak.
