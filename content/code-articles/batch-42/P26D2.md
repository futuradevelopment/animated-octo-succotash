# P26D2: Ford's electric coolant-pump electronics are too hot

P26D2 identifies an overtemperature report from Ford's electric engine-coolant pump control module. The distinction between electronics temperature and engine coolant temperature matters: the monitor uses information produced inside the pump assembly and delivered to the PCM over LIN.

## The controller reports its own condition

Ford's documented criterion is a pump-controller temperature above 130°C. The monitor runs continuously, lists five seconds to register a malfunction, and requires the absence of a LIN communication fault. The figure is a historical controller-monitor threshold, not a general allowable coolant temperature or an instruction to heat the pump for testing.

The same communication link carries other pump information, including electrical-supply and operating-state data. If that link is invalid, an apparently missing or static temperature value cannot automatically be interpreted as a healthy pump module.

## Thermal protection changes the surrounding evidence

Ford lists overtemperature among the pump conditions considered when evaluating its communication and speed behavior. An event in which the module reports excessive heat should therefore be understood together with the other pump faults, rather than assuming every loss of normal operation is an independent mechanical seizure.

P26CB, for comparison, uses high-current and low-speed behavior over repeated restart attempts. P26D2's thermal observation is a different reason the recorded operating state may be abnormal. The summary does not establish the underlying heat source or prescribe replacement from this code alone.

Appropriate verification preserves the event's temperature, supply and communication information, then follows the exact vehicle's cooling-system procedure. A reading that falls after the vehicle rests shows cooling has occurred; by itself, it does not establish why the electronics exceeded the threshold or whether normal operation will remain stable.
