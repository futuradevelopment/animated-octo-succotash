# P26D0: interpreting Ford's coolant-pump low-voltage report cautiously

P26D0 concerns the system-voltage information associated with Ford's electric coolant-pump controller. The pump reports its internal voltage and temperature over LIN, and the PCM uses that information to distinguish electrical supply conditions from mechanical pump faults. The published low-voltage threshold, however, needs caution.

## What the source establishes

The monitor operates continuously when there is no U019F LIN communication fault and lists five seconds to register a malfunction. Ford says the pump-controller voltage is compared with the PCM's system-voltage information. This establishes a relative comparison between observations, not simply a universal low-battery number.

Valid communication is essential because it carries the pump's report. A missing LIN message is assessed separately and cannot be treated as a trustworthy low-voltage measurement from the pump.

## What the printed inequality does not resolve

The summary describes P26D0 using pump-controller voltage below PCM battery voltage plus four volts. Taken literally, that relationship would include many nearly equal readings that do not obviously describe a low-voltage discrepancy. The text is not silently changed here to a different sign or threshold. The exact vehicle's diagnostic information must resolve that limit.

Useful evidence can still be collected without adopting the questionable number: the two reported voltages, communication validity, event conditions and related electrical faults. P26D1 is the neighboring high-voltage report, while P26CB assesses mechanical current-and-speed behavior.

A cleared code or one normal battery measurement is insufficient verification of a comparison fault. The relevant question is whether the pump's reported supply behaves consistently with the PCM's reference under the approved check, using a confirmed calibration-specific threshold.
