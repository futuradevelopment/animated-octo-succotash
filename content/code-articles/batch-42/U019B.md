# U019B: Ford's battery controller stops receiving charger messages

Ford's battery energy control module manages battery charging and discharging using information from several controllers. In its documented U019B example, the expected battery-charger control module message is absent for five seconds. The monitor concerns the communication relationship, not a direct test of the charger's power-conversion capability.

## Missing data is different from invalid data

Ford provides a separate U049C condition when a charger message arrives but reports invalid states. That distinction gives the scan record useful direction: one result concerns message availability, while the other concerns the usability of received information. Treating both as a generic failed charger would discard that difference.

The five-second missing-message criterion is also more specific than the summary table's broad monitoring-duration description. It identifies the persistence the manufacturer associates with this communication event. An instantaneous loss of a scan-tool display is not automatically equivalent to the controller's recorded test.

## The receiving module's role matters

The BECM controls battery-side conditions and uses the charger information within its charging sequence. Its source description also covers battery sensing, cooling and contactor control. A missing charger message can therefore affect the coordination of those functions without proving that the battery itself is defective.

Useful evidence includes which module stored U019B, other missing-message codes and the charging or wake-up state at the event. The summary does not isolate wiring, module power or internal electronics as the established cause. Verification follows the exact vehicle's communication procedure and confirms sustained availability of the expected message during the appropriate operating sequence. A successful connection to the charger at one later moment is weaker evidence than demonstrating that the original communication gap no longer recurs.
