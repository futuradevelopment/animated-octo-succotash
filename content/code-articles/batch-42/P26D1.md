# P26D1: Ford compares coolant-pump voltage with the PCM reference

Ford's P26D1 monitor concerns the voltage reported by the electric engine-coolant pump's controller. It is part of the pump's electrical monitoring, separate from a blocked-impeller indication or an overheating-engine observation. The comparison depends on information passed from the pump to the PCM over LIN.

## Two voltage observations must be distinguished

The source gives a typical high-voltage condition in which the pump-controller reading exceeds the PCM battery-voltage reading by more than four volts. The monitor operates continuously, lists a five-second duration, and requires the LIN communication fault U019F to be absent.

That relative criterion means a scan-tool battery value alone cannot describe the entire test. A technician needs to know which module supplied each reading and whether the pump's report was valid at the time of the event. It would be inappropriate to substitute an unrelated absolute battery-voltage threshold for this documented comparison.

## Keep supply information separate from pump mechanics

Ford's pump controller also reports its electronics temperature, which supports P26D2, and evaluates current and rotation for mechanical faults. Those observations can help describe the larger event, but none changes P26D1 into a direct measurement of coolant flow. A pump that rotates normally during a later check can still have had an intermittent supply or reporting discrepancy.

The summary does not identify a particular connector defect, wiring location or mandatory replacement for this result. The practical value of the code is to focus the approved diagnostic procedure on the pump's supply information and its agreement with the PCM reference. Confirmation should include valid communication and consistent readings under the relevant operating conditions, rather than a fault-memory clear alone.
