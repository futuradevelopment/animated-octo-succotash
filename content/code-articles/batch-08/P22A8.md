# P22A8: NOx sensor 2 heater-sense short to ground

The documented P22A8 failure is a short to ground at the HTemp heater-sense connection of the post-catalyst NOx sensor. HTemp supplies the heater-resistance measurement path. A low sense-circuit fault is therefore not interchangeable with P22A4, which concerns heater control.

## Evidence the monitor uses

The sensor reports the ground-short category while the controller applies an 80-of-160 sample counter on a 25 ms task. Powertrain relay voltage must exceed 11 V, sensor supply must exceed 9.90 V, and the bus relay must be on. Dewpoint must have been reached. Communication loss and invalid CAN data are exclusions. The row specifies Type B, two trips.

The source notes internal 1.5-second electrical-fault debouncing for Continental generation 3.5 sensors. That is a stated sensor-family behavior, not proof that every platform will show the identical delay.

## Diagnostic distinction

A plausible exhaust reading does not eliminate a heater feedback defect: concentration and heater-sense validity are separate observations. Conversely, an HTemp report does not establish which external terminal or internal component caused it. Use vehicle-specific circuit identification before any electrical testing. Confirmation should include a qualified, completed heater-sense check without the ground-short status. A scan immediately after clearing codes, before dewpoint qualification, leaves that question unanswered.
