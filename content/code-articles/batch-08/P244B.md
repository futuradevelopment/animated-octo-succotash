# P244B: filter pressure comparison suggests a downstream sensing problem

The cited P244B strategy is aimed at a downstream pressure-sensing pipe that leaks or becomes disconnected. It should not automatically be read as proof that the particulate filter itself is blocked. The table documents two different implementations depending on sensor capability.

## Two ways to test the downstream path

One implementation measures pressure downstream of the filter relative to ambient. The other evaluates differential pressure divided by exhaust mass flow over a calibrated window, against a map influenced by soot estimate and upstream temperature. The installed hardware determines which comparison is relevant.

Valid exhaust-flow information, qualified pipe-temperature conditions and absence of competing sensor faults are required. The continuation adds exhaust-valve, backpressure and temperature conditions. Reporting is Type A, one trip.

## Important source limitation

The downstream-relative-pressure continuation prints a less-than threshold despite the “too high” heading, and some enable ranges are inconsistently presented. This article therefore avoids converting those cells into universal numerical test limits.

First identify which pressure architecture the vehicle uses, then evaluate its appropriate comparison and sensing paths. A differential-pressure measurement cannot substitute for the separate downstream-to-ambient measurement without knowing the architecture. Verification must confirm completion of the relevant monitor; neither the code name nor one high pressure reading establishes the required repair.
