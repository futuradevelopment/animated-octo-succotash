# P263B: The MIL output remains high during an on command

P263B evaluates the malfunction-indicator lamp control circuit while the output driver is commanded on. In the described GM implementation, a high signal in that state supports a short-to-power classification. The high designation is electrical; it does not mean that the lamp is unusually bright or that an engine parameter is excessive.

## Command state changes the meaning of voltage

The companion low-circuit monitor P263A observes the driver-off state. Applying that interpretation to an on-state reading would confuse two different checks. Record the controller’s command together with the signal being evaluated.

For P263B, four failures out of five samples are required, with a 50 ms sample interval. The monitor also requires run/crank voltage above 11 V and remote vehicle start not active. A single transient during a different operating state does not reproduce the stated confirmation logic.

## Keep the fault local to its evidence

The table identifies a lamp-control electrical problem and does not promise a particular visible lamp state. The indicator itself is part of the circuit under suspicion, so its appearance should not replace controller evidence.

Verification needs the matching wiring and driver procedure, including the correct interpretation of the on command. The source does not locate the shorted path, identify a failed output stage, or justify replacing an entire control module. It also does not diagnose the emissions fault that the lamp may separately be intended to display.
