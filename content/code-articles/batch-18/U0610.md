# U0610: Mass-airflow sensor B communication timing failures

U0610 has the generic meaning lost communication with mass or volume airflow sensor B. The cited GM implementation monitors the timing of the airflow sensor signal. Its fault criteria identify either a time-overflow error or a maximum-period violation reported for that signal. These are communication timing failures, not a direct statement that measured airflow is too low.

## Timing integrity comes before airflow plausibility

The row specifies a 1.5-second duration and two-trip behavior. Ignition must be on, battery voltage must exceed 9 volts, and additional enable and inhibit tables apply. A separate airflow plausibility monitor compares measured and modeled airflow. That distinction matters: a rationality comparison assumes useful measurement data, while U0610 questions whether the signal is arriving correctly.

There are unresolved details in the original table. Its strategy calls the signal permanently low, but its pinpointing field says the PWM level is HIGH. It also prints an upper battery bound of 655.34 volts. Neither detail is a suitable instruction to diagnose a particular short or test at an extraordinary voltage.

## Use the code without guessing the electrical cause

Retain the reported timing-error status and any high/low line detail available from the matching vehicle diagnostics. Confirm what sensor B means in the installed system before selecting a connector or physical bank. Review sensor supply, signal timing, and related communication records using that system's procedure.

A frozen or substituted airflow value can be a consequence of unusable communication. It is not proof of an intake restriction, a contaminated sensing element, or the need to replace the sensor. The contradictory level wording remains a source-review issue.

## Source and applicability

2025 GM 25OBDG07A Part 1 documented monitor only. Vehicle, engine and calibration applicability must be confirmed from the matching service information; no model fitment is inferred.
