# U3006: Brake-controller input power A undervoltage paths

U3006 is described generically as control-module input power A circuit/open. In these GM brake-controller tables, the corresponding input-power-1 code has several undervoltage paths. The generic word open does not mean that every occurrence proves a disconnected wire; the controller records specific supply conditions and their effect on internal tests.

## Three different observations lead to U3006

During initialization, the initial motor test can be interrupted when motor voltage is below 5 volts and the UB_VR supply is below 6.2 volts. This path is evaluated immediately under its initialization conditions.

A second path tracks charge-pump tests that cannot run because of undervoltage. It requires at least three such occurrences, checked cyclically every nineteen seconds, with a listed duration of fifty-seven seconds. This is evidence of inhibited testing, not a direct measurement that the hydraulic pump cannot produce pressure.

A third path detects the UBB supply below 6.2 volts while UBVR remains above 9 volts, qualified for 0.05 seconds. That identifies a supply imbalance with a redundant source still available.

## Preserve which supply and test were affected

All three paths require ignition on and carry Type B, two-trip behavior. Record the brake controller's failure detail, both supply readings, and whether the event occurred during initialization or subsequent operation. A current battery reading alone cannot reconstruct an earlier brief drop on one module supply.

Use the matching wiring information to identify the UBB and UBVR feeds. The code does not independently identify a failed battery, fuse, connector, or control module, and it should not be interpreted as proof of an open circuit without confirming the actual electrical condition.
