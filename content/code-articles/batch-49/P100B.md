# P100B: Electric VTC diagnostic duty needs clarification

## What the motor reports

Honda P100B concerns the diagnostic feedback from the electric variable valve timing motor to the PCM. The monitor watches the EVTM signal while the electric VTC relay is commanded on. The excerpt describes continuous monitoring, a five-second persistence period and a minimum 12-volt battery supply of 10.5 V. It is therefore an electrical self-reporting fault, not direct proof that the camshaft has mechanically seized.

The document identifies an internal motor circuit failure as a possible cause. That description supports investigation of the motor electronics, but it does not replace the matching circuit procedure or establish which assembly should be ordered for a particular vehicle.

## The duty-cycle conflict matters

The general explanation says normal relay-on feedback is 80 percent duty, with a five-percentage-point tolerance. The malfunction-threshold paragraph instead refers to values other than 40 or 60 percent, each with its own tolerance. Those statements cannot safely be combined into a single test limit. This article retains a source-review flag, and no duty-cycle pass/fail specification is recommended from this excerpt alone.

Preserve the freeze data and verify the current Honda diagnostic information before interpreting a captured waveform. The source classifies this as a one-drive-cycle fault with MIL illumination and stored pending and confirmed records. Clearing those records removes useful context; it does not resolve the contradictory specification or demonstrate that motor feedback has recovered.
