# P1D64: The battery monitor loses the crash-cutoff pulse signal

## Missing pulses are not proof that a collision occurred

Honda P1D64 monitors crash-cutoff information sent from the SRS unit to the battery condition monitor module on the CDS circuit. The described fault is absence of the expected pulse signal. It therefore does not, by itself, prove that the vehicle has experienced a crash or that an airbag has deployed.

The source lists an open or shorted CDS line, an SRS-unit fault and an internal battery-monitor circuit fault among the possibilities. The circuit links restraint-system information with high-voltage control, so both the exact signal path and the relevant module records matter to diagnosis.

## The source gives two different durations

The monitor table states at least 5.5 seconds, while the explicit malfunction-threshold paragraph states at least 3.5 seconds without received pulses. This unresolved discrepancy is retained as a review flag; neither value is presented here as a settled reproduction timer. The enable table specifies a 9.7 V minimum 12-volt supply and an initial delay after ON-mode supply eligibility.

Honda describes one-drive-cycle storage with the power-system indicator on and the MIL off. Preserve SRS and battery-monitor records before clearing anything. Qualified personnel should use current Honda information and the necessary restraint-system and high-voltage precautions. Do not bypass the CDS signal or inject substitute pulses to suppress the warning, since that would interfere with a safety-related information path.
