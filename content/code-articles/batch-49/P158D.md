# P158D: A low ISOC voltage points to the current-sensor circuit

## The low value is a signal voltage

Honda P158D is set when the battery-current sensor's ISOC signal remains below 0.16 V for at least 500 milliseconds. The battery condition monitor module uses this signal to manage battery charging and discharging. A voltage below the diagnostic limit describes an implausible measurement circuit; it should not be interpreted as a direct measurement that the high-voltage battery is empty.

The factory enable table allows monitoring after the specified initial supply delay in ON mode, with 12-volt supply voltage of at least 7.7 V. A technician needs the recorded operating state and current-sensor data to decide whether the low signal is persistent or intermittent.

## A missing supply can imitate a failed sensor

The documented possibilities include an open VCCISOC supply, an ISOC short to ground, failure of the sensor and an internal monitor-module circuit fault. The supply and ground-short alternatives are why this DTC cannot identify the sensor alone as the failed part. The excerpt also does not provide the pin-level checks needed to distinguish those possibilities safely.

Retain freeze data and use the vehicle-specific Honda circuit procedure before any component decision. Honda describes pending storage on the first qualifying detection and confirmed storage with MIL illumination when the fault repeats on the next drive cycle. Work involving access to high-voltage battery components requires qualified personnel and the prescribed isolation process; do not turn a low-voltage signal threshold into permission to probe an energized battery assembly.
