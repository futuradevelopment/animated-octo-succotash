# P1A89: The PCU reports an excessive V1 voltage measurement

## The monitor uses the V1 voltage information

Honda P1A89 concerns V1 voltage information used by the PCU to regulate the voltage control unit. The labeled criterion states that the V1 voltage exceeds 340 V for at least one second. Monitoring requires at least 5 V on the internal supply described as the high-voltage-side 6 V supply.

The factory text calls the 340 V quantity a sensor “output voltage.” That wording does not establish a low-voltage connector signal specification or identify a safe probe point. This article retains a review flag because the excerpt does not clearly distinguish the sensed high-voltage quantity from the sensor's electrical interface.

## A reported high value still needs the proper diagnostic path

The possible-cause section names an internal PCU failure. It provides no independent reference reading, pin-level test or evidence that the traction battery itself is overcharged. A technician must use the correct Honda diagnostic information to determine what the V1 value represents and how it is verified for the specific vehicle.

The documented fault class stores pending, confirmed and freeze-data records in one drive cycle with MIL illumination and a power-system indication. Keep those records before resetting the system, especially any companion voltage-sensor or supply faults.

Do not apply a meter to an assumed V1 terminal based on this threshold. Service involving the PCU and its high-voltage circuits requires qualified personnel, isolation and verification under the matching manufacturer procedure.
