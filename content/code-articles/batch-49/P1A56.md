# P1A56: Motor-current sensing reaches an implausible voltage band

## Both ends of the sensor range are monitored

Honda P1A56 carries a high-current title, but its documented criterion includes either end of the phase-current sensor's electrical range. A signal above 4.53 V or below 0.47 V for at least 190 milliseconds meets the listed threshold. Reading only the title would miss the low-voltage branch of the monitor.

The gate-drive circuit must be operating and the 12-volt supply must be at least 8 V. Consequently, a static check performed while gate drive is inactive does not reproduce the monitor's stated enable conditions. The recorded conditions help explain why an intermittent fault may not return immediately after clearing.

## Current control depends on more than one component

The possible-cause list includes motor power cabling, rotor-position-sensor wiring, the position sensor itself, the traction motor and an internal PCU fault. Those alternatives connect current-control behavior with both power delivery and rotor-position information. The code alone cannot distinguish actual current stress from a control or sensing problem.

Preserve freeze data and companion motor-control faults before deciding which branch to test. Honda specifies one-drive-cycle MIL illumination, a power-system indication and stored pending and confirmed records. The advanced-diagnostics excerpt gives no safe procedure for probing energized motor cables. Any access to the motor or PCU needs qualified high-voltage service personnel, the correct wiring information and the prescribed isolation checks.
