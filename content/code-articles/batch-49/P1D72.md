# P1D72: Motor temperature appears stuck during a high-load event

## The controller expects a temperature response

Honda P1D72 evaluates whether the traction-motor temperature reading changes when the motor is working under substantial load. The documented enable condition is an absolute motor torque of at least 260 N·m. The monitor compares the temperature during that operation with its initial value and uses a persistence period of at least eight seconds.

This is a response-plausibility check, not a direct declaration that the motor is too cold. A reading that remains essentially fixed during a qualifying load event can be suspect even when its instantaneous temperature looks reasonable.

## The tolerance units are inconsistent

The threshold sentence presents the allowed deviation as “1 °F (1 °C).” Those are not equivalent temperature differences. This article retains a review flag and does not select one value as the definitive tolerance. The central supported point is the unusually small change over the stated loaded interval; the exact calibration should be verified in current Honda information.

The possible causes are the traction-motor temperature sensor and an internal PCU fault. Neither the code nor the excerpt establishes which is responsible. Preserve freeze data showing the initial temperature, subsequent reading and load conditions before clearing memory.

Honda specifies one-drive-cycle MIL storage with a power-system indication. Reproducing high motor torque is not an appropriate owner experiment, and sensor access around the traction drive requires a qualified technician using the correct thermal and high-voltage precautions.
