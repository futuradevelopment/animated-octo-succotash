# P1E26: The two brake-pedal travel channels lose their expected relationship

## A correlation fault is about two signals together

Honda P1E26-00 compares the primary brake-pedal stroke signal with its redundant channel. The factory confirmation includes pedal applications after a clear and power cycle. A repeated code leads to investigation of whether the BPS and BPSR paths are shorted together, rather than treating either channel's individual voltage as the entire diagnosis.

The distinction is useful: two channels can each produce a plausible-looking value yet fail the relationship expected by the controller. Conversely, a shared electrical connection can force readings to track one another incorrectly. The excerpt supplies no numerical correlation tolerance, so one should not be invented.

## Disconnection separates the harness from the pedal assembly

The procedure first examines the relationship at the brake-controller connector. If continuity is present, it repeats the check with the pedal sensor disconnected. Continuity remaining under that second condition directs repair toward a wire-to-wire short; disappearance directs the branch toward the pedal assembly. A separate branch considers controller connections and authorized service information before a controller/simulator decision.

The source specifies a three-minute OFF-mode wait with the driver's door open before connector work. Qualified brake personnel must follow the complete Honda sequence and correct connector identification.

Keep the full P1E26-00 record and freeze data before clearing. Do not bypass the redundant channel or connect the signals together to make their values agree; that would defeat the independence the monitor is intended to check.
