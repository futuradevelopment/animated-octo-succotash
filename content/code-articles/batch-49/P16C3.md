# P16C3: The DC-DC converter reports a temperature-sensor abnormality

## The battery monitor receives the diagnosis

Honda P16C3 is stored when the battery condition monitor module receives continuing temperature-sensor abnormal information from the DC-DC converter. The converter's own processor detects the condition and sends the report over CAN. The specified persistence period is at least two seconds.

This architecture matters when reading a scan: the module retaining the DTC is not necessarily the module containing the temperature-sensing circuit. The code also does not, by itself, establish that the converter has overheated. Its definition concerns abnormal sensor information, which is distinct from a verified high-temperature operating event.

## Observe the correct warning behavior

The factory enable conditions require ON mode and at least 10.2 V at the battery condition monitor module's supply. The confirmation description calls for at least ten seconds in ON mode. The listed possible cause is an internal DC-DC converter circuit failure, but the excerpt supplies no terminal checks or standalone replacement procedure.

Honda describes a pending record on the first qualifying drive cycle and confirmed storage with the power-system indicator on after the fault returns on the second. The MIL remains off. A scan and power-system warning can therefore be significant even without an engine-warning lamp.

Retain the converter and battery-monitor information before resetting either module. Any inspection involving the converter's high-voltage connections requires trained personnel and the matching isolation procedure; a CAN-reported temperature-sensor fault does not make those connections safe to access.
