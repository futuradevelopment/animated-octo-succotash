# P100A: The electric VTC circuit is traced from its fuse to feedback

## The factory sequence starts with controlled confirmation

Honda P100A concerns the electric variable valve timing motor circuit. The cited procedure uses the HDS VTC test after a clear and power cycle to determine whether the fault repeats. If it does not, the document directs attention to intermittent connections at the motor and PCM and to any recorded on-board snapshot.

A repeated fault is then divided by the condition of the specified B12 fuse. A blown-fuse branch investigates shorts in the battery feed and relay-to-motor supply paths before distinguishing the relay board from the motor. Replacing the fuse repeatedly without investigating its circuit would bypass the decision the procedure is trying to make.

## An intact fuse does not end the electrical investigation

The other branch checks the relay board, battery feed, relay-control line and motor supply path. Later steps consider the EVTP control signal, EVTM feedback and the motor ground path. These are different circuit functions; the DTC title alone does not identify which one has failed.

Only after those checks does the document reach its conditional PCM-substitution decision. The excerpt therefore does not support immediate replacement of the PCM or motor merely because P100A is stored.

Keep the diagnostic record before clearing and have the exact Honda connector and power-state instructions applied by a competent technician. This summary explains the order of fault isolation, not a universal pinout. Avoid direct-power feeds or improvised jumpers that could damage the control electronics or move the actuator unexpectedly.
