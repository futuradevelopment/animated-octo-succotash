# P1D99: Coolant-pump speed feedback loses its normal pulse duty

## Frequency and duty describe different parts of the signal

Honda P1D99 monitors the PCU electric coolant pump's NEWP feedback. The controller calculates pump speed from signal frequency, while the source describes the normal pulse duty as fixed at 50 percent. The fault criterion is a duty at the zero-percent or full-duty extreme for at least five seconds.

This distinction is useful: an abnormal duty can indicate loss of a usable pulse train, rather than simply a pump turning slightly slower than requested. The enable conditions require ON mode and at least 10 V on the 12-volt supply. The code therefore needs to be interpreted in the context of both pump operation and its feedback circuit.

## Check the feedback and supply branches separately

The factory possibilities include an open or grounded NEWP line, an open IG PCU EWP relay path, relay failure, pump failure and an internal PCU fault. Those alternatives show why the pump itself cannot be condemned solely from the DTC name.

Honda provides a controlled confirmation route through the HDS electric-water-pump test. That is preferable to improvised power feeds or bypassing the relay. The first qualifying drive cycle stores a pending record; recurrence on the next produces confirmed storage and MIL illumination, with a power-system indication identified in the monitor table.

Preserve the feedback and freeze information before clearing. Diagnosis must account for hot coolant and high-voltage equipment near the PCU; use the correct vehicle procedure and qualified personnel for any component access.
