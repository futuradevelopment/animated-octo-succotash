# P1083: Resolve the BMW rich/lean mapping before choosing a diagnosis

**The code mapping requires review.** The imported BMW catalog calls P1083 a lean Bank 1 mixture fault, but the reviewed M54 factory summary groups P1083/P1085 under a rich label. This draft does not resolve that conflict by choosing one direction or prescribing a mixture repair.

## What the source does establish

The table places the entry in fuel-trim limit monitoring and describes a controller restriction lasting at least 60 seconds. It requires closed-loop fuel operation and includes qualifying mixture, temperature, and airflow conditions. The monitor is therefore assessing a sustained correction boundary, not simply a momentary oxygen-sensor voltage.

The source's neighboring rich/lean entries are grouped in compact rows. That layout and the contradictory label make exact code-to-condition confirmation necessary before applying the directional interpretation to a vehicle.

## Preserve evidence that can resolve the conflict

The full BMW fault number, calibration, occurrence conditions, and actual fuel-correction direction are more useful than selecting parts from the generic title. Adding fuel to compensate for a lean observation and removing fuel to compensate for a rich observation lead to different diagnostic branches; an unresolved mapping cannot safely choose between them.

The table supplies no component replacement instruction or ranked cause list. It does not establish a failed oxygen sensor merely because oxygen feedback participates in mixture control.

Before this draft is treated as a checked diagnostic explanation, the P1083 mapping should be corroborated against the exact vehicle's authoritative fault data. The sustained closed-loop correction concept is supported, while the rich-versus-lean identity remains explicitly unresolved.
