# P1529: Exhaust VANOS actuator circuit low

The BMW canonical description assigns P1529 to the “B,” or exhaust, camshaft actuator circuit on Bank 1. In the M54 summary it is grouped with VANOS electrical-continuity monitoring, rather than camshaft timing deviation.

## The monitor is sensitive to driver operation

BMW's electrical row uses a voltage boundary below 2.9 V when the low-side driver is inactive and a current window of 0.02–2.0 A when it is active. It evaluates the circuit continuously over 1.25 seconds. Because the cells group several electrical variants, the complete service procedure is required to apply the correct measurement to the correct command state.

A low-signal code is not enough to establish a mechanical exhaust-camshaft fault. It calls for investigation of whether the controller can operate and observe the actuator circuit as intended. The summary does not name a specific damaged wire or give a connector pin assignment.

## Distinguish it from slow camshaft movement

The adjacent mechanically stuck monitor compares actuator activity time with an expected time plus an additional allowance. P1529 does not use that criterion. A slow response and an electrically low control path are different pieces of evidence, even though both concern VANOS.

The lamp requirement is two driving cycles. A repair check should establish valid electrical response during the relevant actuation states before treating an acceptable camshaft angle as proof of success. Apply these monitoring facts only within the stated emissions-table scope and the matching vehicle's detailed wiring and diagnostic procedure.
