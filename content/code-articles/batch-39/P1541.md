# P1541: Fuel-pump relay J17 has an open control circuit

The ATQ DTC table maps P1541/VAG 17949 to an open circuit at fuel-pump relay J17. That identifies an electrical relay-control problem. It does not alone establish that the in-tank pump motor has failed.

## Pump operation and relay activation are separate checks

Volkswagen's fuel-pump procedure begins with a battery of at least 11.5 V and a sound fuse 28. It distinguishes a pump that runs under the specified workshop remote-control test from one that remains inactive. If the pump runs in that controlled test, the procedure directs attention to relay activation.

A nonrunning pump then receives a supply-path assessment before the delivery unit is condemned. This branch structure is the useful diagnostic point: a silent pump can result from missing electrical delivery rather than a failed motor.

## Do not confuse flow testing with the open-circuit code

The same procedure contains delivery-rate, current-draw, and residual-pressure checks. Those address different questions and use controlled fuel-system equipment. P1541 does not, by itself, prove a low delivery rate or a leaking non-return valve.

Fuel systems require approved handling and pressure-release procedures; the source is not a reason to improvise a relay bypass or open a fuel line.

Verification should resolve the relay's open-circuit condition and confirm normal commanded pump operation. If a separate delivery concern remains, it should be tested on its own evidence. Replacing the fuel pump without checking J17 activation can leave the actual P1541 fault untouched.
