# P1326: Cylinder 2 knock control reaches its limit

Volkswagen P1326/VAG 17734 identifies the knock-control limit for cylinder 2. It is not the same as a low- or high-voltage knock-sensor circuit code. The ATQ chart includes abnormal mechanical noise, wiring, sensor attachment, and fuel type among its diagnostic considerations.

## A control-limit result is not proof of detonation damage

Loose components or broken brackets can produce noise that deserves investigation. The chart specifically calls for correcting abnormal engine noise, rather than immediately condemning the cylinder or its sensor. The code establishes that the control limit was reached, not why the relevant vibration was detected.

The component procedure uses measured-value groups 22–28 for knock-control assessment. It also emphasizes an exact 20 Nm sensor tightening torque for this engine and gold-plated terminals when servicing sensor connections. Mechanical attachment and connector quality are therefore part of the signal's integrity.

## Cylinder number and sensor number are different

Cylinder 2 identifies the controlled combustion location. It does not mean that the car has a dedicated “cylinder 2 sensor” to replace. Volkswagen identifies bank-related knock sensors in the component procedure, so component location must be taken from the exact engine information.

Verification follows appropriate electrical and mounting checks and evaluates recurrence under the prescribed operating conditions. The factory procedure includes road testing, which should be conducted professionally and safely. The source does not publish a numerical knock-retard limit for P1326, and no such value should be invented from the code title.
