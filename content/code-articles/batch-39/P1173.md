# P1173: Throttle-drive angle sensor 2 reads too high

Volkswagen P1173/VAG 17581 identifies an excessively high signal from throttle-drive angle sensor 2, G188. In the ATQ system, G188 is part of throttle module J338 along with motor G186 and angle sensor 1, G187. The accelerator-pedal sensors are separate components.

## The two throttle signals move in opposite directions

Volkswagen's component check observes display group 62. Sensor 1's percentage increases as the valve opens, while sensor 2's percentage decreases. G188's underlying voltage therefore moves from the high side toward the low side during opening.

This is a useful distinction when evaluating a “high” G188 signal: it is not valid to expect both throttle channels to rise together. A comparison based on that mistaken expectation could misdiagnose a normally operating second channel.

## Follow the electrical and mechanical evidence

The test requires an undamaged, clean throttle valve and coolant temperature of at least 80°C. It then distinguishes motor resistance, supply voltage, harness integrity, and pedal-input checks. These are separate branches; the DTC does not prove that the complete throttle module is defective.

The code table flags the EPC lamp for this fault. If the module is replaced, adaptation is required, and the component procedure also calls for transmission adaptation on automatic-transmission vehicles.

Verification should demonstrate smooth, opposing angle-sensor behavior and successful applicable adaptation. The published percentage ranges describe the service display and need not be fully traversed during the test; they should not be confused with a supplied P1173 setting threshold.
