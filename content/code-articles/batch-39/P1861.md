# P1861: The transmission receives a pedal-sensor fault message

Volkswagen P1861/VAG 18269 reports a G79-related fault message from the engine controller. Although the transmission chart calls G79 a throttle-position sensor, the ATQ component procedure identifies it at the accelerator pedal alongside G185.

## Check the origin of the message

The chart directs reading and repairing the ECM's fault memory. It also lists open/short wiring between G79 and the ECM, a faulty G79, and an incorrect or faulty transmission controller. A fault received by the transmission is therefore not automatically a failed transmission sensor.

A specific service note prioritizes connector corrosion or water ingress. That is useful evidence for the initial inspection, particularly when the problem is intermittent.

## The pedal's two signals have a defined relationship

The ATQ component test expects both pedal percentages to rise smoothly, with G79 approximately twice G185. This differs from the opposing angle signals inside the throttle body. Confusing those sensor families would lead to the wrong plausibility expectation.

Measured-value groups 008 and 009 are referenced in the transmission chart, while the engine's pedal procedure uses group 62. The tool and controller context should accompany the data interpretation.

Verification should resolve the originating pedal/ECM fault, confirm valid pedal information, and establish that the transmission no longer receives the error message. Replacing the TCM before investigating the ECM record and the specifically noted connector conditions would skip important branches of the manufacturer procedure.
