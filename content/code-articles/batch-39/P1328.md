# P1328: Knock sensor 2 exceeds its signal limit

BMW's 168101/P1328 monitor concerns knock sensor 2, identified here with cylinders 3 and 4. It recognizes a signal consistently above 4.8 V and records the fault after the condition remains present for more than 20 crankshaft revolutions.

## High signal is the starting evidence

The chart calls this engine noise above the permitted limit, but its stated test is an electrical signal threshold. It lists a loose sensor screw and defective wiring as potential origins. Those facts prevent a reliable diagnosis from stopping at the everyday meaning of the word “knock.” The code by itself does not establish fuel quality, damaged bearings, or any particular combustion problem.

The monitor requires engine speed above 1,500 rpm. It also includes an airflow condition whose printed units are unusual, so this explanation does not repurpose that line into a test specification. Matching service data and the vehicle's captured operating conditions are needed for reproduction.

## Check the signal path and attachment

BMW's service direction is to consider the sensor mounting, check the harness to the DME, and then replace the sensor as appropriate. The distinction from the corresponding low-signal fault is the direction of the measured error; the relevant physical and electrical connections still need examination.

Reduced power is listed as a possible effect. A repair should be verified with a credible signal under the appropriate operating conditions, rather than by the absence of audible knock alone. The published chart does not provide a mounting torque or connector pinout, so those must come from the exact vehicle repair procedure.
