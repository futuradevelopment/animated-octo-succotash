# P1568: The throttle module has a mechanical malfunction

P1568/VAG 17976 identifies a mechanical malfunction of throttle module J338 in the ATQ chart. It is separate from the electrical-drive entry P1558 and directs the technician to the throttle-module check.

## Mechanical travel affects adaptation

Volkswagen explains that adaptation learns several throttle positions with ignition on and the engine stopped. The sequence includes movement to an emergency spring position and a spring-return check. Dirt or binding that prevents the valve reaching its mechanical stop can cancel this learning process.

Low battery voltage and faulty wiring can also interrupt adaptation. Consequently, a failed adaptation attempt is not, by itself, proof that the mechanical assembly must be replaced. The procedure requires at least 11.5 V, coolant within 5–95°C, a closed throttle, and no stored faults before the learning routine.

## Observe movement and feedback together

The module check compares its two angle signals, which move in opposite directions. Smooth feedback and the ability to reach the required positions provide different information from an electrical continuity check alone.

The source flags the EPC lamp for P1568. It also explains that deenergized throttle operation can produce surging idle and delayed accelerator response, but those symptoms do not isolate one failed part.

Verification should establish unobstructed operation and successful applicable adaptation after the cause is corrected. The procedure contains a display-group inconsistency in one illustration, so the exact tool workflow should follow current matching service instructions rather than a guessed sequence from that isolated line.
