# P1678: The EPC warning-lamp wire is shorted to ground

P1678/VAG 18086 identifies a short to ground in Volkswagen's EPC warning-lamp circuit. The ATQ procedure treats the lamp and its wiring as a diagnostic subject in their own right, separate from the engine-load-control faults that the lamp normally announces.

## A lamp that stays on needs context

The prescribed functional check expects illumination at ignition on and extinguishing a few seconds after starting when appropriate. If the lamp remains lit, the procedure specifically checks the ECM-to-cluster connection for a short to ground.

That branch is a useful match to P1678. Persistent illumination can reflect the reporting wire's electrical state; it does not automatically establish a jammed throttle valve or a failed accelerator pedal.

## Do not ignore other stored faults

The EPC lamp normally accompanies an ECM fault entry when the electronic load-control system detects a problem. A technician should distinguish a lamp-circuit fault from any separate engine-control faults also present. Correcting the warning wire would not repair an unrelated throttle problem, and replacing a throttle component would not necessarily repair this wire.

The source requires sound fuses and uses controlled test-box checks before a controller replacement decision. It provides no universal lamp-driver voltage threshold for P1678.

Verification should demonstrate the correct ignition-on check, appropriate post-start lamp behavior, and no recurrence of the ground-short entry. Simply extinguishing the lamp or erasing memory is insufficient if the reporting circuit has not been shown to operate correctly.
