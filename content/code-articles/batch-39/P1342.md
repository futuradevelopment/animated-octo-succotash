# P1342: Cylinder 1 misfire recognized during starting

BMW P1342 identifies cylinder 1 in the start-period misfire family. The M54 summary evaluates crankshaft-speed fluctuations rather than directly testing the ignition coil. The initial monitoring branch considers an emissions-threshold exceedance within the first 1,000 revolutions after the engine starts.

## Timing is central to the diagnosis

The chart distinguishes that early-start assessment from the later running-misfire branch, which requires repeated exceedances after the first 1,000 revolutions. A cylinder that becomes smooth once warm can therefore still have produced relevant evidence during the monitored starting period.

The listed emissions criterion is greater than 1.8% over 3,000 ignitions, with additional engine-speed, air-mass, and changing-load conditions. Those conditions make this a calibrated monitor, not a rule that any single uneven crankshaft event establishes a failed part.

## Cylinder identity does not identify the cause

Cylinder 1 is where the controller assigns the misfire observation. The table does not distinguish spark, fuel delivery, compression, or mixture causes and gives no instruction to replace a coil solely from P1342.

The corresponding catalyst-damage/fuel-cutoff family is listed separately, with a shorter revolution window and immediate lamp behavior. Do not assume those protective actions from this start-period code alone.

Verification should retain the original start conditions and evaluate the early running period after the underlying cause is established. A warm idle check by itself does not reproduce the event that defines P1342. The source's exact emissions test-group scope must also match the vehicle before applying its numerical criteria.
