# P1524: Intake VANOS actuator circuit high

The BMW definition of P1524 identifies the intake, or “A,” camshaft actuator's high-signal circuit on Bank 1. The M54 monitor summary places it in the VANOS electrical-continuity group, alongside the corresponding exhaust-side entry.

## Electrical validity comes before timing performance

The table evaluates the low-side driver differently when it is active and inactive. The active branch lists a current window of 0.02–2.0 A; the inactive branch uses a voltage boundary of 2.9 V. Its electrical checks are continuous with a 1.25-second interval and a two-driving-cycle lamp requirement.

Those combined cells do not provide a separate high-signal pin-level test for P1524. A technician must use the matching actuator procedure to interpret the circuit under the correct commanded state, rather than applying a current window to an inactive driver.

## A timing code would ask a different question

The same source separately monitors camshaft alignment to the crankshaft and whether the actuator takes too long to achieve a commanded angle. P1524 is in the electrical family. It does not by itself prove that the camshaft is mechanically mistimed or that VANOS is hydraulically stuck.

The next useful check is the intake actuator's electrical control path, with the exact vehicle wiring and test equipment. The summary contains no connector pinout or automatic replacement instruction.

Verification should establish valid actuator electrical behavior and then confirm the applicable VANOS function. A mechanically plausible camshaft angle alone cannot prove that an intermittent circuit-high condition has been resolved.
