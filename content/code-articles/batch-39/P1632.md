# P1632: Throttle adaptation could not meet its prerequisites

BMW 101401/P1632 reports that required throttle-valve adaptation could not be performed because its general conditions were not satisfied. It identifies an unsuccessful prerequisite check, so it does not by itself demonstrate a mechanically defective throttle body.

## Conditions that can prevent adaptation

The N63 chart requires Terminal 15 on, the engine off, electrical-system voltage above 10 V, and no other throttle-related diagnostic codes in memory. It also specifies intake-air and coolant temperatures above −10°C, with coolant below 142°C, and a five-second condition.

A convenience-start event after DME programming or replacement is explicitly listed as another reason adaptation can be prevented. That makes recent repair and programming history particularly relevant. A code appearing immediately after module work should be interpreted alongside how the vehicle was started and whether the adaptation routine had the opportunity to run.

## The next decision is why adaptation was blocked

BMW directs the technician to determine whether another stored fault or the start sequence prevented adaptation. Replacing a throttle body without answering that question can leave the actual prerequisite failure unresolved.

The service note says adaptation is triggered following replacement of the throttle valve or DME. The chart also lists power reduction and governed engine speed as possible consequences. Those effects make an appropriate workshop assessment necessary; a simple erase-and-restart result cannot establish that adaptation completed.

Successful verification should demonstrate that the qualifying conditions were met and adaptation finished, with related throttle faults resolved. This chart does not provide a universal manual pedal sequence or an aftermarket-tool adaptation procedure.
