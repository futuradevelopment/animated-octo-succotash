# P0129: BMW ambient-pressure plausibility and a sudden pressure drop

BMW's 105102/P0129 chart describes a plausibility check on ambient pressure. Its concern is an excessively rapid reduction in the barometric-pressure value, with a stated recognition difference of 50 hPa. This gives the code a more specific interpretation than simply assuming the car is operating at high altitude.

## Why the pressure history matters

The controller uses Terminal 15 and an electrical-system voltage above 10 V as enabling conditions. The fault is entered immediately when recognized. The chart does not supply a sample interval for the 50 hPa decrease, so that number should not be converted into an invented pressure-change-per-second specification.

For diagnosis, retain the measured pressure history and occurrence information. A low but stable ambient-pressure value and an implausible sudden drop are different observations. The published monitor supports investigating the latter; it does not prove that any reading below sea-level pressure is defective.

## The controller replacement condition

This particular service instruction identifies the DME as the potential source and makes replacement conditional on a continuously present fault or multiple recorded occurrences. An isolated code therefore deserves careful identity and recurrence checks before an expensive control module decision.

The chart reports no particular drivability symptom and no electronic power reduction for this entry, while listing the US emissions lamp as enabled. Those flags describe this calibration's reporting behavior. They do not establish that another vehicle with P0129 uses the same internal sensor arrangement or replacement procedure.
