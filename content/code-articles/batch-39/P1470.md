# P1470: The leak-detection pump has an electrical circuit fault

In the M62 TU summary, P1470 belongs to the leak-detection pump's electrical monitor. The table distinguishes continuity toward ground, an open circuit, and a supply-side overcurrent condition within this one code group.

## Three electrical checks share one code

The stated criteria include voltage below 3 V for the ground-related branch, below 5 V for the open branch, and current above 2.4 A for the supply-related branch. These represent different circuit states. They are not three simultaneous measurements that identify one particular failed component.

The monitor operates with battery voltage between 9.5 and 17 V and engine speed above 40 rpm. It is evaluated immediately and continuously, with the emissions lamp associated with two driving cycles.

## Separate the circuit from the pneumatic result

The same table describes evaporative leak checks using the time between pump strokes and separate reed-switch functional checks. P1470 is the electrical-continuity entry. It therefore does not establish a leaking fuel cap, a particular leak diameter, or a stuck reed switch solely from its number.

The next useful investigation is the pump's applicable electrical path, using the exact vehicle wiring and controlled tests. The summary contains no connector pinout or instruction to replace the pump without distinguishing the circuit branches.

Verification should demonstrate correct electrical operation and, where required, completion of the separate evaporative test. A smoke test alone cannot establish that an electrical continuity problem is resolved, just as clearing P1470 cannot prove that the fuel-vapor system has passed its leak monitor.
