# P1471: Leak-detection pump V144 has a supply-side circuit fault

The ATQ chart identifies P1471/VAG 17879 as a short to B+ affecting leak-detection pump V144. Its service direction includes the pump and evaporative-canister system, but the code's defining branch is electrical rather than a measured leak size.

## The pump contains more than one electrical path

Volkswagen's component test gives two distinct resistance ranges across different contact pairs: 640–720 ohms and 17.5–27.5 ohms. Those values are not alternatives for a single winding. They demonstrate why identifying the exact connector contacts matters before declaring the pump good or bad.

The separate supply check requires fuse 29 to be sound and tests the feed with the engine idling. If supply is absent, the procedure follows the wire toward the relay panel. The output-test procedure additionally distinguishes a pulsed command reaching the pump from a missing command.

## Keep the B+ branch separate from leakage

A pump that fails to click can have an electrical command or component problem; it does not automatically prove a leaking tank. Conversely, a sealed evaporative system does not eliminate a short to supply in V144's circuit.

Professional testing should use the correct connector and equipment rather than direct improvised energization. The DTC table does not provide a universal circuit-high voltage threshold.

Verification should establish correct pump electrical behavior and the relevant evaporative function. Replacing a fuel cap without resolving the supply-side circuit fault would not address the evidence represented by P1471.
