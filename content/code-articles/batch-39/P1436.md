# P1436: An electrical fault in the secondary-air pump relay circuit

P1436/VAG 17844 identifies an electrical malfunction in the secondary-air pump relay J299 circuit. Unlike the adjacent supply-short and ground-short entries, its title does not specify the direction of the electrical fault.

## Use the output test to separate the branches

Volkswagen's ATQ procedure commands J299 and expects the pump motor V101 to operate in intervals. If the pump remains inactive, a controlled connector test determines whether the pulsed supply is reaching it. Correct supply with no motor response is different evidence from a missing supply.

The missing-supply branch includes the relay fuse, relay feed, and relay/control wiring. The source therefore supports examining the control chain instead of assuming that every P1436 occurrence is a failed pump motor.

## Relay behavior is not airflow measurement

A relay that clicks or a motor that runs has passed only part of the system assessment. P1436 is not a published secondary-air flow threshold, and the chart does not establish catalyst or passage condition from this electrical code.

The actuator test has defined prerequisites, a sequential component order, and a ten-minute timeout. It also operates the fuel pump and is aborted when engine speed is recognized. Those constraints should be respected when interpreting an apparent lack of response.

Verification should confirm the correct relay-driven pump response and no recurring electrical malfunction. The procedure can eventually lead toward a relay, motor, wiring, or controller decision, but only after its relevant branch is supported. The P-code alone does not select one of those parts.
