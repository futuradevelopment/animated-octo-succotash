# P013D: bank-2 downstream lean-to-rich response

P013D checks the bank-2 downstream oxygen sensor’s lean-to-rich transition response. The cited monitor evaluates a filtered timing quantity after correcting the raw response for exhaust mass flow.

## Why direction and correction matter

The printed threshold is above 0.8 second, with a flow-dependent correction of 0.03 to 0.08 second. The row lists two counts/EWMA and one-trip reporting. This direction has its own timing calibration; the rich-to-lean threshold from P013C should not be substituted.

The monitor requires commanded primary lambda of 1 and an operating engine. Its enable list includes speed, load, integrated airflow, coolant temperature, and an interval without a gear change. Further flow and catalyst-temperature windows appear in the continuation.

## Interpreting a slow result

An observed delay during a shift may not represent an eligible test because the monitor specifically controls the gear-change interval. Similarly, a stopwatch measurement of a raw voltage transition omits the correction and filtering used by the controller.

Useful diagnosis retains the test direction, operating state, and actual monitor result. Verification should show the lean-to-rich diagnostic completing successfully in its required window, not just demonstrate that the sensor voltage eventually rises. The source defines detection behavior but does not identify a particular replacement part or establish a universal response-time specification for all oxygen sensors.
