# U3018: disagreement between two module power inputs

U3018 is a correlation check between two sensed 12-volt power inputs to a control module. The documented BCM monitor compares circuit A with circuit B and fails when their absolute difference exceeds the allowed amount for long enough.

## Difference matters more than either reading alone

The table gives a difference threshold above 4.00 and describes the quantities as the two 12-volt battery-power circuit voltages. Failure requires four seconds within a five-second window. A brief discrepancy is therefore different evidence from a disagreement that occupies most of the observation period.

The diagnostic must be enabled and the battery-present condition satisfied. The starter-state wording in the table is awkwardly expressed with a Boolean value; it should be resolved in the correct service data before someone designs a crank-state test from it.

## Interpreting and confirming the result

Two inputs can disagree even when one looks normal in isolation. Conversely, equally low readings can agree and would not establish a pass of every other supply diagnostic. This makes simultaneous input information more useful than an isolated battery measurement for understanding U3018.

The row assigns Type B, two-trip reporting. Verification should show the two sensed inputs tracking within the permitted difference while this comparison is enabled, and should distinguish a completed pass from a diagnostic that never ran. The code does not by itself identify a battery, harness, connector, or control module as the failed part.

## Documentation and applicability

This explanation is limited to the cited sections of GM’s 2025 25OBDG04B file. The file does not establish that this monitor or calibration applies to every vehicle; confirm the reporting module and vehicle-specific service information.
