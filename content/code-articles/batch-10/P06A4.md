# P06A4: transmission speed-sensor supply 1 undervoltage

P06A4 monitors speed-sensor supply VSS1 in the transmission-controller table. This implementation is especially important because the catalog’s reference-voltage D wording does not mean the monitored rail is necessarily a five-volt sensor supply.

## The documented threshold is eight volts

The table fails VSS1 below 8,000 millivolts, with 250 millivolts of hysteresis and at least 75 milliseconds of confirmation. Battery voltage must be above 8.75 and below 18 volts. Cranking and Stop key status are excluded, and P0882 or P0883 disables the check.

These conditions distinguish an inadequate regulated sensor supply from an observation made during an excluded vehicle-power state. They also show why applying a generic five-volt-reference test would be inappropriate here.

## What to confirm

Establish which speed-sensor supply the vehicle’s controller identifies as VSS1 and retain its voltage together with battery and power-state information. The code does not directly state that the shaft speed itself is low; it concerns the electrical supply used by the sensor network.

Verification should show the VSS1 low-supply diagnostic completing after its battery and state prerequisites are met. A normal speed value seen later is narrower evidence than a demonstrated stable supply and monitor pass. The table does not name a particular sensor as the cause of the rail undervoltage.

## Documentation and applicability

These details describe the monitor in GM’s 2025 25OBDG07A Part2 file. Its application-matrix filename does not match the download index, so model and engine applicability remain unresolved. Use the vehicle’s correct service information before applying these calibration details.
