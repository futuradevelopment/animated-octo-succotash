# U3000: why the module and failure subtype matter

U3000 is a family of control-module faults, not one specific failed component. In the front-camera section examined here, GM associates it with memory, electronics, programming, calibration, configuration, and software-compatibility checks.

## Distinguish the failure before choosing a repair

A memory-checksum failure is different evidence from a camera that has never been calibrated. The table also distinguishes missing application data, missing option configuration, and incompatible software. Those branches cannot responsibly be collapsed into a generic instruction to replace the camera.

Several checks operate continuously with supply voltage between 9 and 16 volts and the relevant diagnostic enabled. The not-programmed check instead runs at startup. A restart can therefore provide a test opportunity for one subtype while proving little about the conditions that produced another.

## Effects and verification

The documented default action depends on the consumer of camera data: adaptive cruise control may be disabled, or front-camera information may stop being used. The table calls these safety, non-MIL, emissions-neutral diagnostics. An unlit engine warning lamp is therefore not evidence that the camera system passed.

Record the reporting module and complete failure subtype before interpreting U3000. After an appropriate professional repair or calibration, verification should address that same subtype and confirm the affected assistance function through the applicable service procedure. The separate HDLM extract contains inconsistent module wording; this article relies on the explicit FCM table instead.

## Documentation and applicability

This explanation is limited to the cited sections of GM’s 2025 25OBDG04B file. The file does not establish that this monitor or calibration applies to every vehicle; confirm the reporting module and vehicle-specific service information.
