# P233E: A low regulator supply circuit can create a high voltage drop across its driver

P233E identifies a low supply-circuit condition for fuel-pressure regulator B's solenoid. The GM monitor evaluates the regulator's high-side driver while it is on. Its description uses a high voltage across that driver as evidence of a ground-directed fault.

## Measurement reference changes the meaning of high and low

Voltage across a switching driver is not the same quantity as output voltage relative to ground. The source associates the on-state across-driver finding with less than 0.5 Ω between the regulator high-side connection and controller ground. That relationship explains why the generic low-circuit name can coexist with a high across-driver voltage observation.

Powertrain-relay voltage must exceed 11 V, run/crank must be active and engine cranking must be excluded. The controller confirms sixty-one failures within 122 samples, evaluated every 6.25 milliseconds, with one-trip reporting.

## Keep the command and reference with the trace

An output measurement without its reference points cannot establish the documented criterion. Likewise, an off-state reading does not reproduce this powered-on assessment. Preserve driver command, supply state and the exact electrical status at the event.

The monitor does not directly diagnose low rail pressure or identify a defective pressure-regulator valve. It also provides no connector pin or safe stand-alone probing instruction. The applicable circuit and high-pressure fuel-system service procedures are necessary before locating the ground-directed path or choosing a repair.
