# P245D: EGR cooler bypass circuit high under motor command

The documented P245D strategy checks whether commands to the high-pressure EGR cooler bypass valve are shorted to the power supply. Its component description names the bypass DC motor. “Circuit high” does not mean that exhaust temperature or bypass opening is excessively high.

## Current and operating state belong together

A hardware-reported current above 8 A through the H-bridge switches is the failure criterion. This check runs with the H-bridge driver on, rather than proving a short merely from voltage observed while the output is idle. A technician needs the output command and driver status alongside any electrical measurement.

The sampling rule is 160 failures in 200 samples on a 12.5 ms task. Type B, two-trip reporting is listed. Enable conditions include powertrain relay supply above 11 V, completion of cranking, an enabled diagnostic system and a determinate hardware error status. A low-supply or reset interval cannot be substituted for the qualifying operating conditions.

## Why the companion code matters

The ground-short monitor uses the same overcurrent limit. Consequently, an overcurrent trace by itself cannot distinguish the P245D power-short classification from the low-circuit alternative. Preserve both codes and the controller's hardware fault information before clearing the record.

Check the applicable schematic for the motor's two driven connections and the controller output arrangement. Neither “high” nor the 8 A criterion identifies a particular connector pin. Position feedback and cooler-flow complaints may provide additional evidence, but they do not replace this electrical assessment. The summary table supplies a detection strategy, not a basis for replacing the complete cooler assembly.
