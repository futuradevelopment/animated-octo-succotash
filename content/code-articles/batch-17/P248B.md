# P248B: Reductant heater A supply high relative to the powertrain relay

The GM P248B row checks excessive DEF tank-heater supply voltage relative to the powertrain relay. This is a differential electrical check. A high battery-voltage reading on its own does not establish the condition, and a low tank temperature does not demonstrate this particular fault.

## The reference voltage determines the meaning

The failure criterion is tank-heater supply voltage minus powertrain-relay voltage greater than 2.0 V. Preserve both readings under the same conditions: substituting battery voltage for the named relay reference can change the interpretation. The tank heater must be commanded on for this monitor.

The table requires run/crank voltage above 11.0 V and powertrain-relay voltage above 11.0 V. It additionally lists run/crank voltage above 6.0 V or engine running, excludes engine cranking, and requires no U010E reductant-module communication fault. These are stated prerequisites, not instructions to force a heater on or bypass its control.

## Qualification and next diagnostic evidence

Sixteen failures out of 20 samples are needed, with each sample taken at 500 ms. Type A, one-trip reporting is specified. A brief supply transition should be distinguished from the sustained differential captured by that count.

Record the two supply channels, command state and communication status before clearing the code. If the channels disagree, identify where each value is measured or reported in the correct wiring and controller documentation. The table does not locate a short or prove a failed heater element. A common system-voltage rise and a tank-heater supply that rises above its relay reference are different electrical patterns; the latter is the comparison relevant to this monitor.
