# P2161: A transfer-case speed drop can compete with the no-activity timer

P2161 is an intermittent, erratic or high vehicle-speed sensor B entry. In this GM application, the strategy targets an unrealistic drop in the transfer-case output speed signal. The source-specific meaning is therefore about the speed history, not simply a permanently high reading.

## The change is assessed within a stable operating opportunity

The table lists a raw speed delta above 650 rpm with a 25-millisecond update rate. It also describes a failure-time threshold above three seconds and a subsequent count threshold. Range changes and stability conditions qualify the opportunity; the source includes a delay after a four-wheel-drive mode change and a period of stable speed before assessment.

These conditions explain why a transient during a legitimate range transition should not be treated as the same event. Preserve the raw speed on both sides of the drop and the range-change timing rather than looking only at the final value.

## A shared event can lead to different completed codes

GM explicitly notes that a drop may also start P2160's low-activity timer. The two timers can then race to completion. Which code appears first does not, by itself, identify a separate physical cause.

Other speed and crank-signal validity conditions also matter to the comparison. The table supports investigating an implausible speed transition but does not locate a failed sensor, harness section or controller. That isolation requires the exact transfer-case signal architecture and its applicable service procedure.
