# P248C: Reductant heater B supply low in the GM line-heater monitor

This GM source describes P248C as DEF line-heater supply undervoltage. The monitored heater serves the line, so a generic heater B label should not automatically direct diagnosis to the tank heater. Confirm the vehicle's heater-channel mapping before selecting connectors or parts.

## Look for a supply deficit during heating

The calculation is system battery voltage minus line-heater supply voltage. A result above the printed 3.00 threshold is the failure condition. No unit accompanies that threshold in the original table; use the relationship to interpret the monitor, and obtain the applicable service specification before making a pass/fail electrical measurement.

A heating request is essential. The row also requires calibration enable, powertrain-relay supply in range, active run/crank and exclusion of cranking. Communication with the reductant control module must be present; U010E is identified as the communication fault that prevents the stated condition.

## Keep the line and tank observations separate

Record battery voltage and line-heater supply at the same time as the line-heater command. A tank-heater supply reading cannot stand in for the line channel, even when both heaters share parts of the upstream supply. Correlating both channels can help establish whether the observed deficit is confined to one monitored branch, but this table does not supply their wiring topology.

The decision requires 16 failures in 20 samples at a 500 ms time basis, with Type A, one-trip behavior. Verify whether the fault returns during an eligible heating request. A cold line or an inactive heater alone is insufficient evidence for this electrical code, and the monitor cannot identify the failed component without circuit-specific testing.
