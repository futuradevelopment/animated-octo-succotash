# P0526: A nearly absent fan-speed signal needs a valid speed expectation

P0526 identifies a fan-speed sensor circuit fault. The cited GM monitor asks whether the feedback remains meaningfully above zero while the fan is expected to be rotating. That is a signal-rationality assessment, not direct proof that the fan's mechanical drive has failed.

## The measured signal and the enabling expectation differ

The malfunction description uses a 4 rpm feedback boundary for considering the circuit operational. A separate enable refers to a minimum fan-speed threshold of 39 rpm. Those values serve different purposes: one judges the observed feedback, while the other helps establish an operating opportunity in which feedback should exist.

The diagnostic also requires supply of at least 11 V and no relevant speed-sensor or output-driver faults. It confirms 250 failures within 300 samples and uses two-trip reporting. A result outside the stated opportunity should not be compared directly with those counters.

## Do not turn a misprinted unit into an invented delay

The table includes a time-above-minimum-speed prerequisite but prints its value with rpm units. Inspection of the original page confirms the inconsistency. This article therefore does not guess a number of seconds or use that cell as a reproduction instruction.

Preserve the measured speed, expected-speed condition and diagnostic eligibility. Further checks must establish whether the feedback is absent because of its signal path or because the assumed rotation is not occurring. The monitor summary alone cannot choose a sensor, harness or fan replacement.
