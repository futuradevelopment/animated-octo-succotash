# P001B: A low intake-actuator circuit result depends on the powered-on state

P001B describes a low control-circuit condition for the intake camshaft profile actuator A on bank 1. The cited GM row treats it as a short-to-ground classification of intake profile actuator 1. Its most useful detail is the command state: the driver is powered on when its voltage feedback is judged abnormal.

## Why an uncommanded reading is different

The controller uses internal voltage thresholds calibrated to represent a signal-to-ground impedance below 0.5 Ω. That is the electrical criterion behind the monitor, not a published connector-pin procedure. A low signal observed when the controller is deliberately leaving the output inactive does not answer the same question.

Supply must be above 11 V, the diagnostic must be enabled, and the ignition must be in crank or run. The row explicitly requires an on command. Four failing samples out of five provide confirmation, followed by the documented one-trip reporting behavior.

## Evidence that narrows the problem

A useful capture aligns actuator command with feedback and supply, rather than collecting a voltage without timing context. If the event appears only during a command, preserve that relationship for the vehicle-specific circuit checks.

The finding does not prove that the sliding cam mechanism is stuck, nor does it specify whether the low path lies inside the actuator, wiring or controller. Those are subsequent isolation questions. Treating P001B as a timing-chain or actuator replacement instruction would go beyond the evidence in this monitor summary.
