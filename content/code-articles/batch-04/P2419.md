# P2419: EVAP switching-valve circuit-low row contains conflicting fault wording

P2419 is labeled EVAP switching-valve control circuit low in the reviewed GM table. The row belongs to the conventional vented leak-detection system with fuel tank zone module, but its detailed text contains an important inconsistency.

## What can be read reliably

The component heading names a circuit-low fault, and the threshold column specifies less than 0.5 ohm between output and controller ground. Those details are consistent with a low or ground-short signature.

However, the strategy and malfunction columns repeatedly describe an open circuit, apparently carrying wording from another row. The article therefore does not treat that open-circuit prose as a verified explanation of P2419. The exact driver-state and fault-class procedure should be confirmed in the applicable service information before a detailed test is designed.

## Useful diagnostic boundaries remain

The row identifies the switching-valve electrical control circuit, excludes an EVAP-device communication fault, and lists 50 failures out of 63 samples at 250-millisecond intervals. It is classified Type B, two trips.

These facts distinguish the code from a direct proof of mechanical valve position or a measured fuel-vapor leak. A valve-performance conclusion cannot be drawn solely from this electrical code.

Preserve the circuit-low description and communication status, then use the vehicle-specific electrical procedure to resolve the abnormal path. Verification should establish correct switching-valve circuit behavior and no recurring monitor failure. The listed impedance is not an instruction for resistance testing an energized circuit, and the source does not mandate replacing the valve.
