# P2420: EVAP switching-valve driver detects a short to power

P2420 is the switching-valve control circuit-high diagnosis for the reviewed GM vented EVAP system with a fuel tank zone module. The monitor evaluates the electrical low-side driver, rather than directly sensing whether the valve is mechanically open or closed.

## Fault detection while commanded on

The controller checks voltage during the driver's on state. A value outside its accepted range indicates a short-to-power condition. The source lists less than 0.5 ohm between output and controller power as the controller design specification for that fault signature.

The command state matters because a voltage observation has meaning only in relation to what the driver is trying to do. The impedance figure is not a general instruction to measure a live circuit or an expected valve-coil resistance.

## An active fault triggers an extra test

When P2420 is active, the source says the switching valve is commanded on for 15 seconds as an intrusive test. A recorded valve command may therefore belong to fault evaluation rather than the normal vapor-system sequence.

The standard decision uses 50 failed samples out of 63, sampled every 250 milliseconds, and requires the EVAP-device communication fault to be absent. Missing communication changes whether a valid electrical result is available.

Verification should confirm proper on-state driver response and no renewed failure after an applicable completed test. Type B, two-trip behavior is documented. The code does not identify a vapor leak, prove mechanical sticking or establish that the switching valve must be replaced.

## Source and applicability

The documented control architecture and calibration require confirmation for the vehicle being diagnosed. [GM diagnostic table, PDF page 651](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=651).
