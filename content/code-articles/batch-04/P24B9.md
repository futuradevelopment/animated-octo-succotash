# P24B9: EVAP pressure correlation compares the vented system with ambient pressure

P24B9 describes a correlation check between leak-detection-pump pressure and barometric pressure in the reviewed GM EVAP sequence. A crucial limitation is that the displayed calibration marks this diagnostic disabled. Its documented algorithm explains the code, but does not prove the test is active on a particular vehicle.

## Two checks serve different purposes

With the vacuum pump off and switching valve vented, the controller compares the ELCP absolute-pressure reading with barometric pressure derived from the MAP sensor. A difference above 3,000 Pa increments the failure counter during a 14-second evaluation.

The first ambient check can report failure, but does not immediately report a pass. A similar final check later in the sequence must finish before the passing result is reported. An acceptable early reading therefore does not establish successful completion of the entire correlation diagnosis.

## Off-state eligibility is extensive

The documented sequence uses scheduled wake-up windows hours after propulsion stops, with fuel-level, temperature, pressure and voltage restrictions. Key-up, service-bay testing, refueling and certain device-control activity can abort it. The counter criteria are 50 failures in 63 samples at 100-millisecond intervals.

Related pressure, valve, pump and communication faults also affect availability. Before interpreting a missing result, establish whether this monitor is actually enabled in the vehicle's calibration and whether the sequence reached its final stage.

Verification requires consistent ambient-pressure correlation in the applicable completed test. This table does not identify which pressure source is wrong or prove that the EVAP system has a leak.
