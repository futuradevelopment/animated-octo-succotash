# C0512: right-rear wheel-speed signal current below its limit

C0512 covers the right-rear wheel-speed circuit/open monitor in this GM brake module. The controller evaluates current on the signal line and flags a value below 3.8 mA for 0.12 seconds.

## Why an open circuit is not the only explanation

The OEM strategy explicitly describes either an interrupted circuit or a short to ground. The measured low-current condition cannot, by itself, choose between them. A diagnostic that orders a sensor solely from the word “open” skips that distinction.

The listed enable conditions are ignition on and successful completion of the wheel-sensor undervoltage checks. No vehicle-speed prerequisite appears in this C0512 row. This is therefore different evidence from a comparison that only becomes meaningful as the vehicle moves.

## Bound the next investigation

Use the complete brake-module status and the correct circuit documentation to identify the right-rear signal path. A supply-related fault may interfere with prerequisite tests and should not be ignored just because C0512 names one wheel. The direction and wrong-component entries below it on the source page have their own codes; they do not establish sensor installation direction as the cause of C0512.

Verification should establish that the enabling tests finish and that the low-current diagnostic passes after the verified cause is addressed. Clearing memory without monitor completion is not a repair test. Electrical access and work around the brake system require the vehicle-specific professional procedure.

## Source and applicability

This explanation covers the documented 2025 GM monitor, not every vehicle using C0512. Confirm the vehicle’s module and calibration before applying its limits. [GM diagnostic table, PDF page 60](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=60).
