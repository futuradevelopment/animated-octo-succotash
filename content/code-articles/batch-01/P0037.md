# P0037: bank 1 sensor 2 heater circuit-low when switched off

P0037 identifies a bank 1 sensor 2 oxygen-heater circuit-low condition in the referenced GM ECM. It is an off-state check of the low-side driver for behavior consistent with a short to ground.

## Why heater operation is not enough

The controller’s voltage comparison is designed around a short-to-ground specification below 0.5 Ω between output and controller ground. A circuit that draws current under an on command has not necessarily demonstrated that it releases correctly under an off command. The latter is the state this monitor evaluates.

The check requires crank or run, engine speed above 400 rpm and voltage above 11 V. It uses 20 failing samples out of 25, with 250 milliseconds between samples. Some controllers may also set P0036, according to the source.

## Evidence that changes the next step

Retain the heater command state and companion codes. The documented P0036 overlap means two codes should not automatically be counted as two defective components. Also distinguish the heater-control circuit from the oxygen-measurement signal: “circuit low” here is not a diagnosis of a lean exhaust mixture.

Vehicle-specific electrical testing is needed to identify whether the abnormal path lies in the connected circuit or its control hardware. Verification should establish a successful enabled off-state test after the verified cause is corrected. A cleared memory or a plausible warmed-up sensor signal does not independently verify this monitor.

## Source and applicability

This explanation covers the documented 2025 GM monitor, not every vehicle using P0037. Confirm the vehicle’s module and calibration before applying its limits. [GM diagnostic table, PDF page 92](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=92).
