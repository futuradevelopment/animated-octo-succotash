# P0128: coolant warms too slowly for the calculated engine energy

P0128 checks whether engine-outlet coolant reaches its target after the engine has supplied an allowed amount of modeled energy. This GM monitor is more specific than a fixed timer that simply expects a universal operating temperature.

## Ambient temperature changes the target

For the primary ambient range of 10–52°C, the target is the radiator flow-control device’s start-to-open temperature minus 11.1°C, with a floor of 67°C. A colder secondary range uses a different offset and floor. The permitted accumulated energy comes from supporting calibration tables, so no single warm-up time can be assigned from this page alone.

The source accounts for energy entering and leaving the cooling system, including combustion and several heat-loss paths. It requires an eligible soak, running window, distance and valid supporting inputs, and runs once per ignition cycle. The tertiary-range text is not sufficiently informative to generalize here.

## Supported causes and useful evidence

The OEM description explicitly allows a coolant sensor biased low, a thermostat stuck open or another cooling-system warm-up problem. It does not prove the thermostat is defective merely because P0128 is present. Capture starting coolant and ambient temperatures, warm-up behavior and the validity of model inputs.

Verification should complete the enabled energy-based monitor under the applicable range after the actual cause is addressed. A fully warm reading after an extended drive does not establish that warm-up occurred within the permitted energy budget. Vehicle-specific cooling-system diagnosis is needed before deciding on a repair.

## Source and applicability

This explanation covers the documented 2025 GM monitor, not every vehicle using P0128. Confirm the vehicle’s module and calibration before applying its limits.
