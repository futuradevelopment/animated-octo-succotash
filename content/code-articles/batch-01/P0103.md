# P0103: Continental MAF output frequency is too high

P0103 is the mass-airflow circuit-high frequency diagnostic for the Continental MAF strategy documented by GM. It detects an output at or above 14,500 Hz.

## This sensor’s transfer direction matters

The ECM receives a digital square wave and translates its frequency into mass airflow. In this implementation, high frequency corresponds to low airflow. Thus “circuit high” should not be paraphrased as excessive engine airflow or a universally high analog signal voltage.

Monitoring begins with engine run time above one second, speed at least 300 rpm and relay voltage at least 9.1 V. Those criteria must be present for at least one second. The diagnostic qualifies 150 failures out of 190 samples, taken once per cylinder-firing event, and is classified Type A, one trip.

## Capture the unconverted signal

Preserve raw frequency alongside the ECM’s airflow value and event conditions. A high-frequency failure does not prove a specific wiring defect or establish that the sensing assembly requires replacement. Matched vehicle information is needed to determine the abnormal signal’s origin.

Verification should confirm appropriate signal frequency under the required running conditions and a completed monitor result. Since sampling follows firing events, a fixed stopwatch interval is not a substitute for diagnostic completion. Applying a frequency-to-airflow relationship from another MAF type could reverse the conclusion; the 14,500 Hz limit and inverse relationship are scoped to this documented controller/sensor arrangement.

## Source and applicability

This explanation covers the documented 2025 GM monitor, not every vehicle using P0103. Confirm the vehicle’s module and calibration before applying its limits. [GM diagnostic table, PDF page 132](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=132).
