# C05D5: master-cylinder cut-off valve electrical supervision

C05D5 identifies the brake master-cylinder cut-off valve in the documented GM electronic brake system. Its listed checks concern the coil circuit and driver electronics, not a direct hydraulic test of whether the master cylinder leaks.

## Electrical evidence behind the name

The controller watches overcurrent, driver overheating, power-ground loss and incorrect switching feedback. A greater-than-20% current difference around the switching point is one possible failure. Another branch detects interruption using open-load or undercurrent feedback.

The resistance monitor rejects calculated values above 13.7 Ω or below 4.8 Ω. It runs periodically outside valve control and without a hydraulic request. Continuous branches use a 0.03-second interval, while periodic internal and resistance checks run on a 20-second cycle. Several branches require relay supply above 6.9 V.

## Separate cause from function

The valve’s hydraulic role does not turn every C05D5 result into a sealing diagnosis. Obtain the full failure subtype to distinguish a load-path problem from driver logic or feedback failure. A static resistance test cannot verify every switching and internal-monitor branch, even when its reading is plausible.

Successful verification requires the failed branch to execute again with the correct supply and operating conditions. The leakage rows before C05D5 on the source page belong to another diagnostic and must not be imported into this code. Brake valve testing and hydraulic work should follow the exact vehicle’s professional service procedure.

## Source and applicability

This explanation covers the documented 2025 GM monitor, not every vehicle using C05D5. Confirm the vehicle’s module and calibration before applying its limits.
