# C0507: right-front wheel-speed air-gap and standstill checks

C0507 identifies right-front wheel-speed range/performance in this GM brake-controller table. The shown BoschVDA and ContiVdaR rows distinguish a moving-wheel magnetic-quality check from a stationary protocol check.

## Motion and standstill are different tests

The air-gap-related branch looks for inadequate magnetic signal over at least five wheel rotations. It runs above 1.24 mph; the table gives approximately eight seconds at 3.1 mph and 22 seconds at 1.24 mph. Those examples explain why a brief movement may not complete this test. They are monitor timing examples, not instructions to road-test a vehicle with unresolved brake concerns.

At standstill, the controller instead checks whether both speed pulses and the expected VDA standstill protocol are absent. That branch requires sensor supply above 6 V and uses 3.6 seconds. Zero wheel speed by itself is expected when stopped; loss of the required status information is the distinguishing evidence.

## What to establish next

Identify the sensor protocol and recorded failure subtype before deciding whether to investigate magnetic pickup or standstill communication. Confirm the prerequisite wheel-sensor undervoltage checks have completed. A passing stationary test does not verify the five-rotation branch, and a good moving speed trace does not establish correct standstill protocol. The magnetic-flux unit is unclear in this extraction, so no physical air-gap measurement or flux limit is prescribed. Use the vehicle-specific brake diagnostic procedure for verification.

## Source and applicability

This explanation covers the documented 2025 GM monitor, not every vehicle using C0507. Confirm the vehicle’s module and calibration before applying its limits. [GM diagnostic table, PDF page 55](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=55).
