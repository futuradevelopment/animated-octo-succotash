# P1114: Intermittent low coolant-temperature signal

For the monitor documented in GM's 2002 4.3L truck and V8 OHV engine table, P1114 records a coolant-temperature signal that repeatedly falls into the low-voltage fault region. GM associates that electrical direction with a short toward ground in the ECT signal path or sensor. “High temperature” in the row heading describes how a low signal can be interpreted; it does not prove that the coolant actually overheated.

## Why the pull-up state matters
The table specifies two different low-voltage boundaries: 0.234 volt with the low-resistance pull-up and 0.035 volt with the high-resistance pull-up. A technician therefore needs the applicable circuit state before treating a voltage as normal or abnormal. Combining the two values into one universal sensor limit would be misleading.

The monitor samples once per second and requires 55 failed tests in a 1,000-test sample. It can run after ten seconds of engine operation; the early-running branch also uses intake-air temperature below 50°C. Those details explain why intermittent evidence can accumulate without a continuously failed display reading.

## Interpreting a sudden temperature jump
A rapid indicated-temperature rise accompanied by a voltage collapse calls for electrical correlation before a cooling-system conclusion. Conversely, independently confirmed high coolant temperature remains a separate concern that this circuit code cannot dismiss. Preserve the relationship between the displayed temperature, raw signal and operating state.

Verification should reproduce the relevant pull-up and running conditions and establish that the low-signal events have stopped. These thresholds belong to the cited monitor, not every vehicle represented by the broad GM catalog entry.
