# P023B: Intercooler coolant-pump control circuit held low

P023B checks the charge-air/intercooler coolant-pump output circuit in GM's 2015 OBDG06 table. The ECM detects that the commanded output state and actual driver state disagree because the control path is shorted toward ground. This is an electrical-control diagnostic, not a direct measurement of intercooler cooling capacity.

## Prerequisites distinguish an enabled failure
The monitor must be enabled, the engine must not be cranking, powertrain relay voltage must be at least 11 volts and ignition run/crank voltage must be at least five volts. The row specifies 20 failed observations in 100 samples, taken every 100 milliseconds.

These conditions matter when reviewing a low-state measurement. A circuit deliberately commanded low or observed during an excluded cranking interval cannot be classified solely from its voltage. The meaningful evidence is whether driver feedback matches the applicable command in the enabled state.

## Electrical response and coolant flow are separate
An operating-temperature complaint does not independently prove this electrical condition. Likewise, hearing a pump at another moment does not exclude an intermittent driver-state failure. Correlate command, feedback and supply context before deciding whether the load, wiring or controller side is responsible.

Verification should establish correct electrical response and successful monitor evaluation after the cause is addressed. Any separate coolant-flow or thermal-performance concern needs its own supporting evidence. The table applies only where the documented intercooler pump is fitted, and it supplies no universal pinout, current specification or automatic pump-replacement instruction for all vehicles using P023B.
