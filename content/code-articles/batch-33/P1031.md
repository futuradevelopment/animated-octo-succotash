# P1031: Oxygen-sensor heater current-monitor circuit fault

P1031 identifies an electrical check of the upstream oxygen-sensor heater current-monitor control circuit in GM's 2000 Car V8 group 1 table. That document covers the 4.0L L47 and 4.6L LD8/L37 engine group. It describes a circuit driven toward battery voltage, rather than a measurement of exhaust mixture or catalyst efficiency.

## What the monitor establishes
The diagnostic operates with system voltage between 9 and 18 volts and engine speed above 425 rpm. Its decision requires 100 failing samples within a 120-sample window. Consequently, a brief present-day normal reading does not explain away a stored result: the controller accumulated repeated electrical failures under enabled conditions.

The monitored item is the heater **current-monitor control circuit**. That wording matters because an upstream oxygen sensor has separate sensing and heating functions. A heater-control fault does not, by itself, establish that the oxygen-sensing element is inaccurate or that the engine is running rich.

## Evidence to distinguish
A useful diagnostic record separates the current-monitor control state, heater command, supply voltage and oxygen-sensor signal. Compare the circuit against the wiring information for the actual calibration before deciding which component owns the unexpected high state. A short toward supply and an incorrect exhaust reading require different evidence.

After a documented repair, verification needs the engine running within this monitor's voltage and speed conditions and enough samples for the diagnostic to decide again. This historical table supplies no connector pin numbers or universal replacement instruction. Its engine-group heading does not make these criteria valid for every GM vehicle carrying P1031.
