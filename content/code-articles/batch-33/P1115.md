# P1115: Intermittent high coolant-temperature signal

P1115 is an intermittent high-voltage ECT circuit diagnostic in GM's 2002 4.3L truck and V8 OHV engine table. The row describes an open circuit or a signal held toward the high side. Its “low temperature” annotation refers to the temperature interpretation of that electrical fault; it is not evidence that the cooling system is operating too cold.

## A source limitation affects the numbers
The OEM row repeatedly identifies the coolant-temperature circuit, but its numerical threshold cells unexpectedly say “Raw IAT.” That apparent labeling inconsistency makes it inappropriate to present those numbers as verified ECT test limits. Use the exact vehicle service information for voltage specifications; the supported electrical direction and accumulation logic remain useful.

GM describes one sample per second, with 55 failures in a 1,000-test sample. The enabling conditions distinguish operation beyond 60 seconds from an earlier branch requiring intake-air temperature above 0°C. A temperature display that looks sensible at one instant therefore does not resolve an intermittent stored event.

## Separate the temperature from its signal
The useful comparison is between physically plausible coolant temperature and the sensor circuit's behavior when the reported temperature drops. An electrical interruption and genuinely cold coolant are different explanations. The code does not independently identify whether the sensor, connection, wiring or controller side caused the high signal.

Confirm the calibration-specific circuit limits before electrical testing, then verify that the monitor runs without further high-signal failures. This article deliberately preserves the source discrepancy instead of silently converting an IAT-labeled value into an ECT specification.
