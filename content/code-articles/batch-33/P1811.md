# P1811: Shift duration remains long at maximum adaptation

P1811 means that an adaptable shift remains too long even after the controller has reached its adaptation limit. In GM's 2002 3.0L L81 Saturn L-series transmission table, the specified duration is greater than 0.60 second, with adaptations already at their maximum value.

## Both parts of the definition matter
The controller evaluates the result after each adaptable shift and requires two qualifying counts. A long shift by itself is not the complete documented condition. Likewise, a high adaptation value without the excessive shift duration does not establish this particular failure.

The table classifies the diagnostic as Type C and lists frozen adaptations and maximum line pressure among the responses. A later harsh shift can therefore reflect a commanded protective state. It should not be interpreted as independent proof that a second mechanical fault appeared after the original long shift.

## Keep the pre-fault shift evidence
Record which adaptable shift failed, its duration and its adaptation state before resetting learned information. Removing that context can make the controller's reason for reaching the limit harder to understand. The code identifies exhausted correction authority, but the table does not name a specific hydraulic leak, solenoid or clutch as the cause.

Verification should show that the relevant shift completes properly without exhausting adaptation after the documented corrective action and any required relearn. GM notes that the diagnostic is considered passed each ignition cycle, so a new cycle alone is not a meaningful repair demonstration. Other GM transmission tables use different time limits; the 0.60-second value is specific to this source.
