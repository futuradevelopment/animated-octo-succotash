# P1795: Invalid throttle-blade position received over CAN

P1795 records an invalid throttle-blade position flag received by the transmission controller in GM's 2000 3.0L L81 Saturn L-series table. The transmission module uses engine-control information sent over CAN. The monitored failure is the validity of that received information, not a direct electrical test of a throttle sensor at the transmission.

## Receiving a message is not the same as trusting it
GM explains that the diagnostic first checks whether CAN messages are arriving through the bus-failure timer. It then examines the received validity bits. The P1795 condition is an active throttle-blade-position-invalid flag for two seconds, with system voltage between eight and 18 volts.

This creates two different branches of investigation. Lost communication concerns whether information arrives; P1795 concerns information that arrives carrying an invalid status. Confusing them can send diagnosis toward the network wiring when the source controller is deliberately reporting unusable position data.

## Trace the validity flag upstream
Record the transmission module's received flag alongside relevant engine-module throttle information and accompanying faults. Determine whether the engine-side data is already invalid before transmission reception. Do not interpret a plausible displayed throttle number as proof that its accompanying validity flag is clear.

The table lists protective transmission actions including frozen adaptations and maximum line pressure. Those actions can affect behavior without proving an internal mechanical transmission failure. Verification should establish that valid throttle-blade information is received and accepted after the underlying cause is corrected. The exact CAN implementation and fallback behavior remain limited to this historical Saturn application, despite the catalog's broader GM aliases.
