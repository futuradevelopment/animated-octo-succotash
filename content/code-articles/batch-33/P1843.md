# P1843: Shift-solenoid A control circuit held toward supply

P1843 is the high-voltage branch of the shift-solenoid A electrical diagnostic in GM's 2000 Car V8 group 1 table. GM describes a continuous short toward 12 volts in the control circuit. It is distinct from the open-or-grounded condition addressed by the neighboring low-voltage diagnostic.

## The controller compares an active command
The table requires shift solenoid A commanded on, system voltage between eight and 18 volts and qualifying engine operation. Forty-three failed samples out of 50 establish the fault. The controller's internal failure mode, rather than a driver-observed shift sensation, supplies the electrical decision.

A control circuit held toward supply is not the same as an overcharging vehicle electrical system. Nor does the code establish that a mechanically stuck solenoid caused the high-state indication. These explanations involve different measurements and should not be merged simply because they can affect shifting.

## Keep supply and controlled output separate
A useful diagnostic record identifies the commanded state, the control path's electrical behavior and whether the internal high-fault status agrees with that evidence. Use the exact solenoid-A wiring for the application before testing; numerical circuit names and terminal assignments are not universal.

After the demonstrated cause is corrected, verify that the driver controls the circuit properly while the monitor is enabled and accumulates passing evidence. The source gives no automatic solenoid or transmission replacement instruction. Its 4.0L L47/4.6L LD8/L37 document scope remains part of the interpretation, even though the catalog presents a broad 1–2 shift-solenoid label.
