# P1432: Secondary fuel-level signal remains electrically low

P1432 identifies a low secondary-tank fuel-level signal in the dual-tank applications covered by GM's 2002 engine diagnostic table. The source describes the sender as stuck outside its permitted range on the low side. That is an electrical-range finding, not a direct measurement that the tank contains no fuel.

## A low reading must persist
The documented criterion is fewer than 20 analog-to-digital counts for 100 seconds. The monitor runs continuously and is classified Type C with no warning light. Two consequences follow: a momentary low reading is not the complete criterion, and the absence of an illuminated engine lamp does not exclude a recorded failure.

The value is expressed in controller A/D counts, not volts or liters. Converting it into an invented universal voltage threshold would add information the table does not provide. Use the matching vehicle service information to interpret a raw electrical measurement.

## Distinguish range from plausibility
P1432's low-range condition differs from a sender that stays at a believable value while fuel moves between tanks. That latter problem needs a change-over-time comparison, whereas this diagnostic can recognize a persistently invalid low input without proving a transfer imbalance.

A useful record correlates the secondary sender's raw signal, displayed quantity and duration of the event. Verification should demonstrate that the signal remains inside its valid electrical range for an appropriate observation interval after the actual cause is addressed. The table does not identify a failed sender, connector or controller, and it does not justify fuel-system disassembly without the correct application-specific procedure.
