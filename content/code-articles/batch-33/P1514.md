# P1514: Airflow disagrees with the throttle-based estimate

In the cited GM 2002 electronic-throttle monitor, P1514 indicates that measured engine airflow exceeds the estimate derived from throttle position. The table compares two independent airflow calculations with that estimate: one based on manifold pressure and one based on the mass-airflow signal.

## Both comparisons must fail
Each listed difference must exceed 150 milligrams per cylinder. Separate counters increase by two for an error and decrease by one for a passing comparison; both must exceed a threshold of 20. The checks occur every 18.75 milliseconds. This logic matters because one unusual MAF reading alone is not the complete documented P1514 decision.

The engine must be running above 500 rpm, ignition must have been on for more than a second, and relevant throttle-actuation, position-sensor, processor and serial-communication diagnostics must not be present. Those prerequisites help the controller compare trusted inputs rather than evaluate airflow amid an already identified control-system failure.

## Three quantities, one relationship
Preserve the MAP-based airflow, MAF-based airflow and throttle-based estimate together. Determine whether both measured estimates depart in the same direction, or whether one input is the outlier. Also distinguish an airflow-model discrepancy from a simple inability of the throttle to reach a commanded position; the neighboring position-performance monitor evaluates a different relationship.

The dictionary's “Throttle Body Performance” title is broader than this OEM airflow test. The code does not independently prove that the throttle body needs replacement. Verification should demonstrate restored agreement under the matching ETC calibration's enabled conditions, not merely a normal throttle display at idle.
