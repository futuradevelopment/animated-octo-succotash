# P1842: Shift-solenoid A control circuit open or held low

In GM's 2000 4.0L L47/4.6L LD8/L37 engine and transmission table, P1842 checks the shift-solenoid A control circuit for an open or short toward ground. The controller must command solenoid A on and receive the corresponding open or ground-short fault indication.

## A circuit result, not a measured shift ratio
The decision requires 43 failures within 50 samples. System voltage must be between eight and 18 volts, and the engine must satisfy the stated running-speed and non-fuel-cutoff conditions. These prerequisites make the command state part of the evidence: a deenergized circuit observed outside the test window is not automatically faulty.

The catalog calls this the 1–2 shift-solenoid low-voltage circuit. The OEM row uses the solenoid-A designation and evaluates electrical integrity, not a numerical first-to-second shift time or a slipping gear ratio. A shift complaint needs to be correlated with this electrical finding rather than substituted for it.

## Open and grounded require different evidence
The DTC combines two electrical possibilities. Determine which condition is actually present using the exact circuit diagram and service method. A low monitored state alone does not distinguish a broken current path from an unwanted connection toward ground.

Verification should show correct commanded behavior and a completed electrical monitor after the cause is addressed. The table does not establish that the solenoid coil, internal harness or controller is the failed component. Its historical calibration scope and solenoid naming must be retained when comparing another transmission application.
