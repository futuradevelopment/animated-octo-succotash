# P1374: Incorrect low-resolution crank pulse count

P1374 compares the low-resolution 3X crank reference with the 24X signal in GM's 2000 Car V6 group 2 diagnostic table. The controller is looking for an incorrect number of 3X signals per engine cycle. This is a pulse-count relationship, rather than a direct statement that the engine has lost mechanical timing.

## What the controller can observe
The source describes pulsed signals and enables this comparison after more than three seconds of engine operation. It lists 290 failed samples out of 300 as the failure criterion, with continuous monitoring. That high proportion indicates a repeated correlation problem in the sampled interval; a single clean pulse captured later does not establish that the whole signal relationship was valid.

The document groups 3.1L LG8, 3.4L LA1, 3.8L L36 and supercharged L67 applications. The exact vehicle calibration still needs checking before its signal architecture is assumed. The code's short dictionary label, “3X Reference Circuit,” omits the fact that the OEM row compares two references.

## Keep both references in view
A useful diagnostic capture identifies whether 3X pulses are missing, additional or inconsistent relative to the reference cycle, and whether the 24X reference itself remains coherent. Looking only at engine rpm can conceal a problem in one of the two information paths.

Verification should show the expected pulse relationship after the enabling time and through enough engine cycles for the monitor to decide. The table supplies no connector assignments or proof that a particular sensor or module is defective; those decisions require the matching service procedure and actual signal evidence.
