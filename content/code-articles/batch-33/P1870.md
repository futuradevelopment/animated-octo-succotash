# P1870: Excessive converter-clutch slip in the 3T40 monitor

P1870 is broadly labeled transmission component slipping, but GM's 2000 Cavalier/Sunfire 2.2L LN2 and 3T40 table gives a narrower test: excessive torque-converter-clutch slip when the clutch should be engaged. This article describes that particular monitor rather than assigning its thresholds to every P1870 application.

## A commanded engagement must already exist
The source requires the TCC to have been commanded on for six seconds. Its qualifying conditions include slip above 140 rpm, engine speed between 1,500 and 4,000 rpm, vehicle speed between 35 and 82 mph and throttle between ten and 50 percent. Relevant throttle and vehicle-speed sensor diagnostics must not be present.

The controller increments a slip counter when the enabling conditions are met and fails at two or more counts. The listed observation duration is five seconds. A brief slip value during an intentional disengagement or a shift is not equivalent to this qualified event.

## Preserve the command-and-slip relationship
The useful evidence is converter command together with slip, speed and operating state. Distinguish failure to achieve the expected engagement from a separate electrical code that prevents the clutch command from being delivered correctly. The monitor does not identify one internal part as the cause.

Verification should use the exact service procedure to establish the expected engaged slip behavior after the underlying issue is corrected. Do not treat this summary as an instruction to reproduce a road-speed test casually. Other GM transmissions use different P1870 criteria; the 3T40 scope is essential.
