# P1509: High idle and the idle-airflow response test

P1509 is the high-idle branch of GM's 2000 4.3L L35/LF6 truck idle-air-control diagnostic. The source defines high idle as 100 rpm above the desired value and describes a test intended to determine whether an engine mechanical problem accounts for the condition. It also evaluates an airflow change greater than three grams per second.

## The initial check has to qualify
The nonintrusive check requires a warm engine, more than 30 seconds of running, throttle below one percent and vehicle speed below two mph. Supply voltage must be between 10 and 16 volts, and relevant throttle, speed, coolant and MAP faults must not be active. The enabling state must persist before testing begins.

If that check fails, the table describes an intrusive stage under a different airflow and vehicle-speed window, with stable throttle and engine speed and a commanded IAC movement. This two-stage structure matters: a high idle observation alone is not the full diagnostic sequence.

## Desired speed and air response answer different questions
Compare actual idle with the controller's desired idle before declaring the speed excessive. Then consider whether the commanded idle-air change produces the expected response. A speed error and an airflow-response discrepancy provide different evidence about control authority and the underlying condition.

Verification should follow the matching professional service procedure and show restored idle control under the enabled conditions. Do not improvise the moving-vehicle intrusive test from this summary. The table does not prove that the IAC motor itself is defective or identify one mechanical cause solely from P1509.
