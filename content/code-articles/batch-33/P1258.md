# P1258: Engine overtemperature protection mode recorded

P1258 records activation of engine overtemperature protection in the cited GM 2002 engine diagnostic table. The listed trigger is coolant temperature at or above 129.4°C for more than ten seconds, with no coolant-sensor diagnostic trouble codes present. The code is set when protection mode becomes active.

## Protection is a response, not a root cause
This distinction changes the investigation. P1258 does not name a failed thermostat, pump, fan or coolant sensor. It reports the controller's response to its temperature information. A repair decision needs evidence explaining why that temperature condition occurred, rather than treating the code itself as a component verdict.

The absence of a coolant-sensor DTC is an enabling condition, not proof that every temperature reading was perfectly accurate. Compare the recorded temperature history with independently supported thermal and cooling-system evidence. Also distinguish a historical protection event from the engine's present temperature; a cooled vehicle can retain a record of an earlier event.

## Confirm recovery without recreating overheating
Document the temperature trend and circumstances that preceded the protection event. The monitor's ten-second requirement explains why a brief display fluctuation and a sustained qualifying condition are not equivalent evidence. Verification should establish normal temperature control during an appropriate service check after the actual cause is addressed.

Do not deliberately drive the engine back to the protection threshold to prove a repair, and do not open a hot pressurized cooling system. The source is a monitor specification rather than a complete overheating procedure. Confirm its applicability to the exact engine calibration before using the numerical trigger as a diagnostic reference.
