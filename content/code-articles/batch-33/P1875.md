# P1875: Four-wheel-drive low indication disagrees with ratio

P1875 checks whether the four-wheel-drive-low circuit agrees with the drivetrain ratio in GM's cited 2000 4.3L truck transmission table. The monitor has stuck-on and stuck-off branches. It does not simply treat any low-range switch indication as a fault.

## Two opposite disagreements
For stuck on, the low-range signal is asserted while the transfer-case ratio indicates that low range is not engaged. The listed engine-to-transfer-output ratio window is 0.8 to 1.2, with a five-second duration. For stuck off, low range is not indicated even though the ratio is in the 2.5-to-2.9 window; that branch lasts ten seconds.

The table also requires D4 range, suitable torque and transmission temperature, vehicle movement, and usable speed, throttle, shift-solenoid and TCC information. Its two branches use different converter-slip conditions. These prerequisites prevent a single switch reading during an unqualified transition from being treated as complete proof.

## Compare indication with independent ratio evidence
Preserve the low-range signal, calculated ratio and converter state together. A switch-circuit disagreement and a transfer-case mechanical concern are different possibilities; the code alone does not decide which side of the comparison is wrong.

Verification should demonstrate agreement under the correct application-specific service test after the cause is established. Do not change transfer-case modes or conduct loaded drivetrain tests from this summary. The source covers a historical truck group, and its inclusion of two-wheel-drive model families in the heading does not establish that every vehicle has the monitored low-range hardware.
