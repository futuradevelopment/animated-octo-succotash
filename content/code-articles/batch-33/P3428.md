# P3428: Cylinder-4 deactivation driver reports a supply-side short

P3428 is the cylinder-4 deactivation circuit-high diagnostic in GM's 2015 OBDG08A table. The row identifies a short toward power in the low-side control path. The cylinder number identifies the controlled output, but the DTC does not establish a cylinder-specific mechanical defect.

## An active-command electrical check
The source evaluates this branch with the driver on and describes a very low impedance between the monitored signal and controller power. It requires powertrain relay voltage of at least 11 volts and engine speed of at least 400 rpm. The failure count is at least 20 errors in 25 samples, spaced 250 milliseconds apart.

A circuit held toward supply cannot be interpreted in the same way as an open conductor simply because both can prevent normal solenoid control. Preserve which electrical branch is reported. Also distinguish the output's feedback state from the engine's mechanical response to deactivation.

## Use the exact cylinder-4 control path
Identify the correct output using the actual engine and controller information. Determine whether its electrical state follows the active command, rather than assuming that a high reading anywhere in the deactivation system belongs to this circuit. The table supplies no pin numbers or component placement.

Verification should show restored command-following behavior and a successful monitor evaluation after the demonstrated cause is addressed. The source's impedance statement defines the monitor and is not a safe standalone resistance-testing procedure. These criteria are specific to the cited GM document; the generic P3428 label does not make them universal across engine designs.
