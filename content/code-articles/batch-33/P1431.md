# P1431: Secondary-tank fuel level does not change as expected

P1431 is a secondary fuel-level performance diagnostic for the dual-tank applications identified in GM's 2002 engine table. The controller tests whether the sender's reported volume changes consistently with distance traveled and fuel transfer. A signal can remain within its electrical range and still fail this plausibility check.

## Three kinds of missing change
One branch looks for less than three liters of volume change over 65 accumulated miles when the level exceeds 30 liters. Another compares tank changes during transfer: the primary tank gains more than four liters while the secondary tank fails to show the corresponding decrease after the stated transfer and delay conditions. A third branch examines an extended primary-full, secondary-empty combination over more than 275 miles.

These are distinct evidence patterns. The transfer branch uses information from both tanks, whereas the distance branch watches a prolonged lack of change. A single stationary fuel-gauge reading cannot reproduce either history.

## Interpret volume, transfer and history together
Preserve which tank levels were reported, whether the transfer pump was commanded and how much qualifying travel accumulated. An in-range stuck sender is different from an electrically low sender signal; the neighboring circuit-low diagnostic addresses that latter condition separately.

The table classifies P1431 as a no-light Type C diagnostic. Lack of a warning lamp therefore does not prove that this monitor passed. Verification should show plausible secondary-tank change under the applicable operating branch, using the vehicle-specific fuel-system procedure. Do not infer dual-tank hardware from the code catalog alone, and do not perform improvised work on fuel plumbing to recreate the test.
