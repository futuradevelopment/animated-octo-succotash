# P1351: High ignition-control output during cranking

In GM's 2000 4.3L truck engine table, P1351 is an electronic spark timing output-high diagnostic. GM associates this particular monitor with an open-circuit failure and specifies an EST voltage above 4.9 volts. That is a calibration-specific interpretation of a high control-line state, not a universal instruction to replace an ignition coil.

## A cranking-only decision
The test requires EST to be enabled and engine speed below 250 rpm. It accumulates 20 failed tests during cranking, over approximately three seconds, and runs once per ignition cycle. A normal observation after the engine starts does not reproduce the interval in which this monitor makes its decision.

Those limits also distinguish the code from a general high-voltage problem in the vehicle supply. The measured quantity is the spark-control output, and the operating state is low-speed cranking. Evidence collected from an unrelated ignition circuit or at steady road speed cannot directly confirm this failure.

## Matching the circuit to the event
Use the exact engine wiring information to identify the EST control path and its expected behavior during the crank interval. Preserve whether the control line remains high when the monitor is enabled, and separate that observation from secondary ignition performance. The table's open-circuit interpretation is a direction for investigation, not a completed diagnosis of the harness or controller.

After the cause is corrected, verification needs an appropriate new ignition-cycle cranking event. Avoid improvised probing of high-energy ignition components. These facts come from the cited historical truck monitor and require confirmation before use on another P1351 application.
