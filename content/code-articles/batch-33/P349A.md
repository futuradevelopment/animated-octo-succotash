# P349A: Cylinder-3 half-engine operation fails its airflow performance check

GM's 2020 OBDG07 P349A monitor looks for unwanted air pumping from cylinder 3 during half-engine operation. Its focus is the achieved deactivation behavior, rather than merely whether the electrical driver can switch a solenoid on and off.

## The transition must settle before judgment
The table requires synchronization of the operating mode and air-charge determination, enabled valve-lift diagnosis and suitable engine speed. The stated speed window is 800 to 2,900 rpm. Its continuation includes a calibrated settling interval after half-engine switching and limits throttle-position change during the observation.

These conditions are important because airflow naturally changes when the engine changes mode or demand. A transient pressure change should not be treated as proof that cylinder 3 remains active. The monitor uses accumulated error counts tied to supporting lookup tables, so no single universal duration is inferred here.

## Compare intent, electrical control and response
Determine whether the controller requested the relevant mode, whether its control information is valid and whether the resulting airflow evidence supports the intended state. An electrical circuit DTC and this performance DTC answer different questions. A correct electrical waveform alone does not complete the mechanical-performance assessment.

Verification should establish the expected cylinder-3 response under the exact service procedure after the actual cause is corrected. The source does not select a failed valve mechanism or sensor from P349A alone. An inconsistent bank-2 pressure-ratio entry in the published enable logic is omitted here and remains a reason to confirm the complete calibration-specific test before attempting to reproduce it.
