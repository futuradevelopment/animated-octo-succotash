# P00C4: Bypass valve B fails a commanded-open transient check

In GM's 2015 OBDG06 table, P00C4 detects bypass valve B behaving as stuck closed in a documented parallel-turbo arrangement. The controller infers the problem from airflow or boost behavior during a qualifying transient. It is a mechanical-performance comparison, rather than the separate bypass-valve electrical circuit diagnostic.

## A command and a pressure event must coincide
The row requires bypass valve B commanded open and engine speed of at least 1,500 rpm. It also uses compressor pressure ratio, boost relative to ambient pressure and a falling-manifold-pressure event to establish the test window. Relevant boost-pressure, airflow and bypass-circuit fault information affects whether the diagnostic can run.

The monitor evaluates filtered behavior over a short accumulation interval, listed between 0.30 and 0.75 second, and samples every 25 milliseconds. Two failed tests out of three establish the result. A steady boost reading under constant demand is therefore not an equivalent reproduction of this transient test.

## Response evidence before component conclusions
Preserve the valve-open command and the corresponding pressure/airflow response together. Distinguish a valve that does not produce the expected response from a circuit fault that prevents a trustworthy command, or sensor information that makes the response calculation unreliable.

The code alone does not identify a broken linkage, actuator or turbocharger. Verification should demonstrate the expected response through the proper application-specific service test, without improvising a loaded boost event. The source's parallel-turbo and valve-B scope is essential; the generic code label does not prove that another vehicle uses this exact arrangement.
