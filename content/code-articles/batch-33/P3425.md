# P3425: Cylinder-4 deactivation circuit loses continuity

P3425 identifies the cylinder-4 deactivation solenoid's open-circuit monitor in GM's 2015 OBDG08A table. The controller watches the low-side output driver's electrical feedback. A missing electrical path is the supported finding; a failed mechanical deactivation mechanism is not established solely by this DTC.

## The monitor needs engine operation and supply voltage
GM enables the test with engine speed at least 400 rpm and powertrain relay voltage at least 11 volts. Its open-circuit definition uses an impedance of at least 200 kilohms, evaluated in the driver-off state. The sampling interval is 250 milliseconds, and at least 20 of 25 observations must fail.

Those prerequisites matter when a vehicle is checked with the engine stopped or voltage depressed. Such a check may help another part of the service procedure, but it is not the enabled monitor described here. Similarly, one normal observation after an intermittent interruption cannot account for the whole stored sample history.

## Cylinder identity and continuity are different questions
Confirm which control circuit belongs to cylinder 4 on the actual engine, without inferring its physical location from a generic diagram. Then determine whether the electrical path is complete. A mechanical noise or rough-running complaint may require separate evidence even when it occurs with this code.

Verification should show restored output-circuit integrity and a passing enabled diagnostic after the cause is corrected. Treat the source's impedance as a monitor criterion, not an energized resistance-test instruction. The table does not independently select the solenoid, harness or controller as the failed part.
