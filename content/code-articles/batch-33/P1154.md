# P1154: Unequal bank-2 oxygen-sensor transition times

P1154 measures the relationship between the rich-to-lean and lean-to-rich transition times of bank 2 sensor 1 in the cited 2002 GM engine table. It is a response-balance diagnostic: a sensor can move in both directions yet still fail because one direction is disproportionately slow.

## What must be true before comparison
For this monitor, GM lists a transition-time ratio below 0.25 or above 8.0 as the failure region. The observation lasts 50 seconds and occurs once per trip. The table requires closed-loop fuel control, engine speed between 1,200 and 3,000 rpm, airflow between 23 and 50 grams per second, coolant above 65°C and more than 160 seconds of engine operation.

Those conditions matter because a cold, idling or open-loop snapshot is not an equivalent test. Several oxygen-sensor circuit codes and other engine-control faults also inhibit the evaluation. An incomplete monitor under those circumstances supplies no passing evidence.

## Reading the result usefully
Compare both switching directions over the enabled interval, rather than judging the sensor by its highest voltage alone. Next distinguish an asymmetric response from a signal that never switches: the latter is a different kind of evidence, with separate circuit and activity diagnostics in this system.

The ratio establishes an observed response imbalance, not a proven replacement part or universal root cause. Verification should demonstrate balanced transitions and successful monitor completion under the applicable warm, closed-loop operating conditions. The numerical limits are specific to the cited 2002 diagnostic group; other GM years can use different ratios and enabling windows.
