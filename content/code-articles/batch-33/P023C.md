# P023C: Intercooler coolant-pump control circuit held toward power

GM's 2015 OBDG06 P023C monitor checks for a charge-air coolant-pump control output shorted toward power. Its decision comes from a disagreement between commanded and actual output-driver states. The word “high” describes the electrical fault direction, not an intercooler temperature or an excessive pump flow rate.

## Context for the high-state finding
The source requires an enabled diagnostic, a noncranking engine state, at least 11 volts at the powertrain relay and at least five volts on the ignition run/crank supply. Twenty failures within 100 samples establish the result, with a 100-millisecond sampling interval.

A supply-level reading can be appropriate in one commanded state and inappropriate in another. The monitor's state comparison is therefore more useful than an isolated voltage number. Preserve the command alongside feedback instead of assuming that any high observation proves a short.

## Compare control delivery with pump operation
Determine whether the electrical output can change as intended under the application-specific test. Keep that question separate from whether coolant actually circulates effectively through the charge-air system. A driver circuit fault and a mechanical or hydraulic pump concern require different evidence, even if both can affect cooling performance.

Verification should show the output following its commands and the monitor completing without the high-circuit failure after the actual cause is corrected. The code alone does not establish that the pump or ECM is defective. These criteria belong to the cited GM monitor on applications equipped with the relevant intercooler coolant pump; do not infer that hardware from the generic label alone.
