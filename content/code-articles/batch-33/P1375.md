# P1375: The 24X reference remains high while other pulses arrive

P1375 is a high-state 24X crank-reference diagnostic in GM's 1998 4.0L L47 Aurora table. The monitor finds the 24X line high while more than eight 4X reference pulses arrive. The source's fuller heading allows either missing crank-sensor A/B information or a 24X line held high; it does not select one cause automatically.

## A high state has to conflict with rotation evidence
The test requires engine speed above 496 rpm and more than seven cam edges since key-on. Four failures within ten tests establish the result, with checks every 250 milliseconds. The arriving low-resolution pulses are important: they provide evidence that the 24X line should be doing more than remaining in one state.

A high logic level observed without the relevant pulse interval is therefore incomplete evidence. The monitor is not a general measurement of battery overvoltage, and a scan-tool engine-speed value does not prove that the 24X signal toggles correctly.

## Distinguish source loss from a held line
The useful next distinction is whether the reference path is electrically held high or lacks the upstream information needed to generate its transitions. That requires the matching signal-routing information and appropriate waveform evidence, rather than an assumption based on the code's abbreviated title.

After addressing the established cause, verify that 24X transitions return while the 4X and cam references remain coherent and the monitor completes. The document provides no universal pin assignments or replacement verdict. Its architecture and thresholds are specific to the cited historical application.
