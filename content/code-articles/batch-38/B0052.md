# B0052: Mazda's passenger buckle signal passes through the SAS controller

Mazda B0052:11 is a passenger-side buckle-switch circuit fault reported in the BCM chapter. The detection statement says the SAS control module observes a short to ground twice consecutively. This architecture differs from the driver-buckle page, which uses a different input module.

## Why the module distinction is useful

The source's diagnostic path begins with the SAS connector and the passenger-buckle signal circuit. Reading only the broad passenger seat-belt sensor title can obscure that route and encourage replacement of the wrong electrical supply unit.

The stated precondition is removal of the passenger buckle switch. This is part of a controlled workshop procedure; it should not be interpreted as a normal-use buckle state or a request for DIY disconnection. The electrical monitor and the driver's experience of a seat-belt reminder are related observations, not identical tests.

## Keep the repair conclusion evidence-based

Possible causes include the SAS connection, a grounded signal circuit, the SAS controller and the BCM. The procedure assesses SAS repeatability before a later BCM decision, with CAN diagnosis in the recurring-fault branches. A stored B0052 alone does not choose among these causes.

Because the input involves restraint-system equipment, a qualified technician should follow the full SRS and buckle service requirements. The useful evidence is the complete :11 suffix, which module stored it, whether the electrical condition repeats under the prescribed procedure, and whether other faults explain the report. An extinguished reminder is not sufficient repair verification.

Scope: Mazda3 01/2019 onward manual d9n1-1a-22d_ver10, in the controller and equipment variant named by the source. Confirm vehicle applicability before using its diagnostic path.
