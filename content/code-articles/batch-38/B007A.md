# B007A: Passenger pretensioner B is the retractor circuit in this SAIC procedure

The B007A family names passenger seat-belt pretensioner B deployment control. SAIC's SDM page identifies the passenger pretensioner retractor and separates ground or battery shorts, low or high circuit resistance, and incompatible configuration through the failure-type suffix.

## The suffix determines what evidence is needed

The :11 and :12 variants concern unwanted electrical contact. The :1A and :1B variants concern the monitored deployment resistance. The :56 version compares configured equipment with the circuit identified shortly after power-on. A configuration mismatch is not automatically a request to reprogram the controller.

The source's first diagnostic decision uses the SDM's passenger-pretensioner resistance parameter. The outcome directs the technician toward the harness and retractor or toward the controller assessment. This is distinct from testing whether the seat belt buckles or retracts mechanically.

## What a supported repair should demonstrate

Harness and connection faults, the retractor and the SDM are all named possibilities. A useful repair record should explain why one was implicated and confirm the corresponding condition returned to normal. The manual gives a separate forty-power-cycle history rule; waiting for history to age is not a way to repair the circuit.

Pretensioners are pyrotechnic restraint devices. Direct probing, resistor substitution and retractor disassembly are outside this overview. A qualified SRS technician must use the correct equipment-specific service procedure, including any configuration and completion requirements, rather than relying on the base B007A label alone.

This interpretation is limited to the cited MG4 UK manual revision; it does not establish applicability to every MG4 build or another manufacturer.
