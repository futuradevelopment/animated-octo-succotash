# U0115: The local PCM link that limits Mazda transmission functions

In the Mazda CW6A-EL transmission controller, U0115:00 identifies a missing PCM signal on the local CAN connection between the two controllers. The detection statement requires battery voltage of at least ten volts. The generic ECM/PCM B label must therefore be read with the reporting TCM and its local network context.

## Why several transmission functions may change together

Mazda lists inhibition of learning, manual mode, neutral-idle control, i-stop, AAS, fifth and sixth gears, and accelerator-demand downshifts among the responses. These are documented control restrictions; their presence does not individually prove several mechanical transmission faults.

The page names stored PCM DTCs, PCM malfunction and TCM malfunction as possible causes. Its first substantive diagnostic branch reads PCM codes and follows them where present. An engine-control problem can thus be relevant to a transmission complaint even when the owner first notices restricted shifting.

## What the source does not prove

The code alone does not establish a failed valve body or a damaged CAN wire. Mazda's completion sequence uses a pending-code check, directs recurring faults into CAN diagnosis and only later reaches the specified control-unit repair branch.

Keep the complete :00 record, supply conditions and PCM fault report together. This procedure is explicitly for CW6A-EL; applying its response list to another transmission would overstate the source. A qualified workshop should perform network and transmission verification without using a public-road exercise to provoke the fault.

Scope: Mazda3 01/2019 onward manual d9n1-1a-22d_ver10, in the controller and equipment variant named by the source. Confirm vehicle applicability before using its diagnostic path.
