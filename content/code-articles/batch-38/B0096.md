# B0096: The right B-pillar sensor can fail internally or lose its data path

For the documented SDM implementation, B0096 identifies the right B-pillar side-impact sensor. The :49, :56, :86 and :87 variants respectively concern internal electronics, incompatible configuration, invalid transmitted information and loss of communication.

## The source defines two different signal problems

Invalid-data detection includes sensor deviation and acceleration information outside the expected range. Communication detection includes short or message failures observed within 200 milliseconds. A full scan should preserve which of these actually occurred; they are not interchangeable descriptions of a bad sensor.

The monitor runs with ignition on and the SDM functioning normally. The source explains that the side sensor provides acceleration information for the restraint calculation, rather than directly commanding an airbag on its own.

## A diagnostic route with safety limits

The procedure examines the SDM-to-right-side sensor path, then the sensor and finally the SDM. A faulty connection is therefore a supported possibility, even when the code's component name appears specific. The page's deployment-response text is inconsistent about side identification, so this article does not use it to promise which airbags remain available.

A qualified technician should verify the correct sensor identity, original failure type and repaired signal status. Historical clearing after forty failure-free power cycles does not itself explain the original fault. Do not strike the sensor, alter its mounting, probe restraint connectors or attempt a deployment test based on this explanation.

This interpretation is limited to the cited MG4 UK manual revision; it does not establish applicability to every MG4 build or another manufacturer.
