# B0001: The driver airbag stage-one subcode identifies the circuit fault

Mazda's B0001 procedure covers driver-side airbag inflator No.1. Its suffixes separate a ground short (:11), power short (:12), open or high resistance (:13), contact with another airbag circuit (:19), and low resistance (:1A). A scan report that omits the suffix loses evidence needed to understand the failure.

## The clock spring is part of this path

The source includes the clock spring and its connections among possible causes, alongside deployment wiring, the airbag module and the SAS control module. That is a reason to evaluate the complete circuit, not proof that the clock spring is always responsible for a steering-wheel airbag code.

Mazda also distinguishes standard deployment control from two-step control by the presence of a seat-track position sensor. The correct equipment variant matters when choosing the service procedure even though both can appear under this code family.

## Interpreting a workshop diagnosis

The procedure separates connector and wiring findings from the eventual module decision, then checks whether the same pending fault returns. Its diagnostic resistance information is an explanation of what the controller detects, not an instruction to measure an inflator with a meter. Mazda expressly warns against using detection conditions as a substitute for the approved inspection sequence.

Retain the complete code, module identity and other restraint faults for a qualified SRS technician. Do not probe deployment connectors, substitute resistors or attempt airbag disassembly based on this overview. A cleared warning alone is not evidence that the circuit's underlying problem has been resolved.

Scope: Mazda3 01/2019 onward manual d9n1-1a-22d_ver10, in the controller and equipment variant named by the source. Confirm vehicle applicability before using its diagnostic path.
