# P0915: Shift-stroke position needs its own circuit and angle checks

## Identify the correct stroke measurement

P0915 in the cited Toyota sequential transmission concerns the shift stroke sensor value being outside its standard. The shift sensor and select sensor are separate inputs in this system. A successful select-stroke test does not establish that the shift-stroke circuit is healthy, even though the factory component checks use a similar position-to-voltage principle.

The procedure starts with open/short checks of the shift-sensor harness and connectors to the transmission ECU. It then removes and tests the shift sensor against specified angular positions. This sequence separates circuit integrity from the accuracy of the sensor's actual response.

## A low output can be correct at the correct angle

With the defined 4.5-volt supply, the table expects 4.127–4.306 volts at 44.8 degrees and 0.185–0.364 volt at 135.5 degrees. Thus, a low reading is not inherently defective: its meaning depends on position and test supply. The source supplies scaling formulas for its permitted supply range. These values are specific bench criteria, not generic setting thresholds for every P0915 application.

## Confirm the final procedure before replacing an ECU

The reproduced last step has an initial-learning heading, yet its text discusses checking after ECU replacement and then directs another replacement if unsuccessful. That inconsistency requires confirmation against current Toyota information; this article remains marked for source review.

A qualified technician should document the measured angle/output relationship, circuit findings and the required learning outcome. Do not apply vehicle battery voltage to the sensor or improvise actuator movement. The fault description identifies an out-of-range measurement, while the diagnostic evidence determines whether the circuit, sensor or controller process is responsible.
