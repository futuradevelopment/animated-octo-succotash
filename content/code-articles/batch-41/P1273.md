# P1273: GM checks channel 1 against channel 3 in its pedal assembly

In the cited GM three-sensor accelerator assembly, P1273 is the channel 1-to-channel 3 agreement monitor. The factory procedure for the 2001 Chevrolet 6.6-litre diesel specifies more than ten percent disagreement between their interpreted positions. The generic catalog's brief sensor-1 wording should not hide the second channel involved in the comparison.

## Opposite voltage movement is expected

The factory position table shows channel 1 voltage increasing with pedal travel while channel 3 voltage decreases. Opposite movement is therefore part of the design, not evidence of failure. The controller compares the positions represented by those signals, rather than demanding the same voltage or slope.

The diagnostic is enabled in crank or run with ignition voltage between 7 and 16 V and the listed input/reference faults absent. Its setting text gives less than one second, which should not be converted into an unsupported minimum-duration rule.

## Correlation does not identify the guilty channel

An agreement failure establishes that the two observations cannot both be accepted as expected. It does not, by itself, determine whether channel 1, channel 3 or a related circuit caused the mismatch. The third sensor and the full accompanying fault set remain important context.

GM's response policy varies with single- versus multiple-sensor faults, up to idle-only operation when all three are out of range. The procedure stores failure records without requesting the malfunction lamp. Use the exact assembly's professional diagnostic procedure before translating this comparison into a replacement decision.
