# P1271: GM compares accelerator-pedal channels 1 and 2

The cited 2001 Chevrolet 6.6-litre diesel uses three accelerator-pedal sensors in one housing, with separate signal, low-reference and 5 V reference circuits. P1271 compares channels 1 and 2. Their electrical values are not expected to be identical: the factory position table shows channel 1 rising and channel 2 falling as pedal travel increases.

## Compare interpreted position, not equal voltages

The fault criterion is more than ten percent disagreement between the two channels. Treating that as a demand for matching raw voltages would misunderstand the redundant design. Each channel's voltage has to be interpreted using its own relationship to pedal position.

The diagnostic requires crank or run, 7 to 16 V ignition voltage and absence of the listed channel/reference faults. The setting text uses less than one second; it does not support an invented longer persistence threshold.

## The combination of faults controls the response

GM distinguishes faults confined to a single sensor from certain combinations involving multiple sensors. With a single affected sensor, the remaining two can provide the accelerator calculation without commanding reduced power. Some multi-sensor combinations limit response, while all three out of range permit idle only.

That policy means P1271 alone should not be converted into a guaranteed reduced-power symptom. The procedure stores failure information without requesting the malfunction lamp. Review the complete fault set and exact assembly before professional testing; the agreement result does not identify which channel caused the discrepancy.
