# P1574: GM compares stoplamp status with independent evidence of braking

The cited GM P1574 procedure uses wheel-speed behavior and brake-contact transitions to assess the stoplamp-switch signal. The PCM receives non-drive-wheel speed and stoplamp status from the brake controller over serial data. The diagnostic therefore depends on more than whether a lamp visibly illuminates.

## The controller expects related signals to agree

The described event combines a non-drive-wheel speed decrease of at least 2.5 mph within 0.4 seconds with a transition of the torque-converter-clutch or extended-travel brake contact, but no corresponding stoplamp transition. This is an inference that braking occurred while one reported signal did not respond.

The enable conditions exclude specified speed, brake-switch and communication faults. They also require the stated wheel-speed window and an inactive, non-failed ABS/traction system. These are monitor conditions, not instructions for a driver to reproduce abrupt deceleration on a public road.

## Follow the signal through the brake controller

GM's diagnostic aids distinguish a problem on the stoplamp input to the brake controller from a problem communicating that information to the PCM. Replacing a bulb or switch without identifying that path could leave the actual failure unexplained.

The documented response includes defaulting stoplamp status to not applied without requesting the malfunction lamp or a driver message. That internal default does not prove the physical lamps are off. Professional diagnosis must verify the actual signals and braking-system condition using the matching vehicle procedure. The code does not independently establish safe brake operation.
