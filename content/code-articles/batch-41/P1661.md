# P1661: GM detects disagreement in the malfunction-lamp control circuit

P1661 in the 1996 Chevrolet Impala SS procedure concerns the malfunction indicator lamp's control circuit. Ignition voltage feeds the lamp, and the PCM turns it on by supplying a ground path through an internal driver. The controller monitors whether the circuit voltage agrees with its command.

## The lamp cannot be the only witness

Above 600 rpm, a disagreement between commanded driver state and actual circuit state must persist for at least five seconds. A commanded-on lamp should have low control-circuit voltage; a commanded-off lamp should have high voltage. The comparison needs both the command and the electrical response.

When the diagnostic fails, the PCM requests the lamp on and stores P1661 whether or not the lamp can actually illuminate. This explains why an unlit warning lamp does not rule out a lamp-control fault. The source also preserves operating conditions in diagnostic records.

## Check the circuit before blaming the controller

The diagnostic aids suggest considering other lamps sharing the ignition feed when that feed may be open. The test description also emphasizes observing both commanded states. These distinctions help separate a supply problem, an uncontrolled ground and a missing commanded ground.

The factory text describes PCM failure as an extremely unlikely result after circuit and connection checks, rather than the starting assumption. Use the exact professional wiring procedure and preserve recorded evidence before clearing the code. P1661 identifies failure of the warning system's electrical behavior; it does not demonstrate a separate engine malfunction.
