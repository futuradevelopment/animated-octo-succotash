# P0540: GM intake-heater circuit monitoring compares relay commands with feedback

The P0540 procedure indexed for the 2001 Chevrolet K 2500 6.6-litre diesel describes an intake-air heater operated through a relay. The ECM controls the relay coil's ground path. The diagnostic compares circuit feedback with the commanded operating state, rather than directly measuring how warm the intake air becomes.

## Cold-operation conditions explain when it runs

The listed system-voltage window is 10 to 18 V. One operating branch requires a running engine with intake-air temperature below 23 °C. Another uses ignition on, engine off for more than three seconds, and coolant below 49 °C. These branches should not be collapsed into a requirement that all temperatures and engine states occur simultaneously.

A heater observation made outside these enabling conditions may not reproduce the monitored event. Likewise, seeing the relay commanded on does not establish that its power output reached the heater. The test description explicitly distinguishes the relay's output supply from its control function.

## Keep the unresolved state wording visible

The printed setting conditions combine several voltage bands and paired conditions, including ignition-off wording among relay-state comparisons. That wording does not support a confident simplified voltage test. This article remains marked for source review and does not select one branch as a universal pass/fail limit.

The reliable interpretation is a command-versus-feedback circuit investigation under the correct cold-operation conditions. Confirm revised service information before numerical diagnosis. Heater power circuits can carry substantial current; professional testing must follow the actual wiring and protection requirements.
