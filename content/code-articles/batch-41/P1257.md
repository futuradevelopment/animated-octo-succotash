# P1257: GM supercharger bypass control fails to limit torque as expected

The cited GM P1257 procedure describes a supercharger bypass system. The PCM normally permits requested boost, but can command the bypass valve open to recirculate air when reduced boost is needed, including reverse selection, rapid deceleration or very high load.

## The threshold is not one universal boost pressure

The setting conditions require intake-air temperature above minus 10 °C and engine torque exceeding a maximum threshold for at least six seconds. That threshold varies with commanded gear and engine speed. The source does not provide one fixed pressure limit that can be applied to every operating point.

Its diagnostic aids distinguish a control-circuit short to ground, a binding bypass valve and a sticking or misadjusted actuator. The test description explains that a short to ground can hold the bypass closed despite an open command. It also links missing inlet vacuum during deceleration to a bypass that remains closed.

## Confirm the actual supercharged application

Operation CHARM indexes this procedure under a 1996 Riviera VIN K page, while the procedure itself describes supercharger equipment. This article retains a source-review flag for that application mismatch and does not claim that the indexed engine necessarily carries the system.

The supported interpretation is the documented supercharger strategy and its control distinctions. Verify the correct engine and calibration before professional diagnosis. The procedure's second-trip indication and recorded conditions do not establish which listed fault is present, and no deliberate high-load road test is prescribed here.
