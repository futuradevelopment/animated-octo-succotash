# P2012: The Subaru runner-control monitor listens for a driver fault flag

## Circuit low describes a driver-diagnostic result

In the 2012 Forester turbo factory criteria, P2012 concerns the bank 2 tumble-generator valve motor circuit. The ECM receives a diagnostic overcurrent signal from the motor-driving integrated circuit. The low state in the abnormality table refers to that fault-indication signal; it is not a published low-voltage limit to apply to either motor terminal.

The component illustration shows a bidirectional motor driver. That arrangement makes a simple assumption that one motor wire is permanently power and the other permanently ground unsuitable for interpreting the monitor. Use the matching circuit diagram and controller test procedure for actual electrical diagnosis.

## The command transition and repetition matter

Subaru checks the fault indication immediately after the tumble-generator output changes from OFF to ON. The abnormality judgment requires the overcurrent indication at ten successive 96-millisecond checks. Battery voltage must be at least 10.9 volts, and diagnosis is continuous when enabled. The MIL is specified to illuminate when the fault qualifies.

For normality, the diagnostic indication must return to the high, acceptable state; the source describes a judgment in less than one second. A normal indication after connector handling can therefore differ from the stored event without proving the original problem was imaginary.

## Preserve the distinction in the repair record

Record the bank, supply condition and whether the fault repeats when the motor is commanded. The criteria do not provide a numerical motor-current threshold or justify replacing the intake assembly from the generic code name alone. Have a technician trace the bank-specific driver and motor circuit, then repeat the appropriate Subaru inspection procedure after repair.
