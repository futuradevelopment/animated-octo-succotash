# P1667: GM reverse-lockout solenoid control disagrees with its command

For the 1996 Pontiac Firebird procedure, P1667 identifies the reverse-lockout solenoid circuit. The PCM controls the solenoid's ground path. In the described system, energizing the solenoid permits reverse selection below the stated low-speed boundary; de-energizing it above that boundary prevents reverse selection.

## The code checks electrical control, not driver gear choice

The source gives a 5 mph operating boundary for the lockout function. Separately, its diagnostic requires engine speed above 600 rpm and a mismatch between commanded driver state and circuit feedback for at least five seconds. Those are different conditions serving different purposes.

The circuit should be low when commanded on and high when commanded off. A failure of that relationship does not, by itself, identify whether the solenoid winding, wiring or control output caused the problem.

## Voltage history and warming behavior matter

GM notes that low system voltage can contribute and recommends reviewing captured data. Its test description also considers a coil that changes behavior as it warms, distinguishing an opening winding from excessive current. These are professional electrical checks, not instructions to hold an unidentified circuit energized.

The procedure stores failure information without requesting the malfunction lamp. Confirm the exact reverse-inhibit system before diagnosis and preserve its intended protection. Do not force reverse engagement or bypass the lockout to test the complaint. The factory discussion treats PCM failure as an unlikely conclusion after the relevant circuit checks.
