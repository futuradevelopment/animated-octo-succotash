# P0779: A good N216 winding does not clear its harness of suspicion

## Follow the branch after a passing valve test

P0779 maps to pressure control valve 2, N216, in the cited Phaeton factory manual. Its generic label is pressure-control solenoid B intermittent. The referenced diagnostic procedure explicitly continues beyond the valve's resistance measurement when that measurement is satisfactory. This matters because replacing the valve would not correct an intermittent connection elsewhere in its circuit.

The first inspection is the transmission's 16-pin connector, including corrosion and water ingress. Only after that connector is acceptable does the procedure test the regulator valve. For N216, the specified resistance is 6–8 ohms at approximately 20°C; the manual says a hotter valve has higher resistance.

## Keep the controller-side path in the test

A passing valve measurement sends diagnosis to the TCM wiring. Volkswagen specifies a maximum wire resistance of 1.5 ohms and a separate check that the relevant wires are not shorted to each other. The component mapping identifies N216 independently from the other four pressure regulators, so a neighboring valve's passing result does not establish this path is intact.

Those checks require the prescribed isolated connections and the correct wiring diagram. Do not interpret a powered-circuit resistance reading or a guessed terminal pairing as equivalent evidence.

## Document what was intermittent

Save the fault context and any connector or wiring defect actually found. The DTC table supplies no fault-duration or current-deviation threshold, and a momentary normal measurement may miss the original event. A defensible repair record shows the N216 path tested, the defect corrected and subsequent operation verified. TCM replacement is a later branch in the source, not the implication of the word intermittent.
