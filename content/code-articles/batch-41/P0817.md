# P0817: Starter permission depends on the park-neutral signal path

## A starting complaint can originate in range information

The 2008 Outback 3.0-liter factory chart associates P0817 with an open or short in the park/neutral signal output. Its listed consequences include inability to start in the intended positions and loss of the intended start restriction. Secure the vehicle and have that interlock checked professionally; deliberately attempting to start in a driving range is not an appropriate owner test.

## Follow the chart before condemning the starter

The procedure first asks whether the transmission controller reports a PRNDL-related fault and whether communication with the engine controller is available. Those branches matter because starter permission depends on usable range information and the inter-controller path. Replacing a starter motor does not resolve an incorrect permission signal.

The chart also checks the designated fuse. A repeatedly blown fuse sends diagnosis toward a short in its supply path rather than repeated fuse replacement. Isolated wiring checks then distinguish continuity, expected below one ohm, from unwanted grounding, for which the cited isolation criterion is at least one megohm. These are vehicle-specific measurements performed with the specified connectors disconnected.

## Verify the state change safely

A later professional test observes the signal with ignition on and engine off. The chart expects no more than one volt in Park and at least eight volts in Drive at its identified terminal. That is an electrical state comparison, not an instruction to crank in Drive.

If the expected states are present, connector contact and the engine-controller neutral-switch indication remain relevant. Preserve range-related codes and communication results alongside P0817. The factory sequence makes an interlock-path diagnosis reviewable before any controller or starter replacement is considered.
