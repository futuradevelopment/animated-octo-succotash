# P0094: GM diesel leak detection depends on fuel-pressure control evidence

The factory P0094 procedure indexed for the 2001 Chevrolet K 2500 6.6-litre diesel describes a closed-loop fuel-pressure system. The ECM compares measured rail pressure with its target and changes the pressure-regulator duty cycle. Injector duration also depends on measured rail pressure and the requested fuel amount.

## A leak label is not a location

The diagnostic is available with ignition on when the listed rail-pressure-sensor and reference-voltage faults are absent. Its diagnostic aids specifically mention supply restriction and air entry if the system needs priming to start. Consequently, the code label does not establish that liquid fuel is visibly escaping from a particular pipe or injector.

The useful investigation is whether pressure control and fuel supply behave as the correct service procedure requires. A pressure discrepancy can need investigation even when no external wet area has been identified. Conversely, this monitor does not establish the amount or location of a leak on its own.

## The published criterion needs correction

The source describes a difference between rail pressure and commanded pump flow, then gives that difference in MPa. Pressure and flow cannot be subtracted in that way. This article is marked for source review and does not turn the printed 30 MPa figure into an acceptance test.

The supported system description remains useful, but the numerical failure criterion needs verified calibration information. High-pressure diesel diagnosis belongs with qualified equipment and procedures; loosening a pressurized line is not a diagnostic shortcut. Save the recorded operating conditions before clearing information needed to resolve the fault.
