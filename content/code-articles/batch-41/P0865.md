# P0865: Subaru transmission-diagnosis input is held low

The 2002 Subaru Outback procedure describes P0865 as low input on the automatic-transmission diagnosis signal received by the engine controller. This is a defined inter-controller circuit. It should not automatically be treated as a modern CAN-bus communication failure merely because a generic description mentions communication.

## Establish whether the low condition is present now

The chart begins with an ignition-on voltage observation at the ECM and compares it with 1 V. If the circuit has already returned from the low condition, the source directs attention to contacts at both controller connectors. A warning lamp can remain associated with a stored event even after the electrical condition has changed.

When the low condition is present, the procedure isolates the ECM and TCM and checks the signal wire for a short to ground. That separates a harness fault from a module-related result. The specified disconnected state is essential to interpreting a resistance measurement.

## Read the transmission’s own diagnostic information

After the circuit checks, the chart asks whether the automatic transmission has its own trouble code. That information is investigated before the final module decision. P0865 alone therefore does not establish that the TCM must be replaced.

Subaru lists two consecutive failing driving cycles for detection and requires its post-repair inspection sequence. Preserve the complete fault set, identify the exact signal circuit and use the matching professional procedure. Clearing an engine-controller code without examining the transmission-side evidence can leave the reason for the asserted signal unexplained.
