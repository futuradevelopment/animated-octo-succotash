# P1812: GM records sustained high transmission-fluid temperature

For the 1996 Chevrolet Impala SS, P1812 means transmission-fluid temperature remained above 130 °C for 15 minutes while the listed temperature-sensor circuit faults were absent. The duration is central: this strategy is not described as a single instantaneous hot reading.

## Separate actual heat from a skewed measurement

The factory procedure describes a negative-temperature-coefficient thermistor integrated with the transmission fluid-pressure manual-valve position switch. As temperature rises, sensor resistance and signal voltage fall. The PCM's temperature interpretation therefore depends on both the thermal condition and the accuracy of that electrical relationship.

GM's diagnostic aids explicitly include a mis-scaled sensor as a possibility. They also identify operating history, such as towing, and a torque-converter stator problem as relevant context. The absence of a low- or high-input circuit code does not by itself prove that the sensor is accurate across its range.

## An unlit malfunction lamp is consistent with this strategy

The procedure classifies P1812 as Type D, freezes shift-adaptation updates and does not request the malfunction lamp. That response should not be mistaken for evidence that sustained overheating is harmless. The stored record still deserves investigation.

Professional diagnosis should compare the temperature evidence with the correct sensor specification and the vehicle's operating history. The source does not establish a universal towing limit or authorize continued operation under an overheating condition. Confirm this older transmission strategy before applying its 15-minute threshold to another vehicle.
