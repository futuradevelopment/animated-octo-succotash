# P0323: GM compares two distributor timing signals for interruptions

The 1996 Chevrolet Impala SS 5.7-litre factory procedure describes two distributor timing inputs to the PCM: a high-resolution signal with 180 pulses per crankshaft revolution and a low-resolution signal with four. P0323 uses their relationship to detect an interruption in either input.

## A comparison can reveal a missing signal

Both circuits switch between 0 and 5 V as the camshaft turns. The source identifies intermittent opens, shorts to voltage or ground, and an ignition-module problem within the distributor as possible explanations. Those possibilities are alternatives to investigate, not proof that every P0323 requires a distributor replacement.

The diagnostic aids note that a misfire may accompany the event. They recommend examining captured timing-pulse data and recorded operating conditions when the problem is intermittent. A normal signal observed later does not establish that both inputs remained intact during the earlier event.

## Preserve the evidence of an intermittent event

This procedure stores the diagnostic and failure information without requesting the malfunction lamp. It also distinguishes a currently failed test from a history record, which has a separate warmup-cycle clearing policy. An unlit lamp therefore cannot exclude a recorded timing interruption.

The factory discussion calls attention to distributor contamination or damage and, if components require replacement, the distributor ventilation system. This is a source-scoped diagnostic consideration, not a claim that contamination is always responsible. Confirm the exact distributor system and use its professional testing procedure before deciding which circuit or component failed.
