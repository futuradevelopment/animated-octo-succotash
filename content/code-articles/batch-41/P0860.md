# P0860: The sequential transmission has lost its engine-data exchange

## Why this communication link affects shifting

On the MR2 sequential manual transmission, the transmission ECU and engine ECM exchange information over CAN. The transmission side sends requested engine speed and torque, warning and diagnostic information. The engine side supplies operating speed, throttle opening, temperatures and torque information. P0860 identifies loss of that exchange; it does not by itself establish that the gear lever module needs replacement.

The cited monitor begins after five seconds from ignition-on, subject to its referenced monitor-disable conditions. It operates continuously and qualifies the absence of communication seven times, with an immediate MIL response. The table does not describe seven driving cycles or a seven-second timeout, so those interpretations should not be substituted for the printed criterion.

## Companion codes change the diagnostic route

Toyota specifically warns that simultaneous P0860 and P1646 can invoke an engine-stopping fail-safe response. An engine that has stopped in that situation requires investigation of the transmission control system as well as the engine complaint. Avoid further driving when engine stopping or unpredictable transmission operation makes the vehicle unsafe.

The chart first reads the complete code set. P0860 accompanied by P1646 or other sequential-transmission codes follows a different route from an isolated P0860. The subsequent checks assess the transmission ECU and its harness connection to the ECM before reaching a final ECM replacement branch.

## Retain the event context

Record freeze-frame data and which controllers can communicate before clearing codes or disconnecting modules. A later successful scan does not explain an earlier communication loss. Verification should establish restored exchange under the relevant conditions and account for companion faults, rather than merely extinguishing a warning lamp.
