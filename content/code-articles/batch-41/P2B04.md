# P2B04: GM looks for evidence that cylinder 5 actually received an injection pulse

For P2B04, GM's 24OBDG03B Part 1 distinguishes a commanded injection from evidence that the injector actually opened. The monitor concerns cylinder 5 and detects a missing pulse when the injector pintle or armature did not move as expected.

## A command is only the beginning of the event

The controller observes voltage-flux feedback from the injector coil through the enable and command circuits. An analogue-to-digital conversion supports a calculated opening magnitude. Failure means that the required opening signature was not detected or that the calculated result violated the calibrated missing-pulse limit.

This is electrical evidence of the opening event. It is not a direct measurement of injected fuel quantity and should not be described as one. A visible command in diagnostic data therefore does not, on its own, demonstrate successful delivery of the intended pulse.

## The feedback must be ready for interpretation

The source requires the small-pulse general diagnostic enable and ready status for fuel-pulse voltage-feedback data. It lists 50 to 100 samples with cylinder-event sampling and continuous monitoring when enabled. The count should not be converted into a fixed duration without the missing event-rate information.

A supporting calibration table uses an opening-magnitude quantity indexed against fuel-rail pressure. Its numerical entries are internal diagnostic parameters, not an instruction to apply that voltage to an injector terminal. The row classifies the fault as Type B, two trips. Confirm the specific controller and service procedure before testing; injection-system electrical and pressure hazards require appropriate professional equipment. P2B04 identifies the monitored cylinder, not a proven replacement part.
