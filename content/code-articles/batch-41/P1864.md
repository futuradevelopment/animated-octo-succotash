# P1864: GM monitors the torque-converter-clutch on/off solenoid circuit

The cited 1996 Chevrolet Impala SS procedure identifies P1864 with the torque-converter-clutch on/off solenoid. That solenoid works with a separate pulse-width-modulated solenoid to control fluid at the clutch valve. Confusing the two devices can send diagnosis toward the wrong circuit.

## The fault concerns an electrical state relationship

The on/off solenoid receives ignition voltage, while the PCM provides its ground path. The source describes mismatches such as feedback remaining high with an on command or remaining low with an off command. It lists a 10-to-17 V system window, ignition on and five-second persistence.

These are command-versus-feedback checks. They do not directly measure clutch friction condition or establish that the converter is mechanically worn. The source's test description separates power supply, ground-control capability and combined solenoid/harness resistance as different parts of the professional investigation.

## Some operating changes are commanded responses

The documented default inhibits converter-clutch engagement, freezes shift-adaptation updates and can inhibit fourth gear in hot mode. The malfunction lamp is requested. A disengaged clutch after the fault therefore may be a protective control response rather than independent proof that the clutch cannot apply.

The diagnostic aids also note that a short to ground can accompany a stuck-on clutch code. Review the full fault set and exact wiring before making a repair decision. This article does not reproduce the source's ambiguous printed slip-speed range or provide an external solenoid-jumper test.
