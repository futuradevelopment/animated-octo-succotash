# P1689: GM delivered-torque feedback is an electrical signal between controllers

The cited GM procedure groups P1689 with related delivered-torque circuit faults. The brake controller supplies a 5 V signal circuit, and the PCM switches that circuit to ground to represent delivered engine torque. The information is carried by pulse timing and duty cycle, not by a mechanical torque sensor at this circuit.

## Distinguish the controller’s electrical check

The source describes a nominal 128 Hz signal with a five-percent frequency tolerance and a 25-to-95-percent duty-cycle range. The duty cycle represents torque. For P1689, the PCM's listed failure criterion is circuit voltage below 4.75 V or above 5.25 V.

That voltage criterion must not be confused with the related brake-controller code's invalid-signal timing rule. The shared procedure contains different decisions for different modules. Selecting the wrong row would change the diagnosis despite the common circuit description.

## Traction-control behavior can be a consequence

The diagnostic requires ignition on and engine speed above 500 rpm for one second. The source states that the traction-control system, when equipped, is disabled and its warning indication can be commanded. That response is consistent with loss of trustworthy torque information; it does not prove a mechanical loss of engine output.

The diagnostic aids also warn that a short to power can damage the PCM. Circuit investigation therefore needs the exact professional procedure and appropriate equipment. The code does not authorize bridging the signal, replacing a controller from the label alone, or treating a duty-cycle value as a direct torque measurement.
