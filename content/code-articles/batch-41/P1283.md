# P1283: GM detects excessive voltage on accelerator-pedal channel 2

P1283 in the cited 2001 Chevrolet 6.6-litre diesel procedure concerns accelerator-pedal channel 2 above 4.75 V. Unlike channel 1, channel 2 normally decreases in voltage as the pedal is pressed. That opposite relationship is essential when interpreting the high-input label.

## A high signal is not a high pedal angle

The factory position table places the second channel's higher normal voltage near the released-pedal end of travel. Even there, the listed normal range remains below the fault boundary. P1283 therefore reports an excessive electrical input, not a valid full-throttle request or a generic statement about how far the pedal moved.

The sensor is one of three in a shared housing, with separate signal, reference and low-reference circuits. The monitor runs during crank or run with ignition voltage between 7 and 16 V and the specified reference faults absent. These details identify the exact comparison that must be matched during diagnosis.

## Channel redundancy affects the observed response

The procedure distinguishes an isolated sensor fault from combinations affecting multiple sensors. The remaining two channels can support response when only one is affected, whereas some multiple-channel faults impose reduced power and all three out of range permit idle only.

GM stores failure information without requesting the malfunction lamp for this procedure. Review the full fault set and the correct channel's transfer relationship before professional circuit testing. The number 2 selects an electrical channel, not a proven failed pedal assembly.
