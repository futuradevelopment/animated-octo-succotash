# P1272: GM compares the second and third accelerator-pedal signals

The short catalog label for P1272 does not fully describe the cited GM monitor. In the 2001 Chevrolet 6.6-litre diesel procedure, the comparison is between accelerator-pedal channels 2 and 3, and failure means their interpreted positions differ by more than ten percent.

## Similar signal directions do not make channels interchangeable

Both channels' voltages decrease as pedal travel increases in the factory position table, but their voltage ranges differ. A technician therefore needs the correct transfer relationship for each channel. Comparing raw voltages for equality would not reproduce the controller's position-agreement test.

The assembly contains three sensors with separate reference, low-reference and signal circuits. The diagnostic runs in crank or run with ignition voltage between 7 and 16 V, provided the specified channel and reference-supply faults are absent. These prerequisites distinguish a valid agreement test from an input already rejected for another reason.

## Read the entire pedal-fault pattern

The procedure's response depends on how many sensors are affected. Some single-sensor fault combinations allow the other two channels to remain in use; particular multiple-sensor combinations impose reduced response, and all three out of range restrict operation to idle.

P1272 consequently identifies a relationship requiring investigation, not a proven failed channel 2 or a guaranteed operating mode. GM records failure information without a malfunction-lamp request in this procedure. Preserve that information and match the exact three-sensor system before deciding which circuit needs professional testing.
