# P0714: The Phaeton temperature sensor has a rising resistance curve

## Intermittent temperature faults need the correct sensor curve

Volkswagen's Phaeton diagnostic table associates P0714 with an intermittent transmission-fluid-temperature sensor A circuit and directs inspection of ATF level and the G93 sensor. The referenced electrical procedure supplies a temperature-dependent resistance table. Using a generic assumption that every automotive temperature sensor decreases in resistance as it warms would reverse this particular check.

For G93, the listed values are approximately 0.8 kilohm at 20°C, 1.3 kilohms at 60°C and 1.9 kilohms at 120°C. Resistance increases across those reference temperatures. Compare a measured resistance with the actual fluid temperature and the correct sensor terminals, not an arbitrary room-temperature limit.

## The connector comes before component replacement

The procedure first inspects the transmission's 16-pin connector for contact corrosion and water ingress. An unsatisfactory connector is repaired before proceeding. If the sensor meets its resistance criteria, the chart continues to wiring between the transmission and TCM, with a maximum continuity resistance of 1.5 ohms and separate short-circuit checks.

These checks are performed under specified isolation conditions, with ignition off, the vehicle secured, adequate battery voltage and sound transmission grounds. They are not measurements to improvise on a powered controller circuit.

## A later normal reading does not explain the event

Because P0714 is intermittent, a passing static measurement can coexist with a prior failed connection. Preserve the temperature and operating context when the code occurred. Use the matching ATF-level instructions and circuit procedure to establish the fault, then verify stable temperature reporting after repair. The table does not provide a universal failure-duration threshold for this DTC.
