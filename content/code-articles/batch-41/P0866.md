# P0866: Subaru transmission-diagnosis input remains high

For the 2002 Subaru Outback, P0866 is high input on the automatic-transmission diagnosis signal to the ECM. The factory chart does not assume that every high-input result has the same cause. It uses voltage observations at both ends of the inter-controller path to distinguish different electrical conditions.

## A very high reading and a normal changing signal differ

The chart first considers a reading above 10 V as evidence requiring investigation of a battery-supply short. Other branches distinguish a signal that changes within the stated 1-to-4 V region from one held above the expected range. A later changing signal can mean the circuit is currently normal even though a diagnostic record remains.

The procedure also compares observations at the ECM and TCM. A high reading at one end with the expected response missing at the other can direct diagnosis toward an open connecting path rather than an immediately condemned controller.

## Power and ground remain part of communication diagnosis

The final branches examine connector contact and the TCM's supply and grounding. These checks explain why replacing a module before confirming its electrical support can fail to resolve an apparent communication problem.

Subaru specifies two consecutive failing driving cycles and a post-repair inspection sequence. This source describes a dedicated diagnosis-input circuit, not a universal network-voltage test. Professional diagnosis must use the correct connector and connection states. The article does not recommend bridging controllers or applying voltage to the signal line.
