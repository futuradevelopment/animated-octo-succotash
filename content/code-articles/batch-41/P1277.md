# P1277: GM accelerator-pedal channel 1 falls below its low-input boundary

P1277 in the cited 2001 Chevrolet 6.6-litre diesel procedure means accelerator-pedal channel 1 voltage is below 0.25 V. The factory pedal-position table places that channel's normal released-pedal voltage above the fault boundary. A low input should therefore not be interpreted simply as the driver's foot being off the pedal.

## Identify the first channel before using the limit

The assembly contains three sensors, each with its own signal, reference and low-reference circuits. Channel 1 rises in voltage as pedal travel increases. The other channels use different relationships, so the first channel's low threshold is not a universal pedal-sensor limit.

The monitor requires crank or run, ignition voltage between 7 and 16 V, and absence of the listed reference-supply faults. The source's setting text says less than one second; this article does not replace that wording with an invented persistence requirement.

## A single-channel fault has a defined fallback

GM states that faults confined to one pedal sensor can leave the remaining two available for calculating response without commanding reduced power. Certain multi-sensor combinations change that fallback, and all three out of range restrict the engine to idle.

The code therefore needs the rest of the recorded pedal-fault pattern. The procedure stores failure information without requesting the malfunction lamp. Confirm the correct channel and service circuit before professional diagnosis; the low-voltage result alone does not prove that the pedal housing itself must be replaced.
