# P1270: GM cannot convert accelerator-pedal input into usable digital data

The factory P1270 procedure for the cited 2001 Chevrolet 6.6-litre diesel describes an analogue-to-digital conversion problem. The ECM receives accelerator-pedal position information as analogue data and must convert it for its powertrain software. This code identifies failure of that processing task, rather than simply a pedal signal that is too high or too low.

## Recognize the diagnostic's operating window

The procedure enables the monitor with the ignition in crank or run and voltage between 7 and 16 V. Its setting-condition text says the conversion problem is present for less than one second. That wording should not be silently reversed into an at-least-one-second rule or generalized to another controller.

The relevant distinction is between the incoming analogue information and the microprocessor's ability to process it. A code label containing accelerator-pedal sensor does not make the pedal assembly the demonstrated cause of a conversion failure.

## No lamp does not mean no recorded event

GM states that this diagnostic stores fault information and operating conditions without illuminating the malfunction indicator lamp. An equipped driver-information centre may display a message. A lack of an engine-warning lamp consequently does not establish that no diagnostic event occurred.

The current failed-test state clears after the monitor runs and passes; history retention is handled separately. Preserve the failure record and verify the exact controller before following the applicable service procedure. This description does not establish an internal repair method or justify either pedal or ECM replacement without the required diagnostic evidence.
