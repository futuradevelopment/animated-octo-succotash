# P1287: GM pedal channel 3 uses its own low-voltage threshold

The low-input boundary for P1287 is below 1.49 V in the cited 2001 Chevrolet 6.6-litre diesel procedure. This is the third accelerator-pedal channel's limit. Applying the first channel's much lower boundary would miss the specific diagnostic being evaluated.

## Different ranges are deliberate

The factory position table shows channel 3 voltage decreasing as pedal travel increases, while its expected full-travel range remains above the P1287 threshold. A low reading is therefore not automatically a legitimate request for more acceleration. The controller first needs the signal to remain within its valid electrical range.

The three sensors share a housing but have separate signal, reference and low-reference circuits. The diagnostic requires crank or run, 7 to 16 V ignition voltage and absence of the listed reference-supply faults. Those conditions belong to this documented strategy and should not be transferred indiscriminately to a different pedal design.

## Use the other fault records to understand fallback

GM describes continued use of the two remaining sensors for faults confined to a single channel. Certain faults across multiple channels restrict response; all three out of range permit idle only. P1287 by itself does not determine which of those responses occurred.

The procedure records failure information without a malfunction-lamp request. Preserve that evidence and confirm the correct channel before professional testing. A third-channel low input does not establish the failed part, and the source does not support bypassing redundancy to restore accelerator response.
