# P1504: Ford idle-air control output has an electrical-load fault

In the 1999 Ford table, P1504 means the PCM detected an electrical-load problem on the idle-air-control output circuit. That is different from the idle-speed comparison used by P1506 or P1507. An engine idling too fast or too slowly is not, by itself, the complete P1504 criterion.

## Follow the IAC supply and control paths

The explicit possible-cause field lists an open IAC circuit, an open supply to the solenoid, a control circuit shorted to power or ground, a damaged valve and PCM damage. Those alternatives explain why an electrically failed output can involve wiring as well as the valve coil.

The row gives a historical IAC solenoid resistance range of 6–13 ohms. Use it only for the applicable component and approved test conditions, not as a universal specification for modern electronic throttles or every idle actuator.

## Keep the source’s displaced text separate

The diagnostic-aid cell also repeats the resistance range and inserts mechanical idle concerns such as a stuck valve and vacuum leaks. Their placement is ambiguous relative to the electrical-load definition. This draft flags that mixed wording and does not make those extra phrases the basis of a P1504 repair.

A technician should demonstrate the supply, circuit, coil or controller fault using the correct procedure, then verify the monitored electrical load. A smoother idle after unrelated work or a cleared code does not prove the IAC output circuit now passes its evaluation.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the applicable systems in Ford’s US-market 1999 PCED. Confirm the vehicle and test application before using its limits. [Ford diagnostic descriptions: P1504 row](https://www.fordservicecontent.dealerconnection.com/pubs/content/~WVX2/~MUS~LEN/14/VX24002.HTM).
