# P1479: Ford high-speed cooling-fan relay control fails its electrical comparison

P1479 in Ford’s 1999 description monitors the high-speed fan primary control circuit. With the command on and the control grounded, excessive current is a failure condition. With the command off, the PCM expects supply voltage through the high-speed relay or constant-control-relay-module coil.

## The two commanded states reveal different failures

The aid explains that an open or grounded circuit is detected when the PCM is not grounding the control, while a short to power is detected during the grounded command. The key-on self-tests cycle the circuit on and off. A single reading without the command state does not reproduce the complete evaluation.

Possible causes include open or shorted control wiring, missing relay supply, relay/module damage and PCM damage. The code concerns the primary control path; it does not independently prove a failed fan motor.

## Confirm high-speed control rather than only visible fan movement

A fan operating at another speed is not evidence that the high-speed control passes. Record the high-fan command and monitored fault state, then use the exact circuit arrangement to locate the demonstrated electrical problem.

After correction, verify appropriate circuit behavior through the required on/off evaluation. Keep personnel and equipment clear of a fan that may start unexpectedly. The source provides no universal relay-terminal numbers, and clearing P1479 does not establish that engine-cooling performance is adequate under load; electrical control and actual cooling need their respective evidence.
