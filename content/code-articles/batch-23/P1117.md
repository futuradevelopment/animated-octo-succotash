# P1117: Ford coolant-temperature signal intermittently opens or shorts

The 1999 Ford description identifies P1117 as an intermittent open or short in the engine-coolant-temperature circuit during engine operation. Its diagnostic aid focuses on observing the ECT PID for sudden changes associated with disturbing the relevant harness or sensor.

## Investigate the temperature signal’s interruption

A normal ECT value after the event does not disprove an intermittent circuit problem. The useful observation is a repeatable discontinuity on the coolant-temperature channel, together with the circumstances that provoke it. The row does not supply a numerical discontinuity threshold, so it cannot justify an invented maximum temperature step.

The documented candidates include wiring, connector, sensor and PCM damage, plus low coolant. These are alternatives to investigate, not a recommendation to replace all of them. Actual coolant condition and electrical signal behavior need to be distinguished before selecting a repair. Cooling-system checks must follow precautions for hot, pressurized coolant.

## Treat the source anomaly explicitly

The row’s diagnostic-aid cell also ends with statements about the throttle-position circuit and sensor. Those statements do not match the ECT heading or its monitor description. They have been excluded here, and this draft retains a source-review flag rather than presenting them as coolant-system causes.

Use the vehicle-specific ECT procedure to resolve the actual fault, then repeat the condition that produced the abrupt reading. A code-clear operation or a single plausible temperature sample is weaker evidence than a stable signal through the previously reproducible event.
