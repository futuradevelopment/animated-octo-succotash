# P1168: Ford fuel-rail pressure remains in range but is too low

Ford labels P1168 as fuel-rail pressure that remains within the signal’s usable range but is too low for the monitor. The comprehensive component evaluation watches FRP and fails when the reported pressure drops below its calibrated requirement. This is distinct from proving that the electrical signal has gone completely out of range.

## Separate real pressure loss from a misleading input

The source lists low fuel pressure, low or absent fuel, a damaged rail-pressure sensor and excessive circuit resistance. That combination leaves two broad possibilities: pressure really is inadequate, or the pressure information reaching the controller is unreliable.

A technician should establish the applicable fuel system and compare the FRP input with the proper service procedure before choosing a pump or sensor. The row does not identify a universal fuel-pump replacement or a permissible pressure for every Ford engine.

## Why this draft omits a numerical pass/fail limit

The diagnostic-aid cell includes both a low-pressure threshold and a higher-pressure failure statement, together with repeated cause text. The arrangement does not clearly assign every statement to P1168 versus the neighboring high-pressure entry. Those values have therefore not been promoted into a universal test instruction, and the source ambiguity remains flagged.

Verification needs evidence that the relevant pressure is credible and satisfies the correct calibration during the monitor’s operation. Clearing the code or observing one plausible pressure value cannot resolve an intermittent supply problem or establish that the source’s disputed threshold applies to the vehicle.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the applicable systems in Ford’s US-market 1999 PCED. Confirm the vehicle and test application before using its limits. [Ford diagnostic descriptions: P1168 row](https://www.fordservicecontent.dealerconnection.com/pubs/content/~WVX2/~MUS~LEN/14/VX24002.HTM).
