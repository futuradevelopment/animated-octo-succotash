# P1125: Ford throttle-position signal drops out intermittently

P1125 in the Ford 1999 PCED concerns abrupt throttle-position signal changes during engine operation. The description refers to an event in the preceding 80 warm-up cycles. That history helps explain why the code can remain relevant even when a present-time throttle reading looks normal.

## A sudden jump is the evidence to reproduce

Ford’s aid watches the TP voltage PID while the sensor connection or harness is gently disturbed. For the documented application, an abrupt reading below 0.49 V or above 4.65 V indicates an intermittent fault. Those thresholds are historical test values, not instructions to sweep or force every throttle mechanism to arbitrary positions.

The source lists poor connector continuity, poor harness continuity and an internal sensor open or short. A repeatable signal interruption when one connection is disturbed supports a different investigation from a smooth but consistently offset throttle reading.

## Preserve the relationship between movement and signal

Record where the disturbance occurred and what the voltage did. If the signal is stable with the vehicle stopped, that observation cannot erase evidence from an earlier intermittent event. Conversely, replacing the sensor solely because P1125 names throttle position does not address a reproducible harness fault.

Use the correct sensor circuit identification and avoid moving linkages or rotating engine parts during checks. Verification should include stable TP information under the condition that previously caused the interruption, followed by the applicable diagnostic evaluation. The description supplies no evidence that the PCM is the most likely cause.
