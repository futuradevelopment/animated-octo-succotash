# P1116: Ford coolant temperature is outside self-test conditions

P1116 is Ford’s historical coolant-temperature self-test range code. The 1999 description gives an ECT signal window of 0.3–3.7 V, but it also specifies different temperature requirements for the two self-test modes. Those requirements are essential when deciding whether the test result represents a fault or an unmet test condition.

## Warm-up requirements change with the test

The key-on, engine-off self-test requires coolant temperature above 10°C. The engine-running self-test requires more than 82°C. Thus an engine warm enough for the first evaluation can still be insufficiently warm for the second. A record should identify which self-test produced P1116 and the ECT reading at that time.

Ford lists overheating, thermostat malfunction and low coolant alongside sensor, connector and PCM damage. The diagnostic scope therefore includes the cooling system’s actual condition, not just the temperature sensor’s resistance or electrical output.

## Check whether the reported temperature is credible

A technician must distinguish an engine genuinely outside the test conditions from a temperature signal that misrepresents it. That distinction determines whether to investigate warm-up or cooling behavior, or follow the applicable ECT circuit procedure.

Do not open a hot pressurized cooling system to investigate this code. The quoted temperatures describe test eligibility; they are not instructions to keep operating an overheating engine. Once the cause or unmet condition is addressed, repeat the correct self-test with the required temperature established. The row does not provide a universal thermostat specification or prove a failed ECT sensor.
