# P1183: Ford engine-oil-temperature signal intermittently fails

P1183 in the 1999 Ford PCED describes an engine-oil-temperature circuit that intermittently opens or shorts while the engine runs. It is a circuit-behavior code, not direct proof that engine oil exceeded a temperature limit.

## Distinguish the sensor from the signal path

The documented possible causes are the harness, connector, sensor and PCM. Ford’s diagnostic aid uses a controlled substitute signal and compares that known input with the voltage reported by the scan PID. The historical example is a 1.0 V simulated input.

The distinction is useful even without reproducing the electrical procedure here: if a known input is represented correctly, attention shifts toward the sensing side; if it is represented incorrectly, the receiving circuit and PCM remain in question. That is more specific than replacing the EOT sensor solely because its name appears in the code.

## Intermittent faults need event context

A successful comparison at one moment does not reproduce every temperature- or movement-sensitive interruption. Preserve when the engine-running event occurred and whether the relevant signal can be disturbed consistently. Use the exact connector, approved equipment and vehicle procedure for any signal simulation; applying an arbitrary external voltage is not an appropriate shortcut.

After the demonstrated cause is corrected, verify both the credibility of the EOT input and its stability under the relevant conditions. The source supplies no universal EOT sensor location, oil-change remedy or justification for assuming actual oil overheating from P1183 alone.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the applicable systems in Ford’s US-market 1999 PCED. Confirm the vehicle and test application before using its limits. [Ford diagnostic descriptions: P1183 row](https://www.fordservicecontent.dealerconnection.com/pubs/content/~WVX2/~MUS~LEN/14/VX24002.HTM).
