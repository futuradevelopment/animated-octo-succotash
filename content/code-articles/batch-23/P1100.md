# P1100: Ford MAF signal intermittently changes beyond its limits

For the applicable 1999 Ford system, P1100 records an intermittent mass-airflow signal problem. The comprehensive component monitor looks for abrupt airflow or voltage changes beyond calibrated limits while the engine is running. The description refers to an event within the preceding 40 warm-up cycles, making fault history relevant even when the signal now appears normal.

## Preserve the intermittent evidence

Ford lists poor continuity in the MAF connector or harness and an intermittent internal sensor open or short. Those alternatives explain why the code does not automatically condemn the sensing element. A connection that becomes unreliable with movement can produce the same monitored result as an internal interruption.

The source’s diagnostic aid uses the MAF voltage PID while gently disturbing the sensor connection or harness. For that historical application, a sudden excursion below 0.23 V or above 4.60 V is evidence of an intermittent fault. These are the published test limits, not universal MAF voltages for modern Ford engines.

## Match the disturbance to the signal

The informative observation is a repeatable change in the live signal associated with a particular connection or harness area. A steady acceptable value after the engine has cooled or the vehicle has stopped does not reproduce that evidence.

Have a technician use the proper connector information and work clear of moving or hot components. Record the conditions that trigger the dropout, then verify signal stability under those same conditions after the identified fault is corrected. Replacing a MAF without resolving a reproducible connector interruption leaves the original diagnostic explanation unanswered.
