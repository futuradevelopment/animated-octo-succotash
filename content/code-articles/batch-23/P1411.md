# P1411: Ford secondary-air delivery does not produce the expected exhaust response

The 1999 Ford P1411 description concerns secondary-air delivery downstream. The system does not detect the expected presence of added air in the exhaust. Its diagnostic aid states that the AIR pump must be capable of driving the heated oxygen-sensor indication lean for the test to be meaningful.

## Air delivery is more than pump operation

Ford lists the electric pump, a leaking or blocked pump hose, and a leaking, blocked or stuck bypass solenoid. These alternatives explain why a running or audible pump alone does not establish that air reached the intended exhaust path.

The oxygen response is the documented evidence of delivery. The row does not provide a universal oxygen voltage or a numerical flow-rate requirement, so the reader should not substitute an arbitrary lean threshold for the applicable test.

## Trace the path and verify the response

A technician should identify the fitted AIR arrangement and distinguish a failed source of air from an interrupted or misdirected delivery path. The source’s solenoid possibilities include both stuck-open and stuck-closed conditions; selecting one solely from the code would be unsupported.

Use the correct procedure around hot exhaust parts and electrically operated pumps. After the demonstrated fault is corrected, confirm that the secondary-air operation produces the expected oxygen response under the required conditions. Clearing P1411 or observing the pump command change does not prove delivery. The code also does not independently diagnose the catalyst or authorize replacing the oxygen sensor without testing its role.
