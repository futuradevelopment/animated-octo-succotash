# P1131: Ford bank-one upstream oxygen signal remains lean without switching

The Ford 1999 table labels P1131 as missing switching from HO2S-11 while that upstream bank-one sensor indicates lean. It says the evaluation fails after switching is absent for a calibrated interval. That describes what the controller observes; it does not prove which part caused the observation.

## Confirm the signal before naming the cause

The row directs possible-cause research to P1130. That referenced entry includes oxygen-signal wiring and terminals, fuel-delivery problems, intake and exhaust leaks, EGR concerns and base-engine conditions. The shared list makes clear that a lean-indicating trace is not, by itself, evidence that a new oxygen sensor will solve the fault.

Record the bank-one upstream channel and the associated fuel-control behavior. Also distinguish this lean-indicating result from P1132, whose title identifies a rich-indicating result on the same channel.

## A limitation in the historical wording

The description associates a lean indication with correction of an over-rich condition. Without the complete test-state explanation, that sentence is not a reliable basis for asserting the engine’s actual mixture direction. This draft flags that ambiguity and uses the explicit sensor indication and missing-switching criterion instead.

A technician should resolve the circuit-versus-mixture question through the applicable Ford procedure. Verification should establish that the relevant channel responds and switches during the required evaluation, with the underlying fuel or circuit concern addressed. Simply interpreting “lean” as a guaranteed vacuum leak would overstate what this source establishes.
