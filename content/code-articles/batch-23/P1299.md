# P1299: Ford has activated cylinder-head overtemperature protection

P1299 in Ford’s 1999 description means cylinder-head overtemperature was detected and the fail-safe cooling strategy was activated. That second fact distinguishes it from a temperature reading alone: the controller has entered a protective response to the sensed overheating condition.

## Protection is a response, not a repair

The listed causes include cooling-system concerns, low coolant and base-engine problems. Activation of protection does not identify which one is present or prove that damage has been prevented. It also does not establish that the engine can continue operating safely.

The row refers to the electronic engine-control system description for the full strategy. It does not provide a universal trigger temperature, explain every actuator action or promise a fixed distance the vehicle can travel in this state. Those claims should not be invented from the phrase “fail-safe.”

## Preserve the overheat evidence and resolve its cause

Record temperature warnings, the relevant CHT information and the circumstances leading to protection. Do not deliberately continue an overheating run to reproduce the result. Cooling-system inspection must respect hot coolant and pressure hazards.

A technician should identify the actual cooling, coolant or engine fault with the correct vehicle procedure. Verification after correction should demonstrate stable thermal behavior under suitable controlled conditions and the appropriate diagnostic result. Simply resetting P1299 removes a record; it does not establish restored cooling capacity or rule out consequences of the earlier overheating event.
