# P1120: Ford throttle signal is below its learned closed-throttle range

Ford’s 1999 P1120 description concerns a throttle-position input below the expected closed-throttle reference. The title refers to RATCH being too low. An important detail is that the signal may remain within the broad calibrated self-test range while still failing this more specific low-position evaluation.

## A plausible voltage can still be wrong for this check

The documented failure band is 3.42–9.85 percent, expressed alternatively as 0.17–0.49 V. The row applies this observation with the key on and the engine either stopped or running, and its aid also refers to continuous-memory evidence. Those are historical calibration values, not a universal closed-throttle specification.

This explains why “the sensor is not at zero volts” is an insufficient dismissal. The monitor is evaluating the low reference region, not merely searching for a fully grounded signal.

## Follow the reference and return paths

Ford lists frayed TP wiring, connector corrosion, loose sensor pins, an open reference-voltage supply and reference voltage shorted to signal return. Those possibilities direct attention to how the position signal is established. They do not demonstrate that the throttle blade itself is physically defective.

Record the TP voltage or percentage with its operating state, then use the correct wiring and pinpoint information to identify the affected path. Do not adjust a throttle stop simply to move the displayed number into range. Verification should show that the applicable TP reading no longer falls into the documented low band under the same test conditions.
