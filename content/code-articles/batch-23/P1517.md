# P1517: Ford bank-two intake-runner monitor input is outside its expected range

Ford’s 1999 P1517 entry concerns the bank-two intake-manifold-runner control input. The description says the monitor-pin signal falls outside the expected range during continuous or key-on, engine-off self-test. For actuators with two monitor switches, the individual code identifies the affected bank.

## The signal’s meaning changes with operating state

The aid describes concern when the IMRC monitor PID is below the reference voltage at closed throttle, and when it remains near or above 1 V at an engine speed of at least 3,000 rpm. These are application-specific clues, not a reason to run an engine at that speed without the prescribed controlled procedure.

The distinction is that a feedback value must be interpreted with the mechanism’s expected position. A voltage judged without throttle and engine-state context can be misleading.

## Check the runner hardware as well as its reported state

Ford lists binding, seizure, damage or obstruction of the IMRC hardware as possible causes. This prevents a bank-two “input error” from being treated automatically as an electrical sensor failure. Actual runner movement and the reported position must be compared.

A technician should identify the bank-two mechanism for the exact engine and follow its diagnostic sequence. After a demonstrated cause is corrected, verify that physical movement and monitor input agree under the relevant conditions. A successful bank-one check does not establish that the bank-two channel passes, and the source provides no universal actuator location or replacement instruction.
