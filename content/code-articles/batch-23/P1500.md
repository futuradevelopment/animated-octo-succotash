# P1500: Ford vehicle-speed interruptions interfere with other OBD tests

Ford’s 1999 P1500 description identifies an intermittent vehicle-speed input. It adds a useful consequence: the speed fault interferes with other OBD evaluations, including catalyst efficiency, EVAP and oxygen-sensor monitoring. The code therefore helps explain why apparently unrelated readiness tests may not complete normally.

## Do not diagnose the interrupted monitors first

An incomplete catalyst or EVAP evaluation in this context does not automatically prove a catalyst or vapor-system defect. The speed input needed for the evaluation may have become unreliable. Preserve the sequence of faults and monitor status before deciding that every affected system needs a separate parts repair.

The source lists intermittent connections, wiring that intermittently opens or shorts, VSS damage and PCM damage. It does not identify which connection or give a universal dropout duration.

## Establish the intermittent speed evidence

The useful investigation is whether the vehicle-speed input loses credibility under repeatable conditions and whether that event aligns with the interrupted evaluation. A normal speed reading while stationary cannot exclude a fault occurring during vehicle operation.

A technician should use the correct vehicle-speed circuit and a controlled diagnostic procedure, avoiding road testing while distracted by a scanner. After a demonstrated speed-input fault is corrected, verify reliable speed information and allow the previously affected monitors to run under their proper conditions. Clearing P1500 alone does not show that the speed fault is repaired or that the catalyst, EVAP and oxygen evaluations have subsequently completed.
