# P1128: Ford upstream oxygen signals appear assigned to the wrong banks

The 1999 Ford description gives P1128 an upstream bank-assignment meaning. During a commanded fuel change, the oxygen-sensor monitor checks whether the expected upstream signal responds for the intended bank. Failure to observe the proper response leads to this code.

## Follow signal identity, not only sensor activity

The relevant channels are the upstream sensors identified in the manual as HO2S-11 and HO2S-21. A sensor can show changing voltage while its signal is associated with the wrong bank. Thus “both sensors switch” does not answer the bank-assignment question the monitor is asking.

Ford lists crossed upstream connectors, crossed wiring at those connectors, and crossed wiring at the applicable 104-pin harness connection. These are documented possibilities, not proof of a particular crossed terminal on every Ford. The correct diagram and engine bank identification are required before tracing connections.

## Keep upstream and downstream diagnosis separate

The same manual assigns the corresponding downstream bank-response issue to P1129. Investigating the rear sensors alone would not explain an upstream P1128 result. Record the exact sensor channels and their response to the appropriate professional test rather than relying on scanner labels without confirming their meaning.

After a routing or connection fault is established and corrected, verification must demonstrate that the commanded bank and observed upstream response agree. The description gives no universal pinout, fuel-command magnitude or oxygen-switching threshold, and it does not support replacing both upstream sensors before checking their identities.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the applicable systems in Ford’s US-market 1999 PCED. Confirm the vehicle and test application before using its limits. [Ford diagnostic descriptions: P1128 row](https://www.fordservicecontent.dealerconnection.com/pubs/content/~WVX2/~MUS~LEN/14/VX24002.HTM).
