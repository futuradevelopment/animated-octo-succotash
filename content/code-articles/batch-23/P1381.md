# P1381: Ford bank-one cam timing remains too far advanced

Ford’s 1999 P1381 description concerns bank-one cam timing that exceeds the calibrated advance limit or remains advanced. It is a position result. The diagnostic aid specifically directs investigation of the VCT unit and distinguishes this branch from an electrical circuit test.

## Follow the position and hydraulic evidence

The listed causes include incorrectly set cam timing, absent oil flow to the VCT piston chamber, low oil pressure, a solenoid valve stuck closed and binding in the advance mechanism. These possibilities explain why a valve can be implicated mechanically without the code proving an open solenoid coil.

The source supplies no universal advance-angle threshold, oil-pressure number or timing-mark procedure. Those details must come from the exact engine’s workshop information. A numeric cam reading cannot be evaluated against an invented cross-model specification.

## Do not substitute the P1380 circuit branch

P1380 in the same manual is explicitly an electrical VCT circuit check. P1381 instead concerns the unit’s position behavior. If both appear, preserve both results and follow their applicable procedures; their shared component name does not make one test replace the other.

A technician should establish whether timing setup, oil delivery or the mechanism accounts for the over-advanced position. After a demonstrated correction, verify that the cam responds and returns within its permitted position under the required conditions. Erasing the code or replacing an electrical solenoid without confirming the documented hydraulic/mechanical cause does not establish that this position monitor now passes.
