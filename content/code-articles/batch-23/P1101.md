# P1101: Ford MAF reading is outside the self-test window

P1101 in the 1999 Ford PCED concerns a mass-airflow reading outside the expected self-test range. Its meaning depends on whether the engine is stopped or running. Applying a running-engine range to a key-on, engine-off measurement can turn a valid observation into a false conclusion.

## Use the correct test state

The published diagnostic aid gives a key-on, engine-off MAF voltage ceiling of 0.27 V. For the engine-running self-test it gives a 0.46–2.44 V window. Ford also refers to the airflow-equivalent information in pinpoint test DC. These values belong to the documented historical test, not to every sensor or engine carrying P1101.

A reading outside the applicable window is described as a hard fault indication. That is distinct from P1100’s sudden intermittent change: P1101 can be supported by an out-of-range self-test reading without demonstrating a movement-sensitive dropout.

## Separate supply, connection and sensing problems

The source includes low battery charge, a partially connected MAF, contamination, an open power-ground connection, an open MAF return to the PCM, and damaged electronics among possible explanations. The list gives no ranking or failure probability.

The useful sequence is to document the test state and reading, verify that the electrical and connection conditions permit a valid test, and follow the matching DC procedure. A dirty-looking sensor alone is not confirmation. After correction, repeat the same self-test under the applicable conditions and confirm that the reading falls inside that test’s specified range.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the applicable systems in Ford’s US-market 1999 PCED. Confirm the vehicle and test application before using its limits. [Ford diagnostic descriptions: P1101 row](https://www.fordservicecontent.dealerconnection.com/pubs/content/~WVX2/~MUS~LEN/14/VX24002.HTM).
