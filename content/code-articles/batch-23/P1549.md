# P1549: Ford intake-manifold tuning output circuit reports a fault

Ford’s 1999 P1549 description names the intake-manifold communication or tuning-valve system, abbreviated IMCC/IMT. The PCM monitors its output circuit during continuous or key-on, engine-off self-test. That identifies an output-control concern rather than a verified mechanical obstruction inside the manifold.

## Use the tuning-valve evidence that matches the heading

The relevant cause list includes an open IMT control circuit, missing supply, a shorted control path, a damaged valve and PCM damage. The diagnostic aid says an IMTVF fault PID showing YES can indicate a present concern. That provides a specific scan-state observation to preserve alongside the stored code.

The community dictionary’s temperature-valve wording does not precisely match this historical tuning-system description. Confirm the exact fitted system before choosing a component from that catalog label.

## Exclude the source’s unrelated steering statements

The same row also inserts references to a power-steering-pressure sensor and self-test range. Those statements do not match the IMT heading, monitored output or IMTVF aid. They are excluded here and remain flagged for review rather than being converted into an unsupported steering diagnosis.

A technician should establish whether supply, wiring, the tuning actuator or its controller explains the IMT fault. Verification needs correct output behavior and fault-state response under the applicable evaluation. A cleared P1549 or a replaced steering sensor would not establish that the documented intake-tuning circuit concern has been resolved.
