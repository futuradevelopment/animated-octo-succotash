# P1413: Ford secondary-air feedback stays low when the pump is commanded on

P1413 in Ford’s 1999 table means the secondary-air monitor circuit remains low while the PCM commands the electric AIR pump on. The monitor result is a command-versus-feedback disagreement; it does not independently measure air reaching the exhaust.

## Understand why the off-state path matters

Ford explains that, when the pump is off, the monitor is held low through a resistance path involving the pump. A low signal during a valid on command can therefore indicate that the expected energized state was never established.

The listed causes include loss of battery supply, an open AIR path, a path shorted to ground, pump damage, solid-state relay damage and PCM damage. The aid specifically highlights an open circuit between the relay and pump. That makes the supply/relay/load path important before assuming the pump motor is the failed part.

## Confirm both the command and the electrical response

Record the commanded state together with the monitor reading. An on command alone is not proof of voltage at the load, and a pump that sounds active in a different test does not resolve a recorded mismatch.

Use the correct vehicle circuit and professional electrical procedure to identify where the expected state is lost. After a confirmed correction, verify that the monitor follows the valid command. Secondary-air flow testing remains a separate question: P1411 addresses the exhaust response to delivered air, while P1413 specifically identifies the monitored electrical disagreement.
