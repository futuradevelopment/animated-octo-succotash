# P1406: Ford EGR feedback still indicates flow after the valve closes

Ford’s 1999 P1406 description concerns a downstream DPFE hose problem. During the relevant driving evaluation, the EGR valve is commanded closed. The monitor fails when the differential-pressure signal continues to indicate EGR flow after that command.

## Interpret the persistence of the pressure signal

The source lists a disconnected downstream hose, icing that plugs it, and a blocked or damaged EGR tube. Its aid adds checks for water, routing dips and proper sensor mounting. These details matter because an incorrect pressure path can make the controller infer continued flow without proving the valve itself remains open.

The word “downstream” identifies the sensing connection, not an oxygen sensor or a generic exhaust component. Confirm the actual hose arrangement before deciding which side to inspect.

## Use the closed-valve response to verify the cause

P1405 is the related upstream result, where the signal implies negative flow. P1406 instead concerns a flow indication that persists after closure. Keeping the different observed behavior in the record helps avoid a title-based parts swap.

A technician should inspect the documented hose and passage conditions and use the applicable controlled sensor test. Exhaust-system temperature and pressure precautions still apply. After correction, verify that the feedback behaves correctly when EGR is commanded closed during the required evaluation. The description supplies no universal hose-replacement interval and does not establish that installing a new DPFE sensor is the only way to resolve the result.
