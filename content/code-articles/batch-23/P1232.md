# P1232: Ford low-speed fuel-pump relay command has an electrical fault

P1232 monitors the PCM’s low-speed fuel-pump primary control circuit in the applicable 1999 Ford system. The description distinguishes two states: excessive current when the control is commanded on, and missing expected voltage when the control is commanded off.

## Why the off-state voltage matters

With the output off, the PCM expects supply voltage to appear through the relay coil on the control circuit. Therefore, missing voltage can involve the relay’s supply or control path; it does not prove the fuel-pump motor itself is open. The source names an open or shorted control circuit, loss of relay supply, relay damage and PCM damage.

The self-test cycles the output through on and off states. A reading taken without knowing the commanded state loses the distinction on which this monitor depends.

## Use the complete test, not the incomplete aid sentence

One diagnostic-aid sentence about open and grounded-circuit detection omits the needed command-state wording. This draft flags that omission and relies on the complete preceding monitor description. It does not reconstruct the missing state as if Ford had explicitly printed it.

A technician should use the exact low-speed relay circuit diagram and procedure to separate supply, coil and control faults. Verification must cover both commanded states under the applicable test. Restored fuel pressure alone does not demonstrate that the primary electrical monitor passes, and P1232 is not sufficient evidence to replace the fuel pump.
