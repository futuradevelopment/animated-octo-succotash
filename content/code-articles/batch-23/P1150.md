# P1150: Ford bank-two upstream oxygen switching reaches a control limit

Ford’s 1999 P1150 entry identifies HO2S-21, the upstream oxygen channel for bank two. It explicitly refers to P1130’s monitor description: the controller expects oxygen switching and detects a failure when the circuit or fuel-control condition reaches its calibrated limit. The bank assignment is the key distinction between the two codes.

## Identify the bank-two signal before interpreting it

The referenced diagnostic aid describes a conventional oxygen signal switching across approximately 0.45 V, with movement around 0.2–0.9 V. Those figures belong to the published system, not to every oxygen-sensing technology.

Use the actual engine bank definition and the scan-tool channel mapping. An apparently healthy trace from bank one does not answer a no-switch result recorded for bank two. Comparing the two banks can provide context, but it does not replace checking the affected channel.

## A fuel-control limit does not select a replacement part

The shared source lists electrical faults, fuel-pressure and injector concerns, intake leaks, PCV or EGR problems, base-engine issues and exhaust leaks near the sensor. The list is broad because the same missing-switch result can arise from signal-path problems or the mixture being measured.

Preserve the bank-two trace together with relevant fuel-control data and use the matching Ford procedure to distinguish those possibilities. Following a confirmed repair, verify that this upstream channel switches and the relevant control evaluation completes. The cross-reference gives no separate P1150 threshold and no evidence that replacing both bank sensors is necessary.
