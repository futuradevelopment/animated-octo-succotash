# P1000: Ford readiness monitors have not finished

P1000 in this Ford manual describes unfinished onboard monitor testing. It is a completion record, so it does not identify a failed sensor, catalyst or control module. The PCM stores it when at least one OBD monitor has not completed its required drive-cycle evaluation.

## Find what interrupted readiness

The documented background includes a new vehicle, a disconnected battery or PCM, and recently erased diagnostic memory. Those histories explain why readiness can be incomplete without proving a component defect. Ford also lists a monitor failure occurring before the drive cycle finishes. Therefore, an explanation based solely on a recent reset is inadequate if other faults remain present.

On equipped vehicles, the power take-off input is another relevant distinction: PTO operation or a PTO circuit held at battery supply can interfere with testing. That is an application-specific lead, not a reason to investigate a nonexistent PTO system on a passenger car.

## What establishes completion

Read the individual readiness states and associated fault records. The useful next question is which monitor has not run and whether its enabling conditions can be met under the applicable Ford drive cycle. Clearing codes changes the evidence and should not be mistaken for completing a monitor.

The source does not give a universal mileage or time that guarantees readiness. A vehicle can accumulate distance while a required test remains disabled. Verify the actual monitor status after appropriate testing; the absence of a dashboard warning alone does not establish completion.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the applicable systems in Ford’s US-market 1999 PCED. Confirm the vehicle and test application before using its limits. [Ford diagnostic descriptions: P1000 row](https://www.fordservicecontent.dealerconnection.com/pubs/content/~WVX2/~MUS~LEN/14/VX24002.HTM).
