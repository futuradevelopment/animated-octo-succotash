# P1405: Ford EGR feedback suggests an upstream hose problem

The historical Ford P1405 monitor commands the EGR valve closed during driving and observes the pressure difference across the EGR orifice. It reports failure when the DPFE signal implies flow in the negative direction. This particular interpretation points toward the upstream sensing hose and related passage rather than simply reporting low EGR delivery.

## The closed-valve condition explains the hose check

Ford lists a disconnected upstream hose, an upstream hose obstructed by ice, and a blocked or damaged EGR tube. The diagnostic aid also highlights moisture, icing, routing with excessive dips and correct sensor mounting. These are concrete reasons to inspect how pressure reaches the sensor.

A hose that looks present externally may still be restricted or incorrectly routed. Conversely, the code alone does not prove the hose must be replaced; the pressure information and physical condition must support the conclusion.

## Keep upstream and downstream faults distinct

P1406 uses a different closed-valve result: indicated flow continuing after closure, associated with the downstream hose. That distinction helps prevent examining the wrong pressure connection just because both codes mention the same DPFE sensor.

Use the exact vehicle procedure for controlled sensor-function checks and keep clear of hot exhaust components. After a documented hose, routing or passage fault is corrected, verify the pressure-feedback behavior under the applicable evaluation. The row does not provide a universal vacuum value or authorize testing a hot sensor with improvised pressure equipment.
