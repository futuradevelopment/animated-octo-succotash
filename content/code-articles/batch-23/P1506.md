# P1506: Ford actual idle speed exceeds the requested speed

P1506 in Ford’s 1999 description means the PCM sees idle speed above its requested value. The comparison is actual versus desired rpm, not a single universal idle-speed number that applies to every engine or operating condition.

## Preserve the target as well as the measured speed

A high rpm reading is incomplete evidence without the controller’s requested idle and the circumstances of the evaluation. The row does not publish a universal excess-rpm threshold or timer. Those details must come from the applicable calibration and procedure.

The explicit possible-cause list names an IAC control path shorted to ground and a damaged IAC valve. This does not prove that the valve must be replaced; the electrical path and the actuator’s behavior need to be distinguished.

## Interpret the IAC response carefully

Ford’s aid observes whether rpm changes when the IAC is disconnected, with little or no change supporting concern about a stuck or damaged valve. That is a procedure-dependent observation, not a complete diagnosis from a single disconnected connector. A technician must use the appropriate test conditions and consider the actual control arrangement.

Following a demonstrated correction, verify that actual idle follows the requested value during the relevant evaluation. Erasing P1506 or seeing acceptable rpm once does not show that the original overspeed condition has been resolved. This source does not authorize adjusting the throttle stop to force the idle downward or applying its IAC procedure to an electronic-throttle system without confirming applicability.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the applicable systems in Ford’s US-market 1999 PCED. Confirm the vehicle and test application before using its limits. [Ford diagnostic descriptions: P1506 row](https://www.fordservicecontent.dealerconnection.com/pubs/content/~WVX2/~MUS~LEN/14/VX24002.HTM).
