# P1121: Ford throttle position and measured airflow disagree

For the applicable 1999 Ford system, P1121 is a rationality result: the PCM compares throttle-position information with mass airflow and the expected load relationship. The source describes the comparison during engine-running self-test and storage in continuous memory when the signals do not fit the calibrated relationship.

## Read the two signals together

Ford gives two illustrative hard-fault combinations. One pairs very low indicated throttle—below 4.82 percent or 0.24 V—with load above 55 percent. The other pairs throttle above 49.05 percent or 2.44 V with load below 30 percent. These values belong to the published historical diagnostic aid.

The contradiction between the paired readings is the important evidence. Looking only at TP voltage or only at MAF output discards the reason this code differs from a simple circuit-high or circuit-low code.

## Decide which part of the relationship is unreliable

The listed possibilities include an air leak between the MAF and throttle body, incorrect TP sensor seating, a damaged TP sensor and a damaged MAF sensor. Consequently, the code alone cannot tell the technician which sensor to replace.

Preserve simultaneous throttle and load data and determine whether the disagreement can be reproduced under the applicable test. Any loaded or moving-vehicle evaluation belongs in a controlled professional procedure, not improvised road testing while watching a scanner. After a confirmed correction, verify that the paired readings agree through the relevant operating range; clearing the code without checking that relationship does not confirm the diagnosis.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the applicable systems in Ford’s US-market 1999 PCED. Confirm the vehicle and test application before using its limits. [Ford diagnostic descriptions: P1121 row](https://www.fordservicecontent.dealerconnection.com/pubs/content/~WVX2/~MUS~LEN/14/VX24002.HTM).
