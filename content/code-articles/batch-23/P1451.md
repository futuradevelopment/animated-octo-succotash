# P1451: Ford canister-vent solenoid voltage disagrees with its commanded state

Ford P1451 in the 1999 table evaluates the canister-vent solenoid’s electrical circuit. The PCM compares its signal with the permitted range for the commanded vent duty cycle. This differs from a direct finding that the vapor path leaks or the valve is mechanically obstructed.

## Pair voltage with the command

The diagnostic aid gives two historical hard-fault examples: less than 1.0 V when the EVAPCV PID is at 0 percent, and more than 0.5 V when that PID is at 100 percent. These are paired command-and-voltage conditions. A voltage alone, without the duty-cycle state, cannot be interpreted using this aid.

The source allows the relevant observation in output test mode with the key on and engine stopped, or in the engine-running mode. The vehicle-specific procedure must determine the correct connections and test preparation.

## Distinguish supply, coil and control faults

Possible causes include an open supply, an open control circuit, control wiring shorted to ground or supply, a damaged solenoid and PCM damage. The description does not rank them or establish that a new vent valve is automatically required.

A technician should find which electrical path explains the mismatch, then verify appropriate voltage behavior for the commanded states. Mechanical sealing or flow may need a separate EVAP test. Clearing P1451 or hearing a valve click does not, by itself, demonstrate that the monitored electrical response satisfies the proper calibration.
