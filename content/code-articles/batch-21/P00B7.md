# P00B7: Hot engine and radiator temperature separation

GM's P00B7 example infers insufficient coolant flow from temperature behavior. It does not directly measure pump delivery. The table gives a stuck-closed thermostat as an example of the condition being detected, not as a confirmed diagnosis for every vehicle with this code.

## The temperature combination is important

The failure description spans two PDF pages. Engine coolant must exceed 117°C while the engine-to-radiator temperature difference exceeds 45°C. The condition must persist for more than five seconds before failure counting starts. Reading only the first page would omit the second temperature comparison and materially change the meaning.

The published strategy then accumulates failing observations, with 30 failures in a 600-sample window. These details describe this calibration's logic; they are not temperatures to deliberately reach during a home test.

## Separate overheating evidence from sensor evidence

The monitor depends on usable coolant-temperature information. A related radiator sensor circuit fault changes the interpretation of a reported temperature split. Preserve the two temperature readings, their trend and companion faults before assuming a physical restriction.

If the vehicle is overheating, stop operation according to its owner's instructions and let the system cool before professional assessment; do not open a hot cooling system. A repair should be verified through credible temperature behavior and completed monitoring under appropriate service conditions. Simply clearing P00B7 leaves both the potential flow problem and the validity of its inputs unresolved.
