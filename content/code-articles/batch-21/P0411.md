# P0411: Secondary-air flow compared with a pressure-based model

The P0411 strategy in GM's group 04 summary evaluates secondary-air flow during catalyst heating. It compares an air-mass estimate derived from the pressure signal with the secondary-air model. A running pump alone therefore does not prove that the system delivered the expected flow.

## Both too little and too much disagreement matter

The published ratio must remain within its calibrated band; the table lists failure below 0.844 or above 1.156. Those figures belong to this pressure-based implementation. They are not direct airflow-meter specifications for every secondary-air system.

The check is scheduled once per driving cycle when secondary air has been active, beginning near the start of catalyst heating. Intake and coolant temperature windows, pressure relationships and valid altitude information qualify the test. A warm engine with no secondary-air event may never offer the required opportunity.

## Capture the operating event

The most useful evidence connects pump and valve commands with the pressure response and completed monitor result. Sound from a pump does not establish flow through the intended path, while a pressure signal needs its own credibility before the model comparison can be trusted.

The summary does not identify a particular restriction, leak or failed part solely from P0411. Use the applicable service procedure to separate electrical operation from delivered airflow. Following repair, verify a qualified secondary-air monitoring event; an immediate restart without a new code may simply mean that catalyst-heating conditions were not met.
