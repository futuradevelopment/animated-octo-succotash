# P0338: Crank tooth-count plausibility in a GM implementation

P0338 is broadly described as a high crankshaft-position circuit signal. The GM group 02 table instead presents a rationality check of counted teeth between reference-gap events. This article explains that documented implementation without treating it as the definition for every vehicle.

## A signal can exist and still be implausible

A tooth-count comparison evaluates the pattern recognized by the controller, not just the presence of voltage. The source places it beside separate checks for a missing reference gap, unexpected resynchronization and intermittent loss of engine-speed information. Those distinctions help explain why a sensor can appear active while synchronization remains unreliable.

The neighboring checks should not be merged into a single replacement rule. A code combination and the actual recognized pattern provide more diagnostic context than the generic word high.

## Confirm the calibration before using numerical limits

The printed tooth-count criterion is not suitable here for a general workshop tolerance. Its applicability and the relationship to the generic circuit-high label need confirmation in the vehicle's service procedure. No connector voltage, sensor gap or replacement part is established by this summary.

Preserve reference-gap and synchronization information where the diagnostic equipment exposes it, alongside companion crank and cam faults. Verification should demonstrate stable recognized timing information and successful completion of the relevant check. Clearing P0338 or observing engine speed momentarily does not establish that the pattern fault has been corrected.
