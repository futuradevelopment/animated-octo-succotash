# P012D: High inlet-pressure circuit signal is not proof of overboost

P012D concerns the electrical signal from the supercharger inlet absolute-pressure sensor. The GM entry describes either an open sensor ground or a continuous short-to-high condition in the signal circuit or sensor. It does not, by itself, establish excessive boost pressure.

## Recognize the sensor's electrical deadband

This implementation reserves part of the signal range for detecting circuit faults. Its high threshold is above 90 percent of a five-volt range, shown as 4.5 volts. That is a diagnostic signal boundary for this documented sensor, not a universal pressure specification for other cars.

The controller requires 320 failing observations in a 400-sample window, sampled every 12.5 milliseconds. A transient captured on a different time scale should not be assumed to reproduce the controller's complete decision.

## Keep the high and performance faults separate

An open ground can produce a high signal even when the pressure itself is ordinary. That is why a service investigation must establish electrical integrity before interpreting the displayed pressure as physical evidence. The adjacent P012B entry instead tests a signal that remains in range but disagrees with modeled relationships.

Preserve the stored failure conditions and any related sensor faults before work begins. Use the exact vehicle wiring and test procedure to determine where the high signal originates. Successful verification requires a credible pressure signal and completed monitoring; absence of a code immediately after clearing memory is insufficient.
