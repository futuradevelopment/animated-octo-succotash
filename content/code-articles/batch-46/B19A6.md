# B19A6: why the passenger backrest needs its own blower-command diagnosis

A passenger-seat climate failure may involve the backrest rather than the cushion. B19A6 makes that distinction in Ford's Fusion procedure: the passenger backrest blower speed circuit is shorted to ground, causing the controller to stop passenger-seat operation.

## Identify what the module is controlling

The thermoelectric assembly has a blower power feed, a return and a separate speed-control signal. The source identifies VHS21 as the backrest control circuit. The fact that power reaches the assembly therefore does not prove the command circuit is usable.

Ford checks the isolated control path for leakage to chassis ground, then checks its separation from the blower return. Each test requires more than 10,000 ohms in the stated configuration. The measurement points and disconnected components matter; a reading taken through a connected electronic assembly cannot be interpreted as the same harness test.

## Avoid replacing the wrong seat component

With the two specified wiring checks satisfactory, the factory branch calls for the backrest TED assembly. It does not direct replacement of the cushion merely because both areas stopped heating or cooling after protective shutdown. Seat-track motors are outside this particular fault description. Since the service sequence includes the passenger seat side-airbag connection, safe restraint depowering and restoration are required. Keep a record of the active climate mode, repeatability and companion faults so the technician can verify the complaint under comparable conditions.
