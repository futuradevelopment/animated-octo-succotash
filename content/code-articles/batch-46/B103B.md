# B103B: driver-seat cooling can shut down on a grounded TED circuit

The Fusion climate-controlled seat uses thermoelectric devices in both the cushion and backrest, each associated with a blower assembly. B103B describes a driver-seat output problem that causes the Dual Climate Controlled Seat Module to shut that seat down. A working passenger seat therefore does not rule it out.

## The monitor protects an electrical output

Ford lists grounding of the relevant TED output circuits or a sensed TED resistance below 0.9 ohm as triggering conditions. That threshold is not a desired seat temperature and does not describe how warm the upholstery feels. It concerns the electrical load seen by the seat controller.

The diagnostic sequence separates unwanted ground, unwanted voltage and resistance of the TED plus its wiring. It then divides cushion and backrest branches so that a failed element is not confused with a damaged harness. The specified component-resistance checks use a 0.9–10-ohm window under their stated disconnected conditions.

## Seat wiring requires controlled service

This procedure intersects seat-mounted airbag connections and requires the appropriate restraint-system depowering and restoration process. It is a workshop diagnosis, not a reason to probe arbitrary connectors beneath the seat. Ford also warns against inserting probes into female module terminals. Preserve whether heating, cooling or both fail, and whether only the driver side stops operating. Those details help match the fault to the protected output without assuming every TED needs replacement.
