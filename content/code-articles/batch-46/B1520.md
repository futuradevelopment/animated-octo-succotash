# B1520: an open hood-sense circuit can leave that alarm entry point unarmed

Ford B1520 can arise when the Fusion's hood-sense circuit is electrically open even though the hood is physically closed. The normally closed switch supplies a ground path to the Smart Junction Box; opening the hood removes that ground. Wiring damage can imitate the open-hood state.

## Check the switch and its return separately

The source lists an open sense wire, an open switch ground or a switch stuck open as possible triggering conditions. Ford first compares the switch's response with its plunger pressed and released, then checks the sense circuit for unwanted grounding and loss of continuity. The dedicated ground receives its own test.

A replacement switch would not correct a broken return wire. Conversely, a sound harness cannot make an internally open switch report the closed state. The sequence distinguishes those cases before the module is considered.

## Alarm behavior is not proof of mechanical closure

Ford notes that an input always interpreted as open prevents the hood from being armed as a protected entry point. This is a fault consequence, not a method for servicing or bypassing security. Confirm that the hood is mechanically secured independently of any electrical indication. Provide the technician with recent hood-latch or front-end work history and the original code record. After repair, verify both truthful hood-state reporting and the alarm's normal authorized operation rather than relying on silence alone.
