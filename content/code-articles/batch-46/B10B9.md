# B10B9: when the HVAC speed command cannot reach the blower controller

The automatic climate system does not regulate blower speed simply by switching the relay on and off. Its HVAC module sends a pulse-width-modulated command to a separate speed controller. B10B9 concerns that command circuit, which Ford identifies as VH101 in this application.

## What the suffix contributes

With :12, the module detects excessive voltage consistent with a short to battery. With :14, it detects no voltage and describes an open or ground short. A lost command can prevent the controller from responding even when the relay has supplied power.

The :14 diagnostic branch isolates the wire between the HVAC module and controller for continuity, then checks the command under the specified PANEL and high-blower settings. Connector and operating conditions are part of the measurement, so a random voltage reading cannot reproduce the test.

## Avoid skipping the surrounding circuits

The full procedure also checks controller supply and ground, the motor connections and shorts between those connections. Its final branches inspect controller and HVAC terminals before authorizing component replacement. A damaged terminal can imitate an internal electronic failure.

The source lists B10B9:12 in its definitions but omits a corresponding explicit selection in the initial DTC-routing step. It also has inconsistent relay-feed identifiers elsewhere. Those omissions warrant review rather than an invented :12 branch. A completed repair should restore commanded speed changes and leave the HVAC self-test clear of a returning fault.
