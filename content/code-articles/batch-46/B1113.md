# B1113: overheating of the passenger seat's electronic driver

B1113 is an over-temperature fault in the passenger climate-controlled seat's electronic drive system. Ford specifies a TED driver integrated-circuit temperature above 175°C as the trigger. This is an internal electronic-driver temperature, not a statement that the passenger seat surface has reached that value.

## Why only one seat may stop

The dual climate-controlled seat module disables the affected passenger seat when the fault occurs. The other seat can remain available. Each seat's cushion and backrest has a thermoelectric device and blower assembly, so the passenger circuit includes more than a conventional resistance heater.

Normal operation requires the engine running. A scan-tool override with ignition on operates in 15-second intervals, which matters when a technician tries to reproduce the concern during service.

## Inspect the load before condemning the controller

The shared B1113/B111B procedure first checks the passenger TED circuits for ground and voltage shorts, then measures the TED-and-wiring resistance. Ford uses a 0.9-to-10-ohm range for those specified load checks. Further isolation distinguishes the cushion or backrest device from its wiring.

Only the appropriate passing results support the DCSM replacement branch. Probing must not spread the module connector terminals, and seat work requires the prescribed side-airbag precautions. Verification includes restoring the restraint system, repeating the self-test and checking passenger heating or cooling operation under the proper running conditions. A hot-electronics code alone is not evidence to replace the seat trim or cushion.
