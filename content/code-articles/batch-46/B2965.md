# B2965: how one shorted speaker circuit can silence several outputs

B2965 is the speaker-circuit fault code for the base and mid-level audio systems covered here. The ACM detects an open, a short to ground or a short to voltage in a speaker circuit. It does not identify a specific speaker position by the code alone.

## Protection can widen the symptom

The audio control module has internal overload protection. A short in one speaker circuit can cause several speakers to lose sound, so the number of silent speakers is not necessarily the number of failed components. Ford notes that B1342 may also appear during that protection response and cannot be cleared until the circuit failure is repaired.

A speaker walk test helps identify the affected output. The procedure distinguishes a tweeter-only complaint from a main-speaker complaint because their signal paths require different isolation checks.

## Measure audio as an audio signal

The speaker outputs are AC signals. Ford checks for fluctuating AC voltage during radio playback, then isolates the circuit for unwanted voltage, an open or a ground short if the signal is absent. A simple DC supply check does not replace that sequence.

When wiring tests pass, ACM connector inspection comes before the final module decision. Repair confirmation should include the speaker walk test, normal listening and a repeat fault check. Replacing several silent speakers without finding the overloaded circuit can leave both the sound complaint and the protection-related codes unchanged.
