# B1A61: the cabin-temperature input and a conflicting Ford cross-reference

Automatic climate control needs a usable cabin-temperature input to regulate comfort. In Ford's Fusion test, B1A61:11 indicates excessive voltage drop on that sensor's reference circuit; :15 indicates no expected drop, associated with an open circuit or a short to voltage. These are electrical findings rather than proof of unusual cabin temperature.

## Compare the sensor and its supply

The cited circuit uses an HVAC-module reference and return. The procedure first compares sensor resistance with its temperature-dependent table, then checks the disconnected harness supply against a 4.7–5.1-volt window. An acceptable supply reading does not verify every part of the sensor circuit under all conditions.

Further branches isolate an unwanted voltage, an open reference wire, an open return or a grounded reference. This separates the sensor element from the wiring that carries its signal. Ford also checks connector condition before the final module-replacement branch.

## A source inconsistency needs confirmation

Although the heading and circuit description identify B1A61, step D2 labels its outgoing branches with B1A68, the neighboring ambient-temperature code. That mismatch is retained as a review limitation; it should not be silently converted into a wiring instruction. A technician should confirm the applicable factory revision and actual cabin-sensor connector before using those branches. The article does not transfer the ambient sensor's identity or connector pinout to the cabin circuit.
