# B272A: passenger cushion overheating includes airflow and sensor-circuit checks

Ford's B272A procedure considers airflow as well as electrical faults. The passenger cushion TED draws cabin air through a filter and sends conditioned air through a manifold in the foam. A restriction in that path can therefore matter even when the controller and switch still communicate.

## Heating and cooling have different limits

The cited monitor describes TED temperature above 70°C in cooling mode or above 85°C in heating mode for more than 34 seconds. It also lists a disconnected TED or blocked duct among the conditions associated with shutdown. These are internal device conditions, not acceptable occupant-contact temperatures.

The first checks address obstruction and filter condition. Ford then compares passenger cushion and backrest temperature PIDs while operating the seat. A cushion reading that rises disproportionately leads toward installation and manifold inspection; a seat that will not run takes an electrical branch.

## One connector reference requires verification

The thermistor-wiring branch includes ground and circuit-isolation checks, followed by blower continuity. Step AM5 switches from the otherwise named C3305B connector to C3265B for one measurement. That inconsistency is retained as a review issue, so no pin-level instruction is derived from it here. Use the applicable wiring diagram before probing. Seat-mounted airbag precautions apply to disassembly, and a repaired system needs both temperature-behavior and fault-code verification.
