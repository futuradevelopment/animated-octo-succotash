# C1702: finding a voltage feed into the right parking-aid signal

A right rear parking sensor that reports C1702 is associated with a signal circuit short to voltage. Ford lists wiring, terminals, the bumper harness, the sensor and the PAM as the possible diagnostic areas. The code does not justify selecting one of those parts without testing.

## Compare the pattern across sensors

The procedure retrieves both continuous and on-demand PAM results. If several channels report faults, a damaged or corroded bumper harness becomes an early inspection target. If only the right channel is affected, the technician still checks its wire before using a replacement sensor.

The decisive voltage measurement is made after disconnecting the suspect sensor and PAM. Any voltage detected on the specified isolated signal path calls for circuit repair. This test prevents a normal module-supplied reference from being confused with the fault being investigated.

## Do not conceal an inconsistent instruction

Ford's subsequent known-good-sensor step contains an unclear repair decision: its printed branches do not cleanly correspond to whether substitution removes the concern. A technician needs a verified correction before following that replacement logic. The article is marked for review because the ambiguity is in the source itself.

Later PAM inspection covers corrosion, damaged terminals and pushed-out pins. Repair confirmation should include a repeat self-test and controlled functional checks of the right rear detection area. A brief absence of the warning, without reproducing the operating conditions, does not establish that the voltage fault has been removed.
