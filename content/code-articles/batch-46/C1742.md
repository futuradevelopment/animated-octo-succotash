# C1742: why the Fusion parking aid can detect an obstacle but stay silent

The parking aid needs a working sounder as well as functioning distance sensors. Ford C1742 concerns the warning-speaker circuit being open or shorted to ground. It should not be read as a direct verdict on the ultrasonic sensors at the bumper.

## Diagnose the warning path separately

The Fusion system changes the warning tone rate as an obstacle gets closer. The cited procedure first requires diagnosis of other parking-aid faults, then checks the disconnected sounder itself. Its specified resistance window is 40–60 ohms. A sounder outside that range follows a different branch from one that passes.

For C1742 with a satisfactory sounder, the remaining sequence checks the speaker wiring for grounding and opens. The positive-circuit grounding test is repeated with the speaker disconnected to distinguish which side of the circuit needs attention. Continuity checks of the specified positive and negative paths use a less-than-5-ohm criterion.

## Silence does not prove clear space

The neighboring C1743 code addresses a short to voltage and uses a different electrical branch. Keeping the full code record prevents those procedures from being mixed. Only after the speaker and wiring checks does Ford inspect the module connection and consider the Parking Aid Module. Until the audible warning has passed its operation check, the driver cannot rely on its silence while reversing. A repaired code record and an audible functional test answer different questions.
