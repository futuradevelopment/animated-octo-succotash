# B1083: diagnosing the air-inlet actuator by its electrical suffix

The recirculation damper motor changes the HVAC air-inlet position. Ford's module reverses voltage and ground across its two motor circuits to drive the actuator in either direction. B1083 concerns that electrical drive path, which is different from diagnosing a refrigerant or blower-speed problem.

## Keep the three suffixes distinct

Suffix :11 identifies a short to ground, :12 a short to battery and :13 an open circuit. The procedure routes those results to ground isolation, unwanted-voltage or continuity tests respectively. It also checks for a short between the two motor conductors.

A stuck door or binding linkage remains another possible cause of an inoperative inlet door. Passing wiring checks should therefore lead to connector, actuator and mechanical evaluation rather than an automatic HVAC module replacement.

## Verify an inconsistent opening step

The source's first step lists HVAC connector disconnection before requesting HVAC DTCs, without explaining reconnection for module communication. That sequence needs confirmation in corrected service information. This article preserves the issue and does not recommend attempting a scan of an unpowered disconnected module.

Later steps inspect actuator terminals, perform the actuator component test and examine module connections before the final replacement branch. Correct probe adapters matter because damaged terminals can create a new intermittent fault. After a repair based on verified instructions, check that the inlet door responds to its commands and repeat the self-test with the proper connections restored.
