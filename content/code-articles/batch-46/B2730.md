# B2730: driver backrest overheating needs airflow checks and verified mode criteria

Ford B2730 concerns the driver backrest thermoelectric device rather than the cushion or cabin-temperature sensor. The Fusion procedure checks whether airflow through the backrest assembly and manifold is restricted, then uses temperature observations to choose between mechanical and electrical diagnosis.

## Keep the published temperature wording visible

The cited page assigns an 85°C threshold to cooling and a 70°C threshold to heating, sustained for more than 34 seconds. That mode assignment is retained as printed and flagged for verification against the applicable factory revision; no reversed or corrected threshold is inferred here. Neither figure should be used as an allowable seat-surface temperature.

The practical test begins with the blower and filter. Ford compares the backrest and cushion temperature PIDs during operation. A backrest that rises disproportionately leads to installation and manifold inspection, while a disabled seat takes the thermistor and blower-circuit branch.

## Distinguish heat transfer from an erroneous reading

The wiring sequence considers a grounded thermistor path, contact between its circuits and missing blower continuity. Those possibilities explain why the code alone does not select a replacement part. The source also contains inconsistent circuit labeling in its final continuity list, so pin-level work requires the matching wiring diagram. A technician must follow restraint precautions during seat access and verify the repaired climate function under the mode that originally produced the concern.
