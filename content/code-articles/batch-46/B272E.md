# B272E: Ford explicitly qualifies the misleading blower-circuit label

B272E illustrates why a scanner's short description is not a complete diagnosis. Ford's Fusion page labels it as a driver blower circuit short to ground, then explicitly states that this monitor sets for an open circuit or a short to voltage. That qualification must remain attached to the code.

## Understand the shutdown pattern

The driver cushion and backrest blowers have common supply and return paths but independent speed controls. The source says an open affecting both blowers, or a voltage short, sets the fault and disables the seat outputs after four to six seconds. With module supply below 12 volts, an open affecting only one blower can also trigger it.

Consequently, two stopped blowers do not establish that two assemblies failed. Their shared electrical paths and the supply condition can explain the broader symptom.

## Use the specified component test correctly

Ford checks unwanted voltage and harness continuity before evaluating the individual blower assemblies. Its resistance test expects 4,000–10,000 ohms and requires the meter leads in the stated polarity; reversing them can produce misleading results. This is a procedure-specific electronic assembly test, not a general motor specification. The contradictory short title remains flagged for review, while the source's explicit detection explanation is preserved. Diagnosis also requires the restraint-system precautions associated with under-seat airbag connections.
