# B111B: finding an excessive load in the passenger seat TED circuits

The word Low in B111B does not mean that the passenger seat simply produces too little heat. Ford links this overcurrent code to a grounded passenger TED output loop or a detected TED resistance below 0.9 ohm. The climate-controlled seat module responds by shutting down the passenger seat system.

## Locate the affected load

The passenger cushion and backrest each contain a thermoelectric device. The diagnostic sequence checks their drive loops separately, which helps distinguish a cushion fault from a backrest fault instead of replacing both assemblies.

The first isolated checks look for a ground short and unintended voltage. Resistance is then compared across each TED-and-harness loop. A result outside the specified 0.9-to-10-ohm diagnostic range leads to a component-level measurement that separates the device from its connecting wires.

## A disappearing short identifies the next branch

For a grounded loop, Ford disconnects the relevant TED and repeats the isolation check. If the ground condition remains in the harness, wiring repair is indicated; if it disappears, the device becomes the target of that branch. This is more useful evidence than the owner's impression of how warm the seat felt.

The procedure requires restraint-system precautions around the passenger seat airbag and careful module-terminal probing. Once the identified fault is repaired, restore the safety system, repeat the seat-module self-test and verify operation. The driver seat working normally does not rule out this passenger-specific output fault.
