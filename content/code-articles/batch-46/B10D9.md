# B10D9: a missing transceiver message can be a power or wiring fault

The Fusion's instrument cluster needs a message from the PATS transceiver before it can complete starting authorization. B10D9:87 records the missing-message condition. Ford lists supply wiring, communication circuits and even an incorrect transceiver part number among the possible explanations.

## Establish power before judging the message

The cited configuration feeds the transceiver through SJB fuse 36, rated at 5 amperes. Its first test expects more than 10 volts at the specified supply terminal, then examines the ground path. A transceiver without a sound power connection cannot provide a meaningful communication test, even if its antenna and the key are otherwise suitable.

The remaining sequence distinguishes receive and transmit circuits, checking unwanted grounding, opens and inappropriate voltage. It includes a scan-tool-controlled transmit command; passive voltage readings and commanded readings are not interchangeable.

## Replacement operations are not all equivalent

Ford notes that transceiver replacement alone does not require relearning the keys into the cluster. Replacing the cluster follows a different endpoint involving PATS parameter reset and key programming. That difference is one reason to preserve the complete test evidence before ordering either component. The procedure also specifies appropriate back-probing tools to avoid creating terminal faults during diagnosis. A technician should verify the actual circuit and part number rather than treating every anti-theft no-start as a defective key.
