# B11D6: the mirror warning light has its own blind-spot fault circuit

An exterior mirror warning that stays lit does not necessarily mean the blind-spot system continuously detects a vehicle. Ford B11D6 concerns the warning LED circuit itself. In the Fusion procedure, :11 indicates grounding of its supply, while :15 covers an open circuit or a short to battery voltage.

## Separate the warning from the detection system

The side-obstacle modules normally illuminate the mirror indicators for three seconds during their ignition-cycle check. A fault instead produces a system message. The diagnostic route depends on the affected side and which module reported the code; the left and right modules have separate connections.

For a ground-short code, Ford progressively isolates the LED and mirror assembly to distinguish a component fault from wiring farther upstream. The manual notes that unplugging the LED can create B11D6:15 during this test. That expected induced code should not be confused with a second original failure.

## Check the entire indicator path

The open-circuit branch includes the LED, its supply and its ground, rather than assuming that the radar module is defective. Connector checks precede the final module-replacement decision. Record whether the light never illuminates, remains on beyond its initial check or fails intermittently. Those observations choose different branches. Until the warning function is restored, use mirrors and direct observation rather than relying on the affected blind-spot indication.
