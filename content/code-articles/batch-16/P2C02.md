# P2C02: An open fuel-quantity control circuit in the regulator D path

P2C02 identifies an open-circuit diagnosis for the fuel-quantity control valve in this GM fuel-pressure system. The source describes low driver feedback during the off state as the monitored electrical observation. That is different from a rail-pressure performance code, even though both concern fuel regulation.

## What the summary supports

The row lists twenty seconds and a one-trip response, along with battery, engine-speed and general diagnostic conditions. It also describes the open-circuit check as occurring when high current flows through the driver. That wording does not sit comfortably with the stated open-load/off-state observation, and the conflict is not resolved by assuming a particular circuit design.

Accordingly, the article does not convert the row into a connector resistance test or prescribe energizing the valve. The safe test method and the meaning of the driver state must come from the correct application documentation.

## Evidence worth retaining

Record whether the controller reports an electrical open state and whether rail-pressure performance faults are also present. Pressure failing to reach target is a downstream result; it cannot by itself prove that the command circuit is open. Similarly, a currently plausible pressure does not exclude an intermittent electrical interruption.

Confirmation should establish that the relevant circuit diagnostic ran after the repair, not merely that a stored code was erased. Keep the documented time qualification distinct from the neighbouring entries that count events. Any fuel-system access or powered electrical test requires the prescribed professional procedure because this summary supplies neither pin assignments nor isolation instructions.
