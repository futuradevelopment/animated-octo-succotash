# P2866: When rod 5 feedback stays high instead of pulsing

The P2866 monitor checks the high side of rod 5's digital position feedback. A high circuit report does not, by itself, mean that the shift fork has travelled beyond its mechanical limit. The transmission controller first judges whether the incoming electrical signal is usable.

## High level versus high duty cycle

The source identifies a no-pulse condition with pin voltage above 3,500 mV. It also identifies an input duty cycle above 92 percent. A stationary high input and a pulse train dominated by its high portion are therefore separate routes to this code. The failure confirmation is at least 150 milliseconds.

Interpreting a captured signal requires preserving which route failed. A single averaged voltage can hide a duty-cycle problem, while a position value substituted by the controller may conceal loss of the original pulses. These are reasons to compare the signal-validity information with the reported position rather than infer a jammed fork from the code name.

## Conditions that affect confirmation

The monitor excludes cranking and the key-stop state and lists battery/reference-supply faults as disabling conditions. Its VREF2 comparison entries appear contradictory, so their numerical values are not presented here as a workshop specification.

A meaningful follow-up confirms that pulse validity returns while the relevant supply diagnostics permit the monitor to operate. The table does not identify a replacement part or authorize internal transmission work. Matching vehicle service information is necessary before electrical access or component decisions.
