# C0596: Booster current-sensor self-tests inject known offsets

C0596 concerns the brake-booster current-sensing circuit's range and performance. The cited power-up tests deliberately inject known offset conditions and compare the sensor response with its zero-offset reading. This is a measurement-chain self-test, not simply a judgement about how much current the motor consumes during braking.

## Three controlled comparisons

The source includes common-mode, negative-offset and positive-offset checks. Each compares the resulting sampled current or offset difference with its permitted range. The limits are given as calibration symbols rather than complete numerical specifications, so this summary does not invent an external voltage or current test.

The tests require battery voltage between 7.1 and 18 V and motor rotation below the specified near-stationary limit. The maturation is listed as one count at a one-millisecond interval. These conditions distinguish the power-up test from a continuous load-current observation.

## What the result means for diagnosis

Retain which injected-offset check failed and whether the supply and motor-state prerequisites were satisfied. A normal current reading later under load does not independently demonstrate that the measurement chain responded correctly to the controlled startup test. Conversely, the code alone does not identify a replacement motor or driver ASIC.

Testing internal current-sense circuitry requires the exact professional service procedure; do not inject offsets or external signals manually. Verification should establish successful power-up diagnostic completion and the required brake-system checks. Clearing the code without another valid startup test cannot demonstrate recovery of the current measurement used by the controller.
