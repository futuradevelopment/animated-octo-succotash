# P20E8: When DEF pressure fails to follow the commanded setpoint

P20E8 evaluates a reductant pressure shortfall while the documented GM system is trying to control pressure. It compares the desired setpoint with the pressure-control signal, rather than treating every low absolute reading as a failure.

## The demand and operating state matter

The listed criterion is a setpoint-minus-measured-pressure difference above 63 kPa. Monitoring is associated with buildup, closed-loop control or reductant delivery performance states, and estimated DEF quantity must exceed three litres.

The source uses a thirty-second failure accumulation with a shorter success count. A momentary deviation during a state transition is therefore different from the persistent eligible shortfall described by the monitor. A low reading while the pump is deliberately off does not reproduce the test.

## Establish that pressure information is usable

Pressure-sensor faults, pump faults, supply problems and communication conditions can stop the pump and disable this assessment. These exclusions prevent a known invalid input or disabled pumping state from being interpreted as a straightforward pressure-control failure.

Useful evidence includes setpoint, pressure signal, pump state, estimated quantity and the fault sequence. That combination distinguishes missing demand, inhibited operation and a genuine failure to follow demand. The monitor does not uniquely identify a weak pump, leakage or any one restriction. Locating the cause requires the applicable system procedure, and the pressure sensor's validity should be established before physical pressure conclusions are drawn from its value.
