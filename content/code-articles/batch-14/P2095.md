# P2095: When the bank 2 exhaust cam actuator driver fails its on-state check

P2095 describes a high-circuit fault in the bank 2 exhaust camshaft actuator control path for this GM monitor. The generic actuator letter is “B”; the source supplies the exhaust designation for the documented implementation.

## A high response during activation is the fault pattern

The table evaluates the low-side driver while it is on. Voltage that remains high in that state is categorized as a short to power. The listed failure period is one second, subject to ignition, ECU drive-state, supply and other eligibility requirements.

The command-state detail separates this check from a low-circuit diagnosis made while an output is off. Seeing voltage in an inactive circuit is not enough to reproduce the P2095 condition. The controller's own diagnostic status and command provide the relevant context.

## The code narrows the electrical problem, not the replacement

This row contains no requested-versus-actual cam-angle comparison. It therefore cannot establish on its own that the exhaust camshaft is mechanically stuck or that the engine's timing arrangement is wrong. Those would require their own evidence.

For verification, retain the bank/actuator identity, the electrical category and the conditions under which the driver was activated. The applicable wiring information is needed to investigate an unwanted supply connection and distinguish it from driver or load behavior. No universal connector location, resistance test or part replacement follows from this monitor summary. A later normal cam-angle observation also does not independently prove that an intermittent electrical event has been resolved.
