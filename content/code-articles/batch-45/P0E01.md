# P0E01: Toyota generator phase U current-sensor offset fault

## Current is reported when none should flow

Toyota maps P0E0028 to SAE P0E01. The generator inverter's **U-phase current sensor has an excessive offset**: the absolute sensor output exceeds a threshold when current should not be flowing, such as during inverter shutdown. Toyota specifically describes this family as current-sensor malfunctions rather than proof of a high-voltage-system malfunction.

The motor generator control ECU is incorporated into the inverter with converter assembly. Possible explanations include a current-sensor or internal inverter-circuit fault. The page also includes the harness or connector in its trouble-area table; it does not authorize diagnosing a failed generator winding from the code alone.

## Why the other faults come first

This continuously monitored fault uses one-trip detection and commands the MIL and master warning. Toyota does not disclose the numerical offset threshold or detection duration.

Its priority tables address isolation faults before controller, communication, reference-power, resolver, VH-sensor and related inverter faults. The actual inspection then checks the inverter connector. An insecure connector is corrected, damaged or contaminated contacts require harness/connector repair, and a normal connection leads to inverter assembly replacement. There is no separate phase-U sensor replacement option in this procedure.

## Demonstrating a repair

Toyota's confirmation includes ignition-on and READY observations followed by Motor Generator All Readiness. A completed NORMAL result matters more than simply clearing the code; INCOMPLETE requires another valid judgment sequence. The zero-current comparison describes how the monitor works, not permission to assume an inverter is safe to touch. Professional isolation and high-voltage precautions still apply to every physical inspection or assembly replacement.
