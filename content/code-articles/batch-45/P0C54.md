# P0C54: Toyota motor resolver A intermittent open indication

## This intermittent code has a defined context

On the cited Corolla HV, SAE P0C54 maps to Toyota P0C501F. It means the motor generator control ECU detected an **open in the motor resolver sine-phase circuit** while one of a specified group of other electrical faults was stored. Toyota names P0C7917, P0E5717, P0D3319, P1C5D19, P1C5F19 and P1C5E19 as those qualifying companions.

This is drive motor A, MG2, and resolver circuit A is its sine signal. The ordinary open-circuit variant P0C5013 maps instead to P0C50. Preserving the full suffix and companion codes is therefore more informative than describing P0C54 simply as a loose position sensor.

## The investigation follows the electrical evidence

The P0C501F detection row uses one-trip logic but does not itself command the MIL or master warning. Other stored faults can still cause warnings. Toyota's shared overview contains broader MIL wording; the individual row is the relevant indication for this particular variant.

Potential areas include the inverter, transaxle resolver and connecting harness. Toyota prioritizes isolation faults, then relevant controller and reference-power faults. The procedure examines the inverter connector, correcting an insecure connection or damaged/contaminated terminals as appropriate, and then calls for the motor resolver circuit inspection. Inverter replacement follows only when those inspections are normal.

## Confirmation is more than clearing memory

Toyota warns that vibration or heat used during diagnosis can itself cross a detection boundary. Reproduction must therefore be interpreted with the test conditions recorded. Its final confirmation includes operating conditions and All Readiness judgment, performed professionally where vehicle movement is safe. An incomplete judgment does not establish a cure. High-voltage precautions apply even though the reported signal is a resolver circuit.
