# P2647: Honda pressure-switch feedback remains off with VTEC released

## The switch state is wrong for the released command

Honda's P2647 extract identifies **rocker-arm oil-pressure switch circuit high voltage**. With the VTEC solenoid commanded off, the pressure switch should turn on. The fault is detected when that expected switch state does not appear.

This explains the relationship to the catalog's rocker-arm actuator stuck-on description: the Honda monitor evaluates pressure-switch feedback rather than directly watching a mechanical actuator. Its advanced page specifies at least sixteen seconds at idle with auxiliary voltage of at least 10.5 V, using two-drive-cycle MIL logic.

## The chart does not begin with an actuator replacement

P2648 and P2649 are addressed first if also stored. Honda then checks oil level and runs the HDS VTEC test. A normal result leads toward intermittent connections or the recorded operating circumstances. A failed result leads to the pressure-switch circuit checks.

The troubleshooting procedure checks switch continuity in its specified disconnected, power-off state. No continuity in that branch supports switch replacement. When the switch passes, the chart investigates an open VTM signal circuit and an open LG ground circuit. A proven open is repaired; normal wiring leads to authorized service-information review or a conditional known-good PCM test.

## Keep the feedback diagnosis specific

Record oil level, the original snapshot and the exact VTEC-test result before clearing. Neither the word “high” nor the generic stuck-on label independently proves that a valve is mechanically jammed. The source's switch and wiring branches can account for the reported feedback.

A technician should follow the VIN-specific procedure for these tests, including connector handling and any software or PCM substitution. The individual Insight extracts do not state an exact model-year revision.
