# P0C63: Toyota rear resolver cosine intermittent indication

## An intermittent rear position-signal fault

Toyota maps P0C5F1F to SAE P0C63 on the documented Corolla HV. It detects an **open in the rear-motor resolver cosine-phase circuit** when one of six companion codes is stored: P0C7917, P0E5717, P0D3319, P1C5D19, P1C5F19 or P1C5E19. The companion context is an explicit part of this variant's detection rule.

The rear traction motor is MGR, designated drive motor B. Its cosine channel is resolver circuit B. The corresponding ordinary open code uses suffix 13 and maps to P0C5F. The two share a factory procedure, while their detection context and warning indications differ.

## What the shared procedure actually decides

P0C5F1F has one-trip detection but does not itself command the MIL or master warning in its specific row. Warning lamps can still be present because of the associated faults. Exact electrical limits are not published.

Toyota prioritizes insulation issues, then relevant control, communication and reference-supply faults. It next checks the inverter connector and the rear-motor resolver circuit. An improperly engaged connector is corrected; damaged or contaminated contacts require harness/connector repair. A normal resolver-circuit result takes the flow to inverter with converter assembly replacement. The code alone is not a rear-motor replacement verdict.

## Capturing a meaningful result

Keep the initial combined fault record and conditions of occurrence. Toyota warns that diagnostic heat or vibration can itself influence detection, so a reproduced code needs context. Its professional operating sequence and All Readiness judgment establish whether the monitor passes; an INCOMPLETE result requires further qualifying operation. All rear-motor and inverter work must follow the separate high-voltage precautions.
