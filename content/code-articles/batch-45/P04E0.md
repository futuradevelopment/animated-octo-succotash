# P04E0: Honda purge-valve closure detected during a long soak

## Pressure recovery is the evidence

Honda defines P04E0 as an **EVAP canister purge valve stuck closed**. The leak-check module first reduces system pressure. Opening the purge valve should then allow pressure to move back toward atmospheric pressure through the intake side. The monitor flags insufficient recovery, rather than directly observing the valve move.

In the cited Insight diagnostic extract, the pressure change toward the positive direction is **0.4 kPa or less for at least 19 minutes 10 seconds** after the purge valve opens. That prolonged comparison distinguishes this monitor from a brief electrical continuity check.

## The shutdown conditions matter

The enabled sequence includes at least ten minutes since the vehicle was switched on, at least five hours after shutdown, and coolant between 5°C and 35°C when the PCM wakes. The table also specifies adequate barometric pressure and auxiliary-battery voltage, with no refueling detected. A quick restart in a workshop may therefore fail to reproduce the original test conditions.

Honda uses two-drive-cycle MIL logic and identifies a stuck-closed purge valve as a possible cause. Its confirmation offers the applicable HDS purge-flow check or the prescribed delayed monitoring sequence. The available document is an advanced monitor description, not a complete parts-isolation repair chart.

## Use the result within its limits

Preserve the original pressure data and pending or confirmed status before clearing. Lack of pressure recovery supports a purge-path investigation; this extract alone does not justify replacing a valve without the applicable troubleshooting procedure. Verify the VIN-specific Honda information before applying the thresholds. Fuel-vapor testing and any commanded valve operation belong in a suitable professional service setting.
