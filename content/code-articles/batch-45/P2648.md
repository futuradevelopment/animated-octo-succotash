# P2648: Honda detects a low VTEC solenoid return signal

## The electrical return remains low

Honda defines P2648 as a **rocker-arm oil-control solenoid circuit low-voltage** fault. The PCM commands the VTEC output high but sees its return remain low for at least **two seconds**. The monitor runs continuously with auxiliary voltage of at least 10 V and uses one-drive-cycle MIL logic.

This is a command-circuit diagnosis. It should not be treated as the same pressure-switch feedback test used by P2646 or P2647. Honda lists a grounded VTS circuit, a solenoid fault and a PCM problem as possible causes.

## Resistance and wiring lead to different repairs

The troubleshooting procedure first attempts to reproduce the problem with the warmed-engine HDS VTEC test. A normal result redirects attention to intermittent connections and the original snapshot.

If the failure is reproduced, the chart checks the disconnected solenoid's resistance with the vehicle off. The stated room-temperature range is **13.9–16.9 ohms**. Resistance outside that range supports the oil-control-valve replacement branch. Passing resistance instead leads to a check for a VTS wire short to ground.

A confirmed grounded wire is repaired. Only after the relevant electrical checks pass does the chart reach authorized service-information review or a known-good PCM substitution, with replacement dependent on the result.

## Preserve the distinction between command and pressure

Record the commanded test, electrical result and any companion codes. A generic actuator description does not establish low engine-oil pressure, and a low return voltage does not establish a failed PCM. The separate resistance and wiring results matter.

Use VIN-specific Honda information for testing and component selection. Connector measurements and module substitution should be performed by a qualified technician under the manufacturer's electrical-service precautions.
