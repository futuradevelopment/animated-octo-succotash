# P0A47: low rear-motor resolver amplitude in Toyota's hybrid system

For this Corolla HV configuration, P0A47 is tied to Toyota P0A4521. The rear-motor resolver signal is smaller than its expected amplitude range. Toyota describes an open or short in the rear position-sensor circuit; the code is not a measurement of low traction-battery capacity or rear-motor torque.

## Which components remain possible

The detection table lists the inverter with converter assembly, rear traction motor with transaxle assembly, and wiring or connectors. A low-amplitude result can therefore originate in the feedback circuit or the electronics interpreting it. One qualifying trip illuminates the MIL and master warning, but the source does not disclose the numeric signal boundary or time requirement.

The source separately lists P0A4522 for excessive amplitude. Both entries share the rear-resolver diagnostic procedure. The low versus high distinction is meaningful evidence, but it is not a factory instruction to choose different replacement assemblies without inspection.

## Check prior faults before pursuing the rear circuit

Toyota gives priority to applicable insulation faults, then processing, communication and reference-power problems. The rear-motor reference-supply entries in that context include P06B31C and P19F91C. Resolving such faults can change how the resolver result should be interpreted.

The subsequent flow checks the inverter connection and the dedicated rear-motor resolver circuit. If those circuit inspections are normal, Toyota proceeds to inverter replacement; otherwise the identified connection or circuit issue determines the repair path.

The manual also warns that some diagnostic connector manipulations can create a transient detection event. A professional should distinguish those events from the original complaint and complete the prescribed All Readiness verification after repair, following all hybrid-system safety precautions.
