# P0D13: Prius plug-in negative charge-contactor circuit low

## CHRG is the negative charging contactor

Toyota maps P0D1111 to SAE P0D13 in the cited Prius plug-in hybrid battery procedure. The battery ECU detects a **short to ground in the negative charging-contactor control circuit**, designated CHRG. This is the primary relay-control path, rather than a direct finding that the high-voltage contact is mechanically stuck.

The charge relays are housed in the No. 1 traction battery device box assembly. Toyota lists that assembly, the battery ECU and the harness/connectors as possible areas. The code uses one-trip logic and commands both the MIL and master warning, but its exact monitor threshold and duration are withheld.

## What the factory checks establish

The first step checks battery-ECU connector engagement. With that connection sound, the specified isolated CHRG circuit test expects 20.6–40.8 Ω. A normal circuit result takes the flow to battery-ECU replacement; an abnormal result continues to the device-box connector and harness isolation checks.

A defective harness is repaired. If the required harness check is normal, the device-box assembly becomes the indicated replacement. These branches explain why a generic negative-contactor description is insufficient to order a relay assembly or ECU without testing. The resistance value belongs to the exact factory setup, not an owner test at exposed battery terminals.

## Confirming the charging monitor

Toyota requires a qualifying plug-in charge with battery SOC no higher than 70%, then an HV Battery All Readiness judgment. NORMAL confirms the monitor passed; INCOMPLETE or N/A needs another qualifying sequence. Preserve the detailed code and charging conditions before clearing. The available source is a Prius XW60 manual mirror with revision/VIN applicability still to be matched, and all battery-enclosure work requires professional high-voltage precautions.
