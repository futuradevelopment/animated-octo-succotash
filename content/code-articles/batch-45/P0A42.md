# P0A42: excessive resolver-signal amplitude is not motor overspeed

Toyota identifies P0A42 as the SAE equivalent of P0A3F22 in the documented Corolla HV system. Its defining observation is an excessively large motor-resolver signal amplitude. This electrical position-feedback finding does not, by itself, establish that the traction motor has exceeded a mechanical speed limit.

## An amplitude boundary with several possible origins

The detailed detection row describes an open or short in the MG2 resolver circuit that puts its signal outside the standard range. Possible areas include the harness, its connections, the motor within the hybrid transaxle, and the inverter's control electronics. The source supplies no numerical amplitude boundary that could be turned into a general workshop test specification.

One qualifying trip requests both the MIL and master warning. Toyota groups P0A3F22 with the opposite-direction P0A3F21 fault for diagnosis, while preserving the separate high and low SAE mappings. A scan result should retain that direction instead of collapsing both into a generic position-sensor malfunction.

## Avoid a replacement decision based on the label alone

The source's preliminary priority tables include insulation, processing and reference-power problems that can generate resolver faults. The dedicated flow then checks the inverter connection for security, terminal contact and contamination before proceeding through the motor-resolver circuit inspection. A normal resolver inspection is the condition for the subsequent inverter-replacement route.

Toyota notes that connector vibration or heat checks may cross a detection boundary during diagnosis. Such a test-related code must be assessed in context rather than automatically counted as proof of a defective replacement part. The final professional check uses the prescribed operating pattern and All Readiness status, with the complete high-voltage precautions applying to any component work.
