# P2646: Honda VTEC pressure feedback stays on when commanded

## The pressure switch disagrees with the command

The catalog describes P2646 as rocker-arm actuator performance or a stuck-off condition. Honda's Insight documents identify the relevant detection as **rocker-arm oil-pressure switch circuit low voltage**: when the VTEC solenoid is commanded on, the pressure switch should turn off, but remains on.

The monitor requires at least sixteen seconds under its stated running conditions and uses two-drive-cycle MIL logic. This feedback result differs from a direct test of the solenoid's electrical command circuit.

## Oil and companion electrical codes come first

Honda gives P2648 and P2649 priority if they are present. It then checks engine-oil level and runs the HDS VTEC test. A normal test points toward an intermittent event or connection issue. A failed test opens further branches for the switch, wiring and hydraulic operation.

The documented hydraulic branch compares pressure with **392 kPa, or 56.9 psi**. Adequate pressure directs attention to the pressure switch; inadequate pressure leads to inspection of the VTEC system before the oil-control-valve replacement branch. Other parts of the chart investigate a grounded VTM signal and conditionally evaluate the PCM.

## The code does not select a valve by itself

A pressure-feedback fault can require different work depending on whether hydraulic pressure is present and whether the signal wire is sound. Preserve the original snapshot, oil-level finding, companion codes and VTEC-test result so those distinctions are retained.

The pressure test and any PCM substitution belong to the applicable professional procedure. Verify the exact vehicle before using the extract's values. Replacing the oil-control valve solely because the catalog says “actuator” would skip the switch and pressure evidence that Honda uses.
