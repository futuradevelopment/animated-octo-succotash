# P0A01: A cold-soak comparison tests electronics-coolant temperature plausibility

P0A01 checks whether the power-electronics coolant temperature is believable after a long engine-off period. The cited GM cold-start strategy compares that reading with engine coolant and intake-air temperatures. It does not simply ask whether the electronics coolant is hot.

## The reference temperatures must agree first

The example requires engine-off time greater than 28,800 seconds, or eight hours. Failure then uses electronics-coolant versus engine-coolant disagreement above 30°C while engine coolant and intake air differ by no more than 15.75°C. The agreement of the other two measurements gives context to the outlying reading.

The pass relationship is different: electronics coolant must be within 15.75°C of both engine coolant and intake air. A reading between the failure and pass boundaries is therefore not automatically proof of a completed passing test.

## Preserve the actual cold-start evidence

Record the three startup temperatures and the verified engine-off interval. Measurements taken after warmup cannot recreate the original thermal comparison. The row also excludes several intake-air, engine-coolant, airflow and electronics-coolant circuit faults, so companion codes matter to eligibility.

The page describes a plausibility monitor, not a coolant-fill instruction or proof of a failed sensor. Temperature disagreement must be investigated with the matching cooling-system and sensor information. Access around hybrid power electronics requires the appropriate professional isolation procedure, and an apparently ordinary coolant task should not bypass those requirements.
