# P0B43: Block-two high voltage is evaluated without the paired low condition

P0B43 monitors a block-two voltage indication above 23V in this GM implementation. Its high-limit row evaluates block two directly. That differs from the neighboring P0B42 low-limit row, which requires both block two and block three to be low.

## Do not import the opposite fault’s logic

A high block-three reading is not listed as a prerequisite for P0B43. The qualifying conditions are controller run mode, suitable low-voltage supply and no P0A1F controller-performance fault. Failure is counted through 15 qualifying samples out of 20 at 100ms intervals.

The code therefore reports a specific channel's upper signal limit. It does not establish that the whole battery was overcharged or that another block must share the same indication.

## Preserve the channel rather than only a maximum

Save block-two voltage and its identity, the independent pack reading and any rationality or neighboring-channel faults. A “maximum block voltage” display without the channel number can lose the evidence needed to follow the correct diagnostic branch.

The document does not identify the physical block position, sensing-wire arrangement or required replacement. Those are vehicle-specific service questions. A trained battery technician must use the applicable diagram and approved tests, with high-voltage isolation before access. Do not deliberately raise battery voltage to reproduce the code or rely on a scanner to verify electrical safety.

Keep historical and current fault status distinct in that record.
