# P0A02: Low circuit indication corresponds to an implausibly hot coolant reading

In this GM electronics-coolant sensor circuit, P0A02 is the low-circuit indication. The table represents it as sensor resistance below 25Ω, corresponding to an indicated temperature of at least 162°C. Thus “low” refers to the electrical behavior, not to cold coolant.

## Supporting conditions narrow the test

The row requires no active intake-air circuit-low or circuit-high codes. It also specifies a minimum intake-air temperature below 70°C and propulsion-active time greater than ten seconds. The failure decision is 30 failing samples within 50, sampled every 100ms.

Those conditions distinguish this monitor from simply observing a hot value on a scanner. Preserve the electronics-coolant reading together with intake-air validity and the propulsion timeline to see whether the documented comparison was eligible.

## A resistance threshold is not a probing instruction

The printed resistance is a calibration description, not a direction to measure an energized circuit or disconnect a sensor beside live high-voltage equipment. The correct vehicle procedure determines whether and where a safe measurement is permitted.

P0A03 is the opposite electrical extreme on the same page, while P0A01 checks temperature plausibility after a soak. Keeping those categories separate helps avoid declaring an overheating event solely from an implausible sensor signal. The document does not identify a particular failed sensor, harness location or repair; those require circuit-specific diagnosis by a qualified technician.
