# P2B61: Coolant valve needs high drive but does not reach its position

P2B61 is cataloged as a coolant flow control valve stuck closed. Mazda's SKYACTIV-G diagnostic describes coolant control valve circuit range/performance and monitors whether strong actuator demand produces movement.

## Read command and feedback together

The listed trigger is a control duty of at least 89% for two continuous seconds. The functional explanation says the valve fails to operate despite maximum applied control. This can indicate sticking or foreign material, but incorrect position feedback or a circuit defect can produce a similar result. The page does not independently establish the physically closed position named by the catalog.

Mazda lists separate valve-control and position-sensor circuits, with open, grounded or cross-connected paths among the possible causes. Its live-data checks use ENG_COOL_VLV_POS_COMD and ENG_COOL_VLV_POS_ACT. A commanded angle alone is not proof that coolant routing has changed; the actual-angle signal must also be credible.

## Mechanical and electrical branches

The procedure checks valve installation, connector behavior and circuit integrity before inspecting the valve and its position sensor. That order distinguishes a misinstalled or sticking valve from a misleading electrical signal. The listed protective actions restrict engine torque and inhibit i-stop where fitted.

Repeatability uses a warm engine, with coolant between 50 and 95 °C and a defined command-versus-actual relationship, followed by a drive matching the snapshot. These conditions explain why a brief cold-idle check may be inconclusive. Coolant-system work requires the appropriate hot-fluid precautions and model-specific service procedure.
