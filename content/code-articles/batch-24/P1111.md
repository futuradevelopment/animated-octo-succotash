# P1111: Volkswagen lean oxygen-control fault: preserve the fuel-trim evidence

P1111, also identified as 17519 in Ross-Tech's VAG reference, concerns oxygen-control feedback that is too lean on bank 1. The sensor named in that description is reporting the mixture-control problem; the code alone does not establish that the oxygen sensor has failed.

## Record the correction before resetting it

Ross-Tech specifically recommends examining oxygen-sensor adaptation before clearing faults. Its fuel-trim guide distinguishes learned additive correction at idle from multiplicative correction across engine speeds. Those observations can give an investigation direction that would be lost after an indiscriminate reset. Confirm the measuring-value labels for the actual controller instead of assuming one block number works on every Volkswagen.

## Separate air, fuel and feedback paths

The P1111 entry identifies intake leakage, insufficient fuel delivery, injector faults, airflow measurement, exhaust leakage and oxygen sensing as possible contributors. These are alternative explanations, not a list of parts to replace together.

An intake leak and an inaccurate airflow reading can both misrepresent the air entering the engine. Fuel-pump, filter or injector problems concern delivery instead. Exhaust leakage and sensor faults affect the feedback used to judge that mixture. A useful repair record therefore pairs the relevant leak, delivery or signal finding with the original trim pattern, then checks whether the correction settles after the identified defect is addressed.
