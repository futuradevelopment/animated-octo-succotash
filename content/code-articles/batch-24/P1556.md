# P1556: Volkswagen negative boost deviation and the under-delivery investigation

Ross-Tech identifies Volkswagen P1556/17964 as a negative deviation in charge-pressure control, with boost too low in its cause list. Reduced output or limp mode may accompany it. This direction matters: it is not the same reported outcome as a positive-deviation or upper-limit complaint.

## Separate loss of pressure from loss of control

The entry lists incorrectly connected, disconnected or leaking hoses and pipes. It also identifies the N75 control solenoid and a defective charge-pressure control mechanism, including stuck nozzles on applicable variable-nozzle designs. A leak and a control-mechanism problem can therefore require different repairs despite producing a similar power complaint.

Identify the installed turbo-control system before applying the nozzle-specific note. The source is not a model-by-model fitment list and does not prove that every P1556 engine uses variable geometry.

## A useful diagnosis needs more than restored power

Ross-Tech recommends checking the connecting pipework, N75 and the relevant mechanism. The entry does not publish a common pressure target, vacuum specification or actuator travel. Use the actual engine's data and test procedure to establish the failing path.

After correction, verify the pressure-control result and the original complaint under appropriate conditions. Power returning after clearing fault memory can reflect changed control behavior without proving that the underlying under-delivery problem has been repaired.
