# P1634: Volkswagen G185 pedal signal high and the wiring alternatives

P1634/18042/005684 identifies an excessive signal from accelerator-pedal sensor 2, G185, in Ross-Tech's VAG reference. The source points to the pedal assembly and connections between it and the engine controller, rather than the throttle body. Reduced engine output and a warning lamp are possible symptoms.

## A named pedal sensor is not proof of pedal failure

Wiring or connection defects and a faulty sensor are listed possibilities. The special note also identifies oxygen-sensor circuitry as a possible contributor to pedal-signal faults. That is an additional electrical lead to assess on a relevant system, not proof that an oxygen sensor needs replacement.

The page does not establish a measured failure rate or a universal signal threshold. Its qualitative claim about pedal reliability is not used here to assign a diagnosis probability.

## Confirm the correct channel relationship

This P1634 page describes opposite-direction pedal channels, while Ross-Tech's P1639 page describes channels moving in the same direction on different scales. Those generalized accounts conflict. Use the actual controller's specification and measured-value labels before declaring a channel implausible.

Record the G185 signal condition separately from supply, connection and related-circuit findings. The repair should follow the demonstrated defect, not a guessed two-channel relationship or the assumption that all accelerator-position codes require a new pedal.
