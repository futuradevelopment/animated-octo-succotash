# P1602: Volkswagen Terminal 30 low voltage: compare the modules reporting it

Volkswagen P1602/18010/005634 reports low voltage on Terminal 30, the unswitched battery supply in Ross-Tech's explanation. “Terminal 30” is a circuit designation, not an instruction to find a connector cavity physically numbered 30. That distinction prevents an easily avoidable wiring mistake.

## Start with the event history

Ross-Tech lists disconnected battery cables, weak battery voltage and a charging-system malfunction as possible causes. A stored entry after battery work has a different context from an unexplained current supply problem. Record that history before assuming the battery or alternator has failed.

The source recommends checking battery cables, battery condition and the charging system. It does not provide a universal voltage threshold or declare that one component must be replaced whenever P1602 appears.

## The pattern across controllers is useful evidence

The special note distinguishes many modules storing supply faults from only one or two modules doing so. A widespread pattern points the investigation toward a common supply problem; a localized pattern warrants attention to those modules' individual supply paths. This is a diagnostic prioritization, not a guarantee of cause.

Keep a complete scan record and compare the relevant supply findings with the vehicle's history. A local feed defect should not be dismissed merely because battery voltage at the main terminals appears acceptable.
