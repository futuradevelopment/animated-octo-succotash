# P1560: Volkswagen recorded engine overspeed: preserve the event before clearing

P1560/17968/005472 means the engine's maximum-speed limit was exceeded in Ross-Tech's Volkswagen/VAG reference. The source warns that such an event can cause serious damage and lists abnormal engine noise as a possible symptom. The code does not prove that damage has occurred, but it should not be dismissed merely by erasing it.

## Establish what preceded the event

Ross-Tech lists a removed rev limiter or altered engine software, an incorrect shift that drives engine speed too high, and a stuck throttle as possible explanations. These are different event histories, not facts established by the code. Record the circumstances and any available diagnostic information before resetting memory.

The entry supplies no universal RPM limit or duration threshold. The installed engine and controller determine the applicable specification.

## Treat the vehicle condition seriously

A car with abnormal mechanical noise or a throttle-control problem needs qualified assessment before further operation. Clearing the code cannot repair mechanical damage or demonstrate that the throttle is functioning correctly. This is a practical diagnostic boundary, not a claim that P1560 alone proves the engine is unusable.

The stored entry does not establish warranty coverage or a particular failed part. A defensible conclusion separates the recorded overspeed event, its demonstrated cause and the engine's assessed condition. Those are distinct questions for the appropriate vehicle inspection.
