# P1631: Volkswagen G79 pedal signal high: verify the controller-specific relationship

P1631/18039 refers to accelerator-position sensor G79 with a signal too high in Ross-Tech's VAG reference. Reduced engine output and a warning lamp are possible symptoms. The source directs attention to pedal readings, wiring between the engine controller and pedal, and the supply signals specified in the repair manual.

## The pedal and its external circuit are different possibilities

Ross-Tech names wiring or connection faults, engine-controller failure and a defective pedal assembly. It additionally notes that oxygen-sensor circuitry can contribute to pedal-sensor faults. That is a reason to investigate relevant electrical evidence, not an instruction to replace an oxygen sensor whenever P1631 appears.

## Do not assume a universal two-channel pattern

This page describes the pedal channels moving in opposite directions, while Ross-Tech's P1639 page describes channels increasing together on different scales. Those generalized descriptions conflict. Neither should be used as a universal pass/fail rule without identifying the actual controller and its expected signal relationship.

Use the proper measured-value labels and specifications to distinguish an elevated G79 signal from a supply, wiring or correlation problem. The source gives no single voltage threshold valid for all applications. A defensible repair follows that vehicle-specific evidence rather than a guessed pedal curve or the mere presence of the sensor's name in the fault.
