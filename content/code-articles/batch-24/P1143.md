# P1143: Volkswagen upper load-cross-check limit: distinguish pedal from throttle inputs

Volkswagen P1143, also 17551/004419 in Ross-Tech's reference, reports the upper limit of the load-calculation cross-check being exceeded. The entry concerns agreement among load-related information. It does not state that the engine has exceeded a universal safe load value.

## Follow the named signal paths

Ross-Tech directs attention to G70 airflow measurement, pedal-position sensors G79/G185, throttle-angle sensors G187/G188 and the engine controller. Keeping those names separate prevents a common diagnostic shortcut: treating a pedal-sensor reading as proof that the throttle body's own angle feedback is correct.

The page provides no voltage thresholds, correlation formula or engine-specific measuring-block numbers. Use the actual controller's labels and repair information to decide which comparison is valid. A number from a different engine can be misleading even when the component abbreviation looks familiar.

## What would justify the repair?

The useful outcome is evidence identifying the inconsistent input or control path, followed by confirmation that the disagreement is resolved. An airflow, pedal or throttle component should not be condemned solely because it appears in the diagnostic checklist.

The wiki also links to a discussion of modified turbocharged engines. That link is not proof of applicability to every turbo Volkswagen, nor evidence that a standard vehicle needs the modification discussed there. Keep modifications and the original controller configuration in the case record.
