# P1545: Volkswagen throttle malfunction: when contact resistance changes the repair

P1545 describes a throttle-control malfunction in the Volkswagen/VAG sources. Ross-Tech lists a dirty or defective throttle body, wiring faults and intake obstructions. The code alone therefore does not establish that the complete throttle unit needs replacement.

## A documented wiring fault can imitate a throttle failure

Volkswagen bulletin 2018652/6, dated March 9, 2017, identifies poor contact resistance at the throttle-control module on its listed applications. It includes certain 2006–2008 BPY 2.0L vehicles and a separate 2008–2009 CCTA/CBFA 2.0L row. The complete model and engine table must be checked, not inferred from the presence of P1545.

That bulletin describes harness/contact repair and subsequent function checks. Its later throttle-replacement instruction is conditional on faults recurring after the repair. This materially changes the first repair decision on a matching vehicle.

## Keep the Audi application list separate

Audi bulletin 2018260/8, dated September 26, 2014, documents a similar contact-resistance issue for its own listed combinations, including the 2008 TT 2.0L. It also restricts throttle replacement to faults recurring after the wiring repair. Its model/year/engine table is not interchangeable with Volkswagen's.

These historical documents do not establish current warranty coverage or universal part numbers. A technician should confirm the applicable procedure and current vehicle information, then verify operation and any required basic setting. Outside the documented cases, investigate the actual wiring, throttle and intake findings rather than assuming the bulletin's cause applies.
