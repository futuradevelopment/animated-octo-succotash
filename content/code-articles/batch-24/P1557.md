# P1557: Volkswagen positive boost deviation and the N75 control path

Volkswagen P1557/17965/005463 describes a positive deviation in charge-pressure control in Ross-Tech's reference. The listed condition is excessive boost, with possible reduced power or limp mode. A driver can therefore experience less power even though the reported control deviation is upward.

## Check the path that controls pressure

Ross-Tech names hoses or pipes that are connected incorrectly, disconnected, blocked or leaking, together with the N75 boost-control solenoid and charge-pressure control mechanism. These possibilities show why a reduced-power complaint should not automatically be treated as an air leak causing low boost.

For a variable-nozzle turbocharger, sticking nozzles are a documented mechanism to assess. Confirm that the engine actually uses that arrangement before applying the note; the code alone is not a turbo-design identifier.

## Retain the fault direction through verification

The source recommends examining the connecting pipework, N75 and the relevant control mechanism. It provides no universal boost threshold or actuator-test specification. The vehicle's procedure must establish what constitutes acceptable pressure control.

A supported repair identifies the routing, restriction, solenoid or mechanism defect and checks the resulting behavior afterward. A reset that temporarily removes limp mode is not independent evidence that the positive deviation is gone. Keep the original upward-deviation description in the repair record so the investigation follows the correct condition.
