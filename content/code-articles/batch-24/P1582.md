# P1582: Volkswagen idle-adaptation limit and the hidden air or purge paths

P1582/17990/005506 means the idle-adaptation limit has been reached in Ross-Tech's Volkswagen/VAG reference. The entry does not identify a single failed idle-control part. Its diagnostic list covers several ways the engine's idle conditions can be disturbed.

## Include purge and secondary-air leakage in the investigation

Ross-Tech recommends checking fuel pressure and injectors, intake and exhaust leakage, vacuum lines, the N80 purge valve, secondary-air leakage and the throttle body. This is broader than looking only for dirt around the throttle plate. A verified purge or leakage defect changes the repair path compared with a throttle-body problem.

The source does not publish an adaptation threshold or a universal idle-speed target. The actual engine data must establish which observation is abnormal.

## Cleaning and alignment are not the same operation

The special note says throttle removal and cleaning may be needed, followed by throttle-body alignment. Ross-Tech's alignment guide explains that applicable motorized throttles relearn their positions; correct electrical and operating prerequisites still matter.

For early AAA 2.8L VR6 and ABA 2.0L applications, the P1582 note says idle recovery can take time after cleaning. That historical observation is not proof that a continuing fault should always be ignored. Retain the engine-specific context and reassess any unresolved air, fuel or control finding instead of attributing every remaining idle problem to adaptation.
