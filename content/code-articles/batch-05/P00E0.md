# P00E0: charge-air-cooler coolant sensor A has low resistance

P00E0 applies to the charge-air-cooler inlet-water temperature sensor in this GM ECM section. Its raw resistance is below 70 Ω. The page names coolant sensor A; it is a different measured medium from the charge-air temperature sensor even though both descriptions mention the cooler.

## The prerequisite is electrical stability

The calibration must enable the diagnostic, the engine must not be cranking and run/crank-relay voltage must exceed 11 V. Failure requires 30 counts out of 38 samples in a 100-millisecond task. The source lists Type B, two-trip reporting.

The low-resistance result does not itself establish that the cooling liquid is cold, hot, low in quantity or circulating poorly. It identifies an input outside its electrical range. That distinction prevents treating a sensor-circuit code as proof of cooler or pump failure.

## Use the correct sensor identity

Confirm the actual vehicle's inlet-coolant sensor and preserve its raw range status. Applying an air-temperature sensor's resistance threshold to this coolant input would use the wrong monitor. The DTC cannot distinguish every sensor and circuit cause without additional service tests.

Verification should show the correct coolant input remaining within range for an enabled sample window. A normal reading at one instant is weaker evidence than a completed monitor. Follow the vehicle-specific electrical isolation method and cooling-system precautions; the source does not supply a replacement or coolant-opening procedure.
