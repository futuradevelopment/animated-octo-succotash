# P0199: oil-temperature sensor A accumulates excessive signal movement

P0199 detects intermittent oil-temperature behavior using a “string length” calculation. The ECM adds the absolute differences between each oil-temperature reading and the previous reading. The threshold is a cumulative movement of at least 10°C, rather than a requirement for one individual ten-degree jump.

## Why repeated small changes matter

Because each difference is absolute, an upward change followed by a downward change contributes twice to the accumulated movement; opposite changes do not cancel. A trace that ends near its starting temperature can therefore still contain the instability this monitor is designed to detect.

The source lists four failures out of five samples, sampled every two seconds, with the diagnostic enabled and Type B, two-trip reporting. It describes no separate engine-speed or load gate on this page.

## Preserve a series of values

A sequence of temperatures is more informative than the current value or only the highest recorded value. Look for the pattern of movement and correlate it with other sensor-circuit evidence. The monitor's result does not establish that the oil itself heated and cooled at the reported rate.

Verification should show a stable, credible signal over an enabled monitoring interval, with no renewed excessive cumulative movement. The table supplies no component location, wiring path or replacement instruction. Use the vehicle-specific oil-temperature circuit procedure to establish the cause of the electrical or signal instability.
