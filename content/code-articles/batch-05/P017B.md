# P017B: engine-metal warm-up falls short of the energy model

P017B is a warm-up performance monitor in this GM section. The description evaluates whether engine-metal temperature reaches a required target before the engine accumulates its allowed modeled energy. It lists a biased-low metal-temperature sensor and a cooling system warming incorrectly, including a stuck-open thermostat, as possibilities rather than proven causes.

## Energy matters as well as elapsed time

The model considers heat entering and leaving the cooling system. Its target strategy varies with ambient temperature in three ranges, and supporting tables supply the maximum accumulated energy. Engine soak must exceed 1,800 seconds; the shown runtime window is 10–1,800 seconds, with other temperature, motion and fault prerequisites.

The monitor runs once per ignition cycle and uses Type B, two-trip reporting. A warm-up assessment made after the qualifying early interval may not repeat it.

## Source limitation and diagnosis

The page's general criterion mentions engine-outlet coolant while the title, description and target ranges refer to cylinder-head metal. That wording inconsistency prevents presenting a complete numeric pass/fail recipe here. The supported takeaway is the energy-versus-warm-up comparison, with sensor bias and genuine cooling behavior requiring separation.

Preserve the metal-temperature trace and the applicable model/enable status. Verification needs a valid warm-up evaluation, not merely eventual operating temperature. Use the vehicle-specific cooling procedure to resolve the source ambiguity before choosing a sensor or thermostat repair.
