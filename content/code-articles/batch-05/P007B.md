# P007B: charge-air temperature disagrees after a long soak

P007B checks plausibility of the charge-air-cooler upstream air-temperature sensor at power-up. The ECM compares its reading with an average formed from intake-manifold air temperature and fuel temperature. The listed disagreement threshold is 20°C.

## Why the overnight conditions matter

The test requires at least 28,800 seconds, or eight hours, without engine operation. Evaluation occurs at key-on before the engine runs or within the first two seconds of running. It uses ten samples in a 100-millisecond task and Type B, two-trip reporting.

The two reference temperatures must differ by less than 45°C. Relevant reference-sensor and charge-air-sensor faults must be absent, including the fuel-temperature fault noted on the continuation page. That means a failed reference or insufficient soak can prevent the intended plausibility evaluation.

## Interpreting the comparison

Keep all three temperatures from the qualifying start. The charge-air sensor is being judged relative to references, not against a single fixed outside-air reading. A normal value after the intake warms does not disprove a cold-soak offset.

Verification requires another eligible long-soak comparison with usable reference sensors. A quick restart is not the same test. This monitor supplies no evidence of cooler blockage or reduced cooling efficiency by itself, and it does not establish which sensor needs replacement without checking the underlying temperature and circuit information.
