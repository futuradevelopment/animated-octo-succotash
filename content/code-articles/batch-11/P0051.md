# P0051: Low heater-driver feedback is different from low oxygen output

P0051 concerns the heater circuit serving bank 2 oxygen sensor 1. In the cited GM table the controller looks for a short-to-ground condition at the low-side heater driver. The oxygen measurement and its heating circuit must therefore be kept separate during interpretation.

## Low feedback while the driver is off

The relevant observation is low voltage during the driver’s OFF state. This is different from a low oxygen-sensor output reporting exhaust conditions. The table allows half a second for this electrical diagnosis and classifies its reporting as two-trip.

Heater power-stage diagnosis must first be released. The source also requires a sustained battery-voltage eligibility interval and refers to basic-enable and inhibit tables. A heater that has not been authorized for diagnosis cannot be judged solely because a scan display currently shows no heating activity.

## Read the circuit in its operating context

Preserve heater-command state, electrical-fault status and supply conditions from the failure event. A low signal without the command state does not show whether the controller was performing the off-state check described here.

The row does not distinguish a particular wiring location from an internal component fault, and it does not justify replacing the oxygen sensor solely on the code. Use the matching vehicle’s heater-circuit procedure to localize the electrical condition. Avoid treating exhaust sensor voltage specifications as heater-driver specifications; they belong to different circuits and answer different diagnostic questions.
