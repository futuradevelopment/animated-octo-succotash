# P00C0: Bypass-valve circuit continuity differs from a stuck valve

P00C0 concerns the turbocharger bypass-valve control circuit. In this GM example it is classified as a bank 2 open-load check, performed with the output power stage switched off. That is an electrical assessment, not direct proof that the valve is mechanically stuck.

## The off-state feedback is the evidence

The source lists output-voltage alternatives above 3 volts or at or below 2 volts, a one-second detection period and two-trip reporting. Those alternatives should not be reduced to a homemade pass/fail test: the low-voltage branch overlaps the neighboring short-to-ground row, and the summary does not explain all discrimination logic.

The original rendered table confirms that this wording is present in the document. It is therefore retained as a limitation rather than silently “corrected” into a more familiar circuit rule.

## Choose the right diagnostic branch

The practical distinction is between electrical continuity evidence and valve movement evidence. Command state and circuit feedback belong to the former. A pressure-response test for a stuck valve answers a different question and cannot by itself prove that an open electrical path has been repaired.

Identify the correct bank 2 control branch through the vehicle-specific wiring information, then follow its authorized circuit test. This row supplies no connector pinout and no component replacement verdict. An isolated voltage value without the power-stage state and applicable diagnostic logic is insufficient to localize the fault.
