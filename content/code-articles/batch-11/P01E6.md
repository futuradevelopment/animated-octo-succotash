# P01E6: Coolant sensor 3 high is a voltage-boundary diagnosis

P01E6 concerns engine-coolant temperature sensor 3. The cited GM implementation places its measurement at the auxiliary-radiator outlet and evaluates a high electrical signal. It does not directly declare that coolant temperature is dangerously high.

## Voltage is the measured quantity

The table specifies a sensor-voltage value above 4,960.4 millivolts, equivalent to 4.9604 volts, with a 0.1-second detection interval. The distinction between voltage and temperature is central: a “high circuit” result cannot automatically be translated into a high actual coolant temperature.

Ignition must be on, and the basic enable conditions apply. Reporting is classified as two-trip. A brief observation of a plausible displayed temperature after clearing the code does not establish that the electrical monitor has completed its required evaluation.

## Identify the auxiliary channel first

On a system with several coolant-temperature values, confirm which scan parameter represents sensor 3 at the documented outlet. The main engine coolant reading is not necessarily the value monitored here. Preserve the electrical reading and event conditions for this specific channel.

The summary provides no connector pinout, sensor resistance chart or replacement instruction. Vehicle-specific service information is needed to determine whether the high reported voltage results from the sensor, its circuit or another electrical issue. Do not apply the published monitor boundary as an external test supply, and do not infer a radiator location on a different model from the document’s internal channel description.
