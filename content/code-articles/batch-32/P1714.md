# P1714: Ford shift-solenoid A movement signature is missing

In Ford's documented 2002 diesel transmission monitor, P1714 identifies the functional inductive-signature check for shift solenoid A. The hardware expects a characteristic dip and rise in current as the solenoid moves. Failure to detect that signature is different from a simple open-circuit result and different again from measuring the achieved gear ratio.

## Why a resistance check cannot finish the diagnosis

Ford lists P0750 for solenoid A's electrical driver check, while P1714 evaluates behavior during off-to-on transitions. A circuit can therefore avoid the basic electrical fault yet still fail the movement-signature monitor. The source uses ten solenoid events as the monitoring duration, rather than a fixed number of seconds at idle.

The listed functional window includes transmission fluid between 70°F and 225°F and positive drive torque. Confirming those conditions and actual solenoid transitions matters when checking whether a repair was evaluated.

## Keep mechanical evidence separate

Ford also describes mechanical functional checks using calculated gear ratio or rpm change, depending on available speed sensing. Those observations can help diagnose the broader shift complaint, but they are not the signal named by P1714. Use the applicable transmission procedure to evaluate the solenoid and its control circuit. The monitor does not prove that every hydraulic passage is clear or that the entire transmission needs replacement.
