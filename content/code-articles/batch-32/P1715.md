# P1715: Ford shift-solenoid B lacks its movement current signature

P1715 points to shift solenoid B in Ford's documented inductive-signature monitoring system. The controller looks for the dip-and-rise current pattern produced when the solenoid moves during an off-to-on command. This supplies a movement-related electrical observation beyond the basic driver-state test.

## Identify the correct solenoid and test type

The source assigns P1714 to solenoid A and P1715 to solenoid B. It separately uses P0755 for solenoid B's electrical fault. That separation matters when reading a generic scan description such as transmission fault: P1715 is neither a general gear-ratio error nor merely a continuity result.

Monitoring occurs over ten solenoid events with transmission-fluid temperature in the documented 70–225°F range and positive drive torque. A stationary check that never exercises the relevant transitions cannot establish that this monitor has passed.

## Use the signature as evidence, not a complete verdict

Compare the event with electrical and shift-performance faults, and use the transmission-specific diagnostic procedure to investigate the control path and solenoid response. The summary explains what evidence was absent but does not identify a single replacement part. Its separate mechanical checks use obtained gear ratios or rpm changes; those may add context, but they do not replace the current-signature evidence underlying P1715.
