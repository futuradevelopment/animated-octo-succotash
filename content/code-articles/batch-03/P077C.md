# P077C: output-speed circuit voltage drops below its electrical boundary

P077C is the output-speed sensor circuit-low diagnostic. The cited TCM evaluates raw sensor voltage below 0.2500 volt as a ground-short signature. This is an electrical measurement rather than a test of whether output shaft RPM is low.

## No-signal and circuit-low are different observations

A stationary output shaft can legitimately produce little speed activity, but that alone does not prove its signal circuit is below the electrical boundary. The raw voltage channel and the reported rotational speed must be interpreted separately. The source's detection specification associates the low-voltage condition with less than 0.5 ohm to controller ground.

The decision uses an accumulated structure: a failure interval exceeding fifty milliseconds updates a failure count, and that count must exceed sixteen. Do not reduce this to a universal fifty-millisecond DTC setting time.

## Qualifying supply conditions

Run/crank and battery voltage must both exceed 10 volts for more than five seconds. Service fast learn and code-clear activity are excluded, and an active P077D prevents the shown evaluation. Those prerequisites determine whether a later normal reading actually accompanies an enabled test.

Verification should establish a valid output-sensor circuit voltage and a passing monitor under the correct conditions. A later nonzero RPM reading is useful but insufficient by itself. The table does not prove the sensor assembly is defective or authorize resistance measurement on its powered circuit.
