# P0686: low power-relay control voltage can overlap an open fault

P0686 is the low-voltage control-circuit fault for the powertrain relay. In the cited low-side driver implementation, the controller checks for a ground-short signature while the relay output driver is off.

## Why off-state voltage matters

A low voltage while a low-side driver is intentionally active is not the defining condition here. The diagnostic needs the unexpected low reading during the off state. Its circuit-detection specification corresponds to less than 0.5 ohm from output to controller ground, with controller-specific voltage limits.

The monitor requires run/crank voltage above 11 volts and evaluates eight failures out of ten samples at 250 milliseconds per sample. Consequently, an observation made during a deeply depressed supply condition may not represent an enabled result.

## Do not count companion codes as separate failures

The source states that P0685 can also set in certain controllers. That open-circuit companion can be part of an overlapping detection response; it does not alone establish a second damaged wire or relay. The actual architecture must guide interpretation.

Record the command state and paired codes, then follow the specific relay-control diagnostic. A successful repair verification should show the off-state electrical signature has normalized and the enabled monitor passes. This is a control-driver diagnostic, not a direct measurement of every circuit powered by the relay. The table does not justify automatically replacing the controller, energizing the relay externally, or bypassing its normal shutdown control.
