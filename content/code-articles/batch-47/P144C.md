# P144C: Boost exposes an EVAP purge check-valve problem

## Why the fault may need boost to appear

Ford's P144C monitor evaluates evaporative-emission purge check-valve performance once per driving cycle during boosted operation. Its listed observation lasts five to ten seconds, depending on boost. The controller uses tank-pressure behavior alongside engine, temperature, pressure and purge-system inputs.

The source includes more than one configuration. One table describes pressure-rise behavior with fuel level between 15% and 85%; the following dual-path material allows up to 90% and adds separate check-valve evaluations. Treating all of those limits as one universal test would combine different calibrations.

## Identify the purge layout before applying a limit

Record the full code, tank-pressure trace, fuel level and whether boost was present. The diagnostic question concerns how the check-valve arrangement behaves under pressure, not simply whether the purge solenoid has electrical continuity. The correct engine's hose layout and check-valve configuration are necessary before selecting a physical test.

The printed dual-path threshold statements contain ranges that do not align cleanly with their headline values. They are not silently reconciled here, and this article remains marked for source review. The clearly documented operating condition and separate valve-path concept remain useful without inventing a numerical pass/fail specification.

Ford also notes that P04F0 replaces P144C in newer definitions and that uncompleted Mode $06 phases can display default zeros. A successful verification therefore needs the applicable completed test, rather than an empty result interpreted as proof that the check valve is sound.
