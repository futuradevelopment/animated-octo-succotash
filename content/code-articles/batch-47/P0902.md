# P0902: The CFT30 clutch circuit is checked much faster than clutch slip

## A forty-millisecond electrical observation

Ford includes P0902 in the CFT30 forward/reverse clutch-solenoid opens-and-shorts group. The circuit test watches current feedback and uses a 0.040-second duration. The same section assigns separate codes to functional sticking and clutch slippage, with their own speed relationships and timing.

That separation is useful when a vehicle fails to engage normally. A clutch-circuit-low code is not, by itself, proof of worn friction material. The controller first has an electrical observation to explain, and the source does not equate the word low with one universal measured current value.

## Keep electrical and mechanical evidence apart

Save the current-feedback information and all clutch-related codes before repeating engagement attempts. In this specific CVT, the slipping check compares turbine and pulley speeds, while the stuck check looks for a different closeness between those speeds. Those functional observations should not be substituted for the circuit check's forty-millisecond criterion.

A transmission technician should identify the installed CFT30 arrangement and follow its wiring and solenoid test procedure. The OBD summary does not provide a resistance limit or prove whether the circuit, connector, driver or solenoid caused the low classification.

Do not use repeated Drive/Reverse selection as an improvised repair test when engagement is uncertain. Confirmation needs a resolved circuit fault and the appropriate functional checks under controlled conditions. The historical CFT30 example is not a diagnostic specification for every transmission that can report P0902.
