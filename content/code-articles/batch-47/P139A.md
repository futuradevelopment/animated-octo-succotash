# P139A: A reversed differential-pressure signal during EGR operation

## Direction matters to the hose test

Ford assigns P139A to the EGR sensor C hoses-reversed check. After electrical checks have completed, the controller evaluates differential pressure with EGR commanded on. The cited failure criterion is a DPFE signal below −0.5 kPa for 30 seconds. The negative direction is central to this observation; it is not merely an unspecified low EGR-flow value.

The test runs during the driving cycle and requires usable temperature, pressure and valve-control information, including a valve that is not stuck. Typical environmental bounds include ambient temperature above 35°F and below 200°F, with barometric pressure above 22 inches of mercury.

## Preserve the pressure sign and valve command

Record differential pressure together with the EGR command and companion electrical faults. A pressure reading without its sign or without knowing whether EGR was requested loses the relationship the monitor uses. The factory description makes reversed sensing connections a diagnostic direction, but the stored code alone is not a visual confirmation that someone interchanged hoses.

A technician should use the exact engine's routing illustration and inspect the sensing connections after the exhaust system has cooled. Do not improvise hose swaps or work around hot EGR hardware to test a theory.

Ford's flow-monitor section requires the relevant circuit and hose checks to pass before it decides a flow fault. Resolving the pressure-reference problem therefore helps keep a later flow diagnosis meaningful, rather than interpreting every associated code as a separate failed valve.
