# C1020: The VSA master cut valve operates unexpectedly

## A specific valve event with a broad system response

Honda defines C1020-49 as unexpected operation of the VSA master cut valve. The cited monitor can run during both initial and regular diagnosis. Confirmation requires READY TO DRIVE mode, at least 10 volts from the low-voltage supply and a wait of two seconds or more; the listed diagnostic duration is about two seconds.

The source associates the event with red and amber brake warnings plus ABS and VSA indicators. Its fail-safe list includes stopping ABS, electronic brake-force distribution, traction and stability functions, hydraulic-boost failure compensation and some parking-brake functions. Those warnings should not be reduced to an isolated dashboard nuisance.

## Diagnose before attempting normal road operation

Preserve the complete brake-module scan and power-supply information. The possible-cause field lists internal VSA-modulator failure, but it is explicitly not an exhaustive guarantee of cause. A technician needs the matching full brake-system procedure before deciding whether the modulator requires replacement.

The master cut valve is not interchangeable with the suction-valve diagnostic listed under another code. Maintaining the correct valve identity helps keep the original event and any related tests aligned.

Do not attempt to free a hydraulic valve by applying external power or repeatedly forcing braking interventions. With several support functions potentially unavailable, professional assessment of braking capability comes first. Verification must establish that the unexpected operation is resolved and the affected functions return under the correct service checks.
