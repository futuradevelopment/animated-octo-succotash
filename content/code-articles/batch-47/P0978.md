# P0978: Solenoid C current behavior depends on the transmission design

## Two documented implementations

Ford's summary links P0978 to shift-solenoid C current behavior, but its transmission examples are not identical. In the 6R80 and ZF 6HP26 descriptions, steady-state feedback current is compared with a target and P0978 represents low current for solenoid C. In the Aisin F21 description, the code is associated with stuck control current in a variable-force solenoid circuit.

Both examples concern electrical current behavior. They are separate from the gear-ratio and shift-pattern checks used to infer a solenoid or clutch system that fails mechanically. A range/performance label alone cannot choose the correct transmission-specific test.

## Identify the unit before applying a criterion

Preserve the transmission identification, commanded solenoid state and current-feedback data. The general monitor section gives a half-second duration for its current-range check, but it does not publish a universal ampere limit for every unit. A technician needs the appropriate factory procedure to interpret the target and feedback relationship.

This distinction also prevents a symptom such as a missed shift from being treated as direct proof that the electrical fault is a worn clutch. The source explains that functional gear-ratio monitoring answers a different question from driver-current monitoring.

A useful verification demonstrates that solenoid C's current responds correctly in the actual transmission and that related shift checks complete safely. Replacing a valve body solely from P0978, without resolving the unit-specific electrical evidence, goes beyond what this overview establishes.
