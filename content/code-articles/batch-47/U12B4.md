# U12B4: PCM cannot validate park-pawl driver messages

## Two ways the message check can fail

For U12B4, the PCM monitors information from the park pawl actuator driver unit over F-CAN. Honda specifies either no received signals for at least 1.5 seconds or abnormal information on at least 20 occasions. The second condition is a count, not a 20-second timer; collapsing both into a single duration changes the diagnostic meaning.

Monitoring is continuous once three seconds have elapsed after ON mode and battery voltage is at least 10 V. The source describes a one-drive-cycle code that stores pending and confirmed records and freeze data in the PCM without turning on the MIL.

## The actuator driver is not the only possibility

The cause list includes opens and shorts to ground on F-CAN A_H and A_L, a park-pawl driver-unit fault including a blown fuse, and an internal PCM circuit fault. A communication failure therefore does not by itself prove a jammed mechanical pawl or require replacement of the driver unit.

A professional diagnosis should preserve the message-related evidence and establish that the communicating unit has its required supply before isolating the network circuit. This monitor description supplies no connector-pin tests or mechanical park-pawl inspection procedure.

## Parking needs independent attention

Because the named system participates in park operation, do not use an apparently selected P position as the only assurance that the vehicle is secured when its behavior is uncertain. Have the parking function assessed using the vehicle's service guidance. MIL absence is expected in this source and should not be used to dismiss the stored fault.
