# P0903: A clutch-actuator high-circuit result needs electrical diagnosis

## What the circuit monitor contributes

In Ford's 2010 summary, P0903 belongs to the CFT30 forward/reverse clutch-solenoid opens-and-shorts diagnostics. The electrical check continuously compares current feedback with its accepted range and can register a fault after 0.040 second. The document groups the electrical codes together rather than publishing a unique ampere threshold for P0903.

A high-circuit classification therefore should not be translated into an invented solenoid-current specification. Nor does it establish that the clutch is mechanically stuck on. Ford uses a separate functional diagnostic for that condition, based on turbine and pulley speed behavior.

## Preserve the command and feedback relationship

Record the requested clutch state, available current data and companion circuit codes before a technician isolates the fault. The correct application wiring and service procedure are needed to determine whether the controller, harness or controlled load is responsible. The summary alone does not select a replacement component.

The functional tests also depend on selector position and vehicle-speed conditions. Their results cannot be meaningfully assessed by comparing an arbitrary parked reading with a threshold intended for an enabled test. Distinguishing those conditions keeps a rapid circuit event from being confused with a slower hydraulic response.

If engagement is abnormal, avoid repeated attempts to force the vehicle to move. A qualified transmission diagnosis should first resolve the electrical evidence, then verify safe clutch operation. These facts apply to the documented CFT30 example, not to all clutch-actuator systems.
