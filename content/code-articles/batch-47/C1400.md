# C1400: EPS motor current fault can remove steering assist

## Current monitoring and steering effort

For this Honda application, C1400-11 is the EPS motor-current malfunction. The advanced-diagnostics sheet places it in regular diagnosis, with the engine running and 12-volt battery voltage above 7.5 V. The detection duration is about one second; a single drive cycle can turn on the EPS indicator.

Honda specifies that EPS assistance stops for this fault. System diagnosis continues, and assistance can return when current is normal after the next ignition-on event. A temporary return of assistance therefore does not establish that the underlying problem has gone away. Unexpectedly increased steering effort needs professional assessment before normal driving resumes.

## What the source does and does not establish

The listed possible cause is an internal EPS motor/control-unit failure. The source does not provide an amperage limit, motor-resistance specification or external wiring decision tree. Its current-related title should not be converted into an invented current threshold or a recommendation to apply power directly to the motor.

The confirmation sequence combines ignition-state changes with steering movement in READY mode. It is a controlled workshop procedure, not a suggestion to repeatedly force the steering against its stops during ordinary use. A technician should follow the complete EPS troubleshooting instructions referenced by the sheet and verify the exact vehicle application.

## Evidence worth preserving

Record whether the fault is current, whether the EPS indicator is illuminated and whether assistance changes after a controlled restart. Those observations help relate the stored code to Honda's documented fail-safe, while a code-only parts decision leaves the actual current fault unverified.
