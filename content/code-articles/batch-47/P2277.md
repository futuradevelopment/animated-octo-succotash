# P2277: A bank-two third sensor remains on the rich side of its response test

## Rich retention is not an overvoltage diagnosis

In Ford's historical rear oxygen-sensor monitor, P2277 identifies bank 2, sensor 3 stuck rich. The controller seeks evidence that the sensor can show both rich and lean behavior. If normal operation does not provide the necessary excursions, the strategy can force a mixture change to obtain the missing response.

This functional result is separate from a signal exceeding the electrical high-voltage limit. A rich-looking reading can remain within the sensor's electrical range. The diagnostic question is whether it responds appropriately during a qualified evaluation, not simply whether one voltage sample appears high.

## Keep prerequisites with the observed response

The source requires relevant front-sensor checks, heaters and fuel-related inputs to be usable. It also describes constraints on purge activity, temperature and fuel correction during the test. Recording those conditions helps a technician distinguish a failed response from an observation made while the monitor could not validly run.

First verify that the vehicle actually has bank 2, sensor 3. The summary's example of an early four-cylinder PZEV only uses bank-one codes despite presenting broader code tables. A table entry is not a vehicle-application list.

The correct service procedure must then separate sensor response, circuit integrity and the exhaust/fueling conditions affecting the reading. No universal sensor or catalyst replacement follows from P2277. Confirmation needs a valid completed response test on the correctly identified sensor, not only a cleared warning or a momentary voltage change.
