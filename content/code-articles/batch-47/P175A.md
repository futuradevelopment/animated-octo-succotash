# P175A: An auxiliary transmission pump protects itself from excessive heat

## The temperature source changes the interpretation

Ford labels P175A as an overtemperature condition that disables the electric transmission-fluid pump. In the cited plug-in hybrid explanation, the specific threshold is auxiliary-pump circuit-board temperature above 130°C for three seconds. That is more precise than assuming the code proves the entire transmission-fluid volume reached 130°C.

The engine-driven transmission pump only operates while the engine is running. The electric auxiliary pump supports fluid circulation during extended electric driving. A fault in that auxiliary function therefore has an operating context different from a conventional engine-running lubrication complaint.

## Keep heat, speed and current evidence separate

Record the pump's temperature information, commanded operation and companion speed or current faults. Ford assigns separate diagnostics to incorrect speed, excessive current, low current and a stalled pump. Those categories can coexist, but P175A alone does not determine whether a motor, cooling condition or electrical problem is responsible.

The entry table requires ambient temperature of at least 10°C and a 6-to-19-volt low-voltage supply. Its general monitoring-duration row says less than one second, while the detailed temperature criterion requires three seconds. That discrepancy is retained as a review flag rather than silently harmonized.

A qualified technician should use the applicable pump and transmission procedure before further verification. The summary explains thermal protection and does not authorize bypassing it or continuing electric operation to force the temperature higher.
