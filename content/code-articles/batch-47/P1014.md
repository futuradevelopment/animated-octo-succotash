# P1014: Counting interruptions in the wastegate pressure circuit

## A fault that may disappear at rest

The P1014 row in Ford's wastegate pressure monitor identifies an intermittent or erratic circuit. Its distinguishing criterion is more than ten open-or-short events in a driving cycle. A clean voltage reading after the vehicle has stopped cannot exclude that event history.

The same monitor family lists separate sustained limits for low voltage and high voltage. P1014 instead preserves the significance of repeated interruptions. That gives the technician a different evidence problem: finding when the signal loses continuity, rather than assuming a permanently incorrect pressure or a constant supply failure.

## Capture the event, not just the average

Record the freeze-frame conditions, neighboring sensor codes and any available time trace before clearing memory. An averaged pressure parameter can smooth away short disruptions. Circuit inspection and controlled monitoring should follow the vehicle-specific factory procedure, with attention to whether a signal interruption coincides with movement, temperature or another electrical event. Those correlations are investigative leads, not causes established by this code.

The table calls the overall circuit check continuous and lists five seconds as its monitoring duration, while the intermittent threshold is expressed as a count within a driving cycle. Keep those two statements distinct; the source does not say that all eleven events must occur in five seconds.

Successful verification requires a stable measurement through the relevant operating conditions and an opportunity for the monitor to evaluate it. Replacing a turbocharger solely because a stored pressure code remains in memory would bypass the circuit evidence.
