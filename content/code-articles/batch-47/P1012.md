# P1012: Wastegate pressure voltage below the documented electrical floor

## An electrical signal limit

P1012 identifies a low wastegate control pressure sensor circuit signal in Ford's cited monitor. The documented limit is below 0.20 volt. The circuit check is continuous, has no additional entry condition listed, and uses a five-second monitoring duration. This low-voltage classification is separate from the P1011 pressure-versus-BARO plausibility test.

The transfer table starts its illustrated working points at 0.3 volt, corresponding to about 10 kPa. A signal below the fault threshold is therefore outside those illustrated pressure points. Extrapolating it into a precise vacuum reading would hide the electrical concern that the monitor is reporting.

## Diagnose the measurement path first

A technician needs the actual signal voltage alongside reference supply and sensor-ground information, using the wiring diagram for the identified vehicle. A low scan-tool pressure may be the controller's interpretation of a low electrical input; it does not prove a pneumatic restriction by itself. Likewise, the code does not establish whether the fault resides inside the sensor or along its connected circuit.

Record whether the condition is steady and whether other reference-powered sensors have faults before disconnecting components. The factory equation explicitly scales output with reference voltage, which makes supply integrity relevant to interpreting the signal.

After circuit diagnosis, confirmation should show a credible voltage-pressure relationship and a completed monitor. Simply clearing the fault or replacing a wastegate actuator leaves the original low-signal evidence unexplained.
