# B1415: GPS antenna short procedure contains an unresolved isolation detail

## The fault concerns the GPS antenna circuit

Honda B1415 is a GPS antenna short-to-ground diagnosis within the telematics system. The procedure first checks connector quality and verifies the code after a prescribed shutdown and restart interval. It does not identify a map-data problem or establish that poor satellite reception alone is the cause.

For verification, Honda clears the DTC, switches OFF, opens the driver's door and waits more than one minute. After returning to ON mode, it waits at least another minute before rescanning. A nonreturning fault is classified as intermittent.

## Why this article retains a review flag

The persistent-fault procedure checks a GPS antenna terminal to body ground and initially directs TEL/GNSS antenna replacement if continuity is present. Later, it repeats a ground-continuity check at the same stated terminal but directs antenna-lead replacement for continuity. The intervening instructions do not clearly establish a changed isolation state that explains those different decisions.

There is also a separate signal-to-shield short check. These details support the general distinction between an antenna, lead and controller problem, but they do not justify inventing an omitted disconnection step to resolve the repeated ground test.

## What a repairer should establish

Preserve the stored fault and have the applicable current wiring and antenna procedure checked before measurements or parts authorization. Correct connector isolation is essential to interpreting continuity in this circuit. This summary deliberately leaves the unresolved branch visible rather than claiming that B1415 alone proves which of the antenna, lead or telematics unit must be replaced.
