# P100F: A wastegate pressure comparison after engine shutdown

## Why shutdown timing matters

Ford's P100F monitor compares wastegate control pressure with barometric pressure. The cited calibration requires at least 20 seconds of engine-off time for this particular comparison. It registers a malfunction when the pressure difference exceeds 2.5 inches of mercury, with a listed monitoring duration of five seconds. A reading collected immediately after switching off is therefore not equivalent to the qualified observation used by the controller.

The monitor depends on valid wastegate control, wastegate pressure and BARO information. It is a comparison between two measurements; it does not independently establish which measurement is wrong. That distinction matters when the scan tool shows a plausible-looking wastegate pressure but an incorrect atmospheric reference.

## Preserve the comparison before disturbing the system

Record both pressure channels, the engine-off interval and related electrical faults. The adjacent pressure-sensor transfer table provides a means of checking whether the reported voltage and pressure agree for the documented sensor. Its equation scales with reference voltage, so an assumed fixed five-volt supply should not substitute for a verified supply reading.

A qualified technician can then follow the matching factory circuit and pneumatic checks. The OBD summary supplies a detection relationship, not a complete hose-routing diagram or a command to replace the turbocharger. Repeating the comparison under the specified settling condition is more informative than judging a single running-engine pressure value.
