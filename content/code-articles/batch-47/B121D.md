# B121D: Humidity and cabin-temperature communication needs several circuits

## One sensor assembly, several diagnostic paths

B121D concerns communication with the humidity/in-car temperature sensor. The Honda procedure names HUM DATA, TR, a 5 V supply and SENSOR COM, so the fault is not reduced to an inaccurate outside-temperature reading or a single disconnected data wire.

After confirming recurrence with the climate self-diagnostic function, Honda uses a controlled functional check of the climate unit. Its result separates two main routes: checking sensor supply when the controller responds, or investigating the communication circuits when it does not. The specified jumper test is part of a workshop procedure and should not be improvised at an unidentified connector.

## What each route establishes

On the supply route, approximately 5 V at the disconnected sensor supports replacement of the sensor. Missing supply instead leads through checks for an open 5 V wire, an open sensor-common return and a grounded supply line before selecting the controller.

The other route checks HUM DATA and TR individually for opens, then for shorts to ground, and finally for a short between the two wires. Only after those possibilities are excluded does that branch direct climate-unit replacement.

## Keep the diagnostic distinction

This sequence explains why a communication-error title does not automatically condemn the sensor or the controller. Record related HVAC codes and whether the fault repeats in self-diagnosis. A qualified technician needs the vehicle's connector information and the complete procedure to perform the functional check safely and preserve the distinction between a missing supply and a damaged data path.
