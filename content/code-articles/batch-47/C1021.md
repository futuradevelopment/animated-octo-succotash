# C1021: Unexpected suction-valve operation triggers a VSA fault

## Retain the hydraulic component identity

In Honda's cited diagnostic extract, C1021-49 concerns unexpected operation of the VSA suction valve. It is evaluated during initial or regular diagnosis, with at least 10 volts of low-voltage supply and approximately two seconds in the READY TO DRIVE confirmation state.

The valve name matters. This code does not identify the master cut valve, even though the warning and protective responses can look similar. A technician should retain the complete suffix and module result rather than grouping all internal VSA faults under one generic valve label.

## Interpret multiple warnings as one possible response

The documented response includes red and amber brake indicators, ABS and VSA warnings. It can stop anti-lock braking, brake-force distribution, traction and stability assistance, hydraulic-boost failure compensation and some electric parking-brake functions. Several disabled functions may therefore be consequences of the suction-valve event rather than separate failed components.

Save the fault record and any supply or related brake diagnostics before clearing them. The source lists internal modulator failure as a possible cause, while cautioning that its list is not exhaustive. The full service procedure is needed to reach a replacement decision.

Hydraulic brake-control testing belongs to a qualified technician using the exact vehicle instructions. Do not rely on a restart extinguishing some lamps as proof that all braking functions are restored, and do not command valves experimentally. Confirmation should address the original unexpected-operation condition and the system's safe functional recovery.
