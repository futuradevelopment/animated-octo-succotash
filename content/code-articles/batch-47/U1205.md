# U1205: Battery monitor loses motor-controller data on EP-CAN

## Communication can fail because a participant loses power

Honda U1205 means the battery condition monitor's processor is not receiving valid motor-controller information through EP-CAN. The diagram also includes the IGHLD1 relay and its hold and ignition-feed paths. Consequently, a network fault can originate in the electrical supply that keeps a communicating module available, rather than in the traction battery cells.

Listed possibilities include EP-CAN A_H/A_L opens or shorts, relay-circuit trouble, an open IGA line, a relay stuck off and faults inside either participating module. A qualified hybrid technician should consider the relay and supply paths alongside the data pair before condemning a battery monitor or motor controller.

## Two timing descriptions require care

The enable condition is five seconds after battery voltage reaches 10.2 V following ON mode. The detailed threshold distinguishes two cases: no received signals for at least two seconds, or abnormal received information for at least 500 milliseconds.

However, the summary duration row says two seconds or more without that distinction. This article retains a review flag for the discrepancy; it does not silently substitute a two-second threshold for both cases.

## What the warning record means

The source describes a one-drive-cycle fault with MIL and POWER SYSTEM indications. Pending and confirmed records and freeze data are stored in the battery condition monitor. The MIL can clear after three successful diagnostic trips, so save the original data before clearing or disconnecting the battery. The exact relay wiring and repair tests require the matching vehicle procedure.
