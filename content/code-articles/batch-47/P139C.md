# P139C: Downstream EGR sensing trouble drives differential pressure negative

## A negative result sustained for fifty seconds

Ford's P139C row identifies the downstream EGR sensor C hose as off or plugged. The documented malfunction condition is differential pressure below −4.0 kPa for 50 seconds. That longer, negative-pressure observation is materially different from the positive threshold used for the upstream hose monitor.

Electrical checks must be completed first. The controller also depends on usable ambient, manifold, barometric and EGR-related information, including a valve that is not stuck. Those prerequisites prevent a hose interpretation from being treated as independent proof when the underlying measurement system already has an electrical fault.

## Keep the two pressure connections distinct

Record the signed pressure measurement and associated faults before clearing them. The interpretation depends on which side of the EGR restriction reaches each sensor connection. A qualified technician can compare the actual routing and passage condition with the specific engine's factory diagram once the exhaust hardware is safe to approach.

The code does not tell the technician whether a hose is detached, obstructed or producing an incorrect pressure relationship for another reason. It also does not establish that the EGR valve itself requires replacement. Ford evaluates flow only after relevant hose and circuit checks pass, so these faults deserve attention before a flow result is treated as reliable.

The source labels a flow entry condition as near zero but prints a conflicting greater-than value. This article preserves that uncertainty and omits an asserted flow specification. The pressure threshold and fifty-second duration are legible in the original table.
