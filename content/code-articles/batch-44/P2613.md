# P2613: MX-30 two-way refrigerant valve signal short to supply

In the MX-30 electric heat-pump system, P2613 identifies a short to power supply in the two-way refrigerant valve circuit. The heat-pump control unit detects the fault. Mazda states that heating operation and defrosting control are stopped as fail-safe responses. The page gives no numeric voltage threshold or detection timer.

## Refrigerant-valve circuit identification

This circuit diagnosis differs from a general complaint of weak cabin heat. The source lists the valve connector, heat-pump controller connector, signal wiring, valve, and controller as possible fault locations. A refrigerant charge problem is not established by this electrical DTC alone.

The procedure first clears and rechecks the heat-pump controller's fault to confirm recurrence. It then checks both connector locations and the valve signal circuit before applying the separate two-way valve inspection. This order allows a connection or wiring defect to be identified before the valve or control unit is condemned. The dedicated inspection section is necessary for the actual component test; this DTC page does not supply a universal pinout.

Post-repair recurrence leads to CAN communication diagnosis and repetition of the sequence. If communication is normal and the fault remains unresolved, the chart reaches the heat-pump control-unit replacement branch. Other DTCs are reviewed at completion.

Record the exact stored code, whether heating or defrost functions were affected, and the electrical defect found. Do not bridge valve wiring or open refrigerant lines to investigate this signal fault. The vehicle's electrical and refrigerant-system procedures, including any required EV precautions, belong with trained service personnel.
