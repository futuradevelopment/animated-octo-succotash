# P2D46: MX-30 battery module 2 exceeds 70 degrees Celsius

P2D46 is generically named battery pack B overtemperature. In the cited MX-30 electric BECM procedure, it concerns battery module No.2: the BECM detects any cell temperature above 70 degrees Celsius, or 158 degrees Fahrenheit, for 0.5 seconds. The strict above comparison and half-second duration are both part of this source's monitor.

## Module 2 temperature and the cooling branch

The module identifier is local to this battery architecture. It should not be converted into a second complete battery pack or applied to another manufacturer's pack layout. The code also does not identify the physical cell position, temperature-sensor resistance, or a safe inspection point. Those details are absent from this page.

Mazda lists internal battery parts as the possible cause and uses snapshot information and recurrence to guide the procedure. A returning pending fault is followed by CAN communication diagnosis. With communication normal, the chart leads to the high-voltage-battery assembly branch and then reviews other DTCs. That is a conditional factory procedure, not proof that every stored temperature event requires replacement.

The useful evidence is the module-specific recorded temperature context, present or historical status, related battery faults, and whether the fault returns during qualified assessment. The cited chart does not publish an owner cooling method or a procedure for opening the pack to locate a hot cell.

Battery temperature faults require professional EV evaluation. Do not deliberately operate or charge the vehicle to reproduce the threshold, open the battery enclosure, or touch internal cooling components. Preserve the event data so the technician can assess the indicated temperature and the reliability of the monitoring system.
