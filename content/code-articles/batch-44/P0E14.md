# P0E14: MX-30 battery heater contactor stuck-on indication

The generic title of P0E14 describes a high battery coolant-heater control circuit. Mazda's MX-30 electric procedure instead states that the high-voltage battery heater contactor is detected stuck on. This source-specific interpretation should remain visible: the page does not identify an external coolant-heater control pin or publish a voltage threshold.

## The heater contactor's commanded state

The monitor description is brief and gives no timing or command-versus-feedback details. It supports a stuck-on indication, not a conclusion that the heater is continuously producing heat or that a particular contact surface is welded. The possible-cause list mentions stored BECM faults and the PCM, while the actual procedure also contains a high-voltage-battery replacement branch. That mismatch in detail deserves review rather than a simplified parts recommendation.

Mazda preserves snapshot information and assesses whether the same pending fault returns under the recorded operating conditions. If it does, the next step is CAN communication diagnosis and repetition of the diagnostic sequence. With communication normal and recurrence confirmed, the chart leads to the battery assembly branch. A further recurrence during repair verification can lead to PCM replacement after the sequence is repeated.

These are staged decisions. P0E14 alone does not establish which assembly needs replacement, and clearing it is not confirmation that the contactor operates normally. Other DTCs are reviewed at completion and may provide essential battery-controller context.

Battery heater contactors are part of the high-voltage system. Isolation, internal inspection, and assembly replacement require qualified service personnel. Preserve the snapshot, BECM fault list, and recurrence findings instead of attempting to interrupt or energize the heater circuit manually.
