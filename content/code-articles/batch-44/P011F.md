# P011F: Jetta radiator-outlet temperature compared with ambient

P011F in the DJXA factory table compares radiator-outlet coolant temperature with ambient temperature at engine start. The listed threshold is a temperature difference of at least 20 K. A temperature difference of 20 K has the same magnitude as 20°C, but it is not an absolute coolant-temperature limit.

## Retained heat and the six-hour comparison

The monitor requires propulsion to have been off for more than 21,600 seconds, or six hours. Its retained-heat conditions are awkwardly worded: detection after driving with combustion is listed as checked, while the condition after purging the manifold is listed as failed. Those qualifications matter, so the article does not reduce the rule to every six-hour cold start. Exact implementation should be confirmed against the vehicle's current diagnostic information.

The table lists zero seconds of additional monitoring, once per driving cycle, and MIL illumination after two driving cycles. It references radiator-outlet sensor G83, coolant sensor G62 and the GX9 intake-manifold sensor assembly. These references make the job one of evaluating mutually credible temperature information, rather than assuming G83 is defective because it appears in the code description.

The coolant-sensor procedure describes negative-temperature-coefficient behavior and gives a resistance reference of 2,250 ohms, plus or minus 750, at 20°C. It also checks wiring before a conditional sensor substitution. That single reference is not a complete temperature calibration curve.

Record actual off time, temperature readings and relevant sensor faults before clearing memory. Qualified service should resolve retained heat, sensor plausibility and wiring evidence without opening a hot cooling system. A successful repair must satisfy the applicable start comparison, including its retained-heat qualifications.
