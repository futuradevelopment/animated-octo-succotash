# P0D90: MX-30 AC charging-input frequency fault

P0D90 concerns the frequency of AC power supplied from charging equipment to the MX-30 onboard charger. The charger detects a frequency malfunction and stops charging. This differs from the frequency of a charging-status or control-pilot signal. The page does not publish an acceptable hertz range or a detection duration.

## AC frequency and charging-mode applicability

The source's verification wording needs review because it repeatedly calls for quick charging while describing an AC power-input monitor. The correct charging mode and equipment must be established from current, complete vehicle service information. This article preserves that discrepancy instead of supplying a potentially incorrect test method.

Mazda first uses an equipment comparison to distinguish a current recurring fault from a historical event associated with the original supply. If the fault persists, it specifically checks P0D27, P0D28, P0D29, and P0D2A. Those related input-voltage and current codes receive their own troubleshooting before the final frequency-fault decision.

After those dependencies are resolved, a further current recurrence leads through CAN diagnosis and repetition of the diagnostic sequence. Onboard-charger replacement is conditional on continuing recurrence with communication normal. A final scan checks for other faults.

Useful evidence includes the original charging source, mode, snapshot, associated input codes, and current-versus-historical status. The DTC does not justify a guessed mains frequency limit, charger disassembly, or energized supply probing. Electrical supply assessment and high-voltage charging-system work require qualified personnel; owners should preserve the event information and avoid repeatedly using equipment already associated with abnormal supply behavior.
