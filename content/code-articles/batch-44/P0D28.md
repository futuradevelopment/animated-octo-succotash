# P0D28: MX-30 onboard-charger AC input voltage too high

The MX-30 electric P0D28 monitor concerns AC voltage entering the onboard charger from charging equipment. Mazda says the value exceeds a specified limit and stops charging. The page does not publish that limit or a timer. This is an input-side condition, not a direct statement that the traction battery's DC voltage is excessive.

## Input overvoltage and verification wording

There is a material inconsistency in the source: the outline identifies normal charging and AC input, while the verification steps repeatedly say quick charging. The correct test mode must be confirmed in current vehicle-specific service information. This article does not turn that inconsistent instruction into a charging procedure.

The useful diagnostic structure is nevertheless clear. Mazda preserves snapshot data and first separates equipment-dependent behavior from a fault that remains current with different equipment. A historical-only result can support the possibility of a fault in the original equipment. Current recurrence leads to checks at the charge-port assembly, onboard-charger connection, and charge port itself.

When those checks do not explain the fault, CAN diagnosis and another pass through the sequence precede the onboard-charger replacement branch. Other DTCs are reviewed at completion. The code therefore supports investigation of the incoming supply and its detection path, not immediate replacement of the charger or battery.

Keep the equipment identity, original charging mode, snapshot, and current-versus-historical status. Any evaluation of mains input or high-voltage charging components requires qualified personnel and the complete safety procedure. Do not probe energized charging terminals or continue using equipment known to provide an abnormal supply.
