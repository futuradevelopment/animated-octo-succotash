# P0829: Passat DQ250 fifth gear fails to release

The Passat 2015 DQ-250 6F implementation of P0829 monitors fifth gear failing to disengage. The fork remains in the engaged region despite the control request. Volkswagen compares position with the fifth-gear synchronizing point from basic adjustment and adds a 10-percent margin; the shift-fork valve 2 command is at least 5 percent.

## Fifth-gear position and hydraulic evidence

This fifth-gear check uses multiplexer position 1 and safety valve 1 commanded at least 20 percent. Desired main pressure must exceed 2 bar and a main-pressure-loss condition must be absent. The source lists 6,000 milliseconds of monitoring and a two-driving-cycle MIL schedule. These prerequisites describe a particular hydraulic control configuration, not every 5–6 shift in every automatic transmission.

The electrical and engine conditions require terminal 15 above 4 volts, battery above 9 volts, and engine speed above 600 rpm, each for more than 500 milliseconds. Those values are monitor-enabling limits. They should not be interpreted as general minimum operating-voltage recommendations or used to devise an owner test.

The useful diagnostic comparison is requested disengagement versus fork feedback while the required pressure and valve states exist. The learned synchronizing reference and basic-adjustment history are therefore relevant. The code does not independently prove a broken gear, failed valve, or need for a complete gearbox.

Preserve snapshot information and related hydraulic, electrical, or position faults. A qualified transmission technician should use the correct DQ250 procedure to establish the failed response and verify the repair. Repeatedly forcing a shift to trigger the monitor can worsen an engagement problem and is not part of this explanatory article.
