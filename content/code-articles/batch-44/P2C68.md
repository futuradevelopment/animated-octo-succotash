# P2C68: MX-30 electric coolant pump stuck or stalled

Mazda describes P2C68 in the MX-30 electric system as a stuck electric water-pump motor, with foreign material inside the pump identified in the detection description. The broader diagnostic list also includes coolant freezing and unspecified coolant. The code consequently needs both a pump assessment and a coolant-condition assessment; it does not identify the contaminant or prove one particular mechanical failure.

## Distinguishing a stalled pump from its supply fault

After saving snapshot information, the workshop applies the electric water-pump inspection. An abnormal result leads to repair or replacement of the failed location. If the pump checks normally, Mazda asks whether the system contains the specified coolant, whether low concentration could allow freezing, and whether foreign matter has entered the coolant.

Those questions make recent coolant work and ambient conditions useful evidence. However, the DTC page does not publish a coolant concentration, freezing temperature, stall-current limit, or detection duration. Such specifications must come from the proper vehicle service information rather than a generic code definition.

An abnormal coolant finding leads to coolant replacement and advice to use the specified product. The workshop then checks recurrence under the recorded conditions. If the same pending fault remains, CAN communication diagnosis and repetition of the sequence precede the PCM branch. Other DTCs are reviewed at completion.

The diagnostic distinction is between a pump that cannot turn correctly and fluid conditions that can obstruct its operation. A replacement pump alone may not resolve contaminated or unsuitable coolant. Avoid opening a hot circuit or attempting to force a stalled pump to operate; qualified service should establish the cause and verify restored circulation.
