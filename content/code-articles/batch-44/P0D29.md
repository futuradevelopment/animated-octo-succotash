# P0D29: MX-30 onboard-charger AC current indication too low

Mazda's MX-30 electric P0D29 monitor reports low AC current at the current sensor inside the onboard charger. Charging is stopped. The page supplies no ampere threshold, required charging demand, or detection timer, so the code cannot be interpreted by comparing an arbitrary displayed charging rate with a guessed minimum.

## Low AC current and the test context

The possible causes extend beyond the sensor. Mazda includes charging equipment, charge-port and charger connections, an open AC charging path, poor insulation between the two AC charging conductors, the port assembly, and the onboard charger. Low detected current may therefore reflect the delivery path rather than a failed internal sensor.

The source contains two details requiring review. It describes an AC-input fault but repeatedly specifies quick charging for verification. A connection-check heading names the charger and high-voltage cables, while its body names the battery connection. The applicable charging mode and connection location must be resolved in current service information before testing.

The general decision order is equipment comparison, connector and path assessment, charge-port inspection, and recurrence. A continuing current fault after those checks leads through CAN diagnosis and a repeated sequence before the onboard-charger branch. Snapshot data and whether the fault remains current are central evidence; a historical-only event may depend on the original equipment.

AC-path insulation and continuity testing require the correct isolation, instruments, and qualified personnel. This article provides no terminal or disconnection sequence. A repair report should identify the actual path or sensing defect and confirm that the fault no longer recurs under a properly selected charging condition.
