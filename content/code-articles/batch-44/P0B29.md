# P0B29: low cell voltage in Mazda battery module two

P0B29:00 is Mazda’s cell over-discharge check for high-voltage battery module No.2 in the MX-30 Electric manual. Module identity is essential here: the generic battery-B name does not locate a separate accessory battery or describe the voltage of the complete traction pack.

## One cell can satisfy the monitor

The BECM watches every cell in the specified module. Detection requires either a main-sensor reading of 1.9 V or less, or a sub-sensor reading of 1.88 V or less, maintained for 0.5 seconds. Mazda does not require the two measurements to cross their boundaries simultaneously.

Consequently, interpreting only an average cell value could miss the evidence named by the diagnostic. The low boundary applies to any individual cell, and the two sensing paths have slightly different limits. Preserve which path and cell supplied the recorded observation.

## Reproduction follows the stored snapshot

The factory sequence saves the snapshot before code clearing. Its later confirmation uses the recorded operating conditions to establish whether the same pending BECM fault returns. A lack of recurrence under unrelated conditions is weaker evidence than the source’s specified confirmation.

A repeated code does not immediately end the diagnostic process. Mazda routes the technician through CAN communication diagnosis; only with communication normal does the chart proceed to its high-voltage battery replacement action. No external wiring or connector diagram is supplied for this particular internal-battery fault.

## Use an appropriate repair boundary

This procedure shares its overall confirmation logic with the other module-voltage entries, while P0B29 specifically selects module two and the low-voltage direction. It should not be expanded into instructions for manually charging individual cells or opening the pack. A qualified EV workshop must handle the battery and complete the separate final inspection for other stored faults.
