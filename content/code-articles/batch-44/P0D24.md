# P0D24: MX-30 onboard-charger internal overtemperature

P0D24 identifies excessive internal temperature in the battery charge control module, or onboard charger, in the cited MX-30 electric procedure. Mazda detects temperature at or above a specified value and stops charging. The page does not disclose the temperature threshold or its persistence time. This is charger internal temperature, not automatically traction-cell temperature or charging-plug temperature.

## Internal charger heat and cooling evidence

The first diagnostic branch is coolant leakage. After saving the snapshot, the workshop checks the cooling passage and repairs a leak before restoring coolant through the proper service procedure. That ordering recognizes a cooling-system reason for charger heat without assuming the charger electronics are defective.

If leakage does not explain the fault, Mazda assesses recurrence with charging equipment different from the original equipment. A historical-only result can support an equipment-related event. A current P0D24 requires CAN diagnosis and repetition of the sequence; continuing recurrence with communication normal reaches the onboard-charger replacement branch. The source calls the comparison quick charging, and its exact setup should be confirmed in the complete applicable service information.

Useful evidence includes the original charging circumstances, snapshot, leak inspection result, and whether the code stayed current during the controlled comparison. An unrecorded cleared code provides much less diagnostic information. The final check also reviews other DTCs.

Do not open a hot cooling circuit, keep charging to see how hot the unit becomes, or bypass thermal protection. Cooling repairs, supervised charging checks, and charger replacement require qualified service and the manufacturer's high-voltage precautions. This source does not justify replacing the battery pack from P0D24 alone.
