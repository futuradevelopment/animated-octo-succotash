# P0E72: MX-30 charging-control signal frequency too low

Mazda's MX-30 electric P0E72 monitor identifies low frequency in the charging-status control signal received from charging equipment. The onboard charger detects the condition and stops charging. This concerns a control signal rather than the frequency of the AC power feeding the charger. The page gives no numerical frequency threshold or timer.

## The low-frequency charging-control boundary

The diagnostic route first compares equipment dependence. A workshop preserves the snapshot and evaluates whether the fault remains current with different charging equipment. Historical-only status after that comparison can support an event linked to the original equipment. The source calls the comparison quick charging, so its exact implementation should be confirmed in the complete applicable procedure.

If P0E72 persists, Mazda explicitly checks P0CF5 and P0CF7. Those control-signal faults are handled before the final charger decision. This dependency matters: a low-frequency interpretation may need to be reconsidered in the context of another fault in the same signal path. The code alone does not prove that a timing component inside the charger failed.

A further current recurrence leads through CAN communication diagnosis and another diagnostic pass. Onboard-charger replacement is considered only when communication is normal and the fault continues to return. Other DTCs are reviewed at completion.

Record the equipment used, signal-related companion codes, and current-versus-historical status. The source supplies no owner-level waveform test or permission to bridge the pilot circuit. Controlled charging checks and any connector or charger access require the appropriate professional safety procedures; a generic nominal pilot frequency is not the missing Mazda failure threshold.
