# P0920: Veloster shift-actuator circuit and low feedback current

P0920 concerns the shift-actuator circuit in the 2012 Veloster DCT procedure. Unlike the separate position-signal checks, its detecting-condition image evaluates motor-driver information and feedback current. It requires motor supply above 9 V and lists feedback current below 1 A with a driver integrated-circuit error report; detection is recorded as one occurrence.

## Shift-motor feedback and the naming conflict

The documented fallback holds the selected gear fixed. That behavior is a controller response to the actuator fault, not proof of broken internal gears or an instruction to replace the complete transmission. The source identifies the shift motor and its signal circuit as possible diagnostic branches.

Hyundai's scan-tool text calls for evaluating shift-motor voltage as the transmission changes operating state. The harness sequence checks the motor power path, signal and ground before a conditional known-good shift-motor substitution. A normal current response and a plausible position signal answer different questions, so those observations should be kept separate in the service record.

The source has a real naming problem: its signal-check step says to disconnect the select motor while describing measurement at the shift-motor harness. The scan-tool reference images are also reused from the select-motor page. This article does not infer a terminal assignment from those inconsistencies. Current vehicle-specific diagrams and diagnostic information must resolve them before physical testing.

Professional service should identify the correct actuator, establish reliable supply and control evidence, and replace a component only when the supported branch demonstrates the defect. Verification then requires operation within the monitor's enable conditions and checking for returning faults. Clearing P0920 without exercising its motor-control conditions does not establish that the cause has been corrected.
