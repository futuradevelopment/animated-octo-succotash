# P0383: Passat CVCA glow-control driver reads low while off

The Passat CVCA tables identify P0383 as a short-to-ground condition in the glow-time control-module control circuit. The diagnostic signal from the output driver is zero volts while the driver is off. Volkswagen lists 500 milliseconds of monitoring, continuous checks, and a two-driving-cycle MIL schedule. The same criterion appears in the manual's 2015 and 2016 model-year tables.

## Why the off command matters

The off-state condition is essential. This is not a requirement for normal glow plugs to maintain a particular heating voltage, and the zero-volt diagnostic should not be applied to every pin on the glow-control module. The monitor concerns the driver's diagnostic information.

The referenced procedure identifies automatic glow-time control module J179 and explains that the ECM controls glow-plug voltage through PWM operation. Before component decisions, it requires satisfactory fuses and battery voltage. It distinguishes supply and connection faults from the control paths between J179 and the ECM, and separately handles individual glow-plug faults when those codes are present.

One practical detail is that the manual treats a damaged glow-plug terminal or connector as requiring harness replacement rather than a connector repair. That detail is specific to this procedure and should be checked against current vehicle service information.

A sound repair record identifies the failed control or supply path, whether individual glow-plug codes accompanied P0383, and the recurrence result after correction. The chart does not justify changing every glow plug from this code alone. Controlled verification and readiness restoration follow the repair; electrical testing requires the correct wiring information and suitable test leads.
