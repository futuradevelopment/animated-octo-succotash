# P1086: Subaru left TGV position signal reads low

P1086 identifies low input from tumble generator valve position sensor number 2, the left-hand unit, in Subaru's 2001 Forester turbo manual. It concerns the position-feedback circuit. A valve that does not move and a sensor circuit that reads low are different diagnostic problems.

## Separate a present fault from a stored event

Subaru begins with the live position-sensor value and a 0.1 V comparison. If the low reading is no longer present, the manual points toward intermittent connector contact. A stored code therefore does not justify replacing the valve without examining current evidence.

For a persistent fault, the procedure separates supply availability, continuity of the signal path, and an unwanted connection to ground. It also checks connector contact before reaching valve-assembly replacement. An open supply or damaged signal wire can leave the controller with misleading position information even when the valve itself is serviceable.

## Keep the diagnosis on the correct circuit

This left sensor uses a different signal path from the right-hand P1088 circuit. Comparing both banks may provide context, but it does not make their connectors interchangeable. The printed low-input chart also contains inconsistent references to other position sensors; obtain the correct vehicle wiring diagram before terminal-level tests.

Subaru lists rough idle, stalling and poor performance as possible symptoms. Those symptoms alone cannot distinguish the feedback circuit from other engine faults. Repair confirmation requires the manual's memory-clear and inspection routines, including evidence that the low reading has disappeared.
