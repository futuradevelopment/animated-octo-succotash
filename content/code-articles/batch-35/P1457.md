# P1457: Honda EVAP canister-side leak assessment

P1457 identifies the canister-side EVAP diagnosis in Honda's Advanced Diagnostics collection. That side extends from the two-way valve toward the purge valve in the described system. P1456 covers the tank-side assessment and uses different diagnostic principles.

## A leak label can include incorrect valve behavior

Before its retained-vacuum assessment, the P1457 sequence examines fuel-tank pressure while the system should be exposed to atmosphere. Unexpected vacuum can indicate an incorrect vent or purge state. Those preliminary branches show why a P1457 should not automatically be translated into a cracked canister.

The later stages draw vacuum into the canister section, allow it to stabilize, then evaluate the remaining vacuum through its effect on the tank-side pressure signal. If retained vacuum fails to produce the expected pressure change, the monitor interprets leakage on the canister side.

## Verification requires an eligible sequence

The source gives separate conditions for two leak sensitivities, including temperature, fuel level and engine-operation requirements. Other active sensor or EVAP faults can prevent the assessment from running. A normal fuel cap and an empty fault list after clearing do not establish that the canister-side test has passed.

The documented strategy uses two qualifying faulty cycles before the MIL is illuminated. Record temporary codes and freeze-frame conditions, diagnose the relevant valve and leak paths with approved equipment, and confirm completion of the correct monitor after repair. Avoid improvised tank pressurization or applying a tank-side leak procedure without confirming the system boundary.
