# P1301: Subaru exhaust-temperature warning linked to misfire

P1301 has a notably serious heading in the 2001 Forester turbo manual: a fire-related condition associated with increased exhaust temperature. Its actual diagnostic instruction focuses on the cylinder-misfire procedures, not on treating the code as a generic temperature-sensor replacement request.

## Diagnose the combustion fault first

Subaru directs the technician through the P0301, P0302, P0303 and P0304 diagnostic procedures. If that work identifies a fault requiring repair or replacement, the chart calls for correcting it and addressing the pre-catalytic converter. The logic links the underlying combustion problem with possible exhaust-component consequences.

That sequence is materially different from installing a new exhaust-temperature sensor merely because temperature appears in the title. It also explains why replacement of a damaged catalyst without correcting the demonstrated misfire can leave the original cause unresolved.

## A short chart does not make a simple diagnosis

If the misfire investigations do not establish a repairable fault, the source escalates to Subaru distributor-level diagnosis because multiple components may be involved. It does not provide an independent temperature threshold or a universal single-part cure on this page.

The manual lists irregular idle, stalling and poor driving performance as possible symptoms. Avoid continuing operation with severe misfire or signs of exhaust overheating; professional assessment is appropriate. After the established cause and any required exhaust repair are addressed, Subaru specifies memory clearing and inspection mode. Verification should cover both combustion quality and the original temperature-related fault report.
