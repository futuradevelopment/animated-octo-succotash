# P1106: Honda BARO and MAP readings disagree

Honda's documented P1106 monitor checks whether the barometric-pressure reading agrees with manifold absolute pressure when the throttle is sufficiently open. In this design the BARO sensor is inside the engine controller. That fact changes where diagnosis begins: the source is not describing a separate external BARO sensor to replace automatically.

## Why throttle position matters

At a large throttle opening, manifold pressure approaches atmospheric pressure. Honda uses that relationship to compare MAP with BARO. The published variant identifies a difference of at least 21 kPa lasting at least three seconds, subject to its enable conditions.

A comparison made only at idle would not reproduce the same test, because intake vacuum normally makes manifold pressure lower than atmospheric pressure. Likewise, the throttle opening needed to enable the monitor varies with engine speed in the table. A seemingly normal stationary scan is therefore incomplete evidence.

## Diagnose the disagreement, not only the named sensor

The enable conditions exclude several other active faults, including MAP and throttle-related faults. Their status matters when interpreting whether P1106 can run and whether its inputs are credible. The monitor description establishes a disagreement; it is not a complete wiring or ECM-replacement procedure.

This variant runs once per driving cycle and uses two qualifying faulty cycles before illuminating the MIL. Record temporary codes and freeze-frame conditions before clearing memory. Verification must show that the comparison actually ran without failure. Road confirmation should follow applicable service information and safe, legal conditions, not an improvised wide-open-throttle test.
