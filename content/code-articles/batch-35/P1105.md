# P1105: Nissan MAP/BARO switching fault: electrical and vacuum branches

## Identify which test reproduced the fault

The Nissan KA24DE procedure gives P1105 separate diagnostic paths after its malfunction A and malfunction B confirmation routines. That structure matters: a pressure-switching complaint can require electrical testing, pneumatic testing, or both. Starting with a new absolute-pressure sensor skips the source's distinction between the sensor and the valve that selects its pressure supply.

Procedure A begins at the MAP/BARO switch solenoid power supply. Battery voltage should be available under the specified key-on, disconnected-connector condition. A missing feed leads to the fuse, intervening connectors and supply wiring. The controller-side conductor is then isolated and checked for continuity and unwanted connections to ground or power.

## Why a working coil is not the whole test

The component branch checks whether the solenoid actually changes its air passage. The documented switching-time check requires a response in less than one second. Hearing a click alone does not establish that the correct pressure passage opened or that its response was timely.

Procedure B additionally examines the vacuum delivered to the switching valve and pressure sensor. Cracked, blocked, disconnected or incorrectly routed hoses are explicit inspection targets, as is a blocked manifold vacuum port. The chart also includes intake-air leakage and water in the pressure-sensor connector. Those checks explain why an apparently electrical code description can lead to a hose or connector repair.

## Confirm the pressure measurement separately

The final sensor assessment compares its atmospheric reading with its response to a specified applied vacuum. It requires a calibrated gauge and the source's pressure limits; improvised pressure can damage the sensor. The useful repair record distinguishes an absent electrical command, failed air-path switching and an incorrect sensor response. If those checks pass, Nissan directs investigation of an intermittent incident instead of automatic component replacement.
