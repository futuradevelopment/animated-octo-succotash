# P1699: Subaru torque-cut signal circuit high

Subaru defines P1699 as high input in the engine torque-control cut signal circuit in the 2001 Forester SOHC manual. This is a dedicated ECM–TCM coordination line in the documented system, not a generic description of excessive engine torque.

## Isolation changes what a high voltage means

The factory sequence checks the signal with the TCM disconnected after its prescribed warm-up and ignition steps. A voltage remaining above the chart's comparison directs attention to a battery-voltage short in the inter-controller harness. Its endpoint includes assessing consequential ECM damage after the wiring fault is repaired.

The next branch looks for an intermittent unwanted voltage while the harness and ECM connection are disturbed under controlled test conditions. A circuit that is normal when stationary may still contact a power source as wiring moves. That evidence differs from replacing a controller because an old code remains stored.

## Know when the short chart stops

If the intermittent-voltage check does not identify the fault, Subaru directs the case to further distributor-level diagnosis because multiple components may be involved. The published procedure therefore does not support a universal one-part cure.

Electrical investigation requires the correct connector states and vehicle diagram. Do not substitute the P1698 low-input procedure, whose continuity and ground-short branches answer different questions. Subaru specifies two consecutive faulty driving cycles for detection and calls for memory clearing and inspection mode after correction. Verification should address the unwanted voltage and its possible controller consequences.
