# P1738: Honda second clutch pressure switch circuit diagnosis

## Identify the second-clutch feedback circuit

For the 1999–2000 Odyssey, P1738 identifies a problem in the second clutch pressure switch circuit. The switch provides the controller with an electrical indication associated with that clutch's pressure state. The code therefore needs a circuit assessment before conclusions are drawn about internal transmission damage.

Honda begins with freeze data and a key-on voltage check at the controller. If the expected battery-level indication is missing, the procedure disconnects the controller and pressure switch and checks whether the signal wire is shorted to ground. That isolation is important: a low reading with everything connected can have more than one explanation.

## Separate operating response from wiring integrity

Another branch examines the second-clutch signal during the specified second-gear operating condition. The chart expects the signal to change to approximately zero volts in that test state. Its original running-drivetrain procedure requires professional equipment and vehicle support; it is not a driveway test to reproduce with improvised lifting equipment.

If the expected response is absent, Honda checks the supply indication at the disconnected switch connector. A missing supply indication directs repair toward an open circuit between the switch and controller. A separate resistance assessment then distinguishes the pressure switch from remaining controller or terminal-fit possibilities. These are different pieces of evidence, rather than repeated checks of the same component.

## Make the repair decision traceable

A useful diagnosis records the commanded gear, the switch signal under that condition, and the isolated harness result. Replacing the switch solely because its name appears in P1738 skips the short-to-ground and open-wire branches. Conversely, a circuit fault alone does not justify a transmission overhaul. Controller substitution appears only after the relevant circuit checks and loose-terminal inspection; it is not Honda's opening repair instruction.
