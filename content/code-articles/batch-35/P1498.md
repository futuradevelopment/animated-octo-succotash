# P1498: Honda EGR position-feedback voltage high

Honda's P1498 description concerns high voltage from the EGR valve position sensor. The ECM uses that sensor to compare actual valve lift with a stored command appropriate to the operating conditions. The EGR system reduces peak combustion temperatures by recirculating exhaust gas.

## Electrical feedback and exhaust flow are different evidence

The documented monitor detects a position-sensor output of 4.88 V or more for at least two seconds. It is a continuous electrical-range check under the stated enable conditions, including an engine-speed limit of 4,000 rpm.

A high feedback voltage does not independently prove that too much exhaust gas is flowing. It may prevent the controller from reliably knowing valve lift. Likewise, the code alone does not establish that a blocked EGR passage or carbon deposits caused the electrical reading.

## Use command and feedback without inventing a cure

The monitor description explains why position feedback matters, but it does not provide the full circuit test needed to distinguish sensor, wiring, reference, ground or controller causes. Obtain the actual vehicle procedure before replacing the valve or interpreting a scan parameter with a different scale.

Honda classifies this variant as a one-drive-cycle MIL fault and records freeze-frame data when the failure is detected. Verification should show that the correct position-sensor signal remains within its expected range while relevant operation is assessed. A cleared warning lamp or a changed idle after cleaning does not by itself demonstrate that the excessive-voltage complaint is repaired.
