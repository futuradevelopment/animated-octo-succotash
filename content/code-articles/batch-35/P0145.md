# P0145: Honda third oxygen sensor response is too slow

Honda's P0145 description evaluates the response of the third heated oxygen sensor downstream of a NOx-adsorbing catalyst. Its manual-transmission strategy uses the sensor for catalyst diagnosis and checks whether the output moves to the expected side as operating conditions change.

## A changing state is the key evidence

The source assesses response during enrichment and lean-burn operation. A signal remaining in an intermediate band instead of reaching the expected rich or lean region can trigger the slow-response diagnosis. This differs from the neighboring P0143/P0144 tests for a signal held at a low or high extreme.

A snapshot can therefore conceal the original problem. A voltage may look plausible at one instant while the transition takes too long or fails to reach the required state. Diagnosis needs the operating phase and signal behavior over time, interpreted using the correct parameter scale.

## Eligibility is unusually specific

The table requires engine and catalyst warm-up, a sufficient lean-burn interval, an appropriate higher gear and an IMA battery condition, along with the absence of relevant active faults. Those prerequisites explain why ordinary idling is not a complete reproduction test.

Honda describes two qualifying faulty cycles for MIL confirmation. The monitor page does not establish a universal response-time specification for all oxygen sensors or prove a failed catalyst. After model-specific sensor and circuit diagnosis, confirm the expected transitions and monitor completion. Avoid transferring this lean-burn/NOx-adsorber strategy to a vehicle that lacks the documented hardware.
