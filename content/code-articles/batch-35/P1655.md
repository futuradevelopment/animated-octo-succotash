# P1655: Honda CVT engine–transmission signal loss

Honda's CVT-labeled P1655 monitor concerns the A/T–FI signal A/B circuit. The described system uses dedicated TMA and TMB lines between the engine controller and transmission controller, rather than the generic network architecture that might be assumed from a modern communication code.

## The direction of communication matters

The ECM sends information to the TCM on TMA. The TCM sends engine-control information back on TMB. The published malfunction threshold is no communication signal from the TCM for at least five seconds under the stated conditions.

That distinction helps locate the missing information conceptually. A code reported by the engine controller can reflect an absent transmission-controller signal; it is not evidence of a mechanical CVT failure by itself. The monitor description does not identify which module, connection or wire is responsible.

## Check eligibility before interpreting non-recurrence

This variant monitors continuously with a minimum documented supply-voltage condition and without an active related AT–FI fault. If the relevant conditions are not satisfied, failure to reset immediately may not demonstrate a repaired communication path.

Use the correct CVT system diagram and vehicle procedure to assess the two dedicated lines and controller operation. Do not substitute CAN resistance tests simply because the issue involves two modules. Honda classifies the documented monitor as a one-drive-cycle MIL fault and stores freeze-frame data. Repair verification should establish restored transmission-to-engine communication under eligible conditions, not merely removal of the warning lamp from memory.
