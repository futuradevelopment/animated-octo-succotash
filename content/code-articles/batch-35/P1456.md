# P1456: Honda EVAP leakage on the fuel-tank side

Honda's P1456 monitor concerns leakage on the fuel-tank side of its divided EVAP system. In the documented arrangement, the two-way valve separates this section from the canister-side section. That boundary explains why P1456 and P1457 should not be treated as interchangeable leak labels.

## Pressure behavior identifies the failed test

The tank-side strategy evaluates pressure changes after startup and during later vacuum-related stages. It supports different leak-test sensitivities under different enable conditions. Large-leak behavior can include insufficient pressure change while intake vacuum is drawn into the tank; the source gives a missing filler cap or disconnected pipe as examples, not an exhaustive diagnosis.

A code therefore does not prove that the cap is the sole cause. The detected result is that the tank-side system failed the specified pressure behavior. Correct professional leak and valve testing must determine the actual location or control fault.

## Why the monitor may not finish immediately

Initial coolant/intake temperatures, fuel level, battery voltage, running conditions and other active faults affect eligibility. The finer leak assessment can also depend on prior stages. Clearing P1456 and taking a short arbitrary trip may leave the relevant monitor incomplete.

This description uses two qualifying faulty cycles for MIL illumination and records temporary codes before confirmation. Preserve those records and verify monitor completion after repair. Fuel-vapor systems require approved test equipment and pressure limits; do not pressurize the tank with improvised compressed-air methods. The source's exact thresholds belong to its documented strategy, not every Honda EVAP system.
