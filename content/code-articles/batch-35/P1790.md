# P1790: Honda transmission-controller throttle input fault

Honda's P1790 description concerns the throttle-position signal at the transmission control module. The throttle-body sensor uses a moving contact and resistive track to generate a voltage related to throttle opening, with the signal used by both engine and transmission control functions in the diagram.

## The receiving controller is important

The documented TCM monitor checks for a signal at or below 0.09 V or at or above 4.93 V for at least ten seconds. It operates continuously with the ignition on. Either extreme can produce the same code, so P1790 alone does not identify whether the original condition was high or low.

The source explicitly includes an open or shorted harness and an input-circuit malfunction in the detection description. A normal throttle reading in one controller therefore should not automatically clear the separate path into the transmission controller from suspicion.

## Use the operating record to guide circuit diagnosis

Preserve freeze-frame information and compare the appropriate input data using the vehicle-specific procedure. The monitor page does not establish a universal throttle-body replacement remedy or permit judging this sensor from an unrelated scan-tool scale.

Honda classifies this variant as a one-drive-cycle fault that illuminates the MIL and blinks the D indicator. Those indications do not prove internal transmission damage. After the established circuit repair, confirm that the TCM receives a plausible throttle signal throughout the relevant range and that its monitor no longer reports either electrical extreme.
