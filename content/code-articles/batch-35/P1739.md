# P1739: Honda third clutch pressure switch feedback checks

## Why third-gear context matters

P1739 concerns the third clutch pressure switch circuit in the documented 1999–2000 Odyssey application. It must be kept separate from P1738, which uses the second-clutch switch and a different signal circuit. Testing the wrong switch can produce a plausible voltage reading while leaving the actual complaint unresolved.

The service chart starts by preserving freeze data, then checking the third-clutch signal with the ignition on. An unexpected result leads to an isolated check for a grounded signal wire between the controller and the third-clutch switch. Disconnecting both ends prevents a component's normal electrical path from being mistaken for damaged wiring.

## What the operating test adds

The next relevant observation is the switch signal after the transmission has actually been in third gear for the specified test interval. Honda expects approximately zero volts at the stated test points under that condition. Merely selecting a drive range or looking at a stationary vehicle's signal is not equivalent to confirming third-gear operation.

The original procedure involves an operating drivetrain and belongs in a properly equipped workshop. If the anticipated state change is missing, the chart checks voltage at the disconnected switch connector and then the switch-side resistance. Missing connector voltage points toward an open harness; the later resistance branch separates the switch from controller or terminal-fit concerns.

## Avoid a premature mechanical diagnosis

The useful result is a documented chain from the actual gear condition to switch response and isolated circuit condition. A stored P1739 does not by itself establish a worn clutch or identify a repairable hydraulic leak. Equally, clearing the code without checking the third-gear response leaves the original evidence unexplained. Honda reserves known-good controller substitution for the appropriate later branch, after loose terminals and the specified circuit conditions have been assessed.
