# U3019: GM brake-control B/C power correlation

GM's cited BSC2 table defines U3019 as control-module input power circuit B/C correlation. The monitor compares the booster-circuit supply measurement with the motor-driver supply measurement. Its purpose is to detect disagreement or drift between those readings.

## Correlation differs from simple undervoltage

The table evaluates voltage sensor 2 divided by voltage sensor 1 and flags a ratio outside approximately 0.766 to 1.321 for two seconds. Both readings can therefore be nonzero while their relationship is still unacceptable. A single battery-terminal voltage does not establish that the two monitored measurements agree.

The source description refers to voltage-divider drift. That explains the monitored concern without proving that a particular external battery or brake component has failed. The diagnostic procedure for the actual vehicle must determine why the measured relationship is wrong.

## Keep the two measurements and their scaling straight

Interchanging numerator and denominator changes the ratio. Likewise, comparing parameters with different scaling would not reproduce the table's calculation. Use the correct controller data and factory test method instead of applying the numeric range to arbitrary scan values.

The monitor operates in running or shutdown software states and carries a one-trip Type A classification in this document. It is brake-control evidence, so related warnings warrant qualified assessment.

After the demonstrated cause is corrected, verify agreement of the relevant supply measurements and completion of the correlation check. A clear fault memory or normal communication with the controller cannot by itself prove that the internal power readings are consistent.
