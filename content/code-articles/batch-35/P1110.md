# P1110: Subaru atmospheric-pressure circuit low input

In the 2001 Forester SOHC diagnostic section, P1110 identifies low input from the atmospheric-pressure sensor circuit. This manufacturer-specific meaning must be confirmed before borrowing a P1110 procedure from another brand.

## Electrical evidence comes before sensor replacement

Subaru's sequence examines current pressure data and connector contact, then separates the sensor supply from the signal path. Later checks look for interrupted wiring and an unwanted signal connection to ground. The atmospheric-pressure sensor becomes the replacement candidate only after those external paths and its connector have been assessed.

This order matters when a low-pressure reading is interpreted as a bad sensor. An electrical low input need not represent genuinely low atmospheric pressure. The sensor can also lose usable output because its supply or connection is defective.

## An inconsistent chart calls for restraint

The printed procedure mixes intake-manifold and atmospheric-pressure labels in some early steps, while the heading and later connector checks clearly concern the atmospheric-pressure sensor. For that reason, this explanation does not turn the ambiguous first scan-data comparison or inconsistent terminal references into a universal test recipe. Obtain matching vehicle service information before electrical measurements.

A normal reading during a later inspection does not erase the earlier event. Subaru includes poor ECM and pressure-sensor contact as an intermittent branch. Record whether the fault is present now, then confirm the completed repair using its memory-clear and inspection modes. The aim is restored pressure-signal behavior, not simply deletion of the code.
