# P2517: Low-side refrigerant sensor voltage is below its valid range

P2517 is the low electrical input fault for refrigerant pressure sensor B in the cited GM hybrid controller. The source identifies this as the low-side sensor and sets its lower boundary at 2% of the reference voltage, shown as 0.1 V. That is a signal-validity limit, not a refrigerant-charge specification.

## Low voltage does not prove an empty system

A scan tool may translate the voltage into a very low pressure reading. Once the electrical-range check fails, that calculated value cannot by itself establish that refrigerant has leaked away. The monitor does not distinguish every possible sensor, reference, connection or circuit fault, and it provides no instruction to add refrigerant.

The controller requires system voltage above 10.2 V. Its decision uses 16 failing samples out of 20 at 100-millisecond intervals, followed by a two-trip Type B illumination strategy. This sampled history is more informative than treating one low reading as the entire failure criterion.

## Separate circuit validity from pressure response

P2516 tests whether low-side pressure changes after compressor operation, but P2517 inhibits that performance test. Clearing the low-voltage code without establishing valid input therefore leaves the response assessment incomplete.

Preserve the pressure-sensor voltage, system-voltage context and companion faults before relying on pressure trends. Verification should show that the electrical monitor passed and, when applicable, that the separate performance monitor could run. The source does not establish the sensor's physical location or a safe refrigerant service procedure; use vehicle-specific information and qualified equipment for those tasks.

## Source and applicability

GM 2015 15OBDG01 Hybrid Diagnostics, HCP2 (VICM) low-side refrigerant pressure monitor on physical page 572. Per-model applicability and sensor location are not established here; electrical limits are specific to the documented calibration.
