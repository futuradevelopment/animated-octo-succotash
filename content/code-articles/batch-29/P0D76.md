# P0D76: compressor driver temperature is checked against ambient and intake air

P0D76 evaluates the electric-compressor output-driver temperature report, identified as IGBT temperature in this GM example. It is a plausibility comparison after a long off period, rather than a direct declaration that the inverter has overheated during operation.

## The two reference limits are different

GM requires the IGBT temperature to differ from raw outside-air temperature by more than 15°C and from intake-air temperature by more than 10°C. Both discrepancies are required. The failure counter needs 35 failed samples out of 40 at a 100-millisecond interval.

The compressor must have been off for more than six hours. The specified temperature signals, supply voltage, power mode and coolant-to-ambient relationship must meet their enabling conditions. Ignoring that off-time requirement would turn a controlled comparison into an inappropriate test of a recently running compressor.

## Use the whole temperature picture

Retain the driver, intake-air and ambient readings together with elapsed off time. If a reference-sensor fault is present, its status changes whether the comparison can support a conclusion about the driver-temperature report.

The different 15°C and 10°C limits should not be collapsed into one universal allowable difference. Nor does the code alone identify which of the measurements is inaccurate. The source provides monitor logic without a repair or access procedure. Qualified diagnosis must use the correct vehicle information and account for the high-voltage and refrigerant hazards of the electric-compressor assembly.

## Source and applicability

GM 2015 15OBDG01 Hybrid Diagnostics, cited HCP2/VICM output-driver temperature comparison only. No universal compressor thermal limit or model applicability is asserted.
