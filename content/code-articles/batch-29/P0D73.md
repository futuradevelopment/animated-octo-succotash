# P0D73: circuit-low diagnosis is based on an extreme CPU temperature report

The GM P0D73 row is labelled compressor-controller internal temperature-sensor circuit low. Its monitored quantity is the converted CPU-temperature input. A low electrical-circuit designation can therefore accompany a high reported temperature; the two descriptions should not be treated as contradictory physical measurements.

## Understand the diagnostic boundary

The cited failure criterion is a reported CPU temperature above 274°C. It requires 30 failed samples out of 60 at a 50-millisecond interval, system voltage above 10.25 V and Accessory or Run/Crank hardware state.

This extreme sensor-range value is not an allowable controller operating temperature or a safe thermal threshold. The source is describing a circuit diagnostic, not recommending that the electronics can operate near the printed number. Actual overheating concerns need the relevant thermal diagnostics and service information.

## Keep recovery and repair separate

The pass branch uses a report at or below the boundary and lists a controller reset as a prerequisite after failure. A reset condition in a monitor table is not proof that resetting the system repairs its cause.

A technician should retain the original temperature report, operating state and related compressor-controller faults before attempting vehicle-specific verification. The parameter document gives no internal component replacement sequence, pinout or safe access method. Investigation of electric-compressor electronics must follow professional high-voltage and refrigerant procedures, rather than treating a temperature-sensor code as permission to open the assembly.

## Source and applicability

GM 2015 15OBDG01 Hybrid Diagnostics, only the cited EACCM monitor and calibration. Per-code vehicle and engine applicability has not been established; these historical thresholds are not universal service specifications.
