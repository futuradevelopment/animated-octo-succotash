# P2234: bank-two heater coupling is evaluated under controlled sensor conditions

P2234 is the heater-coupling diagnostic for the bank-two primary oxygen sensor in this GM hybrid table. The monitored quantity is a filtered sensor-current change, not merely heater resistance or the presence of a general mixture fault.

## The heater and sensor must be in the right state

The source lists a current change of at least 190 microamps, with a fifteen-second evaluation. It also requires qualified exhaust and sensor-ceramic temperatures, battery voltage, engine load and fuel-system state. Heater duty cycle is restricted to the stated intermediate range rather than simply any on command.

Additional settling conditions follow heater power-stage switching, and the diagnostic scheduler must enable the test. These details matter because a normal switching transient or an unqualified warm-up observation should not be treated as the documented coupling failure.

## Preserve bank identity and operating context

A technician should retain the bank-two signal and heater context from the event. Evidence for bank one does not automatically identify the source of a bank-two report, even when both sensors use similar technology. The full code and actual vehicle mapping remain necessary.

The table does not isolate an internal sensor short from a wiring problem or prescribe replacement of both sensors. It supplies monitor evidence that must be investigated with the matching service procedure. Verification should demonstrate the relevant qualified heater-coupling evaluation, rather than only a cleared code. Physical work must also account for hot exhaust components and the correct electrical isolation conditions.

## Source and applicability

GM 2009 09OBDG13 2-Mode Hybrid Diagnostics, cited bank-two primary sensor heater coupling monitor only. Document-level evidence does not establish per-code vehicle, engine or wiring applicability.
