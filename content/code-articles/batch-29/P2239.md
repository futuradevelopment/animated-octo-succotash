# P2239: pump-circuit diagnosis follows a qualified control-factor change

P2239 is associated with the bank-one air/fuel sensor pumping-current circuit in this GM example. Although the generic label describes a high current-control circuit condition, this row evaluates the control system's response rather than providing a simple external high-voltage test.

## The reference point is when enabling conditions are met

The monitored change is the absolute lambda control-factor movement from the point at which the secondary conditions become satisfied. The printed boundary is greater than 0.025 lambda. The sensor voltage must be within the narrow 1.49–1.51 V region described by the continuation.

The heater and warm-up states must be ready, closed-loop control active and electrical trimming inactive. The source also requires a qualified forced trim amplitude and stable related control states. Those details explain why an arbitrary change in fuel correction is not equivalent to this diagnostic event.

## Keep the controlled response with the signal

Record the relevant sensor value, control-factor baseline and operating-state information. A later voltage reading without the point of comparison cannot demonstrate the change the monitor evaluated. A generic sensor-heater test also does not substitute for this pumping-circuit response assessment.

The table does not identify a failed connector, sensor element or control module, and it supplies no replacement instruction. The exact vehicle diagnostic procedure must establish the cause and verify the controlled response. Do not improvise forced fuel adjustments or energized sensor probing from this parameter summary; the appropriate professional procedure controls those tests.

## Source and applicability

GM 2009 09OBDG04 Engine Diagnostics, cited bank-one air/fuel pump-circuit control-factor response monitor only. Document-level evidence does not establish per-code vehicle, engine or wiring applicability.
