# P2257: secondary-air relay low circuit can include an open connection

P2257 concerns the secondary-air injection relay circuit in this GM 2009 example. The source's low-circuit classification includes a short to ground or a connection that is not present. The title alone therefore does not distinguish those two electrical possibilities.

## Supply prerequisites belong in the record

The monitor requires the stated ignition and battery-voltage conditions and excludes the listed powertrain-relay faults. A one-second electrical condition is listed, with a two-driving-cycle classification. These qualifications matter before interpreting relay feedback as an isolated AIR-system defect.

The neighboring P2258 record instead concerns a short-to-battery classification. Those directions should not be combined into a single assumption that the relay itself must be bad. The appropriate circuit procedure must establish where the detected condition originates.

## Relay electrical integrity is not air-flow verification

A technician should preserve the relay code, supply context and companion powertrain-relay records. The source assigns separate diagnostics to actual secondary-air flow and pressure behavior. An electrical relay fault does not, by itself, establish a blocked passage or a mechanically stuck switching valve.

The table provides no universal connector pinout or instruction to bridge the relay. Use the correct vehicle wiring information and professional diagnostic procedure to separate an open connection from a grounded path or internal component issue. Verification should show correct electrical behavior under the qualified conditions, rather than relying solely on an audible pump or a cleared code.

## Source and applicability

GM 2009 09OBDG06 Engine Diagnostics, cited secondary-air relay low circuit monitor only. Document-level evidence does not establish per-code vehicle, engine or wiring applicability.
