# P2505: Interrupted permanent ECM supply affects engine-off timing

The cited GM P2505 monitor detects an interruption in the permanent battery-voltage supply to the ECM's engine-off-time circuit. Its focus is continuity of that supply path. This is narrower than saying the entire vehicle battery is weak or that every ECM power input failed.

## Why key-off history matters

The circuit supports the controller's engine-off timing information. A supply interruption between operating periods can therefore matter even when the engine later starts and charging voltage looks normal. A current running-voltage reading does not reconstruct whether the permanent feed remained available during the preceding interval.

The table identifies interruption through an open circuit as the monitored event. It does not provide a low-voltage threshold, identify a particular fuse or determine whether the interruption was intentional. A documented battery disconnect and an unexplained recurring interruption should be distinguished in the diagnostic history rather than automatically treated as the same repair problem.

## When the controller checks

This implementation enables the check with ignition on and evaluates it once per driving cycle at a 0.01-second rate when its basic conditions are met. It uses event-based failure recognition rather than publishing a duration for how long the battery must be disconnected.

Preserve timing-related companion faults and recent power-service history before clearing the record. Verification should establish continuity through the relevant off/on sequence and a completed monitor result using vehicle-specific service information. The code alone does not justify replacing the ECM, and the source summary does not supply wiring locations or a complete power-distribution test.

## Source and applicability

GM 2015 15OBDG04 ECM Summary Tables, permanent supply interruption monitor on physical page 247. The source does not establish a particular vehicle, engine or wiring layout for this code.
