# P2238: the pump-circuit check uses missing lean response during fuel shutoff

P2238 has a general positive-current circuit-low description, but this GM 2009 row implements a pumping-current circuit check through sensor behavior during fuel shutoff. It asks whether the bank-one upstream air/fuel sensor becomes lean enough under that particular condition.

## Fuel shutoff supplies the diagnostic context

The source uses an air/fuel sensor voltage below 1.7 V after more than three seconds of fuel shutoff. The engine must be running, starting must be complete and the sensor heater must be at operating temperature. Battery voltage is also restricted to the documented range.

That is not an ordinary idle-voltage test. A reading collected while fuel is being delivered, or before the sensor is heated, does not recreate the condition used by this monitor. The table assigns its own observation and persistence timing to the qualified event.

## Do not apply a generic oxygen-sensor voltage rule

Preserve fuel-cut state, elapsed time and the actual air/fuel sensor channel with the reading. The source's sensor technology and calibration matter; a voltage rule for a different oxygen-sensor type cannot be substituted without the correct vehicle information.

The monitor does not prove which part of the pumping-current circuit is defective. It also does not prescribe sensor replacement from the code alone. Use the applicable circuit and response-test procedure to establish the cause. The parameter table supplies no road-test instruction or safe live-probing method, and physical sensor work requires appropriate electrical and exhaust-heat precautions.

## Source and applicability

GM 2009 09OBDG04 Engine Diagnostics, cited bank-one air/fuel pump-circuit response during fuel shutoff monitor only. Document-level evidence does not establish per-code vehicle, engine or wiring applicability.
