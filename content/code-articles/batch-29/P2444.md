# P2444: AIR pump stuck-on wording can describe a control-circuit fault

P2444 is commonly described as a secondary-air pump stuck on. In the cited GM Group 04 implementation, however, the secondary-air pump row assigns P2444 to circuit continuity toward voltage. The monitor is observing an electrical control condition through the output driver's internal voltage assessment.

## The adjacent codes clarify the meaning

The same row assigns P0418 to an open circuit and P2445 to continuity toward ground. That grouping is important: this implementation is distinguishing control-circuit failure modes. It does not directly measure pump rotation or establish that a relay has physically welded closed.

A pump that can be heard running and an electrical driver fault are separate observations. Either can help a technician understand the event, but neither should silently replace the other in the diagnostic record. The table provides no external probe voltage or connector pin test.

## Both command states matter

The source requires the output to be activated and deactivated for complete checking. Engine speed must exceed 80 rpm, and battery voltage must lie above 9.99 V and below 17.99 V. The listed fault recognition interval is 0.2 seconds, with illumination after two driving cycles.

Those conditions explain why observing only a commanded-on event may leave the check incomplete. Verification should establish that the appropriate electrical monitor completed across its required states, not simply that pump noise stopped after a reset. Use the vehicle's wiring and control strategy to distinguish the driver, circuit and load before considering replacement.

## Source and applicability

GM 2009 09OBDG04 Engine Diagnostics, only the cited secondary-air monitor. The file does not establish the applicability of this code to a particular model or engine; thresholds are calibration-specific.
