# P2440: AIR valve stuck-open evidence comes from a closed-valve phase

P2440 concerns a secondary-air shut-off valve that appears to remain open. In the cited GM single-bank monitor, the revealing operating state is Phase 2: the AIR pump is commanded on while the valve is commanded closed. The controller evaluates the resulting pressure error rather than directly observing the valve's internal position.

## The sequence makes the result meaningful

Phase 1 of the AIR diagnostic must already have passed, and Phase 2 requires more than 0.5 seconds of stability. Those prerequisites matter. An arbitrary pump-on pressure reading taken before the diagnostic sequence is not equivalent to the controlled closed-valve observation.

The failure criterion includes pressure error below a calibrated valve-error table or above 32 kPa. Because the lower boundary is table-based, this summary does not provide a single universal pressure number for condemning the valve.

## Why a warm restart may prove little

This calibration requires more than 3,600 seconds of engine-off time, inlet air above 5°C and coolant between 5°C and 60°C. It runs once per trip when the AIR pump is commanded on. Conditional test weight exceeds two seconds and incorporates barometric pressure, airflow, supply voltage and ambient temperature factors.

A useful event record therefore links valve command, pump command, diagnostic phase and pressure response. Seeing the pump run or clearing the code does not establish that Phase 2 passed. The table supports a pressure-based stuck-open finding; it does not identify a particular mechanical obstruction, locate the valve or establish a part replacement without vehicle-specific checks.

## Source and applicability

GM 2009 09OBDG07 Engine Diagnostics, single-bank secondary AIR application on physical page 124. This article does not extend its calibration to the separate dual-bank implementation or establish per-model fitment.
