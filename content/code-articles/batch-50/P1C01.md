# P1C01: The high-pressure switching valve requires a control-circuit diagnosis

## The monitor concerns the valve command path

The Ariya heat-pump controller's P1C01-71 entry identifies an error in the high-pressure refrigerant channel switching-valve control signal. It is monitored with power on for at least four seconds, and compressor operation stops. The possible causes include the valve, controller and an open or shorted circuit.

This wording does not prove that the valve is mechanically stuck or that refrigerant pressure itself exceeds a limit. A control-signal error must be distinguished from the physical flow condition that the valve is intended to manage.

## Supply, signal wiring and coil condition lead to different branches

Nissan first checks valve supply, then continuity of the control wire and unwanted connections to ground or voltage. Only after the wiring checks does the flow assess the valve itself and distinguish it from the heat-pump controller.

The component reference gives an approximate coil resistance at a stated temperature. That temperature qualifier matters; a measured coil value should be compared with the applicable specification, not treated as a universal reading regardless of conditions or valve type.

Read the result under HEAT PUMP CONTROL and retain related thermal-system faults before erasure. Refrigerant-component service and EV thermal-system access require the correct professional procedures. Replacing the compressor because it stopped would ignore the upstream valve-control condition that can command that protective response.
