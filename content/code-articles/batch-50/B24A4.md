# B24A4: An implausibly hot intake reading can be a circuit short

## Recognized temperature is not automatically actual air temperature

The Ariya HVAC B24A4-11 monitor detects an intake-sensor recognized temperature above 100°C for at least two seconds with power on. Compressor operation is stopped. The possible causes include the sensor, A/C auto amplifier and a signal circuit short to ground.

The wording recognized temperature matters: the controller derives a temperature from the electrical signal. An implausible value can therefore result from a circuit condition rather than air genuinely reaching that temperature. The DTC alone cannot decide between those explanations.

## Compare signal behavior with the actual sensing circuit

Nissan checks the sensor signal at the auto amplifier, then the sensor's supply and component condition. The flow also investigates an unwanted ground connection before reaching its controller branch. This separates the sensor's resistance behavior from a damaged harness and from the receiving electronics.

The source provides a temperature-dependent resistance table, not one resistance value that is valid under every condition. A technician must relate a measurement to the sensor's actual temperature and use the correct intake-sensor specification rather than another HVAC thermistor's curve.

Save the HVAC scan and any observed intake-temperature data before clearing memory. A compressor that remains off can be responding to this unreliable input; replacing the compressor would not address the sensing path that caused the stop condition.
