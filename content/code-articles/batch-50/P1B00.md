# P1B00: Check the battery controller supply before blaming the traction cells

## The monitored voltage feeds the controller

In the Ariya 91 kWh battery procedure, P1B00-16 concerns the Li-ion battery controller's power-supply voltage falling below a specified level. The power-on diagnosis is listed as two seconds or less. The entry does not publish the voltage limit and should not be described as a measured low traction-cell voltage.

Nissan associates the condition with restricted driving output, stopped charging and an EV-system warning. Those responses explain the possible complaint, but do not turn a controller-supply fault into evidence that the entire battery pack has worn out.

## Supply and ground are the first diagnostic path

The factory flow checks the LBC supply and ground circuit. If those checks are normal, it directs an intermittent-fault investigation rather than immediate battery replacement. That distinction is valuable when a stored event cannot be reproduced at the workshop.

Preserve both HIGH VOLTAGE BATTERY diagnostic results and the circumstances in which the warning appeared. Recent low-voltage electrical work may be relevant history, but is not proof of the cause. Circuit access around the traction battery requires trained EV personnel using the correct 91 kWh service information and high-voltage precautions. The source gives no basis for guessing a replacement controller from this code alone.

The confirmation reads both HIGH VOLTAGE BATTERY groups. Recording only one group's result can omit part of the information the published procedure explicitly asks the technician to retain before an intermittent investigation.
