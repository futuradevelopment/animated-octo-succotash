# P1B03: An internal current-sensor report still starts with its circuit

## Sensor self-diagnosis is the monitored evidence

P1B03-01 in the Ariya 91 kWh battery procedure concerns an internal battery-current-sensor malfunction detected by self-diagnosis. The signal is communicated through CAN, and the power-on diagnosis is listed as two seconds or less. The entry does not state that battery current itself has exceeded a numerical limit.

Nissan lists a driving-output limit, charging stop and EV-system warning as the protective pattern. Those effects follow the unreliable sensing condition; they are not independent proof of a shorted traction battery or abnormal driver demand.

## The circuit determines the component branch

The diagnostic flow checks the current-sensor circuit before reaching replacement of the battery junction box, which contains the sensor. An abnormal circuit result is repaired through that path instead. This distinction prevents an integrated sensor's location from being mistaken for a requirement to replace the whole battery assembly.

Retain the complete HIGH VOLTAGE BATTERY results and any simultaneous current-related faults. A technician needs the installed battery's diagrams and isolation process to investigate the circuit safely. This article explains why the sensor report, communication path and integrated component are separate pieces of evidence; it does not supply instructions for opening or probing a live battery junction box.

The replacement reference identifies the sensor as built into the battery junction box. That integration explains the named assembly in the final branch; it does not imply that a separately replaceable universal current sensor will satisfy the procedure.
