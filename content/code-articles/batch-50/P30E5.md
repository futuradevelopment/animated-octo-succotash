# P30E5: The rear oil-pump procedure uses a coolant-pump scan label

## Follow the monitored signal, not just the heading

The Ariya rear-motor P30E5-04 entry is labeled Drive Motor B Coolant Pump Control, but its detection table names the rear traction-motor oil-pump signal. It describes receiving an error notification in READY within ten seconds. This terminology mismatch is retained as a source-review issue rather than silently rewriting the factory label.

The listed response limits rear traction-motor torque to 30 percent. The possible causes include oil-pump and oil-temperature-sensor circuits, the rear motor assembly and rear inverter. The label alone therefore cannot support replacing an unrelated external coolant pump.

## The oil-temperature fault memory is checked first

Nissan first looks for rear-motor oil-temperature-sensor DTCs. If one is present, its circuit is diagnosed; otherwise the flow proceeds to the rear oil-pump circuit. That branch makes companion-code evidence important before interpreting the pump notification.

Retain the complete REAR MOTOR CONTROL scan and any recent drive-unit work for the technician. Because this investigation concerns an electric drive unit, component access and circuit testing require the correct EV service precautions. Obtain the matching manual's terminology and circuit references before selecting parts; this article does not resolve the heading discrepancy by assumption.

The listed electrical possibilities include both the oil-temperature-sensor path and oil-pump path. This explains why a sensor-related companion fault is checked first, even though the headline refers to a pump-control complaint.
