# C105D: A low-fluid signal and an open level-switch circuit share this code

## The code does not distinguish fluid level by itself

The Ariya C105D-7B table includes two conditions: a low brake-fluid-level signal and an open level-switch circuit. Both use a duration of at least five seconds under the stated power-on conditions. Possible causes extend from actual fluid level to the switch, wiring, combination meter and ABS unit.

That dual definition matters because a warning is not automatically proof of an empty reservoir, and an apparently adequate level does not eliminate an electrical sensing fault. Actual low fluid still requires prompt professional assessment of the brake system; merely topping up is not an explanation for why the level changed.

## Begin with the physical condition, then follow the signal

Nissan checks fluid level first and repeats self-diagnosis after the prescribed correction. The later flow examines switch and meter connections, the switch itself, signal continuity and ground before the meter and ABS branches.

This sequence preserves the difference between the measured condition and the path used to report it. A faulty connector can affect the warning without establishing a hydraulic leak, while a genuine low level must not be dismissed as a sensor problem without inspection.

Save the complete brake scan and current-versus-past status before clearing memory. If braking performance is uncertain or fluid loss is suspected, arrange safe professional service rather than driving to force the warning to recur. Hydraulic and electrical repairs require the complete factory procedure.
