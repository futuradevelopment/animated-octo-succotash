# C106F: Nissan Ariya steering-angle sensor fault

## What the brake controller reports

C106F-54 is a steering-angle sensor fault in the cited Nissan Ariya brake procedure. The controller evaluates the CAN signal with the power switch on and normal supply voltage; the listed detection delay is two seconds or less. This entry does not provide a numeric angle threshold, so a particular steering-wheel offset cannot be declared the trigger from this source alone.

Possible causes include incorrect sensor installation, wheel alignment, wiring or connector problems, CAN communication, the sensor itself, and ABS-controller supply or internal faults. The source also lists the 12-volt battery, fuses, and fusible links. That range explains why the sensor name on a scan report is not, by itself, a replacement instruction.

## How the factory sequence narrows the cause

The technician first performs the prescribed steering-angle neutral adjustment and repeats ABS self-diagnosis. A continuing current fault leads to checks of the sensor and ABS connectors. After any connection repair, the controller is tested again before diagnosis advances.

The sensor supply is expected to change from approximately zero volts with power off to 10–16 volts with power on in the cited circuit. Supply troubleshooting separates ProPILOT Assist 2.0 equipment from the alternative installation. Ground integrity, ABS power and ground, and CAN communication are then checked before steering-angle live data determines the final sensor-versus-controller branch.

The manual distinguishes CRNT from PAST and rejects ABS replacement for a historical record alone. It also lists several stability, traction, brake-assistance, and regenerative-coordination functions as suspended. A qualified brake technician should assess those effects and complete the model-matched calibration rather than treating code clearing as a completed repair.
