# B24A9: Low light can explain one sunload-sensor fault variant

## Illumination is part of the test environment

The Ariya sunload-sensor entry B24A9-11 detects a recognized level of at least 1516 W/m², while -15 detects 35.3 W/m² or less. Both use a duration of at least two seconds. These are controller-recognized values, and the respective circuit possibilities include a ground short for -11 and an open or supply short for -15.

Nissan specifically notes that -15 can appear indoors, at dusk or in insufficient light. It calls for appropriate lamp illumination during indoor diagnosis. A dark workshop is therefore not a neutral test condition for this particular sensor.

## Observe response, then separate supply and wiring

The procedure checks that the sensor voltage changes as a light source is moved relative to it. It then investigates supply and, for the relevant branch, ground continuity and unwanted voltage. That changing response gives different information from one static reading in unknown lighting.

Do not replace the sensor merely because a low-light code was recorded during an indoor scan. Preserve the suffix and lighting conditions, then have the technician use the defined confirmation and correct circuit information.

Conversely, suitable illumination does not rule out a real wiring or sensor fault if the condition remains. The source's environmental note explains a specific diagnostic caveat; it is not a general instruction to dismiss all sunload-sensor codes as normal.
