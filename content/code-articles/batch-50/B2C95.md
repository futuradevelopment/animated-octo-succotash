# B2C95: A combination-switch fault can deliberately turn the low beams on

## Unexpected lighting can be a protective response

The Ariya BCM's B2C95-01 entry detects a combination-switch circuit short to battery. B2C95-64 instead describes switch-check diode positions remaining on. Both are monitored with the power switch off in AUTO ACC and use a duration of at least two seconds.

Nissan lists the low-beam headlamps turning on as the response. Lights remaining on in that circumstance can therefore be a consequence of the diagnosed switch condition, rather than independent proof of a failed headlamp relay. The source still lists the switch, wiring and BCM as possible origins.

## The suffix determines how the switch is investigated

For -01, the flow checks for unwanted supply voltage with the relevant connectors disconnected, then examines BCM combination-switch data. That separates the external circuit from the switch information being interpreted by the body controller. The connector pin list belongs to this vehicle's procedure and should not be transferred to another model.

The -64 branch directs the combination-switch symptom table rather than prescribing the same short-to-battery measurement as its entire diagnosis. Preserve the exact suffix and which lighting or wiper commands behaved incorrectly.

A workshop should evaluate the system in the stated AUTO ACC condition and retain the original scan before clearing it. Replacing the switch solely because the low beams stayed on would skip the diagnostic distinction between actual command, circuit condition and protective response.
