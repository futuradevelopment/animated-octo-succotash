# P1C64: Voltage and frequency faults share an overvoltage heading

## The suffix changes the electrical quantity

The Ariya charger's P1C64-17 row describes excessive AC input voltage during normal charging, with a two-second detection time. P1C64-38 instead describes an AC frequency error, with a maximum detection time of 100 seconds. Both stop normal charging, but voltage and frequency are different measurements.

The -38 section retains an AC overvoltage heading despite its frequency criterion. This labeling inconsistency is marked for source review. The source gives no numerical voltage or frequency boundary here, so no universal mains specification is inferred.

## Change the supply environment before condemning the charger

Nissan's confirmation uses a different charging environment and observes normal charging for at least 120 seconds. For -17, the flow also checks VCM faults and compares a known-working EVSE before the charger branch. For -38, the source specifically emphasizes investigating the commercial supply environment.

The charger, EVSE and building supply are separate parts of that comparison. A fault that follows one location provides different evidence from a fault repeated with verified equipment elsewhere. Record the station or outlet and exact suffix rather than describing every interrupted session as a vehicle overvoltage problem.

Charging-installation checks belong with qualified personnel. Do not use extension cables, improvised adapters or repeated attempts on visibly damaged equipment to overcome the stop condition; those actions cannot validate the diagnostic comparison.
