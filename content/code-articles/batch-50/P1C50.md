# P1C50: A CAN-controller label leads to charger-temperature diagnosis

## The temperature row explains the actual monitor

The Ariya charger procedure labels P1C50-4B as CAN controller, but its detection row concerns high internal on-board charger temperature during normal charging. The listed detection time is two seconds. The source provides no numerical temperature limit, and this mismatch between label and monitored condition remains a source-review flag.

Nissan pauses normal charging as the protective response and states that charging can resume once temperature falls below a fixed value. A pause that later clears can therefore be consistent with the thermal response, without proving that the underlying cooling problem has been repaired.

## Cooling-system evidence comes before charger replacement

The procedure explicitly prioritizes the high-voltage cooling system. It checks EV/HEV faults, then coolant quantity and leakage. Those steps distinguish a charger becoming hot because its cooling is impaired from an internal charger fault.

The later observation uses normal charging from a low state of charge for at least an hour before its replacement branch. That extended test is different from the initial shorter confirmation and belongs with a technician who has established safe operating conditions.

Preserve how long charging ran before the interruption and any coolant-related warning. Do not repeatedly restart an overheating charger or use extension leads to work around the complaint. The correct cooling and charging procedures must be matched to the installed equipment before a component decision.
