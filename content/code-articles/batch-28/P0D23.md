# P0D23: Unexpected charger-bus current during precharge

## Why a small current matters here

P0D23 is labeled charger output shorted in GM’s table, but the detection condition is more specific than a general high-current warning. Charger-bus current must exceed 0.35 amp while the multipurpose contactor is open and the charger contactor is in its precharge state. System voltage must exceed 9 volts.

The monitor also requires no active heating or cooling and no listed faults affecting the high-voltage output-current signal. These exclusions explain why a current value that might be expected in another operating mode can be suspicious during this particular sequence. The threshold is not a maximum allowable current for normal battery charging.

## Separate the measurement from the diagnosis

The controller requires four failing observations among five samples taken at 100-millisecond intervals. Its passing period is 0.5 second. A technician should preserve the precharge state, bus-current value and thermal operating mode together; a later normal charging reading does not recreate the conditions that produced the code.

Current-signal fault exclusions are equally significant. If related sensor or communication faults are present, investigate their effect on the current evidence using the applicable service procedure. A reported current is not automatically an independently verified current flow.

The monitor supports an unexpected-current conclusion under constrained conditions. It does not establish the physical location of a short, prove a particular contactor is welded or justify replacement of the charger without further evidence. Precharge involves the high-voltage system, so the table must not be used as instructions for bridging contactors or conducting energized output tests.

## Source and applicability

15OBDG01 Hybrid Diagnostics, cited controller and monitor only. Historical calibration limits are not universal specifications; per-code vehicle, engine and hardware applicability must be verified.
