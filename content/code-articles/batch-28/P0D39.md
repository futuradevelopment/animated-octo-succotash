# P0D39: AC input-current sensor zero-offset rationality

## A zero-offset check rather than an overload test

The P0D39 row in GM’s charger-control tables evaluates the AC input-current sensor’s zero offset. Its failure criterion is an AC current value of at least 1.4 amps under the specified test conditions. The strategy identifies an implausible offset; it is different from concluding that the vehicle genuinely draws excessive charging current.

The monitor requires AC voltage above 80 volts, no P0D3F or P0D40 AC voltage-sensor faults and expiration of the power-factor-correction discharge delay. These conditions make the timing of the observation important. A current reading collected during a different charger state cannot simply be compared with the zero-offset threshold.

## Interpreting persistence and recovery

The failure must be present for 512 milliseconds within a 640-millisecond window. Passing requires current below 1.4 amps for 640 milliseconds. Equality belongs to the failing side of the printed criterion; an indicated 1.4 amps is not below the stated pass limit.

A diagnostic record should separate the current reading used for this offset check from normal charging-current measurements. It should also establish whether the discharge-delay condition was fulfilled and whether the voltage-sensor fault exclusions were satisfied. Otherwise the evidence may describe an unqualified observation rather than the monitor that set P0D39.

These distinctions support a focused sensor-rationality investigation, but the summary does not identify a repairable internal part or authorize a charger replacement. The stated delay condition is not a declaration that high-voltage components are safe to touch. Physical testing requires the applicable isolation and service procedure.

## Source and applicability

15OBDG01 Hybrid Diagnostics, cited controller and monitor only. Historical calibration limits are not universal specifications; per-code vehicle, engine and hardware applicability must be verified.
