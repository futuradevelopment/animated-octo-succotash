# P0B95: Sense S is an electrical-pattern diagnosis

P0B95 names voltage-sense channel S within the cited battery controller's open-line checks. The diagnosis concerns the integrity of a measurement path. It is different from estimating how much energy the battery can hold, even when both concerns appear in a workshop discussion.

## What the controller is comparing

For a general sensing-line interruption, GM compares the affected reading with an adjacent cell reading and uses a difference greater than one volt. A separate common-power-line pattern uses a difference greater than half a volt. The comparison must persist over the documented 600-millisecond window.

The source also describes a power-loss case in which a slave measuring circuit is unpowered and all affected readings become zero. Consequently, the distribution of abnormal readings can be more informative than the lowest displayed number alone.

## Preserve the enabling conditions

Sensing-module communication must be available, and specified balancing or internal-performance faults must not be active. The protection self-test must not be running. Those prerequisites explain why a technician should document companion faults before using an absent retest failure as confirmation.

The letter S is a controller channel designation, not a universal pack-access location. A qualified high-voltage specialist should identify the correct hardware mapping and service procedure. The table's incomplete busbar branch prevents reproducing a complete decision tree, but the supported adjacent-reading patterns remain useful evidence for that investigation.
