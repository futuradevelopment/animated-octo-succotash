# P013E: Initial rear oxygen-sensor delay when fuel is cut

P013E describes a delayed rich-to-lean response from Bank 1 Sensor 2. In this GM monitor, the controller first establishes a rich catalyst condition, then commands deceleration fuel cut-off and watches for the rear sensor to begin responding.

## The decision is based on air mass

The failure combination is a rear oxygen-sensor voltage still above 450 millivolts after more than 60 grams of air have accumulated during the test. The conditioning stage requires the catalyst to have been rich for more than two seconds and rich-stage accumulated airflow to exceed 12 grams.

Those details distinguish this diagnostic from a simple low-voltage circuit check. The question is whether the signal leaves its rich state promptly after a controlled change. A voltage captured before that change cannot answer it.

## Why the first response matters

This is the second stage of the documented rear-sensor sequence, after P2270 and before the later slow-response evaluation. P013E therefore addresses the initial delay, while a subsequent monitor can evaluate the remainder of the transition.

Useful evidence includes the conditioning state, fuel-cut command, rear voltage and accumulated airflow. Learned heater resistance must be valid, and several sensor, fueling and misfire faults can inhibit the procedure. Without those prerequisites, an absent result should not be called a pass.

The source lists Type B, two-trip reporting. It does not identify a single repair. A sound verification repeats the controller's valid sequence and confirms the initial response meets the applicable calibration, rather than merely checking that the voltage can go lean sometime during driving.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the 2025 GM 25OBDG03A monitor on [page 177](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=177), [page 178](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=178). Its thresholds are calibration examples, not universal specifications. Confirm the vehicle, engine and controller procedure before applying them.
