# P0191: Comparing two digital fuel-rail pressure measurements

P0191 is often summarized as fuel-rail pressure sensor range/performance. The cited GM implementation is specifically a rationality comparison between a primary control sensor and a secondary diagnostic sensor. It looks for a skewed primary measurement while the engine is running and commanded pressure is sufficiently steady.

## Agreement and control error both matter

The strategy filters the difference between the two pressure channels and requires an absolute separation of at least 1.00 MPa, together with a fuel-control-error limit. That error uses a filtered relationship based on the square root of the sensor ratio. Its low and high limits are supplied in separate supporting tables, not as one universal number on this page.

The required failure duration is at least 1.50 seconds. This prevents interpreting a brief mismatch during an unrelated pressure transition as the entire diagnostic.

## What to compare next

Record both sensor values, their validity and the commanded-pressure trend together. A disagreement confined to rapidly changing demand needs different interpretation from persistent disagreement during the monitor's steady-command window. The source does not say that whichever displayed value looks more plausible must be correct.

Verify the applicable comparison limits and communication prerequisites before selecting a sensor or controller repair. Following corrective work, confirm the two-channel rationality monitor completes without the qualifying discrepancy. This is high-pressure fuel-system evidence; any physical pressure testing requires approved equipment and the vehicle's professional service procedure.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the 2025 GM 25OBDG03A monitor on [page 206](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=206). Its thresholds are calibration examples, not universal specifications. Confirm the vehicle, engine and controller procedure before applying them.
