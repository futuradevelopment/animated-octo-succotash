# P0300: How crankshaft motion becomes evidence of random misfire

P0300 reports random or multiple-cylinder misfire. The cited GM strategy infers combustion irregularity from crankshaft velocity, deceleration and subsequent acceleration patterns. It compares the rate of qualifying events with separate emissions and catalyst-damage thresholds.

## Why every speed disturbance is not a misfire

The controller checks the motion following a candidate event to distinguish combustion loss from disturbances such as rough road or driveline ringing. Some observation windows can be discarded when their patterns are abnormal. Related crank/cam faults, synchronization status and unlearned crankshaft variation can also affect monitoring.

Thus, absence of new counts during an inhibited or discarded window is not a clean bill of health. Record monitor eligibility together with cylinder counts, speed/load and the fault's operating conditions.

## Pattern and severity guide the next decision

The document handles single-cylinder, random, paired and other patterns with different threshold adjustments. P0300 alone does not identify ignition, fueling or mechanical compression as the cause. Cylinder-specific distribution and accompanying circuit faults supply the next useful discrimination.

The source explicitly associates a flashing malfunction lamp with catalyst-damaging misfire levels. That is a reason to end a reproduction attempt and arrange appropriate diagnosis, not to continue loading the engine to gather more counts.

Verification should show stable combustion evidence in the previously affected operating region while the monitor is eligible. Clearing codes or observing smooth idle cannot establish that a load-dependent misfire is resolved.
