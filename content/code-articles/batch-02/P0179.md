# P0179: High ethanol-sensor frequency and the separate conductivity boundary

P0179 identifies a high fuel-composition sensor signal in this GM FTZM-based implementation. The measured quantity is frequency communicated by the Fuel Tank Zone Module, rather than a generic analog voltage. The documented P0179 interval is above 155 hertz through 185 hertz.

## Why the upper boundary matters

The source's ordinary composition scale reaches 150 hertz at E100. P0179 therefore describes a reading beyond the expected composition range, not simply a tank containing a high ethanol percentage. The document additionally says frequencies beyond its high-conductivity threshold are handled by P2269 instead.

That distinction makes the raw value useful. A scan tool showing a capped percentage may conceal whether the underlying frequency belongs to this circuit-high interval or the separate conductivity diagnostic.

## Check the reporting path

The test needs ignition voltage above 11 volts and a vehicle configuration with the FTZM and flex-fuel sensor. Module-reset and relevant communication faults inhibit it. Its 50-of-63 sample counter runs at 100-millisecond intervals, with Type A, one-trip reporting.

A practical interpretation is to establish that the module is delivering valid data, then examine the actual out-of-range frequency. Do not infer contamination or an electrical repair solely from the code label. After diagnosis, confirm the appropriate frequency monitor passes with communication valid; a plausible displayed ethanol percentage alone is incomplete verification.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the 2025 GM 25OBDG03A monitor on [page 197](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=197). Its thresholds are calibration examples, not universal specifications. Confirm the vehicle, engine and controller procedure before applying them.
