# P0238: A high boost-sensor signal can also indicate an open circuit

P0238 concerns the turbocharger boost-pressure sensor's high signal in this GM table. The diagnostic is designed to recognize a continuous short to power or an open circuit. It compares the output with a limit above 90 percent of the five-volt range.

## Why apparent pressure can mislead

The table maps the boundary to 390 kPa absolute for the documented sensor. That does not mean P0238 proves the engine actually reached that pressure. An electrical opening can also make the signal enter the high region, so apparent extreme boost must first be separated from circuit behavior.

The monitor counts 320 failures among 400 samples at 12.5-millisecond intervals and reports as Type A, one-trip. A present normal reading does not erase the significance of a previously captured continuous high event.

## Verification should address the signal path

Record the high output, its pressure conversion and fault timing. Establish the correct sensor circuit and reference arrangement from vehicle-specific information. A pressure value generated from the same suspect voltage is not an independent second measurement.

The source supports investigating high/open signal behavior; it does not prescribe a sensor, wiring or turbocharger replacement. Confirm that the enabled circuit-high test completes without renewed failures after the actual cause is addressed. Any comparison with pressure demand must use consistent absolute-pressure units and valid sensor data.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the 2025 GM 25OBDG03A monitor on [page 222](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=222). Its thresholds are calibration examples, not universal specifications. Confirm the vehicle, engine and controller procedure before applying them.
