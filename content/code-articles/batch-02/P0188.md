# P0188: Second fuel-temperature channel high and the raw-value naming issue

P0188 refers to Fuel Temperature Sensor B circuit high. The documented GM strategy identifies the second SENT fuel-rail temperature sensor and monitors for an excessively high digital read value. It is a digital range diagnosis, so the code should not automatically be translated into actual fuel overheating.

## Check channel identity before numbers

The source row's title and strategy say sensor 2, but its measured-variable column says sensor 1. That conflict prevents treating the printed numeric limit as an independently verified sensor-2 specification. Correcting the label by assumption would hide a real limitation of the evidence.

Other details are consistent: the page lists 50 failures out of 62 samples at 100 milliseconds each, Type B reporting over two trips, and inhibition by P126F internal errors or P128D temperature-message errors. Relevant SENT communication faults also prevent this out-of-range evaluation.

## A defensible next check

Use the vehicle's message and sensor definitions to confirm which raw temperature field the controller evaluates. Then compare that field with its communication validity and internal status. A stale or invalid message requires a different investigation from a valid high payload.

For verification, retain a completed monitor result after the applicable prerequisites are met. A temporary return to a plausible decoded temperature is useful but cannot resolve the source's channel-label discrepancy. This draft explains the supported diagnostic logic and marks the uncertain mapping for review instead of inventing a universal test value.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the 2025 GM 25OBDG03A monitor on [page 201](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=201). Its thresholds are calibration examples, not universal specifications. Confirm the vehicle, engine and controller procedure before applying them.
