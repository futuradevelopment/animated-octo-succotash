# P0521: Oil-pressure plausibility depends on pump stage and operating state

P0521 evaluates an engine-oil pressure sensor that may be stuck or biased within range. The cited GM section applies to a two-stage oil pump and describes separate running comparisons for the pump's high- and low-pressure states.

## Match the pressure to the commanded stage

Measured filtered pressure is compared with a stage-specific modeled value and allowed deviation. The running test requires speed between 1,400 and 4,500 rpm, modeled oil temperature between 50°C and 110°C, and more than 1.60 seconds since a pump-state change. A pressure reading taken during a stage transition should not be judged against a steady-state expectation.

The strategy also excludes an oil-aeration condition defined from sustained high-speed operation. That gate changes whether a pressure discrepancy is eligible for this sensor-rationality judgment.

## Engine-off logic has an explicit limitation

The section describes an engine-off test but marks that test disabled in the cited calibration. Its printed engine-off threshold should not be presented as an active universal P0521 rule.

Record pressure, pump state, oil temperature and engine speed together. The code cannot by itself distinguish a biased signal from an actual lubrication-pressure problem. Follow the applicable professional pressure-verification procedure before deciding on a sensor or pump repair, and do not keep running an engine with an active oil-pressure warning merely to complete this monitor. Confirm a passing eligible rationality test after diagnosis.
