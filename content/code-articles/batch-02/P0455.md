# P0455: Weak EVAP vacuum can mean a large leak or a purge blockage

P0455 is commonly described as a large EVAP leak. The cited GM monitor is broader: it detects weak vacuum caused by a large leak or purge blockage. During qualifying operation, it closes the vent and controls purge while tracking the volume of displaced vapor.

## Volume and vacuum are compared

The failure combination is purge volume above six litres while tank vacuum remains below 2,740 Pa. That explains why the code cannot locate a loose cap solely from its name. A system that cannot draw purge flow effectively can also fail to create the expected vacuum.

The test runs once per cold start and can abort after 1,400 seconds. Fuel level, startup temperatures, barometric pressure and circuit/message validity determine eligibility. A short hot restart may not reproduce the primary cold-start test.

## Understand a follow-up pass

The source also describes a weak-vacuum follow-up that can run after a failure or on a hot restart. That follow-up can report a pass only. Its purpose and reporting behavior differ from the original failing test, so record which result is being observed.

Use the vehicle-specific leak and flow procedure to separate loss of sealing from restricted purge. Verify adequate vacuum development through a completed applicable monitor after correction. The document discusses cap-related reporting behavior but does not establish a cap as the cause of every P0455.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the 2025 GM 25OBDG03A monitor on [page 295](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=295), [page 296](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=296). Its thresholds are calibration examples, not universal specifications. Confirm the vehicle, engine and controller procedure before applying them.
