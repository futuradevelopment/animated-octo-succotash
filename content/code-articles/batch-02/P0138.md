# P0138: Understanding a rear oxygen-sensor signal above its limit

For the single-bank-exhaust system described in the cited GM table, P0138 means the Bank 1 Sensor 2 oxygen-sensor signal remained excessively high. The monitor is intended to detect a signal circuit shorted high or open. Its threshold is above 1,050 millivolts, with 100 failures out of 125 samples in a 100-millisecond loop.

## Why an open circuit remains relevant

A high reading should not automatically be translated into a rich-running engine. This implementation explicitly includes an open signal circuit among the conditions it is designed to detect. Determining whether the scan value reflects exhaust chemistry or circuit behavior is therefore a necessary diagnostic decision.

The test also waits for heater warm-up and requires all cylinders to be active. It lists more than five seconds of engine run time, more than 30 seconds of accumulated run time, and an additional 210-second delay after the preceding conditions. The published delay applies on both sides of its cold-soak distinction.

## What makes a retest meaningful

Save the signal value, engine operating state and heater readiness before clearing information. Review any accompanying airflow, manifold-pressure, injector or ethanol-composition fault because these appear among the inhibiting conditions.

The source provides Type B, two-trip reporting, not a component-replacement instruction. A verification should show that the high signal no longer occurs once the monitor's delay and operating conditions are satisfied. A brief start-and-idle check may finish before this diagnostic can judge the circuit.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the 2025 GM 25OBDG03A monitor on [page 170](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=170). Its thresholds are calibration examples, not universal specifications. Confirm the vehicle, engine and controller procedure before applying them.
