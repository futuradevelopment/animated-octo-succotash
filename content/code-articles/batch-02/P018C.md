# P018C: Fuel-pressure Sensor B circuit low in two wiring architectures

P018C reports a low fuel-pressure Sensor B circuit signal in this GM table. The controller evaluates a normalized sensor-output percentage, with a published lower boundary of four percent and a count of 64 failures out of 80 samples. Sampling occurs every 12.5 milliseconds.

## Establish where the sensor is connected

The document presents two configuration paths. One covers a sensor wired to the engine controller. The other covers a sensor wired to the Fuel Tank Zone Module, with information forwarded through the sensor bus. Both paths require run/crank to be active and the diagnostic system to be enabled.

The module-wired path additionally requires the sensor-bus relay and the relevant message to be available, with no specified rolling-counter/checksum error. Consequently, investigating only the engine-controller connector can miss the actual signal route on that architecture.

## Low circuit data is not a pressure test

The table expresses its decision in normalized output, not a direct mechanical measurement. Its printed normalization wording should not be used to invent a universal voltage-to-pressure conversion. A low circuit result does not independently prove a failed pump or establish actual fuel pressure.

Save the output percentage, module configuration and message integrity together. Confirm the applicable circuit using vehicle-specific information, then verify that the low-signal monitor passes under run/crank conditions. Any independent fuel-pressure measurement must use the approved professional procedure for that system.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the 2025 GM 25OBDG03A monitor on [page 204](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=204). Its thresholds are calibration examples, not universal specifications. Confirm the vehicle, engine and controller procedure before applying them.
