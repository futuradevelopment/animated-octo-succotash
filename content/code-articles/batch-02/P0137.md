# P0137: Why a rear oxygen-sensor low signal needs operating context

P0137 concerns the Bank 1 Sensor 2 oxygen-sensor signal. In the cited single-bank-exhaust GM monitor, the controller looks for a signal below 40 millivolts and counts failures across a sampling window. The table specifies 320 failing samples out of 400, with a 100-millisecond execution loop and Type B, two-trip reporting.

## Low voltage is not the entire test

This monitor is enabled in closed-loop operation with commanded equivalence ratio between 0.991 and 1.040. Deceleration fuel cut-off must not be active, and the active cylinders' injectors must be enabled. Those restrictions matter: a low sensor value observed while fuel is intentionally cut is not the same evidence as a low value under the stated fueling conditions.

A second distinction is the enabling network. Throttle, manifold-pressure, injector and several EVAP faults can inhibit the test. Low fuel must be false when fuel-level information is valid. An unresolved related fault may therefore change both the sensor environment and whether P0137 can be retested.

## Interpreting the evidence

Record the downstream signal with commanded mixture, closed-loop status and injector/fuel-cut state. A persistently low signal during the enabled window supports investigating the signal circuit described by this monitor. It does not, by itself, establish a failed sensor or identify a fuel-system repair.

After diagnosis, look for a completed monitor under comparable conditions. A normal-looking value during an unrelated operating state is a weaker verification than a recorded passing test.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the 2025 GM 25OBDG03A monitor on [page 168](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=168), [page 169](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=169). Its thresholds are calibration examples, not universal specifications. Confirm the vehicle, engine and controller procedure before applying them.
