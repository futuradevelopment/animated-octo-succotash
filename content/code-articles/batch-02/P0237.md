# P0237: Boost-pressure signal below its electrical range

The cited GM P0237 monitor checks the turbocharger boost-pressure signal for continuous short-to-ground behavior. It fails when the output is below 14.4 percent of a five-volt range. The document associates that boundary with 50 kPa absolute pressure for its sensor calibration.

## Absolute pressure and circuit evidence

Absolute pressure is not gauge boost. A display or meter interpretation using a different pressure basis can make a comparison meaningless. More fundamentally, this code is a signal-circuit-low diagnosis: it does not independently prove the turbocharger is producing too little boost.

The controller requires 320 failing observations out of 400, sampled every 12.5 milliseconds. That is counted evidence over a window, rather than an immediate judgment from one low sample. It uses Type A, one-trip reporting in this table.

## What to establish next

Preserve the raw voltage or normalized output alongside the reported absolute pressure. If they are derived from the same signal, their agreement is not independent confirmation of real pressure. Use the vehicle's circuit procedure to determine whether the signal is being held low and whether its reference and return are sound.

After corrective work, verify the low-range monitor passes while enabled. A plausible pressure at one moment is less conclusive than a completed test. This page supplies a calibration-specific signal boundary but no universal sensor location, pinout or turbocharger replacement criterion.

## Source and applicability

This explanation is limited to the 2025 GM 25OBDG03A monitor on [page 221](https://gsi.ext.gm.com/gmspo/mode6/pdf/2025/25OBDG03A.pdf#page=221). Its thresholds are calibration examples, not universal specifications. Confirm the vehicle, engine and controller procedure before applying them.
