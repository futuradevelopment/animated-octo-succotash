# P305D: An implausibly cold downstream-air reading flags the high circuit

## Cold indication does not prove cold intake air

Volkswagen's CGFA P305D entry concerns intake-air temperature sensor two downstream of the throttle. The strategy describes a short to positive or an open circuit and identifies an indicated temperature below negative 39°C. The condition is observed for 25.5 seconds, with the lamp listed after two driving cycles.

This is an interpreted-temperature signature of the electrical monitor. It should not be used to claim that actual air temperature reached that value. It also does not distinguish an open path from a positive short without further circuit evidence.

## Follow G42/G71 rather than an assumed sensor position

The diagnostic column points to G42 together with manifold-pressure sensor G71. The correct vehicle documentation is needed to identify that sensing arrangement and its connections. A generic description reading “sensor two” does not supply a safe physical location or pin assignment.

Preserve related pressure, reference-supply and temperature faults before clearing memory. A technician can then evaluate the circuit and compare the relevant data channels without substituting an upstream temperature reading for the affected downstream channel.

Do not bridge sensor terminals to make the displayed temperature rise or assume that clearing the code repairs an interruption. Verification requires credible temperature feedback over the monitor's observation period and no return of the electrical condition. The low indicated temperature is diagnostic evidence to investigate, not a direct measurement that justifies a cooling-system repair.
