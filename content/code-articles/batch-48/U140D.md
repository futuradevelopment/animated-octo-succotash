# U140D: The reducing-agent controller supply exceeds 16.5 volts

## High supply voltage is the direct fault criterion

In the CPNA/CPNB table, U140D concerns reducing-agent control-module supply voltage above 16.5 volts. The check is active with ignition on and observes the condition for more than 500 milliseconds. The malfunction-lamp schedule is two driving cycles.

The row does not identify a failed charging component or specify why the excessive voltage occurred. It reports the controller's supply condition. It should not be interpreted as high fluid pressure, excessive reducing-agent concentration or an overtemperature fault merely because the affected module controls an emissions system.

## Preserve the electrical event and local measurement context

Audi directs diagnosis to J880 and its metering-system control-module procedure. A technician should retain related voltage and communication records and identify the relevant power and ground paths before deciding whether the cause is local or part of a wider supply problem.

A voltage measurement made after the event may not reproduce the stored condition. The correct investigation considers where and when the module saw the high supply, rather than assuming that replacement of J880 will cure the source of the voltage.

Do not defeat the module's protection or continue an improvised test with excessive supply voltage. Once the cause is corrected, confirmation should show normal supply under eligible operation and no return of the original monitor. Clearing U140D alone cannot establish that the electrical source of the overvoltage has been removed.
