# P310E: EGR cooler bypass plausibility has an undefined threshold unit

## The operating window is more specific than the title

Audi's P310E entry concerns EGR cooler bypass valve two plausibility. The CPNA/CPNB table lists an eleven-second monitor during a constrained operating window: engine speed from 1,200 to 2,500 RPM, limited speed and fuel-quantity variation, SCR temperature above 150°C and EGR temperature below 300°C.

Its fault criterion is printed as measured temperature below 0.8, without a unit or a definition of a normalized quantity. This article does not convert that value into degrees or invent a ratio. The missing definition remains source-review pending.

## Verify the valve mapping before using the thermal test

The title refers to bypass valve two, while the diagnostic destination names N345 and its general switch-over-valve procedure. The actual valve and sensing arrangement must be confirmed against the installed engine's current factory information before applying any detailed test.

A technician can preserve the operating data, related EGR temperature and actuator faults, and the commanded valve state. Those facts help distinguish an invalid measurement from an unexpected thermal response without assuming that the cooler or valve must be replaced.

Do not force an exhaust-system operating condition to chase an undefined threshold. Once the criterion and component mapping are resolved, confirmation should reproduce the proper stable operating window safely. Clearing the warning or observing a warm engine does not establish that this thermal plausibility check has passed.
