# P13E2: Cylinder-three pressure timing requires a reliable reference

## What the row tells a technician to compare

The CRUA/DEJA entry for P13E2 is headed as a cylinder-three pressure-sensor circuit malfunction. Its strategy evaluates the pressure-derived top-dead-centre position during fuel cut, with coolant temperature above 59.96°C. The monitor observes forty revolutions and lists a two-driving-cycle lamp schedule.

The component references are QX3 and crankshaft speed sensor G28. This is significant because a disagreement in timing needs a trustworthy engine-position reference as well as usable pressure feedback. A voltage-only inspection of the pressure sensor cannot demonstrate that the two signals align correctly.

## A source label prevents an unqualified numeric recipe

Inside this cylinder-three row, the threshold description still says sensor one. The article therefore preserves a review flag and does not silently transfer the printed sensor-one angular limit into a certified QX3 test. The exact channel assignment needs confirmation in the matching factory revision.

Keep any accompanying crankshaft, pressure-sensor and plausibility codes available. A workshop should identify QX3 on the actual engine, verify its relevant circuit and determine whether the reference signal is stable before concluding that a component or mechanical timing part has failed.

The documented fuel-cut and warm-coolant conditions are also essential to interpreting a repeat test. A smooth idle after erasing memory may be reassuring operationally, but it is not evidence that this pressure-timing comparison has completed without a fault.
