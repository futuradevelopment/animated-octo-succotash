# P148F: EGR cooler valve two has an electrical off-state fault

## The electrical condition is documented

In the CPNA/CPNB table, P148F concerns EGR cooler switch-over valve two. Its monitor evaluates a power-stage diagnostic signal below 2.97 volts while the power stage is off. The observation period is 480 milliseconds, with a two-driving-cycle lamp schedule.

This is an electrical off-state check, not a direct measurement of EGR flow or cooler effectiveness. A valve that is not currently commanded to move can still have a detectable circuit condition. The code alone does not establish that the cooler is blocked or the valve mechanically stuck.

## Resolve the conflicting valve references

The diagnostic text identifies valve two as N381, but the linked procedure is titled for N345. That mismatch is retained as a source-review flag. This article does not substitute one valve number for the other or provide a terminal sequence that assumes they are interchangeable.

A technician should match the stored P148F record to the installed engine's actual EGR arrangement and current wiring information before testing. Preserve related actuator and supply faults, and keep the commanded-off state attached to any electrical observations.

Do not replace an EGR cooler or force a valve because of the generic label. Once the correct circuit and cause are established, confirmation should show normal off-state driver feedback and absence of the original electrical condition. A change in exhaust temperature or a warning-free restart alone does not verify this monitor.
