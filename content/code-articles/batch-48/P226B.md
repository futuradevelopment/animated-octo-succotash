# P226B: The supercharger pressure check exceeds its mechanical limit

## A high-pressure result still needs reliable measurement

Volkswagen's CGFA P226B entry identifies supercharger boost pressure too high in a mechanical-monitor category. The fault criterion is boost pressure above 250 kilopascals, with a continuous check and no additional debouncing time shown in the row. The malfunction lamp is listed after two driving cycles.

The table does not provide a separate gauge-pressure conversion or a universal target for other engines. Its pressure figure should therefore be interpreted using the correct factory data definition rather than compared with an unrelated dashboard or aftermarket gauge scale.

## The diagnostic references include both drive and feedback

The source directs attention to the supercharger drive belt and tensioner, and to charge-air pressure sensors G31 and G447. This range is useful because the code does not by itself establish that the supercharger hardware is generating excessive pressure or that a specific sensor is faulty. The mechanical result and the credibility of the measured information need to be considered together.

Preserve related boost and pressure-sensor codes and have a technician identify the installed system before selecting tests. Keep clear of moving belt-drive components; an improvised load test is not an appropriate way to confirm a suspected overpressure condition.

After the underlying issue is addressed, verification should show the correct pressure behavior with trustworthy sensor information under the relevant operating conditions. Replacing a belt or clearing memory without confirming the original pressure monitor does not demonstrate a completed repair.
