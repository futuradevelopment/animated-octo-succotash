# P12A2: A high rail-pressure plausibility result combines two controls

## Understand the relationship behind “inappropriately high”

Audi's CREC P12A2 row concerns fuel-rail pressure reported inappropriately high. In its direct-injection phase, lambda-controller deviation exceeds 27.50 percent while high-pressure controller output is below negative twenty-eight milligrams. The observation period is five seconds.

Eligibility includes 576–3,008 RPM, a fuel-flow request of 34.99–60.0 milligrams per revolution, and more than twenty-five seconds after engine start, along with additional stability conditions. The monitor therefore compares coordinated control behavior rather than simply declaring that a sensor voltage exceeded an electrical maximum.

## Verify the pressure system before selecting a component

The diagnostic instruction is to establish whether real fuel pressure is outside specification before investigating G247. That order helps separate a pressure-control problem from misleading sensor feedback. The G247 procedure then provides the relevant reference-supply and harness checks for the actual installation.

A second multiport cross-check phase in the table has unavailable values. This article keeps that limitation flagged and does not convert the partial parameter list into a universal confirmation drive or an inferred missing threshold.

Record related pressure, mixture and injector faults before clearing memory. A technician should evaluate physical pressure and electronic feedback with appropriate high-pressure fuel precautions. The code alone does not justify opening a fuel line, replacing the pump or condemning G247. Verification must address the relationship that produced the plausibility result.
