# P146F: The line-heater current feedback exceeds its upper limit

## Circuit two has its own feedback qualification

Volkswagen's CRUA/DEJA table associates P146F with reducing-agent heater circuit two, covering the AdBlue line and dosing-unit side of the heater arrangement. The current-sensor signal is considered excessively high above 4.7 volts. The observation is 480 milliseconds, with the malfunction lamp listed after two driving cycles.

Unlike the neighboring circuit-one entry, this row also states that the analogue-to-digital converter module must report no fault. That qualifier matters: a high value is interpreted within a monitoring path considered valid, rather than being accepted without regard to the controller's own conversion diagnostics.

## Do not confuse feedback voltage with heater power

The 4.7-volt figure is not a command to apply that voltage to the heater or a direct measure of heating output. The code concerns the current-feedback channel. A technician needs the correct circuit-two identification, controller information and wiring before deciding whether the fault is external or within the assembly.

The source points to J891, but its checking procedure later combines J891 and J880 connector labels. This documentation inconsistency remains flagged; no mixed terminal map is offered here as a definitive test.

Preserve accompanying heater-control and supply faults, then verify both the command and returned feedback using the matching factory information. A change in ambient temperature can alter heater demand without resolving an electrical feedback problem, so absence of immediate recurrence is not by itself a completed verification.
