# P10A8: The bypass lower-stop adaptation has several failure branches

## The lower-limit title does not describe only one voltage direction

The CREC P10A8 row covers supercharger-bypass adaptation. It lists a lower mechanical-stop voltage below 0.35 volts or above 0.65 volts, a difference from the stored stop voltage above 0.40 volts, and a duty-cycle branch at or below zero percent. The grouped event is observed for 0.01 seconds once per driving cycle.

The conditions include a stationary vehicle, engine speed at or below 64 RPM, coolant between 6°C and 105°C, and suitable intake temperature after ignition-on. These are adaptation conditions, not a general running-voltage test for every flap position.

## Evaluate the stored reference and current feedback together

A technician should preserve the learned-state information and related position or actuation faults before resetting anything. The presence of both high and low stop-voltage branches means that the shortened “lower limit not reached” title should not be used to guess which direction the electrical signal failed.

The J808 component reference contains generic turbocharger/wastegate language despite this supercharger-bypass application. That inconsistency remains flagged, and current CREC information is needed to confirm the assembly and appropriate adaptation routine.

Do not force a stop position manually to make a voltage fit a remembered range. Confirmation should show that the intended stop is learned and accepted through the correct event, with stable feedback and no accompanying electrical faults that undermine the result.
