# P10A5: The bypass position signal exceeds 4.90 volts

## The measured quantity is position feedback

Audi's CREC P10A5 row identifies an excessively high signal from the supercharger bypass position sensor. The criterion is voltage above 4.90 volts for half a second. The strategy labels the condition as a short to positive, with the malfunction lamp listed after two driving cycles.

This is not the motor-current test used by neighboring actuation codes. A flap motor could receive a command while its returned position remains electrically invalid. Conversely, a high feedback value does not by itself establish that the flap is physically at its maximum opening.

## Examine the feedback circuit in the right assembly

The diagnostic destination is J808, whose component section identifies separate motor and position-feedback functions. However, that section's general turbocharger/wastegate explanation does not cleanly match the supercharger-bypass monitor. This source inconsistency remains flagged, and the article does not use it to redefine CREC hardware.

A technician should identify the actual sensor reference, return and signal paths from the current wiring information, then compare the stored code with related actuation or adaptation faults. Do not apply external voltage to force a desired position reading or use a generic potentiometer test without confirming the circuit.

After repair, the relevant evidence is believable position feedback throughout the applicable operation and no return of the high-signal fault. Mechanical movement or a single normal reading immediately after clearing memory cannot demonstrate that an intermittent high feedback condition has been eliminated.
