# U140C: The reducing-agent controller supply drops below 8.5 volts

## The voltage belongs to the monitored controller supply

Audi's CPNA/CPNB U140C entry concerns low supply voltage at the reducing-agent control module. With ignition on, the fault criterion is below 8.5 volts for more than 500 milliseconds. The table lists continuous checking and a two-driving-cycle lamp schedule.

This is a supply-monitor result, not a measurement of reducing-agent level, concentration or heater temperature. It also does not independently identify the vehicle battery as the failed part. The relevant voltage is the one seen by the controller and its local electrical path.

## Diagnose the supply path before replacing the module

The factory reference points to reducing-agent metering-system control module J880. A technician should preserve related supply and function-restriction codes, identify the actual module and assess its power and ground arrangement using the correct wiring information.

A healthy-looking battery reading taken at a different point or time does not prove that J880 maintained adequate supply during the stored event. Conversely, a recorded low-voltage condition is not sufficient evidence to condemn the controller without checking the circuit and operating context.

Do not bypass a supply circuit or substitute a larger fuse to remove the warning. After the underlying problem is repaired, verification should show stable module supply and absence of the original low-voltage monitor during eligible operation. Filling the tank, replacing an unrelated sensor or merely clearing fault memory would not address the electrical condition described here.
