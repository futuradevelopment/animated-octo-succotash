# P148C: Unwanted circuit-two heating with an ambiguous printed unit

## What remains clear despite the unit problem

The CRUA/DEJA table assigns P148C to reducing-agent heater circuit two. Its strategy describes unwanted heating activity when SCR heating is commanded off and gives a 500-millisecond monitoring period. The malfunction lamp is listed after two driving cycles.

The threshold line calls its measured quantity conductance but prints “0.1 Sec” as the associated limit. Seconds are not a conductance unit. This article therefore does not silently reinterpret the number as siemens, current or time, and retains a source-review flag.

## Diagnose the unwanted activity without inventing a limit

The defensible distinction is the commanded-off condition versus continuing activity in the line-heater circuit. A technician can preserve the fault, confirm which circuit is involved and inspect its command and feedback using current vehicle-specific information. The ambiguous printed quantity should be resolved before it becomes an acceptance criterion.

The row directs diagnosis to J891. Its component-check section also contains mixed J891/J880 connector labels, reinforcing the need for the correct wiring diagram rather than a generic terminal recipe. Neither inconsistency justifies assuming that the tank heater and line heater are interchangeable.

Do not manually energize the circuit to compensate for an uncertain specification. Repair confirmation should use the verified control procedure to show that circuit-two activity agrees with the requested state. Clearing P148C alone provides no evidence that unwanted off-state heating has ceased.
