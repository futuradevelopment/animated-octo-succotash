# P199E: The coolant-control open circuit is checked with the driver off

## An off-state electrical check

P199E is the open-circuit entry for cylinder-head coolant control in the CRUA/DEJA table. Volkswagen evaluates the output driver's diagnostic signal when the driver state is off. The observation period is 480 milliseconds, and the listed malfunction-lamp response is two driving cycles.

This command state differs from the neighboring positive- and ground-short entries, which are qualified with the driver on. The distinction explains why an electrical fault can be detected even when the component is not being asked to move coolant. It does not establish a flow measurement or prove that the mechanism is mechanically stuck.

## Resolve the component destination first

The code wording says coolant pump, but its diagnostic column refers to cylinder-head coolant valve N489. The N489 section describes a rotary slide valve. This mismatch remains source-review pending, and the article does not merge the two descriptions into an invented universal component identity.

A technician should use the installed engine's diagram and current factory information to identify the intended circuit before checking an open path. Preserve the original fault and related P199-series records; the commanded state and reported electrical condition are useful evidence together.

Do not use another coolant pump's terminals or resistance values to fill the naming gap. Once the circuit is correctly identified and repaired, verification should address the off-state open-circuit check. Normal coolant temperature or a brief warning-free restart does not, by itself, prove that this monitor has passed.
