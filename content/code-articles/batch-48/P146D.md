# P146D: Tank-heater feedback is high, not necessarily tank temperature

## Identify the measured signal

For the CRUA/DEJA application, P146D belongs to SCR heater circuit one, described as the AdBlue tank circuit. The strategy concerns a short-to-positive condition in the heater current-sensor feedback. Its electrical criterion is signal voltage above 4.7 volts for 480 milliseconds, with a two-driving-cycle lamp schedule.

That voltage belongs to the feedback monitor. It is not a published heater supply rating, a fluid-temperature threshold or proof that the tank has become too hot. Those distinctions matter when a generic scan tool shortens the description to a heating malfunction.

## Keep heater control and circuit identification together

The table refers to reducing-agent heater control module J891. Its companion procedure identifies the heater-control assembly and the circuit-two line-heater element, and starts by establishing supply and wiring conditions. The procedure also mixes J891 and J880 connector references in a later step; this article keeps a review flag and does not reproduce those mixed terminal instructions as a verified pin test.

A qualified technician should use the installed vehicle's wiring diagram to identify circuit one and its feedback path before condemning the tank assembly. Record related heater or voltage faults and distinguish the controller's command from the returned current signal.

A successful repair must restore believable feedback under the relevant operating conditions. Adding reducing agent or clearing memory alone does not demonstrate that a feedback signal previously above its electrical limit has been corrected.
