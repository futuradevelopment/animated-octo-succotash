# U0431: A body-module message arrives but its content is implausible

## Invalid information is different from no information

The CREC U0431 strategy identifies an implausible message received from the body-control system. It begins more than 500 milliseconds after ignition-on and evaluates the invalid-data condition for 0.3 seconds. This is distinct from U0141's missing-message monitor.

The row's title refers to body control module A, while its detailed strategy calls the participant BCM2. That unresolved naming difference remains source-review flagged. The article does not assume that every module labeled A or 2 in another scan tool is the same physical controller.

## Diagnose the content and its source in context

A successful network connection does not prove that every transmitted value is valid. A technician should preserve the reporting module's faults and the complete network record, then identify the intended body-control participant from the installed vehicle's wiring and configuration information.

Audi points to a CAN terminal-resistance procedure, but that is only one part of a correctly scoped investigation. The referenced procedure depends on a specific network connection state; its numbers should not be copied as a universal test of all body-network wiring.

Resolve the module naming before selecting a component or software action. After repair, confirmation should demonstrate that the expected message is both present and accepted as plausible under the applicable startup conditions. A warning cleared from the engine controller, or a network scan that merely lists a body module online, does not verify the data-content check.
