# P190B: A charge-air cooling pump reports thermal shutdown

## What overheated in this monitor

Volkswagen assigns P190B to the charge-air cooling pump V188 in the cited CRUA/DEJA diagnostic table. The controller recognizes an overheating report from the pump output driver. The row gives three attempts over nine seconds and a two-driving-cycle malfunction-lamp schedule. It does not supply a coolant-temperature threshold that an owner can compare with the dashboard gauge.

That distinction matters when the engine temperature looks normal. This code concerns the electrically controlled pump serving the charge-air intercooler circuit; the table is not declaring that the whole engine has overheated. Equally, an overheating report does not identify whether the underlying problem is electrical, hydraulic or internal to the pump.

## Establish the pump's operating conditions

The linked V188 procedure begins with sound fuses and battery voltage, then checks the pump's supply, ground path and connection to the engine controller. Those checks provide evidence before the procedure reaches a pump-replacement branch. A voltage reading at an unrelated coolant pump would not answer the same question.

Record the exact code and related cooling-pump faults before clearing memory. A technician should identify V188 from the matching vehicle diagram and investigate its operating conditions without repeatedly running a pump that reports thermal shutdown. Any cooling-system inspection must wait for safe temperature and pressure. Successful confirmation means the relevant pump monitor completes without returning the fault, not merely that a warning disappears after a restart.
