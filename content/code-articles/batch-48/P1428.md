# P1428: The brake-vacuum pump signal is electrically low

## Low signal voltage is not a vacuum measurement

Volkswagen's CGFA P1428 row concerns the electrical brake-vacuum pump circuit. The strategy labels a short-to-ground condition, with signal voltage below 2.15 volts while engine speed exceeds 80 RPM. Monitoring lasts half a second and the lamp schedule is two driving cycles.

That voltage is part of the pump-circuit diagnostic strategy. It should not be interpreted as a brake-vacuum pressure value or taken as proof that the pump's mechanical section has failed. The stored code identifies an electrical result that needs the correct circuit context.

## Follow the V192/J569 diagnostic path

The factory reference directs a technician to vacuum pump V192 and brake-booster relay J569. Preserve related brake, supply and relay faults so the investigation can distinguish a local signal problem from wider power or control issues. The installed wiring information is necessary before selecting measurement points.

Bridging the relay or powering the pump directly is not a safe substitute for the prescribed procedure. Likewise, a normally starting engine does not establish that the brake-assistance circuit is healthy.

If brake assistance is reduced or pedal behavior is abnormal, arrange professional service without conducting a road test to reproduce the warning. Once the cause is repaired, verify the electrical monitor and the required brake-assistance operation. A warning-free restart alone does not demonstrate that the relevant running check has passed or that braking performance is acceptable.
