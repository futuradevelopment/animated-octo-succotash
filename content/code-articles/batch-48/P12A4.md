# P12A4: The rail-control fault is defined by pressure and delivery effort

## Read the sign of the pressure comparison carefully

The CREC P12A4 table labels the rail-pump control valve as stuck closed. Its strategy describes high-side pressure outside range and includes a requested-minus-measured pressure difference below approximately negative three thousand kilopascals. That sign convention indicates why the order of the two pressure values matters when interpreting scan data.

The row then adds pressure-controller output and flow-control-valve branches. Monitoring lasts five seconds, with eligibility including a running engine after the initial start period and a defined fuel-flow request. The code is therefore more specific than “fuel pressure is wrong,” yet it still does not identify a failed part by itself.

## Compare command, measurement and actual pressure

Audi directs diagnosis to pressure sensor G247. Its checking section explains how the signal participates in high-pressure regulation and provides circuit checks. A technician should preserve the pressure request, measured value and related control faults rather than looking at one pressure number in isolation.

Some enabling fields in this table are explicitly unavailable. The article keeps a review flag for those incomplete conditions and does not fill them with values from another engine or invent a confirmation cycle.

High-pressure fuel work requires the proper professional procedure and equipment. Do not disconnect lines or force a control valve to test the title's “stuck” description. A defensible repair decision follows evidence from the system, its feedback and its electrical control, followed by verification of the original monitor.
