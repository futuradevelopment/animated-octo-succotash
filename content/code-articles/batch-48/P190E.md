# P190E: Missing supply feedback from the charge-air cooling pump

## What the supply-voltage label actually supports

For the CRUA/DEJA calibration documented here, P190E concerns the V188 charge-air cooling pump. The monitor describes a missing live signal from the pump and receives its fault information through the output driver. Its observation period is sixty seconds, with the malfunction lamp listed after two driving cycles.

The row does not state that measured battery voltage must fall below a particular numerical limit. A scan-tool description reading “supply voltage” should therefore not be expanded into an invented low-voltage specification. It also does not establish that the vehicle battery itself needs replacement.

## Check the local power path

Volkswagen's linked V188 procedure verifies battery voltage at the pump harness, the ground connection and the separate path back to the engine controller. These are local circuit checks, even when the main battery starts the engine normally. Connector damage or a problem along the pump circuit can require attention independently of overall charging-system performance.

Keep the stored fault and its operating context available while a technician traces the correct V188 connector using the matching wiring diagram. Avoid bridging terminals or supplying power to an unidentified pump connector; the signal path is part of the diagnosis.

A useful repair confirmation distinguishes restored communication or supply feedback from simply erasing the code. Because this row evaluates the missing signal over a longer interval than neighboring short-circuit codes, a quick key cycle is particularly weak evidence that the original condition has been resolved.
