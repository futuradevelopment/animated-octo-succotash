# P1294: why a Wrangler cannot maintain its commanded idle

P1294 describes a difference between the idle speed the PCM requests and the speed the engine actually achieves. In the 2003 Wrangler TJ diagnostic manual, the check runs while idling, with an automatic transmission in Drive. MAP and throttle-position faults prevent this particular assessment from being meaningful. The manual therefore directs those faults, and IAC circuit faults, to be diagnosed first.

## What the discrepancy means

The documented tolerance is asymmetric: actual speed must remain within 200 rpm above or 100 rpm below target. A deviation lasting 14 seconds is one failure; three failures produce a bad trip, and two bad trips set the code. Those conditions explain why a brief idle flare and a confirmed P1294 are different kinds of evidence. They are calibration details for this manual, not universal Jeep limits.

The diagnostic sequence separates unwanted airflow from restricted airflow. Intake-manifold, brake-booster and PCV vacuum leaks can defeat idle control. The next branch examines air-cleaner and inlet restrictions or leaks, followed by the throttle plate, linkage and IAC seating area. Thus, replacing the idle motor before checking the air path skips several distinct causes in the factory procedure.

## Evidence needed after repair

A technician compares commanded and actual idle under the documented conditions and checks the IAC driver circuits if the mechanical inspections pass. A normal reading now, together with the manual's good-trip information, can indicate an intermittent event. Freeze-frame context matters more than simply erasing the code. This explanation covers the documented 2003 TJ system, not every vehicle using P1294.
