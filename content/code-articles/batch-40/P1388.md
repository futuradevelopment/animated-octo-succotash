# P1388: diagnosing the Wrangler ASD relay control side

P1388 in the 2003 Wrangler TJ manual concerns the circuit that controls the automatic shutdown relay. The PCM checks this circuit with the ignition on and battery voltage above 10.4 volts, and stores the fault when it detects an open or short. This identifies an electrical control problem; it does not establish that the relay's switched output is absent.

## Separate the relay from its wiring

The factory sequence examines the relay coil, its fused ignition feed and the control wire as separate parts of the circuit. A defective relay is one possibility, but an intact relay cannot operate if the ignition feed is missing. Likewise, replacing the relay will not repair an open control wire or a control wire shorted to ground.

The manual begins with fault-history information. A good-trip counter that does not support a currently active failure sends diagnosis toward an intermittent condition. That distinction prevents a temporary connection problem from being treated as a continuously failed component. Connector corrosion, damaged terminals and pushed-out terminals remain relevant even when a component passes a bench check.

## What confirms the diagnosis

A workshop should establish which part of the control circuit failed, correct that cause and complete the prescribed powertrain verification. The nearby P1389 procedure is different: it addresses missing ASD output voltage at the PCM while the relay is energized. Keeping those two descriptions separate avoids confusing relay command with relay feedback. These distinctions come from the 2003 TJ powertrain manual and require the applicable vehicle wiring information.
