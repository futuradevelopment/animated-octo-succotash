# P1616: Volkswagen glow warning lamp short to positive

Volkswagen's Golf Mk 4 distributor-pump diesel manual through April 1999 assigns P1616 to a short-to-positive fault in the glow-period warning lamp K29 circuit. The documented effect is a warning lamp that does not function. This is a fault in the indication circuit; its title does not establish that every glow plug has failed.

## Separate indication from heating

The factory output-test sequence provides a useful distinction. It has a stage for glow-plug relay J52 and a separate stage for lamp K29. The requested response at the lamp stage is flashing. Recording which stage was selected matters: observing a relay response cannot confirm the dashboard indication, and observing the lamp cannot confirm each glow plug's heating performance.

This separation also explains why a starting complaint and a missing warning lamp should not be collapsed into one diagnosis. They may be related, but the lamp's electrical fault still needs its own evidence. A bulb replacement chosen only because the lamp is dark does not address the recorded short-to-positive classification.

## Use a commanded check carefully

The documented final-control routine is a workshop scan-tool sequence that also actuates other engine components. It should be performed under the complete procedure, not reproduced by applying battery power to a lamp wire. If K29 does not respond to its command, Volkswagen refers the technician to the vehicle's current-flow diagrams and electrical fault finding.

A useful completion record therefore identifies the correct lamp command, the observed response and the circuit defect actually corrected. Recheck the indication after repair and confirm that the stored short fault does not recur. These details concern the stated early Golf diesel documentation, not every model using the P1616 code string.
