# P1618: Volkswagen glow relay short to positive: command versus load

P1618 is a short-to-positive fault involving glow-plug relay J52 in Volkswagen's Golf Mk 4 distributor-pump diesel manual section introduced in May 1999. The table identifies relay or wiring faults and associates the condition with unavailable preheating and difficult cold starting. It does not identify an individual failed glow plug.

## What the output test contributes

Volkswagen's final-control routine commands J52 and expects a relay response. The manual also describes an observable change in interior-light brightness as the high-current glow load is switched. Those are two different kinds of observation: relay movement concerns the actuator, while a load-related effect adds information about current demand.

Neither observation, by itself, proves that every plug delivers the correct heat. Conversely, a relay that fails to respond does not establish whether the relay itself or its control wiring is responsible. The manual sends that failed-response branch to current-flow diagrams and electrical fault finding.

## Avoid confusing the warning lamp with the relay

The same diagnostic sequence separately operates warning lamp K29. A flashing lamp during its own output-test stage is an intended command, whereas a flashing warning reported with the fault is part of the complaint. Knowing when and how the observation occurred prevents an intentional test response from being mistaken for a new fault.

This is professional circuit diagnosis involving a substantial electrical load. The useful repair evidence is the identified short or relay defect, the restored commanded response and a repeat check showing that P1618 no longer returns. Replacing a set of plugs solely because the code contains the words glow plug skips the distinction the factory procedure makes.
