# B0061: Flex belt-tension feedback and occupant classification

B0061 concerns the belt-tension sensor in the cited 2012 Ford Flex AWD 3.5L manual. The sensor forms part of the front passenger belt retractor and supplies information to the occupant-classification system. Its signal helps that system identify the possible presence of a child seat; the code alone does not establish who or what is occupying the seat.

## Read the signal in its documented role

The source describes approximately 0.95 volt with no tension and approximately 3.8 volts at high tension. These are application-specific operating examples. They are not universal airbag enable thresholds and should not be used to infer whether deployment would occur.

The occupant-classification module stores the B0061 fault. It also sends a message that can make the restraints controller store B00A0:09 and illuminate the airbag indicator. A second controller's warning can therefore be a consequence of the belt-tension input problem.

## The suffix separates circuit and response checks

Ford's shared Test M covers ground shorts, voltage shorts, open circuits and out-of-range resistance or voltage. Suffix 1C specifically reaches a sensor-output variation check; the open and short branches instead investigate the associated wiring, reference and ground paths. A professional must retain the suffix to choose the right evidence.

Replacement is conditional on the completed branch. If the condition becomes intermittent, the instructions return to terminals and harness integrity rather than assuming that the retractor or module failed.

The belt assembly and nearby wiring are part of the restraint system. Do not probe deployable-component connectors, modify the sensor signal or disassemble the retractor. Verification requires both occupant-classification and restraints checks plus the prescribed SRS prove-out.
