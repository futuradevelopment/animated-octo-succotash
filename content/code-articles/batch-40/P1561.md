# P1561: Volkswagen N146 control difference and the injection-pump decision

In Volkswagen's Golf Mk 4 distributor-pump diesel documentation, P1561 reports a control difference involving metering adjuster N146. The code does not identify an injector number. It concerns the fuel-quantity adjustment system associated with the injection pump, and the fault table includes both pump faults and electrical connections among the possible explanations.

## A control complaint needs electrical evidence

The linked factory procedure checks the adjuster's electrical resistance with the ignition off. For this documented version, the specified range is 0.5–2.5 ohms at the designated pump connection. An out-of-range result and an in-range result lead to different branches; the fault label alone is not the replacement criterion.

When that check passes, Volkswagen directs the technician to examine the circuit between the pump connection and the engine controller for interruptions and unintended connections. A satisfactory component resistance does not prove that the controller can operate it through the installed harness. Equally, a wiring defect does not establish an internal pump failure.

## Keep pump work within the correct procedure

The table associates this fault with a flashing glow warning, running problems and possible stopping. Those effects are not exclusive to N146, so they corroborate the complaint without locating the defect. The professional repair decision should retain the measured evidence and the exact engine wiring information.

This is the Golf Mk 4 manual section introduced in May 1999. Injection-pump replacement or work on fuel delivery belongs to a qualified technician using the complete applicable procedure. There is no basis here for an owner to adjust the pump in an attempt to erase the code. Confirmation should establish restored adjustment operation as well as the absence of the electrical or control fault.
