# C05F1: Ford rear driveshaft speed circuit high and unwanted power

In the cited Ford Escape AWD procedure, C05F1:00 means the rear driveshaft speed-sensor circuitry is shorted to power. High is an electrical classification. It does not establish that the rear driveshaft was overspeeding or that the driver exceeded a road-speed limit.

## Compare the signal problem with its source

The sensor is a Hall-effect device whose signal frequency varies with driveshaft speed. The fault concerns a circuit state that prevents reliable interpretation of that signal. A technician should therefore establish the electrical condition before drawing conclusions about the driveline's mechanical speed relationship.

The shared pinpoint flow checks sensor supply and then distinguishes an interrupted signal-return path from unwanted voltage on that path. The specified isolation conditions matter: voltage found where the circuit should be isolated has a different meaning from normal voltage supplied during operation.

## An electrical repair needs a repeat check

Ford first confirms whether one of the covered speed-sensor codes remains present. If the fault is absent at the time of diagnosis, the flow recognizes that an intermittent event may be involved. A normal momentary measurement does not identify which connection produced the earlier high state.

The repair should explain the unwanted power path or other proven circuit defect and establish restored speed feedback. If the wiring checks lead to a component decision, that decision is conditional on the complete procedure rather than the word high alone. Workshop verification should use the appropriate vehicle information and operating check.
