# C0092: Ford PTU fork feedback: electrical suffixes versus learned position

C0092 refers to power-transfer-unit position sensor B in the cited fourth-generation Ford Escape AWD manual. That is the shift-fork feedback, rather than the actuator cam feedback identified by C0091. The suffix separates signal-circuit complaints from a learned-position performance problem.

## Two different diagnostic routes

The electrical branch covers high signal voltage, duty outside the documented 10–90 percent window and frequency outside 1 kHz plus or minus 40 Hz. The manual first checks whether the companion cam-sensor code is present because the two sensors share power and return. A shared fault takes diagnosis toward those common circuits.

C0092:92 has a separate pinpoint test. It concerns a fork position outside the expected range when the mechanism is connected or disconnected. That branch describes a learned position beyond its threshold and considers actuator/fork or internal PTU wear and damage. It should not be treated as merely another voltage-short code.

## Why the complete suffix matters

The mechanical branch reaches its PTU decision only after the documented professional recurrence check. The electrical branch instead separates signal wiring from the actuator assembly. Applying one branch's replacement instruction to the other fault type would discard the distinction built into the manual.

A workshop should retain the original suffix, companion cam code status and the evidence showing whether the signal or the physical position is wrong. The article does not prescribe an owner road test or actuator manipulation. Source scope is the reproduced Escape AWD procedure; the mirror's broad year grouping does not establish identical applicability to every engine or build.
