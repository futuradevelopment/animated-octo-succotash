# P1764: a low Jeep governor-pressure signal is not zero pressure proof

P1764 in the cited WJ/WG gas manual is set when governor-pressure-sensor voltage remains below 0.10 volt for 8.5 seconds with the engine running. The chart applies to the transmission branch excluding the five-speed 545RFE. A near-zero electrical input should not be mistaken for a verified zero-pressure gauge reading.

## Follow the low-input possibilities

The factory procedure considers a missing five-volt supply and a signal shorted to ground or sensor ground. It checks those possibilities in the external harness and then, when justified, in the internal transmission wiring. This is why replacing the sensor without identifying where the input is being pulled low may leave the same fault.

Other transmission faults, park/neutral faults and fluid condition are addressed before the circuit branches. These preliminary observations help establish a reliable operating context and avoid diagnosing one feedback signal while a related system fault remains unresolved.

A later comparison checks whether the scan-tool voltage agrees with the voltage reaching the PCM. Agreement supports the reality of the low electrical signal, while disagreement requires attention to input interpretation. Neither comparison alone measures hydraulic pressure.

## Verify the repaired path

A workshop should record the defective supply, signal section or sensor found, then show that feedback remains plausible during the specified transmission verification. Intermittent harness and connector problems remain relevant if the low signal is no longer present. Internal-harness inspection and running transmission tests require appropriate professional equipment and precautions. These details describe this Jeep manual's circuit, not every manufacturer-specific P1764.
