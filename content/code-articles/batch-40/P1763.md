# P1763: the Jeep governor-pressure signal is electrically high

The WJ/WG gas-powertrain procedure defines P1763 as governor-pressure-sensor voltage above 4.89 volts for 8.5 seconds while the engine is running. The diagnostic branch excludes the five-speed 545RFE. High in this title refers to the signal voltage; it is not automatically proof of excessive hydraulic pressure.

## An open circuit remains possible

The documented possibilities include open reference, signal and sensor-ground paths as well as a signal wire shorted to voltage. They are investigated outside the transmission and, where necessary, inside it. Consequently, an apparently intact external connector does not establish that the entire sensor circuit is healthy.

The factory sequence considers other transmission faults, park/neutral status and fluid condition before the detailed circuit work. Park/neutral information deserves attention because later comparisons use a known selector state. Treating that state as reliable while ignoring its own fault would weaken the interpretation.

Near the end of the procedure, a technician compares the scan-tool governor-pressure voltage with the signal measured at the PCM. A mismatch points in a different direction from two matching high readings. This is an input-validation step, not a reason to substitute an arbitrary voltage at the controller.

## Evidence after repair

The repair should identify whether the fault lay in external wiring, internal wiring, the combined governor-pressure/temperature sensor or controller interpretation. Historical failures need intermittent-connection assessment when the high reading is absent now. Use the applicable transmission verification after correction; the threshold here belongs only to the cited Jeep diagnostic system.
