# P1494: when the Wrangler leak-detection switch does not change

The 2003 Wrangler TJ procedure defines P1494 by a missing change of state: the leak-detection-pump switch does not change when its solenoid is energized. This is a feedback or mechanical-response fault in the EVAP test system. It does not by itself establish the size or location of a vapor leak.

## Vacuum supply comes before pump replacement

The documented monitor runs after a cold start, with ambient/battery temperature in its permitted range and coolant temperature close to ambient. The diagnostic tree first establishes whether the fault is active, then checks the vacuum supply to the leak-detection pump. A supply hose that leaks or is obstructed can prevent the expected response even if the pump's electrical connection looks intact.

The next decision separates the pump assembly from its switch-signal path. A workshop checks whether the PCM can recognize a controlled change on that input. If it can, attention returns to the pump; if it cannot, the signal wire is checked for an open or a short to ground. This explains why a switch-related code can require a plumbing repair in one vehicle and an electrical repair in another.

## What to verify

Useful evidence includes an adequate vacuum supply, a responsive switch input and successful completion of the relevant EVAP verification. Fuel-vapor system testing should use the specified equipment and professional precautions. The explanation is limited to the source's 2003 TJ leak-detection-pump arrangement; a later Jeep with a different EVAP architecture may use a different diagnostic strategy.
