# P1553: Volkswagen pressure-ratio plausibility and the intake-system checks

P1553 describes an implausible relationship between elevation and intake-manifold pressure in Volkswagen's Polo Mk3 4LV fault table. This is a consistency complaint. The table directs attention to the pressure sender and to systems that can affect manifold conditions, including intake leakage, EGR, tank ventilation and the throttle unit.

## A pressure reading can be electrically wrong

The linked G71 procedure first observes the manifold-pressure value and its response to a throttle change. It separately identifies fixed readings of 91 mbar and 1254 mbar as clues to electrical faults: the low extreme corresponds to an open circuit or ground short, while the high extreme points toward a positive short.

Those values are diagnostic states for this documented sensor circuit, not universal acceptable intake pressures. A displayed pressure should therefore be evaluated for credible behavior before it is used to justify a mechanical intake repair.

## Separate signal integrity from real airflow

The component procedure uses controlled circuit-isolation checks to distinguish the combined G71/G42 sender from its wiring and the controller. A technician needs the application-specific diagram and equipment; these are not grounds for an owner to bridge unidentified terminals.

If the electrical pressure information is credible, the fault table's broader intake-system checks still matter. Replacing G71 solely because pressure appears in the code title skips the possibility that the controller is seeing a genuine pressure relationship it cannot reconcile.

Useful verification records both what was corrected and why the pressure information is now believable. A successful electrical test and a corrected intake leak answer different parts of the diagnosis. The source supplies no universal P1553 ratio threshold or setting duration, so neither should be invented. Scope is the Volkswagen Polo 4LV system shown in the cited manual.
