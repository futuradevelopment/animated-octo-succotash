# P1486: a Wrangler EVAP restriction needs more than a leak check

P1486 is the pinched-hose result in the 2003 Wrangler TJ evaporative-emissions diagnostic procedure. The name is shorthand for a restriction investigation, not proof that a visibly crushed hose is present. The listed possibilities include an obstructed canister, the canister-to-purge plumbing, the leak-detection-pump pressure hose and the pump itself.

## Two monitor results are involved

The leak-detection-pump test must first pass. When the PCM suspects a restriction, it waits for the purge-flow monitor; the pinched-hose fault is set if that monitor also fails. This sequence matters because a system can appear sealed while still being unable to move vapor through the intended path. A generic search for an external leak misses that distinction.

The initial assessment occurs after a cold start within a specified ambient/battery-temperature range, with coolant temperature close to ambient. A warm workshop restart may therefore fail to reproduce the original monitor conditions. The original fault record and good-trip information help establish whether the complaint remains active.

## Locating the obstruction

The factory procedure uses controlled EVAP test equipment and observes how pressure changes as specific sections are isolated. Those responses separate a blocked canister from obstructed plumbing and a pump-related problem. They are workshop tests around fuel vapor, not instructions to apply compressed air to the tank.

A sound repair identifies the restricted section and verifies the relevant EVAP operation afterward. The diagnostic logic here is specific to the documented 2003 TJ leak-detection-pump system; other Jeep EVAP designs need their own procedure.
