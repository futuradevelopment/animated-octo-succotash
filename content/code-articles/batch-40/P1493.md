# P1493: separating a low temperature signal from a low battery

P1493 on the documented 2003 Wrangler TJ system concerns the battery-temperature sensor's signal circuit. The PCM sets it when that input stays below 0.5 volt for three seconds with the ignition on. It is not the battery's terminal-voltage measurement, so a low-battery interpretation of the title would send diagnosis toward the wrong circuit.

## The important isolation result

The factory procedure first checks whether the low signal is still present. It then observes how the reported sensor voltage responds when the sensor is disconnected. A rise into the expected high range supports a sensor-side fault; a signal that remains low requires investigation of the wiring or controller side. The distinction is more useful than assuming that every low-input fault needs a new sensor.

The wiring investigation separates a short to chassis ground from a short to the sensor-ground circuit. Both can hold the temperature input low, but the repair location can differ. Connector condition, power and grounds are checked before the diagnostic tree reaches the PCM.

## Intermittent faults and repair verification

If today's voltage is no longer below the diagnostic boundary, the manual directs the technician to the intermittent-condition procedure. Retaining the original fault record helps connect the event to an intermittent harness or connector problem. After correcting the identified cause, the documented verification checks the relevant powertrain/charging operation.

P1493 and the high-input P1492 use different circuit branches in this manual. Their thresholds and diagnostic ordering belong to the 2003 TJ source, not to all vehicles displaying either code.
