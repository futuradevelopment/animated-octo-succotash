# P1492: high battery-temperature signal voltage on a Wrangler

In the 2003 Wrangler TJ manual, P1492 is set when the PCM sees battery-temperature-sensor voltage above 4.9 volts for three seconds with the ignition on. The word high describes the electrical input. It is not a direct finding that the battery is overheating or that charging-system voltage is excessive.

## An open circuit can produce a high signal

The factory possibilities include the sensor, an open signal wire, an open sensor-ground circuit and a signal wire shorted to voltage. Consequently, a high scan-tool value does not justify a sensor-only diagnosis. Both sides of the sensor circuit need to be considered.

The first diagnostic decision asks whether the high reading is present now. The procedure uses a slightly lower live-test boundary than the code's set threshold, then routes a normal present reading to intermittent diagnosis. Confusing that test boundary with the three-second monitor threshold can make apparently contradictory readings seem more conclusive than they are.

For an active fault, controlled circuit tests establish whether the PCM input can respond correctly and whether unwanted voltage remains on the signal wire when the controller is isolated. Continuity checks then distinguish the signal path from the sensor-ground path. These observations identify the failed section rather than merely repeating the code description.

## Scope and verification

Use the vehicle-specific wiring diagram and the manual's charging-system verification after repair. A battery replacement alone is not confirmation that the temperature-signal fault was corrected. This account applies to the 2003 TJ diagnostic system; it does not transfer its voltage thresholds to other Jeep years.
