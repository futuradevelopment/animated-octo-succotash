# P1156: Volkswagen G71 pressure signal: separating sensor and reference faults

Volkswagen's Golf Mk 4 distributor-pump diesel manual identifies P1156 as an open circuit or short to ground affecting intake manifold pressure sender G71. This article uses the manual section introduced in May 1999, with the pressure comparison procedure specifically covering AGR, AHF, ALH and ASV engines. It does not establish applicability to every Volkswagen carrying this code.

## Why two pressure readings matter

G71 shares a housing with intake temperature sender G72 at the charge-air cooler. The altitude reference F96 is inside engine controller J248. These are different measurement locations, so a disagreement does not automatically condemn the externally mounted sensor.

The factory check compares both scan readings with an independent absolute-pressure reference while the ignition is on. Its permitted difference is 30 mbar in either direction. The reference must measure absolute pressure; a gauge that reads pressure relative to atmosphere answers a different question. This is a useful way to separate a questionable G71 signal from a questionable altitude reference.

## What the fallback can conceal

In the professional isolation test, disconnecting G71 should briefly produce about 400 mbar before the displayed pressure adopts F96's value. A plausible-looking pressure after that transition is therefore substitute information, not proof that G71 is communicating correctly.

The procedure distinguishes the expected fallback response from a failed response, then directs attention to the sensor or its wiring accordingly. Circuit testing uses the correct wiring diagram and controller adapter. The repair record should identify which pressure source disagreed, whether fallback behaved as specified, and whether the original electrical fault returned; a normal-looking substituted value alone is insufficient verification.
