# C0090: Ford PTU actuator faults: command, current and movement

C0090 identifies a power-transfer-unit actuator fault in the cited fourth-generation Ford Escape AWD procedure. Its suffix is essential. The manual distinguishes low supply voltage, no current when commanded, current when no operation is commanded, and a commanded actuator that does not appear to move. These observations do not all imply the same failed part.

## Interpret the electrical state

The PTU uses a reversible motor, reduction gears, an actuating cam, a shift fork and position sensors. The AWD module reverses power and ground between the motor circuits to connect or disconnect the unit. Consequently, identifying a circuit as the power side depends on the command being made.

For this documented system, suffix 18 concerns zero motor current during an ON command. Suffix 19 concerns detected current above 1 A when command duty is zero. One reports missing expected current; the other reports unwanted current. Replacing the motor without preserving that distinction skips important evidence.

## Confirm feedback before calling the actuator stuck

Suffix 71 depends on the cam and fork sensors failing to show movement after a command. Ford explicitly prioritizes position-sensor faults first. An unusable feedback signal can prevent the controller from recognizing movement, so a stuck label is not independent proof of mechanical seizure.

The professional procedure isolates motor-circuit interruptions, ground shorts and shorts between the two conductors before its component decision. Verification should retain the original suffix and show restored commanded operation. This is an explanation of the Escape manual's AWD implementation; the mirror does not provide an exact original publication revision, and its thresholds should not be transferred to unrelated transfer cases.
