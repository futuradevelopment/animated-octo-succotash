# P1475: a leak-detection pump signal fault is not a proven EVAP leak

P1475 concerns a malfunction or missing signal from the leak-detection pump in Ross-Tech's VAG reference. The page includes pressure or vacuum hoses as well as wiring among its possible causes. That combination matters: the monitor depends on more than the electrical identity of the pump.

## Separate the test system from the system being tested

A leak-detection pump is involved in evaluating the evaporative-emissions system. If its own signal or supporting hose arrangement is faulty, the code does not independently establish where a vapor leak exists. As diagnostic reasoning, an unreliable test function needs explanation before treating its result as a confirmed tank or hose leak.

The source's hose and wiring branches should remain distinct. A sound electrical connection does not demonstrate a usable pressure or vacuum path, and an intact hose does not establish that the signal circuit works.

## No universal leak size follows from this code

Ross-Tech's entry does not give a leak-diameter threshold, a tank-pressure target or a pump replacement requirement for every P1475. The proper vehicle procedure determines how the signal and pneumatic support are checked. This independent diagnostic guidance supports investigating the leak-detection function without inventing a specific leaking component from a missing-signal label.
