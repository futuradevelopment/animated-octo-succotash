# P200B: Audi's runner-overtravel monitor uses a signal-voltage boundary

P200B is cataloged as bank 2 intake-manifold runner performance. Audi's CEUC chart describes the code as runner-control overtravel and lists signal voltage below 0.7 V. That is the specific monitor interpretation explained here, rather than a general claim that all P200B faults use the same position or voltage limit.

## Overtravel is evaluated through feedback

The source gives a signal boundary, not a measured linkage angle. As diagnostic reasoning, the low signal must be reconciled with the actual mechanism and feedback system before concluding that the runner physically traveled too far. A sensor-related reading and a confirmed mechanical position are separate evidence.

The row also does not provide a motor-current test or prove that the intake manifold assembly must be replaced. It describes detection, not fault isolation.

## Confirm the correct runner arrangement

Use CEUC service information to identify the relevant bank, mechanism and feedback circuit. The quick-reference row is not a bank-location diagram and supplies no universal stop adjustment. A supported repair explains why the monitored signal crossed the boundary and which defect caused it. This article is limited to the CEUC section of Audi's 2014 A6/S6 reference book and does not transfer that threshold to other engines.
