# P1420: distinguish the N112 electrical fault from a generic module label

The catalog's P1420 label refers broadly to a secondary-air control module electrical malfunction. Ross-Tech's VAG entry instead identifies the N112 secondary-air solenoid valve's electrical fault. Confirm the complete controller description before choosing components: this article concerns the N112 interpretation documented by that source.

## The relay remains part of the question

Ross-Tech lists both N112 and the J299 secondary-air relay among the possible causes. That is useful because a component named in the fault is not necessarily the only component involved in its operation. A valve replacement alone would not explain a relay defect.

As an editorial diagnostic implication, establish which part of the electrical function is failing before making a parts decision. The source does not provide a pinout, a relay bypass method or a universal coil-resistance specification. Use the vehicle's circuit and output-test information for those details.

## Keep electrical and flow diagnoses separate

This page does not establish that secondary-air passages are carbon-blocked or that a pump has insufficient airflow. Those would require their own evidence. The supported conclusion is narrower: investigate the N112 electrical function and the associated J299 possibility where the full VAG fault matches. The terminology difference remains a review limitation rather than being hidden behind a generic title.
