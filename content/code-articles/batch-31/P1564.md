# P1564: low voltage during throttle basic setting can stop adaptation

P1564 identifies undervoltage during throttle-actuator J338 basic setting in Ross-Tech's VAG reference. The source's symptom is an unsuccessful throttle-body alignment. Its stated cause is low battery voltage, and its recommended response includes checking voltage and connecting a charger for the alignment procedure.

## The timing of the voltage problem matters

This fault concerns the supply during basic setting. As diagnostic reasoning, a later acceptable voltage reading does not establish that adequate voltage was present throughout the unsuccessful attempt. Equally, an adaptation failure under low voltage does not independently prove that the throttle mechanism is defective.

The useful investigation therefore preserves the circumstances of the setting attempt and establishes suitable electrical support before assessing whether alignment can complete. This interpretation follows the source's direct relationship between undervoltage and the failed procedure.

## Use the correct support and setting procedure

Ross-Tech's code page supplies no universal charger setting, voltage threshold or adaptation channel. Those details must come from the applicable vehicle and diagnostic-equipment instructions. The supported next step is to address the supply condition during alignment, then evaluate the actual result. Replacing the throttle assembly solely because P1564 was stored would skip the specific cause named by this independent reference.
