# P2404: leak-pump feedback depends on vacuum, power and purge sealing

P2404 concerns implausible feedback from EVAP leak-detection pump V144 in Ross-Tech's VAG reference. The page separates electrical and mechanical pump failure from missing voltage, inadequate vacuum, an EVAP pressure leak and a leaking N80 purge valve. These are distinct reasons the expected pump response may be absent.

## Establish what the pump receives

Ross-Tech recommends checking electrical supply and, where fitted, engine-component relay J757. It also calls for checking the vacuum supply at idle. A pump cannot be judged solely by its name in the code if its required supply is missing. The reference specifically highlights J757 on some Audi A6 applications.

## A purge leak can undermine the test

The source includes testing whether N80 leaks system pressure into the intake manifold. That observation distinguishes leakage through the purge path from a defective leak-detection pump. It also explains why an electrical resistance check alone cannot resolve every P2404.

Vehicle notes include BPY engines and a particular Touareg coolant-pump concern; neither applies automatically to all models. Use the actual repair manual for electrical, vacuum and pressure limits. This article follows independent Ross-Tech guidance and does not prescribe replacing V144 before its supplies and the relevant system leakage are assessed.
