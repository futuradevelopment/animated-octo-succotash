# P1546: N75 short-to-positive is an electrical boost-control fault

P1546 describes boost-control valve N75 shorted to positive in Ross-Tech's VAG reference. The listed symptoms include irregular running, reduced power and limp mode. Those effects can resemble a mechanical turbocharger complaint, but this fault specifically identifies an electrical condition.

## Explain the circuit finding first

Ross-Tech names faulty wiring or connections and N75 as possible causes. It recommends checking the electrical path and the valve. As an editorial implication, replacing a turbocharger without explaining the positive-short indication would leave the source's principal diagnostic branches unresolved.

The fault also does not mean measured boost pressure is necessarily above its allowed range. Short to positive describes the circuit condition; pressure regulation and its mechanical response require separate evidence.

## Use the installed circuit's specifications

The source does not provide a universal N75 resistance, terminal voltage, connector location or direct-power test. The correct engine wiring information is needed to distinguish a valve fault from a connected-circuit defect. A useful repair report should identify which electrical finding justified the work and whether the complaint was resolved under the applicable checks. This article describes Ross-Tech's independent VAG interpretation, not a manufacturer-wide prediction that N75 is always the failed part.
