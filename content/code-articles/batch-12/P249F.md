# P249F: DPF regeneration spends too little time in closed-loop control

P249F means excessive time to enter closed-loop DPF regeneration control. Ford’s diesel monitor expresses the failure as **too little time in closed-loop operation relative to total regeneration time**. The permitted amount is a function of the event duration, not a fixed number of seconds published on this page.

## The event must be an active regeneration

Ford evaluates the check once per regeneration event. Entry conditions include particulate-filter regeneration mode, engine speed of 1,000–3,500 rpm, coolant at least 70°C, and bounded torque and ambient conditions. Hydrocarbon desorption mode must not be occurring.

The monitor also depends on valid temperature-related inputs. A regeneration record collected with an invalid exhaust-temperature signal does not provide the same basis for interpreting temperature-control participation.

## Distinguish control entry from control accuracy

P24A0 on the same page evaluates temperature-controller saturation and desired/actual temperature disagreement. P249F instead concerns how much of the event achieved closed-loop operation. The two observations can guide different questions even though both affect regeneration control.

Useful verification establishes whether a qualified regeneration event occurred and how its control state evolved. The summary does not provide the missing duration map or a universal forced-regeneration procedure. It also does not prove that the DPF itself is blocked or requires replacement. The documented evidence concerns insufficient closed-loop participation and needs the applicable service information for cause isolation.

## Source and applicability

This explanation follows Ford’s 2023–2024 diesel OBD summary. Match the vehicle’s engine and calibration before applying these monitor values; a generic code alone does not establish fitment.
