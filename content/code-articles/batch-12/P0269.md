# P0269: A cylinder-three balance fault can be specific to cold-start control

P0269 reports cylinder three contribution/balance. Ford uses this identifier in both a general balancing description and a distinct **6.7L cold-start fuel-balancing monitor**. That reuse makes the engine’s recorded operating mode an important part of the diagnosis.

## What the cold-start monitor detects

For the cold strategy, correction above 90% of the allowable amount for current conditions sets the fault. The monitor runs in cold-start mode, needs completed wheel learning, and requires the crankshaft circuit and range/performance checks to be satisfactory.

The listed window is 500–3,000 rpm and 3.5–90 mg/stroke injection quantity, with a typical duration of 7.5 seconds. These are qualification and correction-monitor details; they are not instructions to force a cold engine through a workshop driving routine.

## Why a warm retest can answer a different question

The general monitor on another page excludes regeneration and lists additional supporting inputs. A warm running check may provide useful evidence, but it does not automatically demonstrate a passing result for the cold event that originally generated P0269.

Identify cylinder three using the correct engine information, and retain any injector-circuit or crankshaft faults that could affect interpretation. A high balancing correction does not, by itself, distinguish fuel delivery from a mechanical combustion problem. Ford’s summary does not provide that isolation sequence or authorize an injector replacement. The useful starting point is the excessive correction in a verified operating window.

## Source and applicability

This explanation follows Ford’s 2023–2024 diesel OBD summary. Match the vehicle’s engine and calibration before applying these monitor values; a generic code alone does not establish fitment.
