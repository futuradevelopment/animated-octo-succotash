# P228F: An upper adaptation boundary is not the same as rail overpressure

P228F reports that fuel-pressure regulator learning has exceeded its high limit. In the Ford diesel example, the controller compares an adaptation factor against boundaries representing roughly a 20% rail-pressure sensor-slope deviation. It is a **learned-control limit**, not simply a pressure reading above a universal ceiling.

## What makes the test eligible

The listed learning window requires a rail-pressure set point between 600 and 1,300 bar, with fuel temperature no greater than 40°C. The typical duration is ten seconds. Sensor-supply and rail-pressure circuit checks are also prerequisites for the identified 6.7L strategy.

Those details change verification: a reading collected outside the adaptation window cannot alone show whether this learning monitor would pass. Likewise, clearing a stored result without a valid new learning opportunity supplies no evidence that the upper boundary problem has disappeared.

## Keep neighboring faults distinct

P016D concerns failing to learn a new factor within 300 seconds; P228F concerns a factor beyond an allowed boundary. P0088 is separate again, using direct rail-pressure comparisons. These different observations may coexist, but they are not interchangeable diagnoses.

Ford does not publish a sign convention here that turns the high adaptation limit into one fixed pressure error. Do not infer that every P228F means an externally measured overpressure, nor that it proves regulator failure. Use calibration-specific adaptation and sensor evidence, followed by the applicable service procedure, to determine what actually needs correction.
