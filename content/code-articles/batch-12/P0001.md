# P0001: Why an unpowered fuel-volume valve can trigger an open-circuit fault

P0001 identifies the fuel-volume regulator control circuit as open. Ford’s diesel example makes an important distinction: the controller evaluates feedback while the volume-control valve is **not energized**. A fault recorded at that point is evidence about the electrical driver path, rather than a measurement of how much fuel the valve passes.

## What the monitor actually sees

The documented threshold is a control-chip feedback voltage between 2.8 and 4.8 V, against a normal secondary supply of 5 V. The check runs continuously with a typical monitoring duration of 0.3 second. These are internal diagnostic values in the described system; they are not a published connector-pin test procedure.

P0003 uses the same unpowered state but a lower feedback band. P0004 instead examines excessive current while the valve is energized. Keeping that distinction prevents an open-circuit investigation from being confused with an overcurrent event.

## Useful evidence before a repair decision

A diagnostic record should preserve P0001’s status and accompanying regulator codes, then establish which command state produced the fault. A pressure deviation that happens at another time does not identify the electrical discontinuity detected here.

Verification needs the applicable vehicle wiring and driver test information. The monitor description does not locate a broken wire, prove the valve itself failed, or authorize replacing the high-pressure pump. Because the threshold concerns chip feedback, applying power directly to an unknown valve or treating 2.8–4.8 V as a universal harness specification would answer the wrong question.
