# C00A6: Explorer and Aviator transfer-case actuator harness contact

C00A6 on a covered Explorer or Aviator may result from damaged wiring near the transfer-case actuator motor. Ford SSM 52152 describes a wrench indicator or four-wheel-drive service message, with specific C00A6 suffixes or related C00A7/C00A8 entries stored in the AWD module.

## Preserve the suffix and inspect the route

The message lists C00A6:71, C00A6:74 and C00A6:99. Recording the complete fault is more useful than shortening every result to C00A6. It also helps the technician distinguish the original actuator-performance finding from related low or open-circuit entries elsewhere in the listed family.

Ford identifies a wiring route over and into the actuator motor where the harness may rub against an underbody heat shield. That is a concrete inspection location, not a finding that the actuator motor itself is defective. A visible rub or damaged conductor can change the repair decision substantially compared with an intact harness.

## Repair confirmed damage or continue diagnosis

If damage is found, the bulletin directs repair using the Wiring Diagram Manual procedures. If none is found, the next step is normal diagnosis. The source does not authorize replacing a sound harness as a precaution or condemning the transfer case merely because the code contains actuator wording.

Provide the technician with the full AWD scan and exact warning, plus any relevant recent underbody work. Inspection around an exhaust heat shield requires appropriate access and cooling precautions. Do not test four-wheel-drive engagement in hazardous conditions to reproduce the fault. The bulletin supplies a scoped electrical check for the stated AWD vehicles, not a universal mechanical repair procedure.

## Source and applicability

Ford SSM 52152, January 2024: 2020–2023 Explorer and Aviator equipped with AWD, with C00A6:71/:74/:99 or the listed related actuator-circuit faults in the AWD module.
