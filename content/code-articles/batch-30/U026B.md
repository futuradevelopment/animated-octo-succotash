# U026B: Escalade assistance and lighting faults from a fascia harness short

U026B can be one of several communication entries produced by the Escalade front-fascia harness problem described in GM PIT5487F. The same event may affect parking assistance, adaptive cruise control, surround-view cameras, driver assistance or front lighting. Certain blown fuses and fast turn-signal flashing are also included in the bulletin’s symptom pattern.

## Look for a shared physical cause

GM identifies a possible short where the front bumper harness contacts the end of the right-front bumper bar. That location gives a technician a focused inspection target when several seemingly unrelated front-end systems fail together. It does not mean that every U026B originates there, or that every code listed in the bulletin must be present.

A communication fault in this setting is not automatically a failed image-processing sensor. A short in shared wiring can interrupt functions and produce module-level symptoms. The useful evidence is the combination of affected systems, fuse findings and actual harness damage at the specified location.

## Repair must also prevent renewed contact

If damaged circuits are found, GM directs their repair and then careful rerouting of the harness behind the bumper bar with the specified securing arrangement. Repairing conductors without correcting the contact point can leave the condition that caused the damage. Conversely, an undamaged harness does not justify replacing it solely because the code matches.

Provide the workshop with all warning messages, the complete scan and details of any simultaneous lamp failure. Front-fascia removal and electrical repair should follow the vehicle procedure. The bulletin supplies a case-specific inspection and routing correction, not a universal replacement instruction for the camera system or its control modules.

## Source and applicability

GM PIT5487F, April 27, 2020: 2015–2020 Cadillac Escalade models with the described front-fascia harness condition.
