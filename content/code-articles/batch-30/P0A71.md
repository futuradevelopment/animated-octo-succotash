# P0A71: Ford electrified no-start faults can begin with a 12-volt drop

P0A71 can be one of several current-related faults recorded during the electrified Ford startup condition in SSM 50151. The vehicle may not start and may display Stop Safely Now. Ford identifies a drop in the 12-volt circuit as a possible source, particularly when the faults occur at key-on and are difficult to reproduce later.

## A high-voltage module code can have a low-voltage context

The bulletin covers entries set by the SOBDMC or SOBDMB. Before either module is replaced, Ford asks technicians to investigate low-voltage codes such as P0562 or U3003 in other modules. A system-wide scan is therefore more informative than reading only the module that reported P0A71.

Snapshot voltage is the key distinction. If the related records show voltage below 10 volts, the source directs diagnosis of the 12-volt system first. A normal voltage measurement taken later does not erase evidence of a startup drop. Equally, absence of that evidence does not prove that low voltage caused the fault.

## Preserve the event before clearing it

Record the starting sequence, exact warning, accessible modules and all stored snapshots. The service message does not identify one universally defective battery, cable or converter; the 12-volt diagnosis must establish the actual cause. It also does not authorize ignoring current-sensor or control-module faults that remain after the supporting supply is verified.

Follow the stop warning and arrange appropriate service. This is a diagnostic-priority bulletin, not an owner-level inverter test. Its listed narrative models define this article’s scope; broader database tags attached to the archived document are not used to add unsupported vehicle applications.

## Source and applicability

Ford SSM 50151 original narrative: 2020–2021 Explorer and Aviator hybrids; 2021 Corsair Hybrid, F-150 PowerBoost and Mustang Mach-E with no-start and Stop Safely Now. Broader catalog vehicle tags are not used as fitment evidence.
