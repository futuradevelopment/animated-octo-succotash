# U0028: GM truck communication faults during electrical-load events

U0028 can be one entry in a much broader electrical event on the GM trucks and SUVs covered by PIT5405C. The bulletin describes combinations of steering kickback or reduced assist, stalling, blank displays, unusual door-lock behavior and other symptoms. Events may occur more readily while turning the steering wheel at low speed, when electrical demand increases.

## Multiple modules can share one supply problem

GM lists several possible causes: a poor BCM ground at G218, a starter positive cable contacting its shield, high-resistance or loose battery connections, a discharged or defective battery, and a loose steering-rack power connection. These are alternatives to investigate, not five parts to replace automatically. Reduced electric steering assist applies specifically to the light-duty models equipped with electric power steering.

The bulletin distinguishes a battery that fails its test from a battery discharged because of another connection problem. It also calls for loaded cable testing rather than relying solely on an unloaded voltage reading. Those differences help explain why charging a battery or clearing network codes may leave the actual complaint unresolved.

## Useful evidence for the technician

Record which systems failed together and whether steering input, a cold start or a hot restart coincided with the event. Preserve all module codes. A workshop should follow the model-specific inspection and loaded voltage-drop procedures, with particular attention to shorting hazards around the starter cable.

Do not deliberately reproduce steering loss or stalling on the road. This bulletin supports checking shared electrical infrastructure in the described case; U0028 alone does not identify a defective network module or prove which cable or ground needs repair.
